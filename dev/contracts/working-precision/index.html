Working precision and numerical boundaries

The supported array types and parameter conversions are defined by IN-01 and IN-02. These clauses describe the limits of computations on accepted inputs. They distinguish a library kernel's recovery from a solver's complete execution.

FP-01 Native precision and ownership of arithmetic

Statement.

Iterates retain their native Float32 or Float64 working accuracy. Float32 may stagnate earlier. Some tolerances, residual reports and scalar diagnostics are stored in Float64; that storage does not promote the iterates or certify their accuracy. A tolerance tighter than the working precision does not guarantee corresponding coefficient accuracy. BigFloat is useful for independent test references, not a supported solver working type. The loss-scale type refusals are in IN-02.

With subnormal numbers flushed to zero on the solving thread (set_zero_subnormals(true)), the stopping decisions of PGD and APG are unspecified, the exact zero cases of STOP-21 included: the solver's arithmetic and its compiled comparisons can treat a stored subnormal value as zero.

Finite input entries do not guarantee finite intermediate arithmetic. ProximalBase's least-squares kernels selectively recover some overflow/underflow cases with representable results. ProximalSolvers adds its own matrix products, curvature computations, state updates, stopping and finiteness checks, and does not promise end-to-end recovery of every failed product, curvature, step or objective.

The least-squares solvers read Y, X and the stored loss scale through ProximalBase.leastsquares_data. ProximalBase owns the least-squares value in all three solvers and the coefficient gradient in PGD/APG, with the scale as its computation uses it. PGD/APG use value_from_predictor and value_and_gradient_from_predictor!; CD uses value_from_residual, which returns only a value. ProximalSolvers does not repeat those oracle computations. It owns the image X*x and CD residual/curvature caches, CD coordinate correlations and updates, the lambda_max product of the λmax-based stationarity checks, the column norms of the coordinatewise check (STOP-21), steps and line searches, APG's certified bound, stopping rules and rollback. The coordinatewise check reads ProximalBase's least-squares value at the accepted point for its scale and does not recompute ‖Xx - y‖. Exact oracle accuracy and range statements belong to the section “Least squares at a predictor or residual that the caller holds” in the pinned ProximalBase smooth-function guide. ADMM's use of proximal operators and working-precision diagnostics is specified in ADM-02, and its Cholesky checks in T for the graphical lasso in ADM-04. For the least-squares lasso, ADM-05 specifies the Float64 arithmetic, the proximal operator that ProximalBase computes from a cached SVD and the stationarity score. It states that no cutoff on ρ, the penalty or the coefficients applies.

ProxNewton (PN-01) works in Float64 only. It forms its predictors, the weighted design, the model gradients and the objective in Float64, takes the logistic value and gradient from ProximalBase's predictor oracles, and converts the penalty level to Float64 once; the weighted model's saturated rows and the range errors of its inner solve are specified in PN-02 and PN-05. LogisticADMM (LA-01) also works in Float64 only, with ρ in [1e-10, 1e10]. It forms the image X b, the response and residual of its coefficient step, the scalar roots and the residual tests in Float64, takes the loss value and gradient from ProximalBase's predictor oracle and adds the penalty itself, and holds the dual as w = ρ u. Its scalar root forms σ(t) - y from exp(-|t|); for the label 0 and t between about -745.13 and -708.40 this value is subnormal rather than 0, and below that range it is 0 (LA-03). The accuracy of the root is stated there as a tested backward error, and the failure boundary in LA-05.

converged has the algorithm-specific meaning in RES-01. Independently evaluate the objective and KKT residual when accuracy matters, especially for Float32. For nonnegative lasso, use the constrained residual in STOP-16, which accounts for feasible directions at a zero coefficient.

Applies to. All supported solvers and precisions.

Code. src/solvers/image_models.jl; src/cd/leastsquares.jl; src/solve.jl; ProximalBase least-squares oracles at the dependency pin.

Tests. FP-02 Recovered values and different oracle boundaries

Statement.

CD asks value_from_residual for the loss at its cached residual. Its finite-final- objective refusal and the loss's subnormal error bound are specified in CD-09. A recovered finite loss can still give a nonfinite loss-plus-penalty sum. The exceptional recovery is conditional on ProximalBase's range and margin statements; near the overflow threshold a result can remain Inf.

PGD and APG obtain start/trial values from ProximalBase. PGD's start and gradient refreshes use value_and_gradient_from_predictor!, as does APG's stationarity check; the other value-only evaluations use value_from_predictor. When the guide's conditions hold, these oracles can recover a finite value even if the sum of squares would overflow in T. This does not promise a finite gradient.

In particular, if subtraction z-y overflows although the entries of z and y are finite, value_from_predictor can recover the value while value_and_gradient_from_predictor! returns a nonfinite value and nonfinite gradient entries. At such a start PGD refuses a nonfinite combined-oracle value with ArgumentError("start from a point where f is finite"), even if the value-only oracle would be finite. APG can accept the recovered value-only start, then fail its first step with ErrorException("the gradient step did not produce a finite objective"). The recorded example, unchanged-start observations and issue tracking are in ORACLE-02; the general restoration boundaries are in RES-03.

Code-derived possibility, not a reproduced guarantee. PGD evaluates a trial with value_from_predictor and refreshes the gradient at an accepted trial with value_and_gradient_from_predictor!. In a branch that accepts from function values, a finite-valued accepted iterate could therefore hold a nonfinite gradient. The baseline notes explicitly state that this accepted-iterate case was not reproduced and has no test. If another step is attempted, x-gradient/L is nonfinite for every finite L; for a penalty whose prox preserves nonfinite entries (such as NormL1), trial values are nonfinite. The expected code path raises L until next_curvature cannot increase it finitely and raises ErrorException("line search failed: the curvature estimate cannot increase finitely"), after which solve! restores the last accepted iterate and rethrows. maxiter or another stop can end the solve first. The separate probe in ORACLE-02 rejected its nonfinite-gradient trial and raised a different line-search error; it does not establish this possibility for every setting.

Applies to. Least-squares values, PGD/APG gradient refreshes and extreme-range oracle inputs.

Code. src/solvers/image_models.jl: oracle calls; src/line_searches.jl: finite-curvature retry; src/solve.jl: last-accepted-point restoration.

Tests. Existing range coverage is in test/core_regressions.jl and test/initial_curvature.jl; the historical nonfinite-gradient accepted-iterate possibility has no reproducing test, as stated above.

Sources. docs/src/numerics.md:L812, docs/src/numerics.md:L827, docs/src/numerics.md:L868, docs/src/numerics.md:L871, docs/src/numerics.md:L881.

FP-03 Products before loss scaling

Statement.

CD forms the coordinate correlation r' * X_k in the working type before applying the loss scale. PGD/APG form the rounded product X' * d, with d = z-y and z = X*x, then multiply by the scale. If such a product underflows, overflows or cancels before scaling, the solvers do not recover it, even if the exact scaled gradient would lie in range.

A stopping test that reads the same rounded product can be met at a nonminimizer. The λmax-based PGD/APG stationarity checks can read a computed zero gradient and a computed zero lambda_max even where the exact products are nonzero. The coordinatewise check of STOP-21 can read a computed zero gradient entry where the exact product is not zero; its score is then -cᵢ at a zero coefficient and |λ2 xᵢ + cᵢ sign xᵢ|, which can be positive, elsewhere. Its threshold is formed from Φ, the stored loss value plus the ridge contribution (λ2/2)‖x‖²: a computed Φ = 0 refuses each coordinate outside its exact zero scales (a column of stored ±0 entries with no ridge term, and x = y = 0), while a positive ridge contribution can give a normal threshold at a zero stored loss, and a design whose columns are all of the first kind can pass at a nontrivial point of zero loss when every computed score is at most 0. In CD, a zero computed correlation can make a full pass change nothing and satisfy the change test. ORACLE-03 preserves executable underflow and cancellation examples, their high-precision residuals and their objective errors. Keep data and scales in a range where X' * r is representable, and assess important results using independently computed higher-precision products.

The line-search failure mode involving a subnormal or zero computed squared displacement, including its zero-product guard and unhandled nonzero-product case, is specified once in LS-08. It is another limit of working-precision products, not a general promise of convergence at small scales.

Applies to. CD coordinate correlations and PGD/APG gradients and stationarity products.

Code. src/cd/leastsquares.jl: column correlations; src/solvers/image_models.jl: gradient model; src/utils.jl: _lambda_max!, _filled_scales!; src/line_searches.jl: displacement products.

Tests. test/cd_numerics.jl, test/stopping_scale.jl, test/stopping_coordinatewise.jl, test/core_regressions.jl; executable historical examples are retained in ORACLE-03.

FP-04 Equivalent formulations and curvature scaling

Statement.

The PGD/APG initial curvature, its normal-number floor and the relation of its Frobenius bound to the spectral curvature are defined in PGD-02.

An equivalent formulation can change the arithmetic range. For a NormL1 penalty, substituting x = s*u with s > 0 replaces X by s*X and lambda by s*lambda, multiplies curvature by s^2, and maps a start x0 to x0/s. By contrast, replacing X by c*X, y by c*y, and the loss scale alpha by alpha/c^2, while retaining the penalty, preserves the objective and curvature; it preserves the initial curvature up to rounding. These mathematical transformations do not guarantee recovery of an arbitrary extreme-scale solve. ORACLE-01 records the observed examples, solver counts and Julia versions. Overflow of a trial's squared step norm and its scaled handling are specified in LS-06.

Applies to. Least-squares PGD/APG initialization and equivalent lasso rescalings.

Code. src/solvers/image_models.jl: _initial_curvature, _frobenius_upper; src/solvers/proximal_gradient_descent.jl; src/solvers/accelerated_proximal_gradient_descent.jl; src/line_searches.jl.

Tests. test/initial_curvature.jl and test/core_regressions.jl; historical illustrative outcomes are in ORACLE-01.
