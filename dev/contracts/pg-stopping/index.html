PGD and APG stopping

Scope

This is the stopping contract for ProximalGradientDescent and AccProxGradDescent on their supported least-squares problems. See the support matrix. Coordinate descent has its own stopping contract.

Problem

The shared engine minimizes F(x) = f(x) + g(x), with f(x) = s‖Xx - y‖²/2. The penalty determines the stationarity residual below.

Notation

T is the coefficient type, s is the loss scale, x is the accepted iterate, x_previous is the preceding accepted iterate, and L is the accepted inverse step. In the retained proof of STOP-13, s has this same meaning. APG uses y for its extrapolated point in algorithm formulas; the response is identified explicitly where needed. τ and S are defined in STOP-04.

Preconditions and refusals

Input acceptance is specified by IN-01. Option validation and reporting are specified by PGD-04 and PGD-05. A refused stationarity check clears stopping flags; it is not an exception.

Algorithm

STOP-01 A proposed stop is checked at the accepted point

Statement. The iterate, objective and gradient/proximal-residual tests combine by OR: any one can propose a stop. A proposal counts only if the applicable stationarity check passes at the accepted iterate. Otherwise x_converged, f_converged and grad_converged are all cleared, converged=false, and the solve continues unless another exit applies. The residual fields continue to report their diagnostics. The exact-stall rule in STOP-10 applies before this proposal is checked.

converged=true therefore records a computed working-precision check. It is not an exact-arithmetic certificate, a bound on distance to a minimizer, or a promise of a specified number of correct coefficient digits. PGD and APG use the same acceptance policy; their iteration residuals differ. Independent KKT or duality-gap evaluation is needed to assess a returned point.

Applies to. PGD and APG; dense Vector{T}; T = Float32 or Float64.

Stopping

STOP-02 The three proposal tests

Statement. The iterate test is norm(x - x_previous) < xtol * max(norm(x), 1). For norm(x) < 1, its threshold is absolute. Its strict inequality and the exact-stall exclusion are intentional.

For ftol > 0, the objective test holds if either abs(F - F_previous) / abs(F) < ftol or abs(F - F_previous) < eps(abs(F) + abs(F_previous)). The quotient is evaluated as a diagnostic in Float64, is 0 when the objectives are equal (including both zero), and is Inf when F = 0 ≠ F_previous. There is no absolute term in the denominator. The second test holds for equal finite-sum objectives and, when both values are normal and their absolute-value sum is finite, permits a difference of about two units in the last place. If that sum overflows, eps is NaN and this second test does not hold. Neither form establishes accuracy by itself.

For the gradient test, let r be the iteration's gradient/proximal residual, m the largest such residual so far, N = max(‖∇f‖∞, ‖q‖∞) with q the residual of the proximal step, and ρ = (L * ‖x‖∞) * eps(T), the rounding resolution of L x. Form L * ‖x‖∞ first to avoid premature underflow of L * eps(T). If the first product overflows to Inf, the resolution comparison rejects both normalizers. APG uses ∇f and the proximal step at its extrapolated point. The test is

(r < gradtol * m  and  ρ < m)   or   (r < gradtol * N  and  ρ < N).

The comparisons use the floating-point products gradtol * m and gradtol * N; neither normalizer has an absolute term. A zero residual passes only against a positive product, so it does not pass when m = N = 0 or the relevant product underflows to zero. A scale no larger than ρ decides nothing. The running maximum is updated even on an exact-stall iteration.

Applies to. PGD and APG; dense Vector{T}; T = Float32 or Float64.

STOP-03 Scalar and weighted lasso residuals

Statement. For NormL1(λ0), the residual is the least-infinity-norm element of ∇f(x) + λ ∂‖x‖₁, with the working level λ from IN-02. The entry score is max(|∇fᵢ| - λ, 0) at xᵢ = 0 and |∇fᵢ + λ sign(xᵢ)| elsewhere. The full-point call is ProximalBase's kkt_violation with the working penalty used for the objective. With NormL1(λ0, w), the call is kkt_violation(NormL1(T(λ0), w), ∇f(x), x). Entry i uses cᵢ = T(λ0) wᵢ, multiplied and rounded in T. Its score is max(|∇fᵢ| - cᵢ, 0) at zero and |∇fᵢ + cᵢ sign(xᵢ)| elsewhere. The Vector{T} weights are used as supplied.

The scale is S = max(λ, λmax), where λ = T(λ0) without weights and λ = maxᵢ cᵢ with weights, and λmax is the computed s‖Xᵀy‖∞. The equality maxᵢ cᵢ = T(λ0) * maximum(w) uses monotonicity of rounding. Unit weights reproduce the unweighted coefficients and scale. All-zero weights remove the penalty and give the least-squares scale S = λmax; with zero response as well, the zero-scale rule in STOP-04 applies.

For a raw LogisticLoss, ∇f is the logistic gradient and λmax = ‖Xᵀ(½ - y)‖∞, the norm of its value at zero (IN-09).

A zero-weight coordinate has cᵢ = 0, is unpenalized and is scored by its full |∇fᵢ|, even with a large λ0. Each coefficient must already satisfy IN-01: it is finite and normal when both factors are positive; a zero level or weight gives zero. An infinite coefficient iterate gives a nonfinite residual (Inf, or NaN if a NaN is read), which cannot pass.

Applies to. PGD and APG; dense Vector{T}; T = Float32 or Float64.

STOP-16 Nonnegative-lasso residual and scale

Statement. For unweighted NormL1Nonneg(λ0), PGD checks the accepted point with ProximalBase's full-point kkt_violation using the working penalty from IN-02. For IndNonnegative(), PGD uses NormL1Nonneg(zero(T)) for the check because both penalties have the same value and normal cone on the feasible set. With λ the level of that penalty, the entry score is abs(∇f(x)[j] + λ) when x[j] > 0, and max(-(∇f(x)[j] + λ), 0) when x[j] == 0. The residual is the maximum entry score. An infeasible point has residual Inf and cannot pass. A positive derivative at a zero coordinate can satisfy constrained stationarity.

The scale is S = max(λ, λmax), where λmax is the computed symmetric magnitude s‖Xᵀy‖∞. This is not the one-sided penalty level at which zero solves the constrained problem. Apply the computed threshold, tolerance floor, strict comparison and scale guards of STOP-04. Passing records a computed residual check, with the working-precision limitations of STOP-12.

Applies to. ProximalGradientDescent with unweighted NormL1Nonneg or IndNonnegative(); dense Vector{T}; T = Float32 or Float64. The feasible-start requirement and unsupported combinations are specified in IN-07.

STOP-04 Threshold arithmetic and scale guards

Statement. For a dedicated KKT check, form τ = T(max(gradtol, 64 eps(T))) and threshold = τ * S in T. The stop passes precisely when S is finite, threshold ≥ floatmin(T), the computed residual is finite, and that residual is strictly below threshold. The scale and residual are penalty-specific (STOP-03, STOP-05, STOP-06, STOP-16).

The threshold need not be finite: a huge gradtol can make conversion or multiplication produce Inf, and a finite residual then passes. Dividing the residual by S and comparing with the unrounded tolerance is the exact-arithmetic interpretation, not an additional floating-point guarantee. A gradtol below 64 eps(T), including gradtol = 0, uses that floor.

Common positive rescaling of loss and penalty preserves the normalized inequality in exact arithmetic. In working precision the rounded quantities and the normal-threshold guard can change the outcome even when the scales remain representable. For Float32 X = [1], response y = [1], x = 0 and default gradtol, s = λ = 1 passes while s = λ = 1f-35 does not: the latter threshold is below floatmin(Float32).

If both λ and λmax are zero, or the threshold is below floatmin(T), no stop passes; the solve reaches maxiter unless another exit intervenes. For the unweighted lasso, starting at x = 0 with λ ≥ λmax gives G = 0 and stops at the first proposal when S > 0 and the normal-threshold guard holds. Under ftol = 0, the unchanged iterate makes no proposal and can instead run to maxiter with converged=false.

Applies to. PGD and APG; dense Vector{T}; T = Float32 or Float64.

STOP-05 Group residual and group scale

Statement. For GroupNormL2{T}(λ0, groups, w) whose groups partition 1:p, let cₖ = T(λ0) wₖ rounded in T. The residual is ProximalBase's kkt_violation(g, ∇f(x), x) = max(0, maxₖ scoreₖ). A group with any nonzero entry has score ‖∇_G f + cₖ x_G/‖x_G‖₂‖₂; a zero group has score ‖∇_G f‖₂ - cₖ. A zero weight gives ‖∇_G f‖₂.

Use S = max(maxₖ cₖ, Λ), where Λ = maxₖ ‖[∇f(0)]_{Gₖ}‖₂, the computed group counterpart of s maxₖ ‖[Xᵀy]_{Gₖ}‖₂. In exact arithmetic zero is a minimizer iff each group norm of ∇f(0) is no larger than its own coefficient. The guards and computed threshold are STOP-04.

Each group norm is LinearAlgebra's norm: it scales a block before squaring when squaring its largest magnitude underflows to zero. This prevents the squaring operation alone from losing Λ, as tested at a scale s = 1e-300; it cannot recover an entry of s Xᵀy already rounded to zero or a subnormal value. Singleton groups with unit weights have the unweighted lasso's scale where no entry of ∇f(0) has a subnormal square. The two penalties' iterates and residuals can still differ through rounding. Passing bounds the computed residual, not the exact residual.

Applies to. PGD and APG; dense Vector{T}; T = Float32 or Float64.

STOP-06 Elastic-net residual and ridge-independent scale

Statement. For ElasticNet(λ1, λ2), use the least-infinity-norm element of ∇f(x) + T(λ2)x + T(λ1)∂‖x‖₁: max(|∇fᵢ| - T(λ1), 0) at zero and |∇fᵢ + T(λ2)xᵢ + T(λ1)sign(xᵢ)| elsewhere. ProximalBase's kkt_violation(ElasticNet(T(λ1), T(λ2)), ∇f(x), x) receives both levels converted once to T.

The scale is S = max(T(λ1), λmax) and contains neither λ2 nor λ2‖x‖∞. In exact arithmetic the problem is the lasso at level λ1 on X̃ = [√s X; √λ2 I], ỹ = [√s y; 0]: its squared-error loss is s‖Xx-y‖²/2 + λ2‖x‖²/2, its gradient adds λ2 x, and its λ̃max = s‖Xᵀy‖∞. This explains the same scale covariance under a common positive factor on s, λ1 and λ2.

With ElasticNet(0, λ2) and zero response, computed S = 0 refuses all stops even though zero is the ridge solution; the solve can run to maxiter with converged=false, as NormL1(0) does at zero response unless another exit applies. The relevant zero is the computed scale. For X = reshape(Float32[2^24, 1, -2^24, -1], 4, 1) and y = ones(Float32, 4), the exact Xᵀy = 0 need not give a computed λmax = 0: summation in that order can produce λmax = 1, because 2^24 + 1 rounds to 2^24. The resulting check can pass at a point whose exact-data residual is not small relative to computed S.

Initialization can accept finite f(x0) with g(x0) overflowing in T (for example the ridge term (λ2/2)‖x0‖² above floatmax(T)); the common start and rollback policy is RES-01. This KKT check is not the change test used by CDSolver on a dense vector or a one-dimensional SparseIterate.

Applies to. PGD and APG; dense Vector{T}; T = Float32 or Float64.

STOP-07 Generic proximal-gradient stationarity check

Statement. For a supported penalty without a dedicated KKT check (currently NormLinf, IndBox including IndBallLinf, and IndBallL2), use the accepted inverse step L, t = 1/L, and

p = prox_{t g}(x - t ∇f(x))
r = ‖x - p‖∞ / t
S = max(‖∇f(0)‖∞, ‖∇f(x)‖∞)

The proximal evaluation uses the iterations' ProximalBase value_and_prox!. Form the threshold in T as in STOP-04, and require finite S and r, a threshold at least floatmin(T), and r < threshold. In addition, t must be finite and positive; threshold must strictly exceed ρ = L eps(T) ‖x‖∞; and t * threshold must be at least floatmin(T)/eps(T). The last guard excludes a step that subnormal rounding can lose at zero. A zero scale, for example X = 0 or x = 0 with zero response, cannot pass.

A small accepted L makes t large and this mapping a weaker stationarity measure than one formed with true curvature; a penalty whose own curvature is far above L is an extreme case. Passing remains a working-precision indication, with no coefficient-distance certificate. The check requires only the penalty's prox; that does not itself authorize an unsupported penalty.

Applies to. PGD and APG; dense Vector{T}; T = Float32 or Float64.

STOP-08 Work and counters for a proposed stop

Statement. Checks run only when a proposal survives the exact-stall rule. PGD already holds ∇f(x). APG holds the gradient at its extrapolated point and computes ∇f(x) from cached A x, using the same gradient oracle and preallocated xhat and dz buffers. Those buffers are overwritten before their next iteration use. This adds one transposed product per APG check that reaches this evaluation, counted in grad_calls and not in f_calls. A generic check with invalid t returns before computing λmax, this gradient or the prox.

λmax is computed once per solve at the first check that reaches that stage, by one Xᵀy product, and stored in state field λmax; that product is not counted. Weighted lasso adds two scans of w to the KKT pass: one maximum scan and one scan in construction of the penalty with converted level. It creates no coefficient array.

Each generic check with finite positive t adds one proximal evaluation, counted in prox_calls; APG additionally counts its gradient at x. These costs accrue before call limits are tested. The generic check restores its scratch delta_x to x - x_previous before the remaining diagnostics read it.

The dedicated nonnegative-penalty check STOP-16 uses PGD's held gradient and does not add a proximal evaluation.

Applies to. PGD and APG; dense Vector{T}; T = Float32 or Float64.

STOP-09 Objective increases and simultaneous result flags

Statement. With F = f(x) + g(x) and allow_f_increases=false, the first iteration with computed F > F_previous ends the solve. This exit is not a convergence test. With allow_f_increases=true, it is removed, while f_increased still reports the final iteration's comparison.

At any exit the flags describe that same iteration:

FieldMeaning
x_converged, f_converged, grad_convergedTheir proposal tests in STOP-02 held, subject to STOP-10, and the stationarity check passed.
convergedThe OR of those three flags.
f_increasedF > F_previous.
iteration_convergediterations == maxiter; a limit flag, not success.
stagnatedThe condition in STOP-11; it can coincide with a call limit, iteration limit or callback stop.

An increase normally exits with converged=false; it can coexist with converged=true if a proposal and its check pass at that iteration. In particular, with ftol > 0, an increase smaller than about two ulps in the ordinary finite-sum range can pass the objective test. With ftol = 0, only the iterate or gradient proposal can produce convergence at an increase. A successful proposal on the last allowed iteration sets both converged=true and iteration_converged=true. An increase exit on that iteration also sets the latter flag.

Applies to. PGD and APG; dense Vector{T}; T = Float32 or Float64.

STOP-10 Disabling the objective test and recognizing an exact stall

Statement. ftol is finite and nonnegative; -0.0 is stored as 0.0. A positive value that rounds to zero in Float64 is refused. ProximalOptions(ftol=0) turns off both objective-test forms and makes f_converged=false at every iteration; the reported f_residual is still computed. It does not disable objective-increase or limit exits, and the remaining proposals still require stationarity checks.

Only under ftol = 0, an accepted iterate equal to the previous iterate entrywise by value (-0.0 == 0.0) sets neither x_converged nor grad_converged. A difference in any entry is assessed normally, even if its norm rounds to zero. With ftol > 0, an unchanged iterate can propose an iterate stop, which is then checked.

A stall can end if 1/L changes, or for APG if the extrapolated point changes. Neither must change, for example at a zero step or with APG α = 1. A permanently unchanged point with no separate exit reaches maxiter with converged=false and iteration_converged=true; this reports no progress, not that the point is wrong. Zero with an unweighted lasso level at least norm(∇f(0), Inf) is an example of a solution that can remain unchanged.

For APG, an extrapolated point equal to the accepted point while the accepted point differs from its predecessor is not a stall. Its iteration residual L * maximum(abs, x - y) is zero. The gradient proposal then holds when 0 < gradtol * N and ρ < N, or 0 < gradtol * m and ρ < m, after which stationarity is checked.

Applies to. PGD and APG; dense Vector{T}; T = Float32 or Float64.

STOP-11 Float32 PGD stagnation exit

Statement. Under ftol = 0, ProximalGradientDescent on Float32 data stops after an iteration in which both of these hold: the accepted iterate equals the previous one elementwise, and the last rejection of that iteration's line search was decided by the computed curvature of the rejected step, which was finite and replaced the line search's curvature estimate L̂ (the retry's L is then min(Lexact, max(c, L / β)) for the rejected trial's L, so it need not equal the curvature c). The solve ends with stagnated=true and converged=false; x_converged, f_converged and grad_converged are false, and iteration_converged is iterations == maxiter. The stagnation check comes after tracing and after any callback call that is due at that iteration under print_every; it does not change the callback cadence, and a callback that returns true there can still stop the solve itself. A rejection that is not checked (a trial value that is not finite, a NaN estimate, a gradient estimate from a gradient with a nonfinite entry) or whose computed curvature is not finite does not count. A later rejection of the same iteration replaces an earlier one, and nothing carries over from an earlier iteration. An iteration whose first trial is already a zero step has no rejection and does not trigger the stagnation stop (other stops, such as a callback or a limit, still apply); this happens, for example, while L is so large that the first trial step rounds to a zero step, and at x = 0 with a penalty level at or above norm(∇f(0), Inf). An iteration whose accepted iterate differs from the previous one in any entry does not trigger it either, so a solve that keeps moving by rounding error, with no unchanged iterate, is ended by another stop or by maxiter. stagnated=true is not a convergence or accuracy certificate: the point can be inaccurate (a nearly interpolating problem with a small penalty level can stall with a duality gap above the 1e-5 target), and the iterate could still move at a later iteration if the solve continued. For Float64 data, for AccProxGradDescent and with ftol > 0, stagnated is false.

Floating-point rules

STOP-14 Computed objective changes near a minimizer

Statement. An accepted step with the default Lexact = Inf has the non-increase property in exact arithmetic described by the baseline line-search analysis. The computed objective can rise through rounding. Near a minimizer the true per-step change can be smaller than evaluation error, so the sign of the computed change is controlled by rounding.

At tolerances near or beyond working precision, success versus an objective-increase exit can depend on the starting point, CPU vector width, BLAS kernel and compilation (including coverage instrumentation), even when point accuracy is similar. The tests encounter this especially in Float32. An objective proposal may be refused by stationarity, followed by an increase exit before the residual reaches its bound. Check the returned point independently in either case; the historical measurements are in strict-accuracy evidence.

Applies to. PGD and APG; dense Vector{T}; T = Float32 or Float64.

Known limits

STOP-12 Accuracy interpretation and strict-option examples

Statement. Defaults maxiter=200, ftol=1e-6, xtol=1e-8 and gradtol=1e-5 are exploratory settings. Tightening them does not establish a specified error, and reducing gradtol below the floor in STOP-04 does not tighten that computed check. The residual uses rounded gradient products: underflow or cancellation can make a nonstationary point pass. A coordinate on a much smaller gradient scale than λmax can remain inaccurate even when the global relative residual is small. See working precision and stopping evidence.

For PGD, xtol bounds a step scaled by 1/L; the implied proximal-gradient residual is roughly L * xtol * max(norm(x), 1), depending on curvature, coefficient scale and penalty level. Even xtol = 1e-300 can accept a nonzero step, such as from [0.0] to [1e-310]. It is not an accuracy target.

The historical configuration below is a test configuration, not a general recipe. Its measurements, including the fact that they predate the stationarity check, are retained in strict-accuracy evidence.

STOP-13 Independent lasso gap bound

Statement. A check of a lasso solution. Let f(x) = s‖y - Xx‖²/2 with s > 0 and g(x) = λ‖x‖₁ with λ > 0 (an unweighted NormL1), let F = f + g, and let G be the element of ∇f(x) + λ ∂‖x‖₁ of smallest infinity norm, which is the independent KKT residual. Put k = norm(G, Inf) / λ. With the dual point θ = κ s (y - Xx), where κ = min(1, λ / norm(∇f(x), Inf)) and κ = 1 when ∇f(x) = 0, and D(θ) = ⟨θ, y⟩ - ‖θ‖²/(2s), the relative duality gap satisfies

\[\frac{F(x) - D(\theta)}{F(x)} \le 2k + k^2\]

in exact arithmetic, for s > 0, λ > 0 and F(x) > 0. This follows from F(x) - D(θ) = (1-κ)² f(x) + (1-κ) λ‖x‖₁ + κ⟨G, x⟩, 1 - κ ≤ k and F ≥ max(f, λ‖x‖₁). Evaluating k in Float64 from the returned x is an independent numerical check, not a rigorous floating-point certificate: the rounding errors of that evaluation are not bounded here.

Applies to. Unweighted NormL1; positive loss scale s, positive λ, and positive objective F(x).

Code. Analytic statement; no new solver certificate is implemented.

Tests. No implementation change; the stated exact-arithmetic derivation is retained for independent numerical checks.

STOP-15 An accurate fixed point may remain unreported

Statement. Under ftol = 0, an unchanged accepted iterate proposes no stop, so no stationarity check is requested there. The solve can reach maxiter or a stagnation exit with converged=false even when the exact KKT residual is below the bound. This is intentional retained behavior. Evaluate the returned point independently; with ftol > 0, the unchanged iterate can propose a stop and the stationarity check decides.

Applies to. PGD and APG; dense Vector{T}; T = Float32 or Float64.
