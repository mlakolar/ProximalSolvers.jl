Inputs, storage and scalar parameters

These are the common boundary rules for solve!. Solver-specific requirements supplement them: scalar CD, sparse CD, group CD, matrix-response CD, square-root lasso, proximal gradient, accelerated proximal gradient and consensus ADMM.

IN-01 Array types, dimensions and supported combinations

Statement.

A supported vector-response regression solve uses a dense Matrix{T} design X, a dense Vector{T} response y, and coefficients of the same T, where T is Float32 or Float64. Dimensions must be positive and consistent, and the supplied data and coefficient entries must be finite. The sparse-owner gate inspects active values as specified in CDS-01. Mixed array element types are refused, even where ProximalBase function dispatch permits a heterogeneous call. A sparse design is not supported; sparse coefficients do not change the design requirement.

The gradient methods refuse SparseIterate and BlockIterate; scalar CD refuses GroupNormL2, and all CD storage forms refuse NormLinf before any coefficient write or RNG draw. The latter requires a nonseparable proximal operator; CD's coordinate oracle requires a separable penalty. The NormL2 combinations are listed in IN-11. Unsupported inputs and options are refused before mutation. Shape errors use DimensionMismatch; unsupported combinations and options use informative ArgumentErrors. Finite inputs alone do not promise finite intermediate arithmetic; see FP-01.

Least-squares ridge through SqrNormL2 is supported only by CDSolver on a dense vector or one-dimensional SparseIterate, using the same raw loss or single supported positive scalar multiple. Construct it as SqrNormL2(lambda): the coordinate prox requires the declared center type Nothing, that is, SqrNormL2{<:Real, Nothing}. A centered penalty is refused even with a zero center or zero level. A manually constructed union-center type such as SqrNormL2{T, Union{Nothing, Vector{T}}} is also refused even when its stored center is nothing; runtime value alone does not select the prox method. These are input refusals before copying the caller's owner, coefficient writes or RNG draws. PGD/APG, square-root loss, group/block coefficients, matrix-response coefficients and Scaled(SqrNormL2(...), ...) remain unsupported for this concrete penalty. CD-01 specifies the objective and CD-04 the separate numerical step refusals after validation.

Raw LeastSquaresLoss(y, X) means sum(abs2, y-X*x)/2. A single supported positive scalar wrapper scales that loss; use (one(T)/T(n))*LeastSquaresLoss(y, X) when that native normalization is intended. This fixed scaling is distinct from the scaled lasso, which estimates a noise level. The unweighted elastic-net penalty is lambda1 * norm(x, 1) + (lambda2/2) * sum(abs2, x); with weights it is the penalty of IN-03. Full objectives and algorithms are specified by their solver contracts. For complete runnable calls, see Getting started and the sparsity tutorial.

IN-02 Penalty levels and the loss scale

Statement.

For regression solvers, penalty levels must be finite nonnegative real scalars that convert to T without overflow or rounding a nonzero value to zero. A different scalar type is allowed, including a representable Float64 literal in a Float32 solve. A subnormal converted level is accepted for an unweighted NormL1, the supported unweighted NormL1Nonneg in IN-07, NormL2 in IN-11, or the uncentered SqrNormL2 ridge penalty in IN-01; weighted NormL1 and the L1 level of a weighted ElasticNet have the stricter IN-03 rule, which the ridge level of a weighted ElasticNet does not share. For ridge, zero is also accepted. Negative, NaN or infinite levels are refused, as are finite levels whose conversion overflows or turns a nonzero value into zero.

For unweighted NormL1 with PGD or APG, NormL2 with PGD or APG, and unweighted NormL1Nonneg with PGD, public solve! validates the supplied penalty and completes the method's input checks before forming a working penalty with level T(lambda). The solver reuses a penalty already parameterized by T. The working penalty supplies the initial objective, every fused value and proximal evaluation, and the stationarity residual and scale. The caller's penalty is unchanged. IN-03 defines the working penalty for weighted ElasticNet in CD, PGD and APG. ADMM has its own scalar-parameter rules in ADM-01.

For Scaled(LeastSquaresLoss(...), s), the supported loss scale is either:

  • a positive finite Float32 or Float64 that converts to a finite nonzero T, including a subnormal converted value; or
  • a positive fixed-width integer (Int, UInt8 or another Base.BitInteger) or Bool that T represents exactly. Thus 2^24 is accepted for Float32 data and 2^24 + 1 is refused; for Float64 the corresponding examples are 2^53 and 2^53 + 1.

BigInt, BigFloat, Rational, Float16, Irrational, any other scale type, and an integer that T rounds are refused with ArgumentError before any solver writes to the coefficients. An explicit conversion is possible when its result is finite, positive and representable under this rule, for example Float64(s) * LeastSquaresLoss(y, X). The error message suggests that conversion only when it passes the rule and, for an integer, Float64(s) == s; for other types it notes that conversion may round s. The historical API narrowing is recorded in HIST-02.

Validation does not globally convert function parameters or require every intermediate operation to use T. CD, PGD and APG use an accepted loss scale as supplied, combined with T by Julia's promotion. A Float64 scale in a Float32 solve retains its Float64 value, including where its conversion is subnormal in Float32. Arrays and coefficients remain in T; this is not general mixed-precision array support.

CD's LSModel stores s::S, with S = promote_type(T, typeof(s)) when that is a concrete AbstractFloat and S = T otherwise. The coordinate step and the two stopping scales form their products with s in S, rounding to T at their iterate or tolerance boundaries. An integer or Bool scale is used there as T(s). The objective instead comes from the original loss through ProximalBase, which converts integer and Bool scales to Float64, forms its scale product in the promoted type of that scale and T, then rounds to T. For accepted integer scales, T(s) and Float64(s) both equal the real scalar s; the conversions in _lambda_max! and _step_curvature do too. Arithmetic precision can still differ: with Float32 data and scale 3, CD's step and stopping arithmetic is Float32, while the objective product is Float64; with Float64 data both are Float64. The CD scale branches and objective rule give the detailed arithmetic.

The shorthand 1/n produces a Float64 scalar; an accepted value is used as supplied, rather than first rounded to Float32. Prefer explicit native scalars when choosing native arithmetic. In one(T) / T(n), the conversion of n and the division use T; its value can differ from Float64 1/n. The solve uses the value actually supplied, with its loss-scale products in T:

T = Float32
s = one(T) / T(n)
lambda = T(0.1)
f = s * LeastSquaresLoss(y, X)
g = NormL1(lambda)

Applies to. Penalty scalar parameters and the single Scaled least-squares wrapper accepted by the public solvers.

IN-03 Weighted lasso and weighted elastic-net inputs, and rounded coefficients

Statement.

The weighted-lasso penalty is lambda * sum(w[j] * abs(x[j])), represented by NormL1(lambda, w). w must be a Vector{T} with one finite nonnegative entry per coefficient. An infinite weight, another precision, a view, a range or another array type is refused. collect(T, vec(w)) converts another array, taking its entries in linear order. The mutable coefficients must not alias w. The public call is solve!(x, f, g, method), with g = NormL1(lambda, w) and method one of the supported CD, PGD or APG methods.

The level passes IN-02, and T(lambda) must be zero or normal in T: a subnormal level is refused with weights. For every entry, the coefficient c_j = T(lambda) * w_j, with the product rounded in T, must be finite and, when both factors are positive, normal in T. Overflow, underflow to zero and a subnormal positive coefficient are refused with entry j named. The rule is on the rounded coefficient: an exact product slightly below floatmin(T) that rounds up to floatmin(T) is accepted. A zero level or zero weight yields the accepted coefficient zero. A zero weight leaves its variable unpenalized; it does not fix it to zero. CD's separate flat-column rule still sets the coefficient of a zero column to zero, including with a zero weight.

These checks finish before x is copied or written and before the RNG is used. The validation, the PGD/APG stationarity check, and CD's exceptional threshold branch use the checked c_j. PGD/APG score entry j with its own c_j and use the largest c_j for the penalty part of their stop scale; see STOP-03. ProximalBase's ordinary proximal steps instead form thresholds from the supplied lambda and w_j in their working type. This includes the PGD/APG proximal step and CD's prox_block branch. Those thresholds can differ from ones formed from c_j by rounding and, when lambda is not T, by its conversion error. CD-04 specifies the branch selection and the bound on that error. CD retains its change test.

Scaled(NormL1(lambda, w), a) is refused. The weighted-lasso tutorial demonstrates an adaptive lasso with a zero weight using all three solvers; its source script is examples/problems/weighted_lasso.jl.

Weighted elastic net. ElasticNet(lambda1, lambda2, w) means lambda1 * sum(w[j] * abs(x[j])) + (lambda2/2) * sum(abs2, x). A zero weight removes the L1 term for its coordinate. The ridge term applies to each coordinate. The ElasticNet rows of IN-01 list the supported methods and storage for raw least squares and a single admitted loss scale. Scaled(ElasticNet(lambda1, lambda2, w), a) is refused.

Validate the stored lambda1 under the weighted-level rule above. Then validate lambda2 under IN-02, which accepts zero and representable positive subnormals for this ridge level. For w and the rounded coefficients c_j = T(lambda1) * w_j, apply the weight, alias and coefficient rules above. Each solve reads the current weights and completes these checks before copying or writing x and before using the CD random number generator. During the solve the borrowed weights are read-only under IN-05.

After validation the solver uses ElasticNet(T(lambda1), T(lambda2), w) for its objective evaluations, proximal steps and PGD/APG stationarity checks. It converts the levels separately and borrows the supplied weight vector. A penalty with native levels is reused. ProximalBase's ordinary proximal kernels use the working level and the weight as separate factors, which can round differently from forming a threshold from the checked coefficient. STOP-06 specifies the PGD/APG stopping check and CD-04 specifies the exceptional CD step.

Applies to. Weighted NormL1 and weighted ElasticNet with scalar least-squares CD, PGD or APG; group input checks reuse the rounded-coefficient rule in IN-04.

IN-04 Dense group lasso with gradient methods

Statement.

ProximalGradientDescent and AccProxGradDescent accept the group lasso on a dense Vector{T} with the common least-squares inputs and scales above. APG also imposes its dimension bounds. The penalty must be GroupNormL2{T}: its level and weights have the data's element type. Convert explicitly with GroupNormL2(T(lambda), groups, T.(w)). A Float64 group penalty with Float32 data is refused by these methods, although group CD supports it under CDG-01. With g = GroupNormL2(λ, groups, w), the explicit call forms are solve!(x, f, g, ProximalGradientDescent(), options) and solve!(x, f, g, AccProxGradDescent(), options), subject to those type and dimension requirements.

The groups partition 1:p and agree with the layout stored in the penalty at construction. An index above p raises DimensionMismatch; an empty group, an index in multiple groups, an omitted index, or a mutation of g.groups that disagrees with the stored layout raises ArgumentError. ProximalBase's extent and free-coordinate logic use its arrays stored at construction; construct the penalty again to change that layout.

The level and weights pass IN-03, with one rounded c_k = T(lambda) * w_k per group. A zero weight leaves its group unpenalized. x must not alias X, y or the weights. The checks complete before any write to x. ProximalBase supplies the block soft-threshold step. A proposed stop must pass the group stationarity check; APG forms one gradient at the accepted x for each such check and includes it in grad_calls. Singleton groups with unit weights have the NormL1(lambda) stop scale when no entry of gradient(f, 0) has a subnormal square.

Applies to. Dense-vector group least squares with PGD or APG. Scalar CD refuses this penalty; group CD uses its own BlockIterate contract.

PGD alone covers contiguous, permuted and single groups as ranges or vectors, a group of zero columns, and a Float64 loss scale 1e-300.

IN-05 Borrowed inputs and aliasing

Statement.

X and the response are borrowed read-only during a solve: do not mutate them while the solver is running. The mutable coefficient storage must not alias either input. Weighted NormL1, weighted ElasticNet and dense-group PGD/APG also prohibit aliasing the penalty weights. Treat borrowed weights as read-only during the solve, including in callbacks. Matrix-response CD and PGD/APG have additional alias rules in CDM-01 and IN-10. For SparseIterate, alias checks cover the owner's whole backing storage, even when it has no active entries; passing parent(activevalues(x)) or a reshape of it as y or X is refused.

solve! overwrites its supported coefficient storage. Results share that storage; use copy(beta) to retain earlier coefficients. Extended traces copy coefficients and gradients. RES-01 describes the result fields and RES-03 the solver-specific numerical failure boundary. Validation errors occur before mutation; private buffers and counters do not have a general transaction guarantee.

Applies to. All public solves and their coefficient owners; matrix-response CD adds its explicit matrix alias checks in CDM-01.

IN-06 The infinity-norm penalty

Statement.

For NormLinf(lambda), PGD and APG minimize (s/2) * sum(abs2, y-X*x) + lambda * maximum(abs, x), with s = 1 for an unscaled loss. They require dense Vector{T} coefficients, common-type dense inputs, and a finite nonnegative level representable under IN-02. ProximalBase already refuses construction of a negative or NaN level; the other input refusals leave x unchanged. A wrapper such as Scaled(NormLinf(lambda), c) is unsupported. With g = NormLinf(lambda), the default-option calls are solve!(x, f, g, ProximalGradientDescent()) and solve!(x, f, g, AccProxGradDescent()).

The ℓ∞ penalty can tie the largest coefficients at a common magnitude and does not in general give zeros. ProximalBase supplies the proximal operator. In exact arithmetic, zero is a minimizer exactly when lambda >= norm(gradient(f, 0), 1) = s * norm(X' * y, 1); the relevant dual norm here is the 1-norm. Proposed stops use the generic check. CD refuses this penalty for dense vectors, SparseIterate and BlockIterate before mutation or RNG use, as specified in IN-01. See the infinity-norm tutorial.

Applies to. Least-squares PGD and APG with a NormLinf penalty.

IN-07 Nonnegative lasso and nonnegative least squares

Statement.

ProximalGradientDescent, and CDSolver on a dense Vector{T}, accept IndNonnegative() and the unweighted penalty NormL1Nonneg(lambda) with a dense least-squares loss. They target (s/2) * sum(abs2, y-X*x) + lambda * sum(x) over x >= 0, where s is the accepted loss scale and lambda = 0 for IndNonnegative(). The zero-level form NormL1Nonneg(0) gives the same nonnegative least-squares problem. The data and coefficients use the same Float32 or Float64 type and the shapes, finite-data, scale and alias rules of IN-01, IN-02 and IN-05.

The coefficient start must be a finite dense Vector{T} whose entries are all nonnegative (-0.0 is nonnegative). A negative starting entry raises ArgumentError before any write or RNG draw; the solver does not project an infeasible start as an initialization step. The level of NormL1Nonneg must be finite, nonnegative and representable in T without overflow or rounding a nonzero value to zero. A representable level of another scalar type is allowed, as is a subnormal converted level under IN-02.

For NormL1Nonneg, g.w must be nothing. An explicit weight vector is refused even when all its entries are one. APG, a SparseIterate, BlockIterate or Matrix coefficient, matrix responses, square-root loss, Scaled penalties and general penalty compositions are unsupported for these problems. CDSolver on a SparseIterate is deferred: its gate refuses these penalties before any copy of the coefficients or RNG draw. These refusals precede writes. No intercept is fitted.

For PGD the proximal operator enforces feasibility during iterations; its update is specified in PGD-01. A proposed stop uses the constrained stationarity check in STOP-16, with the working-precision guards in STOP-04. The result and failure rules are those of PGD in RES-01 and RES-03. For CDSolver the coordinate step is the least-squares step of CD-01, with the penalty's proximal operator and the exceptional step of CD-04; converged is CD's change test and not a certificate of the constrained KKT conditions (RES-01, CD-03): assess the returned point with an independent constrained KKT score. The cached residual and the reported minimum are those of CD-05 and CD-09. A returned point is nonnegative and finite after a completed run. The NNLS tutorial compares the constraint alone with a positive penalty. The nonnegative sparse-regression tutorial checks feasibility, the objective and a separately computed constrained KKT residual.

Applies to. Dense-vector PGD and CD with least squares and IndNonnegative() or unweighted NormL1Nonneg.

IN-08 Box- and ball-constrained least squares

Statement.

For dense least squares and either gradient solver, use IndBox(l, u) or the symmetric box from IndBallLinf(r). The design, response and coefficient vector obey IN-01 and IN-05. The solver checks feasibility of the supplied starting vector before writing to it.

An array bound is copied by ProximalBase into a vector in linear order. The stored vector must have element type T and one entry per coefficient. Scalar bounds may be Float32, Float64, fixed-width integers or Bool and must convert exactly to T. A lower bound of -Inf or an upper bound of Inf denotes an unbounded side. Equal finite bounds fix the coefficient. The stored bounds must form valid intervals and be disjoint from the coefficient storage. Treat the stored bounds as read-only during a solve.

Use IndBallL2(r) for four coefficients and a finite nonnegative radius of the same type T. At the pinned ProximalBase revision, the rounded projection is checked and corrected for ordinary trial sums of squares and positive radii of at least 2sqrt(4floatmin(T) / eps(T)). Zero radius gives the singleton at zero. At exceptional scales the upstream oracle can return Inf after projection. In that case an iteration with a nonfinite objective raises and restores the last accepted point.

The coordinate-descent solver and coefficient storage other than a dense Vector{T} are unsupported for this indicator. Stopping proposals use the generic proximal-gradient check.

Applies to. Dense Float32 and Float64 PGD and APG with a least-squares loss and the concrete indicators above.

Code. src/solve.jl: _check_penalty, _check_box, _check_problem.

Tests. test/constrained_least_squares.jl checks feasibility, optimality, input rules and both precisions.

IN-09 Sparse logistic regression with proximal gradient methods

Statement.

ProximalGradientDescent and AccProxGradDescent accept ProximalBase's raw LogisticLoss(y, X) with NormL1(lambda) or NormL1(lambda, w) on a dense Vector{Float64} and minimize (ProxNewton and LogisticADMM minimize the same objective, and the four solvers share the core checks of the gate below; LogisticADMM adds checks on memory ranges and on the start (LA-01), and AccProxGradDescent has the limits of APG-08; the solvers and results are specified in PN-01 to PN-05, APG-08 and LA-01 to LA-05)

\[F(b)=\sum_{i=1}^n \{\log(1+e^{\eta_i})-y_i\eta_i\}+\lambda\sum_{j=1}^{p} w_j|b_j|,\qquad \eta=Xb,\]

with w_j = 1 without weights. The loss is the sum over the observations, not the mean: F/n is the mean negative log likelihood plus (lambda/n) * sum(w .* abs.(b)), so a mean-scale level λ is passed as n * λ. There is no automatic intercept: a column of ones can be included in X, and with penalty weight zero its coefficient is an unpenalized intercept. Penalty weights are the weights of NormL1; they are not the observation weights of LogisticLoss.

The accepted inputs are:

  • a LogisticLoss{Float64} whose design is a Matrix{Float64} and whose response is a Vector{Float64} with each entry equal to 0 or 1, constructed without observation weights or an offset (weights === nothing and offset === nothing);
  • coefficients x::Vector{Float64}; X with positive row and column counts, length(y) == size(X, 1) and length(x) == size(X, 2); finite entries of x and X;
  • a NormL1 penalty whose level and weights satisfy IN-02 and IN-03; a zero weight leaves its coefficient unpenalized;
  • coefficients that do not alias X, y, the scratch f.tmp of the loss or the penalty weights.

The gate runs at each call that reaches it, after the keyword check of solve!, and before any write to x. It reads y and X at that call, so an entry of y changed to 0.5, 2.0 or NaN, or an entry of X changed to a nonfinite value, after the construction of the loss is refused. ProximalBase's constructor accepts responses in [0, 1]; this solver accepts binary responses only. Each refusal is an ArgumentError, a DimensionMismatch for the shapes. For a call with supported keywords and options, and with coefficient storage that the solver's gate accepts, the checks run in the order below; the keyword checks of solve!, the option check of ADMMSolver and the storage-type checks of the CD gates (a SparseIterate or BlockIterate of Float32 or Float64) come first. For a SparseIterate, CD's owner gate also refuses IndNonnegative and NormL1Nonneg before it reads the loss, with a message that does not name the solvers of the logistic loss. The order is: a solver other than PGD, APG, ProxNewton and LogisticADMM (these two first refuse a loss that is not a LogisticLoss, PN-01 and LA-01); a Scaled wrapper; the element type of the loss and the array types of X, y and x; observation weights or an offset; the shapes; a penalty that is not a NormL1, then the rules of IN-02 and IN-03, including the alias check of the weights; aliasing of x with X, y or f.tmp; nonfinite entries of x or X; an entry of y other than 0 and 1.

CDSolver (dense Vector, SparseIterate, BlockIterate and Matrix coefficients) refuses a LogisticLoss or a Scaled one, in a call that reaches the logistic refusal (see the precedence above), with an ArgumentError that names ProximalGradientDescent, AccProxGradDescent, ProxNewton and LogisticADMM, without a write to the coefficients or a draw from the RNG; for a BlockIterate this refusal comes before the momentum check. The calls that the owner gate refuses first also refuse before any write or draw. ADMMSolver refuses it on a Vector or a Matrix with an ArgumentError that names LogisticADMM, after its keyword and option checks and before a write to z or to a supplied u (LA-01). A Matrix coefficient is supported by ProximalGradientDescent for a LogDetLoss (PGD-06) and for matrix-response least squares (IN-10), and by AccProxGradDescent for matrix-response least squares (IN-10). The matrix coefficient gates of both solvers send a logistic loss to the gate described above, which refuses the matrix as a coefficient that is not a dense Vector{Float64}, after the earlier checks of its order (for example the Scaled refusal). Scaled(LogisticLoss(y, X), a) is refused because ProximalBase provides no predictor oracles for it. For a positive finite a, the raw loss with the level lambda / a has the same minimizers in exact arithmetic, and is accepted when the penalty with that level passes IN-02 and, with weights, all of IN-03 (its level, weights and coefficients). Other penalties, including wrapped ones, Float32 data, sparse designs, views and other array types are refused.

Iteration. The trial sequence of ProximalGradientDescent, PGD-01, is unchanged. As for least squares, an unweighted NormL1 enters through the working penalty with level T(lambda) of IN-02; a weighted one is used as supplied in the trial steps, and STOP-03 gives its stationarity check. In PGD a trial forms the image z = X x once, in a private buffer, and takes its value from ProximalBase's value_from_predictor(f, z). The gradient of an accepted point comes from value_and_gradient_from_predictor!(G, d, f, z) at the cached image, which writes d and G = Xᵀ d, without forming X x again; d = σ(z) - y in exact arithmetic, and ProximalBase evaluates the loss and d with formulas that avoid cancellation in the saturated tails. The image, derivative and gradient buffers of either solver are private to the solve and disjoint from X, y, f.tmp and x.

AccProxGradDescent. It uses the same ProximalBase functions value_from_predictor and value_and_gradient_from_predictor!, each run only for a finite image (see the paragraph on overflowed images). Each trial forms its image X x and takes its value from value_from_predictor when that image is finite. For the gradient of a step the solver uses the fused oracle value_and_gradient_from_predictor! at the cached image of the extrapolated point, which is the same combination of cached images as the point (APG-01). The stationarity check of a proposed stop forms a separate gradient at the accepted point from its cached image (APG-05, STOP-08). The trial sequence is that of APG-01, the initial curvature is that of PGD-02 with the factor ¼, and the stopping check is the one below. In place of the loss-value test, the gradient-mode test and the recheck of PGD, a nonzero trial can be accepted only if the image bound of APG-06 with s = ¼ is certified. APG-08 states the guards that precede it, what it certifies and what it leaves to the computed values.

Overflowed images. When the image z has a nonfinite entry, a guard skips ProximalBase's predictor oracle and the loss value is Inf. (ProximalBase gives a row with an infinite predictor on the side of its label the contribution zero.) At the start, solve! then refuses the coefficients with the ArgumentError "start from a point where f is finite", before any write. In a trial of ProximalGradientDescent, the line search takes its nonfinite-value branch (LS-02) before its zero-step, curvature and Lexact exits: the trial is not accepted, and the search retries with L/β, beyond Lexact if need be, when L/β, converted to the type of L, is a finite increase of L; otherwise it raises an error and solve! restores the last accepted point (LS-09). PGD refreshes the gradient only at a trial with a finite value. A finite image goes to ProximalBase's loss; for example a logit of 800, beyond log(floatmax(Float64)) ≈ 709.78, gives the loss 800 for the label 0. For AccProxGradDescent a trial with a nonfinite image is rejected and L is raised where L/β is a finite increase (an error and rollback otherwise), and a nonfinite value or gradient at the extrapolated point restarts the step or, without momentum, fails with rollback (APG-03, APG-08).

Curvature factor. For this loss, in exact arithmetic, ∇²f(b) = Xᵀ diag(σᵢ(1 - σᵢ)) X ⪯ ¼ XᵀX, since 0 ≤ σᵢ(1 - σᵢ) ≤ ¼. The line search uses the factor ¼ where least squares uses its loss scale s; AccProxGradDescent uses it in its certified test (APG-08), and the recheck below is that of ProximalGradientDescent. The initial inverse step is that of PGD-02 with s = ¼: min(1, ¼ B²) rounded to T, with B the computed upper estimate of ‖X‖_F (_frobenius_upper); a positive value that rounds below floatmin(T), to zero included, gives floatmin(T), and X = 0 gives 1. The column scan of PGD-02 returns the cap 1 without forming B when, after some column, the Float64 sum of squares of the entries scanned so far is finite and at least 2/¼ = 8; a sum that overflows ends the scan and goes to the scaled _frobenius_upper estimate. A rejected trial that is eligible under LS-03 is rechecked with c = m ¼ ‖fl(X d̂)‖² / ‖d̂‖² (m = 1 in function-value mode and m = 2 in gradient mode). In exact arithmetic, for the trial x and the step d = x - u from the last accepted point u (x_previous in LS-02):

  • f(x) ≤ f(u) + ⟨∇f(u), d⟩ + ⅛‖X d‖² (Taylor's formula with integral remainder and the Hessian bound), so with m = 1, c ≤ L implies TFOCS's function-value test f(x) ≤ f(u) + ⟨∇f(u), d⟩ + (L/2)‖d‖²;
  • ⟨∇f(x) - ∇f(u), d⟩ ≤ ¼‖X d‖², so with m = 2, c ≤ L implies TFOCS's gradient test 2⟨∇f(x) - ∇f(u), d⟩ ≤ L‖d‖²;
  • after a rejection, TFOCS's estimate is L̂ = 2(f(x) - f(u) - ⟨∇f(u), d⟩)/‖d‖² in function-value mode (where it exceeds L) and L̂ = 2⟨∇f(x) - ∇f(u), d⟩/‖d‖² in gradient mode; each is at most c, so a retry min(Lexact, max(c, L/β)) is at least TFOCS's min(Lexact, max(L̂, L/β)) and its step 1/L is at most TFOCS's.

In the code the recheck accepts the trial when the computed c ≤ L; when it rejects the trial again, a finite computed c replaces L̂ in the retry, and a nonfinite one leaves TFOCS's estimate (LS-03).

A scaled displacement (LS-06) gives the same c in exact arithmetic. For least squares the inequalities hold with equality, with s in place of ¼; for the logistic loss they are one-sided bounds, which is what the recheck needs. These statements are in exact arithmetic: the computed c can be below the exact quotient after rounding or range loss (LS-05), and the recheck carries no rounding certificate, as for least squares. After a rejection the retry uses the global factor ¼ along d̂ rather than the local weights σᵢ(1 - σᵢ), which can be smaller, so the step can be shorter than the local curvature allows. With the line search's α < 1, the factor α applied to L at the start of each iteration lets the step grow again; with α = 1, L does not decrease between iterations.

Stopping. A proposed stop passes the check of STOP-03 with the logistic gradient and the guards of STOP-04. The scale is S = max(λ, λmax), with λ = T(lambda) * maximum(w) (T(lambda) without weights) and λmax = ‖Xᵀ(½ - y)‖∞ = ‖∇f(0)‖∞, since σ(0) = ½; each ½ - yᵢ is ±½, exact for a binary response. λmax is formed at the first stationarity check of a solve, with the difference ½ - y in the state's image-length buffer, which the iterations write before they read it. In exact arithmetic the entry of an intercept column in Xᵀ(½ - y) is n (½ - ȳ), one of the candidates in that maximum. Results, counters and rollback follow RES-01 and RES-03.

Unattained minima. The problem need not have a minimizer. Let rᵢ = 2yᵢ - 1 and call coordinate j free when its coefficient cⱼ = λ wⱼ is zero. In exact arithmetic:

  • if some direction d that moves only free coordinates has rᵢ xᵢᵀd ≥ 0 for every observation and X d ≠ 0 (the data are separated along unpenalized directions), then no loss term increases along d and at least one decreases, so F decreases strictly along d and its infimum is not attained;
  • if no direction d ≠ 0 that moves only free coordinates has rᵢ xᵢᵀd ≥ 0 for every observation, the sublevel sets of F are bounded and a minimizer exists. This includes separated data with an intercept column of weight zero, positive weights on all other coefficients, a positive level and both classes present: the only candidate directions move the intercept alone, and the two classes rule them out.

The simplest unattained case is a one-class sample with an unpenalized intercept column. With the other coefficients fixed, F decreases strictly as the intercept increases when every yᵢ = 1, and as it decreases when every yᵢ = 0, so no minimizer exists. On the path where every other coefficient is zero and the intercept tends to +∞ (or -∞), the whole gradient of the loss tends to zero, so the computed KKT residual there can become arbitrarily small; in Float64 it underflows to zero at a large enough intercept. It can then fall below the threshold of STOP-04, and converged = true can be reported at a finite point although no minimizer exists. The solver does not detect this case; converged records only the outcome of the computed checks of STOP-01 and STOP-03, and it is not evidence that a minimizer exists or that the returned point is accurate.

Entry thresholds. With weights, each coordinate is also held to its own threshold τ max(cⱼ, λmax) when the largest coefficient exceeds λmax (STOP-03), so an extreme weight on one coordinate does not loosen the test of the others. A free intercept is held to τ λmax. The fixture X = [fill(1e150, 5) zeros(5)], y = [0.0, 1.0, 1.0, 1.0, 1.0], NormL1(1e300, [0.0, 1.0]) has the minimizer b = [log(4)/1e150, 0.0]. A single scale S = 1e300 admitted a stop far from it; the outcomes measured with the single threshold and with the entry thresholds (#464) are in the entry-threshold evidence. The thresholds still use λmax = ‖∇f(0)‖∞, which a free intercept can inflate when the response is far from balanced (STOP-12).

The deferred logistic combinations are listed in DEF-01.

IN-10 Matrix-response entrywise lasso with PGD and APG

Statement. For least squares with a matrix response, PGD and APG accept finite, same-type dense Matrix{T} inputs X of size n × p, Y of size n × K, and a writable coefficient matrix B of size p × K. T is Float32 or Float64, and n, p and K are positive. Use raw LeastSquaresLoss(Y,X) or one positive scalar wrapper under IN-02 and unweighted NormL1(lambda) at a finite, nonnegative representable level. The objective is s‖Y − XB‖_F²/2 + λ Σⱼₖ |B[j,k]| with native working penalty level λ and supplied loss scale s. All entries participate, including the diagonal of a square coefficient matrix. Rectangular coefficients, rank deficiency and a zero level are accepted. Use s = 1/n for normalization by the number of observations.

Refusals occur before any coefficient write. An invalid shape or empty dimension throws DimensionMismatch. A different loss, a nested scale, an invalid scalar parameter, unsupported storage or element type, weights of any shape, nonfinite data or coefficients, or a reported Base.mightalias between B and any of X, Y and the loss scratch tmp throws ArgumentError. APG applies APG-07 before state creation. Borrowed inputs follow IN-05. On return the result's minimizer is the supplied B, with the failure boundary in RES-03.

Applies to. Matrix-response least squares with PGD or APG and unweighted NormL1.

IN-11 Euclidean-norm penalty with PGD and APG

Statement.

For ProximalBase's NormL2(lambda), ProximalGradientDescent and AccProxGradDescent minimize (s/2) * sum(abs2, y - X*x) + lambda * norm(x, 2), with s = 1 for a raw loss and lambda is the working level. The penalty is the norm of the whole coefficient vector. The objective is equivalent to using GroupNormL2(T(lambda), [1:p], [one(T)]) with one group and an explicit unit weight. The two proximal operators can differ in their floating-point rounding. The GroupNormL2 input rule in IN-04 also imposes a separate range check on the group level.

The coefficients are a dense Vector{T}, T being Float32 or Float64. The data, the loss (raw, or one positive Scaled under IN-02), shapes, finiteness and aliasing follow IN-01 and IN-05. The level must be a finite nonnegative real scalar that passes the representability guard of IN-02; that clause also gives the working-level conversion and the subnormal and zero cases. The level need not have type T, which differs from the same-type requirement of IN-04 for GroupNormL2.

For otherwise valid inputs, PGD and APG refuse a wrapped NormL2, matrix or iterate-owner coefficients, and logistic or square-root least-squares loss with ArgumentError before mutating the coefficients. CD refuses the penalty at its corresponding input gate. A proposed stop uses the dedicated check of STOP-17; the proximal step is in PGD-01.

Applies to. ProximalGradientDescent and AccProxGradDescent with dense Vector{Float32} or Vector{Float64} coefficients, least-squares loss and NormL2.

Code. src/solve.jl: _check_penalty (NormL2 under the two gradient kinds); src/utils.jl: _converted_penalty, _working_gradient_penalty.

Its refusal cases cover the level guard, wrapped penalties, matrix-response PGD and APG, logistic and square-root losses, and dense-vector CD, with unchanged coefficients. It also checks rollback in both gradient methods.
