Stopping audit: observations and limitations

This is historical numerical evidence, not a new test run or a solver guarantee. The current criterion table and its qualifications are in RES-01.

evidence-stopping-352 Audit population and provenance

Statement.

This page preserves measured limits of the stopping tests for scalar CD, group CD, PGD and APG. The numbers come from the Stage 0 audit of issue #352, run at revision b8a2c04 on Julia 1.13.4; Julia 1.10.13 was run as well and gave close but not identical counts. The audit used 11 small designs (20 to 60 rows, 8 to 60 columns) and evaluated each solve in 600-bit arithmetic on the rounded inputs the solver received. At that revision solve! refused a weighted NormL1, so the NormL1 problems of the audit were unweighted, and the counts on this page do not cover a weighted NormL1. These are high-precision evaluations, not proofs, and the counts are for those designs: they are not rates to expect on other problems. The cached-residual evidence reports a separate, later study (issue #378, revision 8cc0222) with its own designs and starts; its counts are a different population and are not to be combined with those of the audit.

Applies to. Stage 0 audit of issue #352 at revision b8a2c04; the separate #378 population is explicitly excluded.

Code. Solver source at the recorded audit revision; historical audit material linked above.

Tests. 11 small designs, 600-bit evaluations of rounded inputs, Julia 1.13.4 and a comparison run on Julia 1.10.13. Counts are not expected rates for other problems.

Sources. docs/src/stopping-limits.md:L1, docs/src/stopping-limits.md:L3.

AUDIT-02 Positive-penalty change-test observations

Statement.

Measured at the default threshold, with a positive penalty. The benchmark target of the repository, a relative suboptimality of 1e-8 in Float64 and 1e-5 in Float32, is a target of the repository's benchmark. It is not part of the documented contract of converged = true.

  • Scalar CD (lasso and elastic net): all 16,218 converged solves that have a tight reference were within the benchmark target, and the worst was 1.3e-9. A further 478 converged solves on the rank-deficient design have no tight reference; their suboptimality is between 0 and 1.4e-6, which does not decide the comparison with 1e-8.
  • Group CD: 48 of 3,529 converged solves were above the benchmark target. With relative = true these are 12 solves, in Float64 only, with the largest excess between 2.8e-7 and 3.7e-7 (a lower and an upper bound; two underdetermined designs at the lowest penalty level). With relative = false they are 36 solves, with the largest excess between 4.6e-5 and 4.7e-5, on the design whose column norms span six decades; its KKT residual was 3.4e-4 λmax in Float64 and 5.3e-4 λmax in Float32.

These group results are inside the contract of converged = true: the flag reports the change test, and the bounds above are not a bound on the suboptimality. See also The stopping statistic of the group lasso.

The scale s * norm(X_k) * norm(y) and lambda_max differed by a factor of 5.5 on the equicorrelated audit design with correlation 0.95. This measured factor is specific to that design. The general scale-mismatch and cancellation limits are specified by CD-06.

Applies to. The audit population at the default change threshold with a positive unweighted penalty; no weighted NormL1 coverage is claimed.

Code. Historical scalar/group CD solves; current change rules are CD-03, CD-06 and CDG-07.

Tests. Historical high-precision audit comparisons and the tight-reference qualifications retained above.

Sources. docs/src/stopping-limits.md:L74, docs/src/stopping-limits.md:L79, docs/src/stopping-limits.md:L84, docs/src/stopping-limits.md:L88, docs/src/stopping-limits.md:L95.

AUDIT-03 Rank-deficient warm starts

Statement.

Related degradation appears in the sweep. With λ = 0 on the rank-deficient design, the exact least-squares minimizer has large cancelling coefficients, and the warm starts taken from it give 80 converged solves per precision at the default threshold, with an exact KKT residual up to 0.063 λmax in Float64 and 0.16 λmax in Float32; the cold starts on the same problems end at 1.7e-8 λmax or below in Float64 and 5.7e-8 λmax or below in Float32. The stopping pass changed nothing in 24 of the Float32 rows and in none of the Float64 rows; the others stopped on a small nonzero change. The empty-pass mechanism is therefore established for the warm-start example, not for all of these rows.

Applies to. The audit sweep with lambda = 0 on the rank-deficient design and the stated warm/cold starts.

Code. Historical scalar CD sweep; the explicit empty-pass mechanism is CD-07.

Tests. 600-bit reference residuals from the historical sweep; no universal empty-pass claim is inferred.

Sources. docs/src/stopping-limits.md:L201.

AUDIT-04 Float32 observations

Statement.

The square-root-lasso counts below were measured at the solve's stop check, before the bounded fallback added in f573224f. They do not count certifications or changed returned points obtained by that later fallback. Its current rules are CDQ-05, and its separate reported evaluation is EVIDENCE-SQRT-406. The original audit counts and qualifications follow.

  • Coordinate descent with relative = false. At the default tol = 1e-7 with relative = false, 130 of 5,700 Float32 scalar CD solves and 40 of 1,830 Float32 square-root solves ran to maxpasses = 100000 in the sweep; the scalar ones that have a tight reference ended within 5.1e-8 of the minimum. With the default relative = true, no scalar solve of the sweep reached maxpasses in Float32. CD-03 specifies that Float32 rounding of the coefficients can keep the change above the threshold. Keep relative = true, or choose a tol well above eps(Float32) times the size of the coefficients.

  • PGD and APG with the default options. In the sweep, 70 of 570 Float32 PGD solves and 67 of 570 Float32 APG solves ended on the objective-increase exit, with converged = false, at points within the 1e-5 benchmark target; the KKT residual there was a median of 4 times the bound. The exit is described in PGD stops when the computed objective increases, which says that a Float32 solve can end on it. On the five benchmark designs with the benchmark options (10 draws each, Julia 1.10.13 and 1.13.3, before and after #319), Float32 PGD ended above the 1e-5 target in 36 to 39 of 50 solves on 1.10.13 and in 37 of 50 on 1.13.3, and 22 to 25 and 15 to 16 of those solves had converged = true; Float32 APG missed 40 to 41 of 50, with 26 to 29 converged = true. For the Float32 PGD solves with converged = true that missed the target, the gap was 2.3 to 7.0 times the independent KKT residual, which was below the bound 64 eps(Float32) ≈ 7.63e-6. The bound is on a KKT residual and the target is on a duality gap, so a solve can meet the former and miss the latter. The conditions and counts are in the benchmark harness notes and in issue #320.

  • Square-root lasso. Certified solves at the default tol with relative = true, on the seven designs of the audit that are not exact or near-exact fits (210 solves per cell). The penalty level is a fraction of the level at which the solution is 0. The last column is an upper bound of the suboptimality of the worst uncertified Float32 point:

    levelFloat64Float32worst uncertified Float32 point
    0.001196 / 21040 / 2106.5e-5
    0.05210 / 210178 / 2103.6e-8
    0.3 and above210 / 210210 / 210

    The 14 uncertified Float64 solves at level 0.001 are on the design whose response dwarfs the residual, which The certificate describes. The bound of the certificate loses the term (1 − t) ‖r̂‖, which is linear in the relative violation of the dual constraint |X_kᵀr̂| ≤ λ ‖r̂‖; and in Float32 the floor forbids tightening, as Tightening and the uncertified stop states. An uncertified point is reported with converged = false and gap = NaN.

Applies to. The recorded Float32 audit and benchmark populations, options, Julia versions and square-root-lasso design exclusions.

Code. Historical CD, PGD/APG and square-root-lasso runs; current rules are CD-03, STOP-09, CDQ-03, CDQ-04.

Tests. The preserved tables and linked benchmark harness/issue #320 report measurements, not contract accuracy targets.

AUDIT-05 Fixed points with the objective test disabled

Statement.

Under ftol = 0 an iteration whose accepted iterate equals the previous one proposes no stop (see STOP-10), so the stationarity check, which runs where a test proposes a stop, is not asked there. A solve that reaches its floating-point fixed point can then run to maxiter, or end with stagnated = true, with converged = false, at a point whose exact KKT residual is below the bound. This behaviour is kept.

In the sweep, with the documented strict configuration (ftol = 0, allow_f_increases = true, xtol = 1e-300, gradtol = 1e-11), Float32 PGD reported converged = true in 12 of 570 solves, and 538 did not converge although the exact residual was below the bound (338 at maxiter = 20000, 200 with stagnated = true). The table counts solves that did not converge although the exact normalized KKT residual is below max(gradtol, 64 eps). It uses the problems with a positive scale S (the 12 solves per cell with y = 0 and λ = 0 are excluded: the check refuses a zero scale by design), 570 solves per cell, and compares the exact residual with the bound; it is not a replay of the computed check. In the table strict is the configuration above, and entries are Float64 / Float32.

Each of the 208 stagnation stops of the sweep was at a point whose residual is below the bound (largest ratio 0.42). Typical cases in the sweep: λ ≥ λmax from each of the three starts (the solution x = 0 is reached and not reported as converged: 20,000 iterations, exact residual 0); a warm start at the reference; and Float32 strict solves that reach a fixed point whose residual is below the bound.

What you can do: with ftol = 0, a result with converged = false at maxiter, or with stagnated = true, can be at a point whose KKT residual is below the bound; evaluate it independently. With the default ftol > 0 an unchanged iterate proposes a stop, and the stationarity check decides.

Applies to. The audit configurations ftol = 0 and the stated strict settings, with positive stop scale and the zero-scale exclusions retained.

Code. Historical PGD/APG sweep; canonical exact-stall and stagnation rules are STOP-10 and STOP-11.

Tests. The exact-residual comparison is not a replay of the computed stationarity check; counts and exclusions are retained above.

AUDIT-06 The choosing-solver strict PGD observation

Statement.

The original choosing-solver example uses X = [1.0 0.0; 0.0 2.0; 0.0 0.0], y = [2.0, -2.0, 0.5], loss scale 1/length(y), and NormL1(0.1). Its known solution is [1.7, -0.925]. With the stricter options ProximalOptions(maxiter=2000, ftol=1e-14, xtol=1e-12, gradtol=1e-10, store_trace=true) and the default allow_f_increases=false, PGD ended on the objective-increase exit with converged=false in a test run on Julia 1.13.3. Allowing objective increases let that solve continue to a stop that passed the check. This is the recorded run, not a universal outcome for those options.

The example reports both coefficient error, available because its analytic answer is known, and an independently computed KKT violation. The default options maxiter=200, ftol=1e-6, xtol=1e-8, gradtol=1e-5 target convenience; neither they nor smaller tolerances promise a specified number of accurate digits. The normative stopping and accuracy limits are STOP-01, STOP-09 and STOP-12. The executable comparison is retained in the solver guide.

Applies to. The original two-coefficient example and its historical Julia 1.13.3 test run.

Code. Historical choosing-solver example; current objective-increase exit is STOP-09.

Tests. Historical observation explicitly retained as such; the example reports independent KKT and analytic coefficient errors.
