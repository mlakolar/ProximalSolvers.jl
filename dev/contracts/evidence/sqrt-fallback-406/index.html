Square-root fallback: historical assertions and evaluation

Statement.

The bounded square-root-lasso fallback was introduced by PR #406 / issue #155, commit f573224f, retained in the integrated upstream revision ef8c9aae. The current rules are CDQ-02 through CDQ-06. This page keeps superseded final-stop descriptions from the original documentation baseline and the evaluation reported with the later change. Historical assertions below are not current solver guarantees or newly executed measurements.

Before the fallback, an undefined residual-direction certificate at an exact fit ended the solve uncertified. The current algorithm can use a dual lower bound from a stage point to certify the original stopped point, or return a changed point with a proved strict objective decrease. Consequently, the old assertions about the final status of the two exact-fit tutorial fixtures describe the old implementation. The old example-index descriptions are preserved in EVIDENCE-EXAMPLES-01; the integrated tutorials now show two successful fallback fixtures and an uncertified hard case. A certified exact-fit square-root point still has no conditional positive scaled-lasso noise-scale minimizer, as specified in CDQ-06.

Applies to. The frozen pre-fallback documentation at a6293e6ff7e654a87babbc5de2314205be13d6c5, and the synthetic evaluation reported with f573224f. These populations and revisions are historical evidence.

Code. Historical source/docstring blocks below; current src/cd/sqrtlasso.jl, examples/problems/square_root_lasso.jl and examples/problems/scaled_lasso.jl at ef8c9aae.

Tests. Original block text is preserved verbatim below, except the relative-link target adjustments recorded with the support and stopping excerpts. The later implementation tests are listed under CDQ-05 and CDQ-06. The evaluation percentages below are reported observations, not new test results or promised recovery rates.

Sources. docs/src/numerics.md:L670, docs/src/numerics.md:L688, docs/src/stopping-limits.md:L35, docs/src/numerics.md:L713, docstring:src/cd/solver.jl:L155, docstring:src/cd/solver.jl:L219, docs/src/support.md:L307, docs/src/working-with-results.md:L91. Later material comes from the immutable upstream numerical explanation and the #155 evaluation record. These new sources are separate from the original block inventory.

Evaluation reported with the fallback

The #155 evaluation used synthetic problems in Float32 and Float64. It reported:

  • Certification in 82% of the runs on noiseless, near-exact and adversarial exact-fit starts, and in 39 of 43 stops at a near-optimal point.
  • Certification in 22 of 160 runs on realistic designs at a quarter of the theoretical penalty level.
  • No certifications in 20 runs on equicorrelated designs with correlation 0.7.
  • No certifications in 16 noiseless runs with p > n at 1e-3 lambda_max; every one of those runs used its fallback budget in the first stage.
  • Exhaustion of the fallback budget in 38% of the runs that reached it.

These measurements do not guarantee recovery for other designs, levels, starts or precisions. In particular, an uncertified returned point may still be a nonoptimal exact fit. The square-root counts in AUDIT-04 are a different, earlier population measured at the stop check before this fallback.

The following text describes the implementation before f573224f. Historical numerical guidance: tightening and final stops

A check that does not certify divides the threshold by 100 and the run continues, at most twice per solve and while tolerance/100 is finite, positive, below tolerance (a threshold that overflowed to Inf is not lowered) and at least a floor in the units of the threshold: 16 eps(T) norm(y) for a relative threshold (relative=true and y ≠ 0, where a change is scaled by norm(X[:, k])), and 16 eps(T) times the largest |β_k| at the check for an absolute one (relative=false, or y = 0), with no tightening at β = 0. The run stops with converged = false, gap = NaN and the warning coordinate descent stopped without a duality certificate when the certificate is not defined (above), when the full pass changed nothing, or when no tightening is left. With relative=true, y ≠ 0 and the default tol = 1e-7, Float32 data get no tightening, because 1e-9 is below 16 eps(Float32); with y = 0 the threshold and its floor are absolute, and a tightening can occur. With y = 0 and a nonzero start, P* = 0, so no relative bound can be certified unless β reaches 0; coordinate descent can approach 0 without reaching it and then stops uncertified. At an exact fit with a positive penalty, coordinate descent can stop at a point that minimizes P along every coordinate but not overall; such a run, and one that stops at the minimizer at an exact fit, are reported uncertified where the recomputed residual is 0, which is the usual outcome. An uncertified stop at the last allowed pass gets the uncertified warning, and passes == maxpasses alone does not tell it apart from the limit.

Historical CDSolver stopping description

A full pass whose largest change is below the threshold runs a duality certificate: a dual point along the residual y - X*beta, recomputed in Float64, with a proved bound on the rounding of each computed term. converged = true only when it proves (P(beta) - P*)/P* <= 1e-4, P* the minimum, and gap then holds that bound, rounded up; its denominator is a lower bound of P* from the dual point. When the certificate does not pass, the change threshold (the reported tolerance) is divided by 100 and the run continues, at most twice per solve, where tolerance/100 is finite, positive and below tolerance (an effective threshold that overflowed to Inf is not lowered), and not below a floor in the units of the threshold: with a relative threshold (relative=true and y != 0), 16 eps(T) times norm(y); with an absolute one (relative=false, or y = 0), 16 eps(T) times the largest abs(beta[k]) at the check, and no tightening at beta = 0. Otherwise the run stops with converged = false, gap = NaN and a warning that differs from the maxpasses warning. This happens where the recomputed residual is 0 with a nonzero coefficient, so that the dual direction is undefined: the usual outcome at an exact fit (y = X*beta) with a positive penalty, where a coordinatewise minimum need not be a minimum (the residual can also round to 0 where y - X*beta is not 0, and an exact fit whose recomputed residual is not 0 can be certified, validly); after a full pass that changed nothing; with y = 0 and a nonzero start, where P* = 0, so that no relative bound can be certified unless beta reaches 0, and coordinate descent can approach 0 without reaching it; and when the threshold cannot be lowered further, for example where the rounding of the recomputed residual is not small against the residual itself (as where the fitted values dwarf it), or for Float32 data at the default tol with a relative threshold, where tol/100 is below that floor. With tol = 0, or a threshold below the changes that rounding produces, the change test does not pass, the certificate does not run, and the run ends at maxpasses with that warning. At a stop of the certificate, minimum is evaluated from the residual that it recomputed, when every entry of it is representable in T.

Historical CDResults description

Result sharing the caller's vector or SparseIterate as minimizer, with minimum, passes, converged, maxchange and tolerance. The compatibility field kkt_checks = 0 is a sentinel. gap is NaN except after a certified square-root lasso run (see CDSolver), where converged = true and gap is the certified upper bound on (P - P*)/P*. For the least-squares lasso and elastic net and the group lasso, converged reports the change test and gap is NaN.

Historical unsmoothed coordinate rule

With a = ‖X_k‖², b = X_kᵀr, the centre c = old + b/a and d the distance of r from the span of X_k, the objective along coordinate k is √(a (z − c)² + d²) + λ|z|. Its minimizer is 0 for λ ≥ √a and shrink(c, λ d/√(a (a − λ²))) otherwise; the step uses the level T(lambda). It forms a, b and ‖r‖² in one pass over the column and the residual, and d² = ‖r‖² − b²/a; where that is below ‖r‖²/16 it forms d again as the norm of r − (b/a) X_k, because the subtraction loses accuracy there. Where a is below n floatmin(T)/eps(T), or ‖r‖² or that recomputed d² is below it or not finite (a column whose squares can underflow, a residual whose squares underflow or overflow, and r = 0), the step is formed in Float64 from the column and the residual scaled by powers of two. In that scaling a small entry can underflow or lose bits, which changes √a, b/√a and d by absolute amounts of at most 3n 2^-1074 times ‖X_k‖ or ‖r‖ (to first order); the error bound of the step, derived in the source, covers this (a tiny d can become 0). Where d/√(a (a − λ²)) or its product with λ is outside the normal range of T, the threshold is formed in Float64 without that product. At every visit, before any of these branches, a residual with an entry that is not finite is refused with an ArgumentError, and so are a column whose squared norm is 0 but which is not zero and a column whose squared norm is not finite; the coordinate and the residual are then unchanged.

Historical residual-direction certificate description

At a full pass whose largest change is below the threshold, the residual r̂ = y − Xβ is recomputed in Float64 over the nonzero coefficients, with the row magnitudes |y_i| + Σ_k |X_ik β_k|. The dual point is θ = t r̂/‖r̂‖ with t ≤ min(1, λ/max_k |X_kᵀr̂|/‖r̂‖), where the maximum is replaced by an upper bound and t is rounded down, so that ‖θ‖ ≤ 1 and ‖Xᵀθ‖∞ ≤ λ hold exactly; weak duality then gives P* ≥ θᵀy. The gap P − θᵀy is bounded by a sum of terms that are nonnegative in exact arithmetic, (1 − t)‖r̂‖ + Σ_k (λ|β_k| − t β_k X_kᵀr̂/‖r̂‖) plus (1 + t) times a bound on the error of r̂, without a difference of θᵀy and P. Each computed term carries a relative and an absolute rounding allowance, and the operations after the passes over the data round outward. The run is certified when the resulting upper bound of (P − P*)/P* (with a lower bound of θᵀy as denominator), rounded up, is at most 1e-4, and that bound is reported in gap. Not certified: a recomputed residual r̂ that is 0 in every entry with a nonzero coefficient (the direction is undefined; this is the usual outcome at an exact fit y = Xβ, and r̂ can also round to 0 where y − Xβ is not 0), a sum of squares of r̂ below n 2^-969 or not finite, and an intermediate value that is not finite. An exact fit whose r̂ is not 0 goes through the bound like any other point, and a certificate there is valid. The check also replaces the cached residual by r̂ rounded to T when every entry is representable in T, and minimum is evaluated from it at a stop of the check. A check costs one pass over X and one over its columns with nonzero coefficients. The proof of the allowance is a comment in src/cd/sqrtlasso.jl.
