Two-function consensus ADMM

ADM-01 Inputs, settings and ownership

Statement. solve!(z, f, g, method::ADMMSolver, options=ProximalOptions(); u=nothing) accepts ProximalBase f = SqrNormL2(λ, c) and unweighted g = NormL1(τ). The center c, initial consensus variable z and any explicit scaled dual u must be finite nonempty Vector{T} buffers of equal length, with one common T of Float32 or Float64. The loss level λ must be finite and strictly positive and the penalty level τ finite and nonnegative. Both levels must convert exactly to T. The solver borrows c for reads. It checks each pair among z, c and explicit u. A pair is refused when Base.mightalias reports an alias or the vectors' memory ranges overlap.

ADMMSolver(; ρ=1, α=1, abstol=1e-6, reltol=1e-4, abstol_dual=abstol, adaptive=false) takes a finite positive penalty, relaxation strictly between 0 and 2, and finite nonnegative tolerances. The solver also checks these bounds after converting to T. A strictly positive tolerance must stay positive. The working proximal step inv(ρ) must be finite and strictly positive in T. A positive subnormal penalty or reciprocal is allowed when both pass these checks.

Function, type, scalar, alias, setting and entry checks precede writes to the caller. Incompatible lengths and empty vectors raise DimensionMismatch. Other preflight refusals raise ArgumentError. The solve runs in private storage. With u=nothing, the initial dual vector is zero. With explicit u, the solve starts from its entries. On normal return, the caller's z and explicit u receive the final iterates, and result.minimizer and result.u refer to those buffers. With u=nothing, result.u holds the solver's dual vector. Reuse the returned z, u and ρ together for a warm start. Copy the buffers to retain the previous result's vectors. During a solve the caller must keep the buffers and problem data stable.

Applies to. ADMMSolver on a dense vector with centered SqrNormL2 and unweighted NormL1. ADM-04 states the inputs for the graphical lasso on a dense matrix.

Tests. test/admm.jl covers input refusals, initial states, ownership and restart.

Sources. Issue #433 and archived ADMM.

ADM-02 Iteration, diagnostics and termination

Statement. For each iteration with previous consensus variable z₀ and scaled dual u₀, compute

\[x = \operatorname{prox}_{f/\rho}(z_0-u_0),\qquad \hat x = \alpha x+(1-\alpha)z_0,\qquad z = \operatorname{prox}_{g/\rho}(\hat x+u_0),\qquad u = (\hat x+u_0)-z.\]

Use ProximalBase prox! with step inv(ρ) for each proximal update. For α=1, use x for x̂. For α<1, form z₀ + α(x-z₀) in working precision. For α>1, form x + (α-1)(x-z₀). With N = length(z), the working-precision diagnostics are

\[r_{\rm norm}=\|x-z\|_2,\quad s_{\rm norm}=\rho\|z-z_0\|_2,\quad \varepsilon_{\rm pri}=\sqrt{N}\,{\tt abstol}+{\tt reltol}\max(\|x\|_2,\|z\|_2),\quad \varepsilon_{\rm dual}=\sqrt{N}\,{\tt abstol\_dual}+{\tt reltol}\,\rho\|u\|_2.\]

When reltol is zero, omit the relative terms. A residual stop requires both finite residuals to be at most their respective finite tolerances. When both computed residuals are zero, also check each component's native stationarity violation at z. For qᵢ = λ(zᵢ-cᵢ), the score is abs(qᵢ + τ*sign(zᵢ)) at a nonzero zᵢ and max(abs(qᵢ)-τ, 0) at zero. If a finite score exceeds eps_dual, stop with status=:stagnated and converged=false. A nonfinite score is a numerical failure. This guard addresses a rounded zero-residual point at which the two proximal updates have lost resolution. A successful status=:converged reports the implemented tests in working precision. The tutorial verifies its fit independently from the observations.

The supported ProximalOptions settings are maxiter, prox_calls_limit and time_limit. Before beginning an iteration, check the iteration cap, space for two proximal calls in the remaining call budget, and elapsed time, in that order. A zero call budget denotes unlimited calls. Time is measured from the end of preflight and checked before each step, so a step may run past its limit. A nonpositive time limit permits zero iterations. A completed step's convergence or stagnation takes precedence over reaching a limit. Nondefault values for the other stored options are refused. The shared compatibility-gap settings retain their constructor rules in PGD-05.

On normal return, iterations counts complete iterations and prox_calls is twice that count. minimum is f(z)+g(z) evaluated once after termination. The ProximalSolvers.f_calls(result) and ProximalSolvers.grad_calls(result) accessors report one evaluation of f(z) and zero gradient oracle calls. The four residual and tolerance fields describe the last completed iteration at the returned ρ. With zero iterations, they are NaN and converged=false. Limits return :iteration_limit, :prox_calls_limit or :time_limit in status. A numerical failure, including nonfinite proximal arguments or outputs, updated dual entries, residuals, tolerances or the final objective, raises an error before committing either caller buffer.

Applies to. Every ADMMSolver solve and the ADMMResults it returns. For the graphical lasso on a dense matrix, ADM-04 replaces the stationarity score, requires a stored α equal to 1 and adds the symmetry and Cholesky rules.

Code. src/solvers/admm.jl.

Tests. test/admm.jl compares with raw objective and KKT references and checks recurrence, diagnostics, termination, counts and numerical failures.

Sources. Issue #433 and the phase-0 audit.

ADM-03 Adaptive penalty

Statement. With adaptive=true, use the previous iteration's residuals and tolerances to propose a penalty change before the next iteration. Compare the scores r_norm/eps_pri and s_norm/eps_dual. The score is zero for zero residual, including 0/0, and infinite for positive residual and zero tolerance. Two infinite scores tie. A primal score greater than ten times the dual score proposes doubling ρ. A dual score greater than ten times the primal score proposes halving it. The finite scores are compared with scaling to avoid overflow or underflow in the individual divisions. Take at most 50 changes.

For a candidate multiplier m, rescale the dual as u/m, preserving the unscaled dual ρ*u up to working-precision rounding. Skip a candidate if its penalty or reciprocal is nonfinite or zero, the penalty cannot be scaled by m exactly, or any rescaled dual entry would become nonfinite or round from nonzero to zero. Updates occur between iterations. The reported ρ was used for the final completed iteration and matches both the diagnostics and the returned scaled dual.

Applies to. ADMMSolver(adaptive=true) on a dense vector. ADM-04 refuses it for a matrix.

Code. src/solvers/admm.jl.

Tests. test/admm.jl checks change directions, scaled dual values, zero tolerances, range limits and agreement at independently measured accuracy.

Sources. Issue #433 and archived ADMM.

ADM-04 Graphical lasso on dense matrices

Statement. With a dense Matrix{T} start Θ, where T is Float32 or Float64, solve!(Θ, f, g, method::ADMMSolver, options=ProximalOptions(); u=nothing) applies the iteration below to the problem

\[\min_{\Theta=\Theta^\top\succ0}\; P(\Theta)=-\log\det\Theta+\operatorname{tr}(S\Theta)+\lambda_0\sum_{i\ne j}\Lambda_{ij}|\Theta_{ij}|,\]

with ProximalBase f = LogDetLoss(S) and g = NormL1(λ₀, Λ), and stops by the residual tests, limits and working-precision exits of ADM-02. The penalty sums over the full matrix, so each off-diagonal pair appears twice, and the zero diagonal of Λ leaves the diagonal unpenalized. LogDetLoss must have weight one; when S is a sample covariance, a normalization such as 1/n is part of S.

After the entry checks of solve! (keywords, options, and the storage and element type of the start), the matrix path checks, in this order: f is a LogDetLoss{T} with weight w == 1; g is a NormL1 whose stored weights g.w are a Matrix{T}; Θ is nonempty and square; size(S) == size(Θ) == size(Λ); the α stored in method, after the ADMMSolver constructor's conversion, equals 1, and method.adaptive == false; the level λ₀ is finite, nonnegative and exactly representable in T; the entries of Θ, S and Λ are finite; Λ is nonnegative, exactly symmetric (Λ[i,j] == Λ[j,i] for all i, j) and zero on the diagonal; S and Θ are exactly symmetric; an explicit u is a Matrix{T} of the same size with finite, exactly symmetric entries; no pair among Θ, u, S and Λ aliases, by Base.mightalias or overlapping memory ranges; the ADM-01 settings hold in T, with a finite positive inv(ρ); and a Cholesky factorization of a private copy of Θ succeeds in T. These checks precede any write to Θ or u. The first failing check determines the exception: DimensionMismatch for an empty or non-square Θ, a size mismatch or a wrong-size u, and ArgumentError for the others. The solver does not average or symmetrize its inputs. With a matrix start, other function pairs, mixed element types, a stored α other than 1 and adaptive=true are refused. The entry check refuses start types other than the dense vectors of ADM-01 and these dense matrices.

Ownership follows the protocol of ADM-01 with matrices in place of vectors. The solve runs in private storage. With u=nothing, the initial scaled dual is the zero matrix; with explicit u, the solve starts from its entries. On normal return, the caller's Θ and explicit u receive the final iterates, and result.minimizer and result.u refer to those buffers. With u=nothing, result.u holds the solver's dual matrix. Reuse the returned Θ, u and ρ together for a warm start. The solver borrows S, stored in f, and Λ, retained by reference in g, for reads. During a solve the caller must keep the buffers and problem data unchanged.

Each iteration applies the ADM-02 recurrence with α = 1: x = prox_{f/ρ}(z₀-u₀) by the ProximalBase LogDetLoss prox!, z = prox_{g/ρ}(x+u₀) by the prox! of NormL1(T(λ₀), Λ), and u = (x+u₀)-z. After each logdet proximal step, x must be finite, exactly symmetric and pass a Cholesky factorization in T; otherwise the solve raises an error before any write to Θ or u. The consensus iterate z after the ℓ₁ step can be indefinite. The loop does not test its definiteness and does not clip or project it; a nonfinite z fails the dual-update check of ADM-02. The residuals and tolerances are those of ADM-02 with Frobenius norms and N = p² for a p × p matrix, so √N = p. The limit order, counters and nonfinite failures are those of ADM-02.

The returned point is the consensus z. Before a normal return at any status, z must be exactly symmetric and pass a Cholesky factorization in T; otherwise the solve raises an error, with no write to Θ or u. This is a working-precision test; it does not prove that a matrix close to singular is positive definite. On a normal return after zero completed iterations, the returned point is the validated start. ProximalOptions requires maxiter ≥ 2, so zero iterations follow from prox_calls_limit = 1 or from a time limit that has expired before the first iteration, such as a nonpositive one.

When both computed residuals are zero and pass their tolerances, and z has passed the return-point check above, the solver checks stationarity at z in place of the ADM-02 score. With G = S - inv(z), the inverse formed from a Cholesky factorization, the score of an entry is |Gᵢᵢ| on the diagonal, |Gᵢⱼ + λ₀Λᵢⱼ sign(zᵢⱼ)| at a nonzero off-diagonal entry and max(|Gᵢⱼ| - λ₀Λᵢⱼ, 0) at an off-diagonal exact zero, with λ₀Λᵢⱼ formed as a product in T. A nonfinite score raises an error. Otherwise, if a score exceeds eps_dual, the status is :stagnated and converged=false.

On a normal return, status is :converged when both residual tests passed and the stationarity check passed where it applies, and :stagnated as above. :iteration_limit, :prox_calls_limit and :time_limit report termination by the limit checks of ADM-02, with converged=false; a residual or stagnation decision on the last permitted iteration takes precedence. minimum follows the objective rule of ADM-02 at the returned z, so a nonfinite objective raises an error after the status decision and before any write. The result is an ADMMResults{T, Matrix{T}}. A converged status reports these working-precision tests and is not an optimality certificate.

Applies to. ADMMSolver with a dense Matrix{Float32} or Matrix{Float64} start, LogDetLoss and NormL1 with Matrix weights, fixed ρ, a stored α = 1 and adaptive=false.

Code. src/solvers/admm.jl: the Matrix method of _solve_admm!, _admm_issymmetric, _admm_passes_cholesky! and _admm_logdet_stationary.

Tests. test/admm_graphical_lasso.jl compares with analytic optima (1×1, diagonal, 2×2 in both regimes, zero penalty through zero weights or a zero level, and large penalty) and a seeded sparse precision matrix using a BigFloat objective, KKT scores and, on those fixtures, a candidate dual matrix that must pass a BigFloat Cholesky check, with its gap compared against a roundoff allowance. It replays one step independently, and checks penalties with declared abstract weight types, limits, stagnation at diagonal and off-diagonal scores, an indefinite intermediate z, a logdet proximal output that fails the Cholesky check, warm starts, refusals and numerical failures without writes to the caller's buffers.

Sources. Issue #436, parent issue #425 and the Stage 0 report.
