Calling the solvers

The call

The two-term solvers minimize $f(x) + g(x)$ through solve!:

res = solve!(x, f, g, solver)            # CDSolver, ProxNewton
res = solve!(x, f, g, solver, options)   # the proximal gradient solvers and ADMM
res = solve!(x, f, g, h, D, CondatVu(), options)  # f(x) + g(x) + h(D*x)

x is the starting point, and solve! overwrites it with the solution. The loss f and the penalty g are functions of ProximalBase. Each solver asks for some of their oracles, and for an iterate of a certain kind:

Solver$f$$g$x
CDSolvera loss with a coordinate descent model, see Losses for coordinate descentseparable, or a group penalty (GroupNormL2, GroupNormLinf, ...)a SparseIterate, a SymmetricSparseIterate, an AtomIterate for a group penalty, or a vector
ProxNewtona GLMLoss, a LogDetLoss, or a smooth loss with a supported quadratic modelseparable for vectors; NormL1 for LogDetLoss; see Other smooth losses for generic matrix modelsa SparseIterate or dense vector; for supported matrix models, a SymmetricSparseIterate or symmetric Matrix
ProximalGradientDescent, AccProxGradDescentsmooth: issmooth(f)proximable: isproximable(g)any array that f and g take, a SparseIterate, or an AtomIterate with the groups of a GroupNormL2
MonotoneAccProxGradDescentsmoothproximableany array that f and g take, a SparseIterate or a SymmetricSparseIterate
ActiveAccProxGradDescentsmooth, with restrictseparable or GroupNormL2, with restrict and kkt_violationa vector, a SparseIterate or an AtomIterate
ADMMSolverproximableproximablean Array

The iterates are those of ProximalBase: a SparseIterate stores the entries that have been nonzero and gives $O(1)$ access to any entry; a SymmetricSparseIterate is a sparse symmetric matrix whose coordinates are the entries of its lower triangle; an AtomIterate is a vector whose coordinates are groups of entries. A solver that takes a dense vector for coordinate descent or the active-set method converts it to one of these, and copies the solution back.

The solvers compute in the element type of x, Float64 or Float32. In Float32, tolerances below eps(Float32) ≈ 1.2e-7 may cause stagnation or rounded residuals and do not imply corresponding coefficient accuracy.

Options

ProximalOptions holds the iteration and call limits, the tolerances and the tracing of the proximal gradient solvers, which pass them on to each inner solve of the active-set method:

KeywordDefaultMeaning
maxiter200the largest number of iterations
ftol1e-6relative change of the objective
xtol1e-8relative change of the iterate
gradtol1e-5the optimality residual of FASTA
allow_f_increasesfalsekeep going when the objective increases
time_limitInfthe longest run time, in seconds
f_calls_limit, grad_calls_limit, prox_calls_limit0the largest number of evaluations of $f$, of its gradient and of proximal operators; 0 is no limit
store_trace, show_trace, extended_trace, print_everyfalse, false, false, 1record or print the progress
callbacknothingcalled every print_every iterations; returning true stops
gaptol, gapevery0, 10with gaptol > 0, stop when the relative duality gap, computed every gapevery iterations, is at most gaptol (see Duality gaps)

The tests of ftol, xtol and gradtol are those of Proximal gradient methods; the solver stops when any of them passes. ADMMSolver has its own tolerances and uses only maxiter, time_limit, prox_calls_limit, show_trace, print_every, gaptol and gapevery. CDSolver and ProxNewton take their settings as keywords of their constructors instead: CDSolver(; maxpasses, tol, order, relative, anderson, momentum, rng, gaptol, gapsafe) and ProxNewton(; inner, maxiter, tol, β).

The call limits are checked after each iteration, which always finishes, so a count can exceed its limit by the evaluations of one iteration.

Reusing solver buffers

SolverWorkspace keeps the solver-owned buffers between standalone fits with a fixed loss, solver configuration and iterate shape:

ws = SolverWorkspace(zeros(size(X, 2)), LeastSquaresLoss(y, X),
                     NormL1(0.1), AccProxGradDescent(), ProximalOptions())
result = solve!(ws)
result = solve!(ws, NormL1(0.05))    # same loss, a different penalty level
reinit!(ws, zeros(size(X, 2)))      # a new start in the existing storage

The three gradient solvers, CDSolver and ADMMSolver support this interface. For coordinate descent omit ProximalOptions; the workspace may also take a configured CDModel, including one with an intercept. Each solve resets its counters and acceleration history. ADMM also resets its scaled dual variable and penalty parameter unless solve!(ws; warm_start = true) is requested.

The workspace shares the caller's starting array and the result's minimizer; ADMM results also share its dual buffer. Copy arrays that must survive another solve. Keep loss data and group partitions fixed, and use separate workspaces for concurrent solves. User function or proximal-operator allocations, traces, results and optional refit/Anderson work are separate from buffer reuse.

minimum is $f + g$ at the minimizer, and gap the relative duality gap there when it was computed, NaN otherwise. For the gradient solvers and ADMM, result.converged reports the selected criterion: with gaptol > 0 and a computable gap, the final gap must meet gaptol, including when a run stops at a limit or a callback. The x_converged, f_converged and grad_converged flags remain diagnostics of the individual residual tests. Without a computable gap, those tests determine gradient solver convergence, and ADMM uses its primal and dual residual tests. Printing a result shows a summary.

Duality gaps

A duality gap bounds the objective error for supported convex problems. Checking a solution gives the optimality conditions, the dual construction, and the distinction between absolute and relative gaps. Use CDSolver(; gaptol) or ProximalOptions(; gaptol, gapevery) to request a gap stopping rule where it is available.

Traces and callbacks

With store_trace = true the proximal gradient solvers record an OptimizationState per iteration in res.trace: the iteration, the objective fg_x, the relative change of the iterate dxx and the inverse step size L. extended_trace = true also stores the iterate and the gradient. show_trace = true prints the same every print_every iterations. A callback receives the trace, or the current state when the trace is not stored, and stops the solver by returning true.

For the penalty paths of coordinate descent, solve_path(...; callback) calls callback(k, λ, x, m) after each level instead; see Penalty paths and screening.

Naming

Options and solver settings use snake_case keywords, such as ProximalOptions(; print_every=...) and CondatVu(; norm_d=...). Invalid settings raise an ArgumentError. Internal solver-state fields are not a stable extension interface. See Migrating to this release for the names removed since version 0.1.0.

Diagonal preconditioning

ProximalGradientDescent(preconditioner = :diagonal) and AccProxGradDescent(preconditioner = :diagonal) use the coordinate curvatures from coordinate_lipschitz!(D, f). This can help when design columns have very different scales. The default :none retains scalar steps.

x = zeros(size(X, 2))
r = solve!(x, LeastSquaresLoss(y, X), NormL1(0.1),
           AccProxGradDescent(preconditioner = :diagonal),
           ProximalOptions(gaptol = 1e-6, maxiter = 5000))

Each trial uses steps 1 ./ (L .* D). Backtracking finds the scalar 1/L in the norm sqrt(sum(D .* abs2.(dx))); coordinate curvatures alone do not majorize the loss. Momentum and objective restarts retain their usual form. The gradient residual and its normalizer use the transformed coordinates u = sqrt.(D) .* x, as do iterate stopping tests and reported iterate residuals. Duality gaps are evaluated on the original loss and penalty without modification. For PGD, ls.Lexact, if supplied, must bound curvature in this metric.

Zero curvatures receive unit metric entries, so flat coordinates still have positive steps. Nonfinite or negative curvatures raise ArgumentError, as does a loss without coordinate-curvature support. The penalty must implement array-step proximal operators, as separable penalties, group norms, the simplex and the ℓ1 ball do. Group ℓ2 and ℓ∞ norms use a scalar optimality equation within each group, since ProximalBase's group-step API assumes a constant metric inside each group. Tuple blocks, SymmetricSparseIterate, and combining PGD's normalized = true with diagonal preconditioning are unsupported. SolverWorkspace computes the metric once and reuses it; its loss data must remain fixed, including across reinit! calls.
