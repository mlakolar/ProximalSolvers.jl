Checking a solution

A solver reports whether its stopping rule was met, which can depend on changes in the iterates or an optimality measure. We distinguish residuals of the optimality conditions from bounds on objective error and explain how to compute each quantity. The appropriate check depends on the loss, the penalty and the accuracy needed in the application.

Optimality conditions

Suppose that $f$ is convex and differentiable and $g$ is a proper closed convex function. A feasible point $x$ minimizes $P=f+g$ if and only if

\[0\in\nabla f(x)+\partial g(x),\]

where $\partial g$ is the convex subdifferential. For the lasso with $f(\beta)=\|y-X\beta\|_2^2/(2n)$ and $g(\beta)=\lambda\|\beta\|_1$, let $c=\nabla f(\beta)=X^\top(X\beta-y)/n$. The conditions become

\[\begin{cases} c_j+\lambda\operatorname{sign}(\beta_j)=0, & \beta_j\ne0,\\ |c_j|\le\lambda, & \beta_j=0. \end{cases}\]

In particular, the second condition explains both $\lambda_{\max}$ and the checks on coordinates excluded by screening. See The lasso for a direct calculation of these violations.

For a group penalty $g(x)=\lambda\sum_k w_k\|x_{G_k}\|_2$ with disjoint groups, a group at zero satisfies its condition when $\|\nabla_{G_k}f(x)\|_2\le\lambda w_k$. On a nonzero group, the condition is $\nabla_{G_k}f(x)+\lambda w_k x_{G_k}/\|x_{G_k}\|_2=0$. These are the block counterparts of the lasso conditions above.

Coordinate residuals

For a separable penalty and a supported coordinate model with positive coordinate curvatures, kkt_residual measures the largest scaled coordinate step that would be taken at the current iterate:

\[R(x)=\max_j\sqrt{a_j}\left| \operatorname{prox}_{g_j/a_j}\left(x_j-\frac{\nabla_j f(x)}{a_j}\right)-x_j \right|.\]

With relative = true, the default, it divides $R(x)$ by the response scale when that scale is positive. The coordinate descent stopping rule defines these scales. For least squares, they are $a_j=\|X_j\|_2^2/n$ and $\|y\|_2/\sqrt n$, without an intercept or weights.

Using the residual, we check all coordinates at the same point without changing the iterate. This differs from the largest change observed during a pass, in which each step starts from the preceding step's updated iterate. Furthermore, its scale differs from the raw lasso violations displayed above.

For a convex objective, a zero residual characterizes optimality under the stated conditions. A small residual alone does not give a numerical bound on objective error without further properties of the problem. The function applies to the separable penalties and coordinate models listed in its reference.

Objective error and duality

For $f(x)=F(Ax)$, the Fenchel dual objective is

\[D(\theta)=-F^*(\theta)-g^*(-A^\top\theta),\]

where the superscript $*$ denotes the convex conjugate. Using any dual feasible point, we obtain the bound

\[0\le P(x)-\inf_z P(z)\le P(x)-D(\theta).\]

Therefore, a duality gap bounds objective error directly, while a bound on the distance between coefficient vectors requires further assumptions.

A dual point for the lasso

For the lasso above, suppose that $\lambda>0$. The dual objective and its feasible set take the form

\[D(\theta)=-y^\top\theta-\frac n2\|\theta\|_2^2, \qquad \|X^\top\theta\|_\infty\le\lambda.\]

Starting from $u=(X\beta-y)/n$, we choose $t=\min\{1,\lambda/\|X^\top u\|_\infty\}$ and $\theta=tu$, where we take $t=1$ if the denominator is zero. This scaling makes the dual point feasible, so $P(\beta)-D(\theta)$ is an upper bound on the objective error. At a lasso solution, the gradient already satisfies the dual constraint and the gap is zero in exact arithmetic.

Using the certificate

duality_gap(f, g, x) returns a named tuple with gap, primal and dual. The solver result instead stores the relative gap in res.gap:

\[\frac{\max\{P(x)-D(\theta),0\}}{\max\{|P(x)|,\mathrm{floatmin}(T)\}},\]

where $T$ is the floating point type. The clipping handles a negative gap caused by rounding, and the denominator avoids division by zero. The absolute gap remains useful when the objective is close to zero, where relative accuracy is sensitive to the denominator. Note that adding a constant to the objective changes the relative gap but leaves the absolute gap unchanged.

With CDSolver(; gaptol = 1e-8), coordinate descent stops on the gap: the rule on the coordinate changes only decides when to compute it, and tightens until the gap is small enough (see Stopping on the duality gap). The paths of solve_path do this by default, at a relative gap of 1e-4. For ProximalGradientDescent, AccProxGradDescent and ADMMSolver, use ProximalOptions(; gaptol, gapevery) to check it every gapevery iterations in place of their other convergence tests.

The certificate is available for least squares and the squared, logistic, Poisson and Huber GLM losses with supported convex penalties, and for the graphical lasso. The duality_gap reference lists the supported combinations, weights and iterate types, and an unsupported combination raises an ArgumentError. A solver asked to use an unavailable gap warns and falls back to its usual stopping rule.

For a linear composite, CondatVu uses the six-argument duality_gap(f, g, h, D, x, y) and the same gaptol/gapevery options. See Primal–dual splitting for its feasible dual construction and conjugate requirements.

With gapsafe = true, coordinate descent also uses the gap to remove coordinates proved zero at the optimum. See Penalty paths and screening for screening, and Fercoq, Gramfort and Salmon (Mind the duality gap: safer rules for the lasso, 2015) for the underlying gap-safe construction.

Nonconvex problems

The convex optimality conditions and dual certificates above do not establish global optimality for MCP, SCAD or other nonconvex penalties. The method pages describe the applicable stationarity conditions and convergence results for these penalties. In particular, we distinguish convergence to a stationary point from recovery of the statistical model that generated the data.
