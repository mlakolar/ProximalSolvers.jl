Reweighting and refitting

Many estimators are a sequence of penalized fits: the scaled lasso estimates the noise level with the coefficients, the adaptive lasso reweights the penalty from a first fit, and a least squares refit on the selected support removes the shrinkage of the penalty. reweight! and refit! run these on one model of the loss, warm started from the previous solution.

Reweighting

reweight!(x, f, g, update, method = CDSolver(); maxiter = 20, tol = 1e-2) alternates

\[x^{(t)} = \operatorname*{argmin}_x\ f(x) + g^{(t)}(x), \qquad \big(g^{(t+1)}, \delta_t\big) = \texttt{update}\big(g^{(t)}, x^{(t)}, m\big),\]

with each solve warm started from the previous solution, and stops when the change $\delta_t$ reported by update is below tol, or after maxiter solves. The model m is current for $x^{(t)}$, so update can read the residuals (residuals(m) for least squares) or the gradient without recomputing them. The ReweightResults hold the last penalty, the next one that update proposed, and whether the change fell below tol.

The scaled lasso

The scaled lasso (Sun and Zhang, 2012) estimates the coefficients and the noise level together:

\[\min_{\beta,\ \sigma > 0}\ \frac{\|y - X\beta\|^2}{2n\sigma} + \frac{\sigma}{2} + \lambda_0\|\beta\|_1 .\]

The objective is jointly convex in $(\beta, \sigma)$: its first term is the perspective of a convex quadratic. For a fixed $\sigma$, multiplying by $\sigma$ shows that $\beta$ solves the lasso of $\|y - X\beta\|^2/(2n)$ with level $\lambda_0\sigma$; for a fixed $\beta$, the minimizer is $\sigma = \|y - X\beta\|/\sqrt n$. Alternating the two, with update = (g, x, m) -> (σ = norm(residuals(m)) / sqrt(n); (NormL1(λ₀ * σ), change)), decreases the objective at every step. At the fixed point,

\[\frac{X^\top(y - X\beta)}{n} \in \lambda_0\,\frac{\|y - X\beta\|}{\sqrt n}\ \partial\|\beta\|_1 ,\]

which is the optimality condition of the square-root lasso $\|y - X\beta\|/\sqrt n + \lambda_0\|\beta\|_1$: the two estimators coincide, and SqrtLeastSquaresLoss computes the same solution in one run.

Weights from a first fit

The adaptive lasso (Zou, 2006) penalizes coefficient $k$ by $\lambda w_k$ with $w_k = 1/|\tilde\beta_k|$ from a first estimate $\tilde\beta$: large coefficients are penalized little, and those at zero in $\tilde\beta$ get an infinite weight and stay at zero. Two solves with one update are maxiter = 2, tol = 0. When the weights are expensive, a two-stage estimator can instead build the model once, m = cdmodel(f, x), and call solve!(x, m, g, CDSolver()) for each penalty.

Concave penalties by reweighted ℓ1

For a concave, nondecreasing $\rho$ on $[0, \infty)$, the penalty $\sum_k \rho(|\beta_k|)$ lies below its tangent at the current $\beta$,

\[\rho(|t|) \le \rho(|\beta_k|) + \rho'(|\beta_k|)\,\big(|t| - |\beta_k|\big),\]

a weighted ℓ1 norm with weights $\rho'(|\beta_k|)$ plus a constant. Minimizing $f$ plus the tangent, then updating the weights, is a majorization-minimization algorithm, and decreases $f + \sum_k\rho(|\beta_k|)$ at every step (Candès, Wakin and Boyd, 2008; Zou and Li, 2008). For $\rho(t) = \lambda\varepsilon\log(1 + t/\varepsilon)$, the weights are $\lambda/(1 + |\beta_k|/\varepsilon)$; the post-lasso example runs it to convergence. For the penalties of ProximalBase (CappedL1, LogPenalty, MCP, SCAD and the group versions), LLA runs these stages, with the weights of ProximalBase.lla_weights!: solve!(x, f, g, LLA()) at one level, through reweight!, and solve_path(f, g, λs, LLA()) along a path, with screening in every stage (see Nonconvex penalties: exact steps or local linear approximation).

Penalty loadings

The lasso with data-driven penalty loadings (Belloni, Chen, Chernozhukov and Hansen, 2012), for heteroscedastic noise, weights coefficient $k$ by $\psi_k = (\sum_i X_{ik}^2 r_i^2/n)^{1/2}$, and recomputes the loadings from the residuals $r$ of the latest fit, for a few iterations. They take the residuals of the least squares refit on the selected support, since the shrinkage of the lasso inflates its own residuals, and with them the loadings.

Refitting

refit!(x, f; support) minimizes the loss over the vectors supported on $S$, by default the nonzero coordinates of x:

\[x \leftarrow \operatorname*{argmin}\ \{f(z) : z_j = x_j \text{ for } j \notin S\}.\]

Coordinates outside $S$ keep their values, so zero them first to refit on $S$ alone. After a lasso fit this is the post-lasso estimator (Belloni and Chernozhukov, 2013), which removes the shrinkage of the penalty.

  • Least squares. With the residuals $r$ of the current fit, the step $\delta$ on $S$ solves $\min_\delta \|W^{1/2}(r - X_S\delta)\|$ by a QR factorization with column pivoting of $W^{1/2}X_S$ (the columns centered when the model fits an intercept, whose value then follows from $\bar y - \bar x^\top\beta$). When $X_S$ has deficient rank, it returns a basic solution.
  • Quadratics. For the other quadratic losses, the step is the Newton step on $S$, $\delta = -H_{SS}^{-1}\nabla_S f(x)$, which is exact. The Hessian is $sA_{SS}$ for a quadratic, and $s\,A_{r r'}B_{c c'}$ between the entries $(r, c)$ and $(r', c')$ of a Kronecker quadratic. On a symmetric matrix, the Hessian between the coordinates $(r, c)$ and $(u, v)$ is $s\langle D_{rc}, A D_{uv} B\rangle$, with $D_{rc} = E_{rc} + E_{cr}$ off the diagonal and $E_{rr}$ on it, a sum of up to four products $A_{\cdot\cdot}B_{\cdot\cdot}$. For a model of another quadratic loss, the Hessian is built column by column from the change of the derivatives after a unit step on each coordinate. The linear system is solved by a pivoted QR factorization, which also handles a singular $H_{SS}$.

Refits along a path of supports. refit!(x, f; support, cache) with a RefitCache keeps the Cholesky factor $R^\top R = H_{SS}$ of least squares and of QuadraticFunction between calls, and a refit of the same f on a support that differs by a few coordinates updates the factor instead of computing it again. A coordinate that enters borders the factor, at the cost of a column of the Hessian and a triangular solve; one that leaves is removed by Givens rotations in $O(|S|^2)$. The step is then $\delta = -H_{SS}^{-1}\nabla_S f(x)$ from two triangular solves. When many coordinates change the cache factors again, and when an entering coordinate is nearly a combination of the others it falls back to the pivoted QR factorization above.
