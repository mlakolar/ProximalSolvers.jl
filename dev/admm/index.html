ADMM

The alternating direction method of multipliers, ADMMSolver, minimizes $f(x) + g(x)$ when both $f$ and $g$ have proximal operators that are cheap to compute, and neither needs to be smooth. Examples are the graphical lasso, where the proximal operator of $\operatorname{tr}(S\Theta) - \log\det\Theta$ takes one eigendecomposition and that of the ℓ1 norm is soft thresholding, and least squares with a penalty, where the proximal operator of the loss reuses one singular value decomposition of $X$ for every step size.

The iterations

ADMM splits the variable in two, and solves

\[\underset{x,\,z}{\text{minimize}} \quad f(x) + g(z) \quad \text{subject to} \quad x = z\]

by alternating minimization of the augmented Lagrangian

\[L_\rho(x, z, y) = f(x) + g(z) + \langle y, x - z\rangle + \frac{\rho}{2}\|x - z\|^2\]

over $x$ and over $z$, followed by a step on the multiplier $y$. In the scaled form, with $u = y/\rho$, and with over-relaxation by $\alpha \in (0, 2)$ (§3.4.3 of Boyd et al., 2011), each iteration is

\[\begin{aligned} x^{k+1} &= \operatorname{prox}_{f/\rho}\big(z^k - u^k\big), \\ \hat x^{k+1} &= \alpha\, x^{k+1} + (1 - \alpha)\, z^k, \\ z^{k+1} &= \operatorname{prox}_{g/\rho}\big(\hat x^{k+1} + u^k\big), \\ u^{k+1} &= u^k + \hat x^{k+1} - z^{k+1}. \end{aligned}\]

The $x$-update minimizes $f(x) + \frac{\rho}{2}\|x - z^k + u^k\|^2$, which is the proximal operator of $f$ with step $1/\rho$, and the $z$-update does the same for $g$. With $\alpha = 1$ this is plain ADMM; values between 1.5 and 1.8 are common choices; their effect depends on the problem. For closed, proper, convex $f$ and $g$ whose split Lagrangian has a saddle point, with exact proximal updates the iterates converge to a minimizer for every $\rho > 0$ and $\alpha \in (0, 2)$ (Eckstein and Bertsekas, 1992). The saddle-point condition includes dual attainment and strong duality; existence of a primal solution alone is not the stated convergence hypothesis (Boyd et al., 2011, §3.2).

The solver starts from $z^0 = x$, the given starting point, and $u^0 = 0$ or a given $u$. It returns $z$, which lies in the domain of $g$: for the ℓ1 norm it is exactly sparse, while $x$ is only close to sparse.

Residuals and stopping

A solution satisfies primal feasibility, $x = z$, and dual feasibility, $0 \in \partial f(x) + y$ and $0 \in \partial g(z) - y$. The optimality condition of the $z$-update is $0 \in \partial g(z^{k+1}) - \rho u^{k+1}$, so the second condition holds at every iteration. That of the $x$-update, $0 \in \partial f(x^{k+1}) + \rho(x^{k+1} - z^k + u^k)$, is, for $\alpha = 1$, $0 \in \partial f(x^{k+1}) + \rho u^{k+1} + \rho(z^{k+1} - z^k)$: the first condition holds up to $\rho(z^{k+1} - z^k)$. So the two residuals are

\[r^{k+1} = x^{k+1} - z^{k+1} \quad\text{(primal)}, \qquad s^{k+1} = \rho\,(z^{k+1} - z^k) \quad\text{(dual)},\]

and the solver stops when both are small, as in §3.3 of Boyd et al.:

\[\|r\| \le \sqrt{n}\,\varepsilon_{\text{abs}} + \varepsilon_{\text{rel}} \max\{\|x\|, \|z\|\}, \qquad \|s\| \le \sqrt{n}\,\varepsilon_{\text{abs,dual}} + \varepsilon_{\text{rel}}\, \rho\|u\|,\]

where $n$ is the number of entries of $x$, $\rho\|u\| = \|y\|$, and $\varepsilon_{\text{abs}}$, $\varepsilon_{\text{abs,dual}}$ and $\varepsilon_{\text{rel}}$ are abstol, abstol_dual and reltol.

The norm is Euclidean by default. ADMMSolver(; norm) measures the residuals, $\|x\|$, $\|z\|$ and $\|u\|$ in another norm, a function of an array: for a stack of $K$ matrices, for example, the sum of the norms of the slices, x -> sum(k -> norm(view(x, :, :, k)), axes(x, 3)). If the function also has a method norm(x, y) for the norm of $x - y$, the residuals are computed with it, without forming the differences.

With gaptol > 0 in ProximalOptions and functions that have a duality gap, such as the graphical lasso, the relative gap at $z$ replaces the residual tests; see Duality gaps.

Choosing ρ

The parameter $\rho$ trades the two residuals: a large $\rho$ enforces $x = z$ strongly and leaves a large dual residual, a small one the reverse, and a poor value can slow convergence by orders of magnitude. With adaptive = true, the solver balances the residuals (§3.4.1 of Boyd et al.): with each residual divided by its tolerance, $\tilde r = \|r\|/\varepsilon_{\text{pri}}$ and $\tilde s = \|s\|/\varepsilon_{\text{dual}}$,

\[\rho \leftarrow \begin{cases} 2\rho & \text{if } \tilde r > 10\, \tilde s, \\ \rho / 2 & \text{if } \tilde s > 10\, \tilde r, \\ \rho & \text{otherwise.} \end{cases}\]

The multiplier $y = \rho u$ does not change with $\rho$, so $u$ is divided by the same factor. After 50 changes $\rho$ stays fixed, which keeps the convergence guarantee of a fixed $\rho$.

Warm starts

A previous result res warm starts a nearby problem, such as the next level of a path of penalties: pass its estimate as the starting point, its scaled dual variable as the keyword u, and its final $\rho$ to the solver,

solve!(x, f, g, ADMMSolver(; ρ = res.ρ, adaptive = true), options; u = res.u)

Of the ProximalOptions, ADMM uses maxiter, time_limit, prox_calls_limit, show_trace, print_every, gaptol and gapevery. Each iteration evaluates two proximal operators and no function values, except to print the trace and to report the minimum.
