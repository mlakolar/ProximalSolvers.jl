Sparse graph-fused regression

An irregular graph connects coefficients expected to be similar. Sparsity selects vertices and fusion encourages equal values across edges. This is not a chain TV problem: the graph has cycles and nonlocal edges, and the underdetermined design couples the coefficients through the loss.

We minimize

\[F(x)=\tfrac12\|b-Xx\|^2+\tfrac\mu2\|x\|^2 +\lambda_1\|x\|_1+\lambda_2\|Dx\|_1.\]

Ridge makes the minimizer unique, and supplies a computable lower bound for comparing algorithms. The lecture derives the certificate and both update rules. Reference routines live in examples/support/primal_dual_lessons.jl; they are teaching code, not API.

using LinearAlgebra, Printf, ProximalBase, ProximalSolvers
Base.include(@__MODULE__, joinpath(pkgdir(ProximalSolvers), "examples", "support", "primal_dual_lessons.jl"))
using .PrimalDualLessons

a = graph_problem(12)
println((observations = length(a.b), coefficients = a.p, edges = size(a.D, 1)))
(observations = 72, coefficients = 144, edges = 295)

Separate the simple operations

Set f to least squares plus ridge, g to coefficient ℓ1, and h to edge ℓ1. The joint prox of g + h ∘ D is unavailable, but prox(g) is soft thresholding and prox(h*) clips edge duals to [-λ₂, λ₂]. No normal matrix or factorization is required by Condat–Vũ. We use the certified bound L = ‖X‖₁‖X‖∞ + μ, rather than an unverified spectral-norm estimate.

tol = 1e-6
pd = native_solve(a; tol)
@assert pd.converged
@printf("Condat–Vũ: %d iterations, relative certificate %.2e\n", pd.iterations, pd.cert.relative)
Condat–Vũ: 975 iterations, relative certificate 7.94e-07

Compare with a factorization-based alternative

ADMM introduces z = [I; D]x. Its x step solves a system with matrix X'X + μI + ρ(I + D'D); its z step is weighted soft thresholding. The factorization is reused at every step. This is a general linear-constraint ADMM reference, distinct from the package's x = z ADMMSolver interface. Both methods stop at the same certificate, checked every 25 iterations.

ad = split_admm(a; ρ = 1.0, tol)
@assert ad.converged
@printf("Split ADMM: %d iterations, relative certificate %.2e\n", ad.iterations, ad.cert.relative)
Split ADMM: 125 iterations, relative certificate 6.60e-07

Strong convexity bounds the distance to the unique minimizer. In particular, the distance between two approximate solutions is at most the sum of their individual certified distance bounds. No assertion about elapsed time belongs in a correctness example.

distance = norm(pd.x - ad.x)
bound = pd.cert.distance_bound + ad.cert.distance_bound
@assert distance <= bound + 1e-8
println((solution_distance = distance, certified_bound = bound))
(solution_distance = 0.00038686297567889295, certified_bound = 0.03075129656170479)

What this comparison teaches

Condat–Vũ works directly with X, X', D and D'. ADMM can take fewer steps after paying for a factorization. Its advantage depends on fill-in and on whether that factorization can be reused across fits. The separate benchmark includes setup, checks both methods against a JuMP/HiGHS QP, and reports two ADMM penalty parameters. A small graph is a correctness example; it cannot establish a large-scale performance advantage.
