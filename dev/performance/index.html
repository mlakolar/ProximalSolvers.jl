CoordinateDescent comparison

Summary

This page compares the time CoordinateDescent 0.6.1 (legacy) and HighDimensionalModels on ProximalSolvers (current) take to reach the same certified accuracy, a relative duality gap of at most 1e-4. Ratios are current/legacy time, so a ratio below 1 means current is faster. Over the 82 of 88 cases that reach the target on both sides, the like-for-like ratio is 0.977 [0.955, 1.00] (12 blocks, campaign of 2026-10-28). Current's best stopping rule gives 0.845, and the package defaults give 0.897. In the primary view, the large path speedup occurs on design C (0.150), where current uses the Gram path; the point estimates on designs A, B and E are within 2% of legacy. The square-root, scaled and weighted lasso groups are 7–14% slower.

No case is a candidate regression. The A/A noise floors are wide (0.516, 0.507 and 0.436 on the log scale for the three views), so differences of that size remain inconclusive, and an inconclusive result is not a tie.

The measurements use Julia 1.13.3, OpenBLAS through libblastrampoline (ILP64), one Julia thread and one BLAS thread, on a shared machine with an AMD EPYC 9754 CPU. Each arm runs in a fresh process. The timed operation is the compiled public-API call, including model construction, initialization and result conversion inside it. It measures the whole package stack. Compilation, input generation and certification are outside timing. The quadratic cases also exclude preparation of X'X/n and -X'y/n.

The matched target is a certified relative duality gap at most 1e-4, or a feasible-lasso self-consistency value at most 1e-4. The latter is the weighted-lasso gap at loadings recomputed from the returned coefficients' residuals, not a convex certificate for the whole iterative procedure. To read the target in terms of coefficients, the calibration records give a comparison. The comparison uses the 648 coordinate-ladder settings whose gap lies between 1e-6 and 1e-2, feasible lasso excluded. For each of them, the largest absolute difference from the coefficients of the tightest setting of the same case and side, over all levels for paths, is a median 2.12 times the relative gap, and between 0.245 and 8.24 times for 90% of the settings. At 1e-4, coefficients therefore typically agree with a much tighter fit to about 2e-4. This is an empirical summary of these records, not an error bound. The calculation is checks/coefficient_error.py in the campaign archive.

The inputs are designs A, B, C and E: independent Gaussian columns for A, AR(1) correlation for B and C, and equicorrelation for E. The extended design D is outside this campaign. Cases cover the lasso, fixed weighted lasso, full lasso paths, quadratic L1, group lasso, square-root lasso, scaled lasso and feasible lasso, with cyclic and random coordinate orders. There is no intercept or standardization in the API calls; input columns have fixed normalization. The comparison suite README specifies the inputs, objectives, settings, certificates and execution commands.

Noise floor

The A/A arm compares current with the same code and settings in a separate process. On the log scale, δ = max(log(1.05), q), where q is the upper 95% confidence bound for the pooled 95th percentile of |log(current/current-AA)|. The order-statistic bound assumes independent, identically distributed observations; dependence between cases within a block limits its nominal coverage.

A candidate requires its per-case log-interval lower endpoint to exceed δ. One-sided tests against δ undergo Benjamini–Hochberg screening at 10% across estimable cases separately in each view. No case meets either screen here. An interval overlapping [-δ, δ] is inconclusive, not a tie.

The A/A arm shows a timing disturbance in blocks 3–5, consistent with contention from other work on the shared machine. Their median |log(current/current-AA)| is about 0.10–0.25, compared with about 0.01–0.05 in the other blocks. Even without those blocks, calls of 1–5 ms drive the pooled upper tail and keep the noise floor wide.

The #110 same-code check

The unchanged #116 quick harness uses two series from one locked environment, with 3 interleaved repetitions each. Across 60 cases, the geometric mean time ratio is 1.002, with range 0.943–1.073. Exactly 2/60 paired 95% intervals exclude 1: 58 are inconclusive and 2 indicate shorter time in the second series. Recorded accuracy and Julia heap bytes are identical between the two series. These unchanged-code checks describe repeatability on those workloads; they do not remove the comparison suite's measured A/A noise.

Limitations

  • The measurements cover one shared machine and these designs. They do not establish performance on other hardware, other estimators or other inputs.
  • Timings describe the whole public-API call, not isolated solver kernels. They do not measure statistical quality, and these calls use no intercept or standardization.
  • The measured noise floor limits per-case sensitivity to about 25–68% on the time-ratio scale, from the nine-block primary check to the headline primary analysis. Smaller changes can remain inconclusive even when an interval excludes 1.
  • A candidate needs an independent rerun in fresh processes before it counts as a confirmed regression. No result here is independently confirmed.
  • The results describe ProximalSolvers 0.2.0 at the commit above. Later releases, including 0.3.0, are not covered by this campaign.

The 0.80 ratio from the retired benchmark/engine is a historical mixed-workload aggregate. It includes CovSel, DiffPrecTest, refit, IHT, gradient and ADMM cases and is not comparable with this suite. Those studies remain in the historical benchmark tree.

Reproducing

The suite README contains the setup, artifact, fixture, calibration, confirmation, validation, analysis and cross-check commands. The campaign pre-release holds the raw records, coefficients, input artifact, lockfiles, commands and analysis outputs, with a SHA-256 list. The suite's checked-in lock has since moved to ProximalSolvers 0.3 and a later HighDimensionalModels revision. Reproducing this campaign uses the archived lockfiles and the commit above.

The independent Python certificate cross-check matches 1,990/1,990 calibration coefficient files and 6,816/6,816 confirmation files, including every archived path level. The confirmation total includes the matched arms and the defaults arms. An independent recomputation from the raw process medians reproduces the headline case ratios, noise floors and screening decisions. Its group bootstrap endpoints can differ slightly because it uses fewer resamples. The same script computes the nine-block check. The archive's checks/ folder holds it (recompute.py), its outputs, the coefficient-error calculation and the cross-check summaries; COMMANDS.md there lists how to rerun each from the downloaded assets.
