Penalty paths and screening

A penalty with a level $\lambda$ defines a family of problems, and the solution is usually wanted along a decreasing sequence of levels, to choose $\lambda$ by cross-validation or an information criterion. solve_path computes it with warm starts and screening, and solve!(x, f, g, CDSolver(); continuation) uses the same machinery to reach one level.

Penalty families

These penalties of ProximalBase have a level, and solve_path(f, g, λs) replaces the level of g by each $\lambda$; the capped ℓ1 and log penalties have paths by local linear approximation (see Nonconvex penalties: exact steps or local linear approximation):

Penalty$g_\lambda(x)$weight of coordinate $k$
NormL1(λ, w)$\lambda\sum_k w_k\lvert x_k\rvert$$w_k$
NormL1Nonneg(λ, w)$\lambda\sum_k w_k x_k$ on $x \ge 0$$w_k$
ElasticNet(λ, λ₂, w)$\lambda\sum_k w_k\lvert x_k\rvert + \tfrac{\lambda_2}{2}\lVert x\rVert^2$$w_k$
Scaled(ElasticNet(α, 1 - α, w), λ)$\lambda\big(\alpha\sum_k w_k\lvert x_k\rvert + \tfrac{1 - \alpha}{2}\lVert x\rVert^2\big)$$\alpha w_k$
MCP(λ, a), SCAD(λ, a)nonconvex, with slope $\lambda$ at zero$1$
GroupMCP(λ, groups, w; a), GroupSCAD(λ, groups, w; a)MCP and SCAD of the group norms $\lVert x_{G_k}\rVert_2$, with slope $\lambda w_k$ at zero$w_k$
GroupNormL2(λ, groups, w)$\lambda\sum_k w_k\lVert x_{G_k}\rVert_2$$w_k$
Scaled(SparseGroupNormL2(α, 1 - α, groups, w), λ)$\lambda\big(\alpha\lVert x\rVert_1 + (1 - \alpha)\sum_k w_k\lVert x_{G_k}\rVert_2\big)$see below

In each, a coordinate at zero is optimal (stationary, for MCP and SCAD) while the gradient of the loss over it is at most $\lambda w_k$ in absolute value, or in Euclidean norm for a group: the right derivative of every penalty at zero is $\lambda w_k$. A coordinate with $w_k = 0$ is not penalized.

The level of ElasticNet(λ, λ₂) is $\lambda$, with $\lambda_2$ fixed. The elastic-net path of glmnet at a fixed mixing $\alpha$ moves both terms with $\lambda$: it is the path of Scaled(ElasticNet(α, 1 - α), λ), whose level is the scale $\lambda$.

The sparse group lasso of R's SGL and sparsegl fixes the mixture $\alpha$ and moves both terms with $\lambda$: it is the path of Scaled(SparseGroupNormL2(α, 1 - α, groups, w), λ) on an AtomIterate(p, groups), whose level is the scale $\lambda$; $\alpha = 0$ is the group lasso and $\alpha = 1$ the lasso. A group at zero is optimal while

\[\big\lVert \operatorname{soft}(\nabla_{G_k} f, \alpha\lambda)\big\rVert_2 \le (1 - \alpha)\lambda w_k ,\]

with $\operatorname{soft}(v, c)_j = \operatorname{sign}(v_j)(|v_j| - c)_+$, a condition that the norm of the gradient does not capture. The left side minus the right is nonincreasing in $\lambda$, so it holds from a root $\lambda_k(\nabla_{G_k} f)$ on, and the root takes the place of $\lVert\nabla_{G_k} f\rVert_2 / w_k$ below: $\lambda_{\max}$ is the largest root at zero, and screening compares the roots with the levels. Between the breakpoints $\alpha\lambda = |\nabla_j f|$ the squared norm of the soft-thresholded gradient is a quadratic in $\lambda$, so the root is exact: Newton's method from zero, which never passes it on this convex function, finds the right segment in a few steps, and the root of the quadratic there ends it. A group with $w_k = 0$ keeps its $\ell_1$ term when $\alpha > 0$. Applied to a dual point instead of the gradient, the largest root is the dual norm of the penalty, which builds the dual point of the duality gap and the gap-safe tests of CDSolver(; gaptol, gapsafe) (Ndiaye, Fercoq, Gramfort and Salmon, 2016).

Each of the separable penalties can be restricted to a box, Constrained(g, IndBox(l, u)), which has the level and the weights of g; zero must be in the box, $l \le 0 \le u$, as glmnet requires of its limits. A coordinate at zero is then optimal while the part of the gradient that points into the box is at most $\lambda w_k$: $(-\nabla_k f)_+$ if $u_k > 0$ and $(\nabla_k f)_+$ if $l_k < 0$. $\lambda_{\max}$ and the strong rule use that part, so that $\lambda_{\max}$ of the nonnegative lasso, Constrained(NormL1(λ), IndBox(0, Inf)), is $\max_k (-\nabla_k f(0))_+ / w_k$.

The largest useful level

Zero minimizes $f + g_\lambda$ exactly when every penalized coordinate satisfies this condition at zero:

\[\lambda_{\max} = \max_{k:\, w_k > 0} \frac{|\nabla_k f(0)|}{w_k} \qquad\Big(\max_k \frac{\|\nabla_{G_k} f(0)\|_2}{w_k} \text{ for groups}\Big),\]

and $\max_k \lambda_k(\nabla_{G_k} f(0))$ for the sparse group lasso.

lambda_max(f, g, x) computes it from the gradient at zero, for iterates like x; for the lasso of $\|y - X\beta\|^2/(2n)$ it is $\|X^\top y\|_\infty/n$. With unpenalized coordinates the formula still uses the gradient at zero, while the smallest level with all penalized coordinates at zero would use the gradient at the fit of the unpenalized ones. A common grid is geometric, from $\lambda_{\max}$ down to $\epsilon\lambda_{\max}$ with $\epsilon$ between $10^{-3}$ and $10^{-1}$.

Warm starts and the strong rule

Each level starts from the solution at the level before, which is close when the levels are close. Screening also uses it to predict which coordinates stay at zero. Let $c_j(\lambda) = \nabla_j f(\hat x(\lambda))$ at the solution $\hat x(\lambda)$. The sequential strong rule of Tibshirani et al. (2012) leaves coordinate $j$ out at the level $\lambda_k$ when

\[|c_j(\lambda_{k-1})| < (2\lambda_k - \lambda_{k-1})\, w_j .\]

The rule is exact if $|c_j(\lambda) - c_j(\lambda')| \le w_j|\lambda - \lambda'|$ along the path: then $|c_j(\lambda_k)| < (2\lambda_k - \lambda_{k-1})w_j + (\lambda_{k-1} - \lambda_k)w_j = \lambda_k w_j$, and coordinate $j$ is zero at $\lambda_k$. For the sparse group lasso the rule leaves group $G$ out when its root is below $2\lambda_k - \lambda_{k-1}$, that is when $\lVert\operatorname{soft}(c_G(\lambda_{k-1}), \alpha(2\lambda_k - \lambda_{k-1}))\rVert_2 < (1 - \alpha)(2\lambda_k - \lambda_{k-1})w_G$, as in sparsegl, and the checks below use the condition of the previous section. The condition often holds but can fail, so the rule is checked. At each level,

  1. the strong set $S$ holds the coordinates that pass the rule's threshold, $|c_j(\lambda_{k-1})| \ge (2\lambda_k - \lambda_{k-1})w_j$, those that are nonzero, and the unpenalized ones;
  2. coordinate descent solves the problem with its full passes over $S$ instead of all coordinates;
  3. one full gradient checks the optimality condition $|\nabla_j f(x)| \le \lambda_k w_j$ of every coordinate outside $S$; the violators join $S$, and step 2 runs again from the current solution.

The gradient computed in step 3 is the one the rule needs at the next level.

The accuracy of each level

Each level stops on a certified relative duality gap,

\[\frac{P_k(x) - D_k(\theta)}{|P_k(x)|} \le \mathrm{gaptol}, \qquad P_k = f + g_{\lambda_k},\]

when the loss and the penalty have one (see Stopping on the duality gap): the default gaptol is PATH_GAPTOL = 1e-4, which puts the coefficients within about 1e-4 of the solution, relative to the largest (about 50 times the target at worst), and gaptol = 1e-3 is the faster setting for cross-validation and prediction; gaptol = 0 gives back the rule on the changes. In step 2, coordinate descent stops on the gap of the problem restricted to $S$, the coordinates outside held at zero, at the cost of one pass over $S$, where the gap of the whole problem reads every column. The full gradient of step 3 gives the gap of the whole problem: without violators the two agree up to rounding, since the coordinates outside $S$ then satisfy their dual constraints; if it still exceeds gaptol, step 2 runs again to a tenfold smaller target. The results report this gap per level. ProxNewton paths stop their Newton steps on the gap of the GLM in the same way, with each inner solve stopping on the gap of its quadratic model at a hundredth of the current gap, and the intercept, when the model has one, fitted exactly before each gap.

Other losses and penalties, a coordinate without penalty whose gradient is not zero, LLA paths and gaptol = 0 keep the rule on the changes of CDSolver, with its tol. solve_path starts the rule at the $\lambda_{\max}$ of its starting point, which need not be zero. The results report, per level, the passes and the number of checks: one when the strong rule made no mistake.

Calling solve_path

solve_path(f, g, λs, method = CDSolver(); x, screening = true, maxnnz = Inf, callback = nothing, gaptol) takes decreasing nonnegative levels. The iterate x is the starting point and holds the last solution on return; it defaults to a zero SparseIterate when the loss has columns, and must be given otherwise, as an AtomIterate for a group penalty or a SymmetricSparseIterate for a symmetric matrix. The path stops early after the first solution with more than maxnnz nonzero coordinates, or when callback(k, λ, x, m) returns true; m is the model of the loss, current for x, from which a callback can compute a validation error, residuals or a gradient. The PathResults hold, per level, the level, the solution as a sparse vector (a sparse matrix for a matrix iterate), the objective, the passes, whether coordinate descent converged, the number of checks, and the certified relative gap (NaN where the rule on the changes stopped the level).

Continuation

solve!(x, f, g, CDSolver(); continuation = K) sets x to zero and solves at the $K + 1$ levels

\[\lambda_j = \lambda_{\max}\Big(\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{\max}}\Big)^{j/K}, \qquad j = 0, 1, \dots, K,\]

geometrically from $\lambda_{\max}$ down to the level $\lambda$ of g, each warm started from the one before and screened as above (screening = true, the default except on a SymmetricSparseIterate, where a coordinate step changes the gradient everywhere, so that the checks cost more passes than they save). The levels before g only provide warm starts, so they are solved to the looser tolerance continuation_tol, $\sqrt{\mathrm{tol}}$ by default, by the rule on the changes, and only g itself to the final accuracy: a relative gap of gaptol (PATH_GAPTOL = 1e-4 by default) when the loss and the penalty have one, tol otherwise; continuation_tol = tol solves every level fully. It returns the solution at g. When the level of g is zero or at least $\lambda_{\max}$, or g has no level (IndBox, ZeroFunction), it solves from zero directly.

On a convex problem the minimizer is the same either way, and continuation is a question of speed. On the lasso design of A lasso path, it took half the passes of a direct solve from zero, or fewer, at $0.01\lambda_{\max}$, and fewer at $0.02\lambda_{\max}$, but more at $0.05\lambda_{\max}$ and above, where the direct solve is short anyway.

For MCP and SCAD it also changes the answer. From zero, coordinate descent can stop at a poor stationary point when $p \gg n$ and the columns are correlated; along the levels, the solution follows a branch of stationary points from the zero solution, as along a path. In Other penalties, with MCP, it ends at less than half the objective of the direct solve, with a far smaller estimation error.

Nonconvex penalties: exact steps or local linear approximation

For a nonconvex penalty the algorithm decides which local solution is reported, so recreating a published estimator means matching both its penalty and its algorithm. Two kinds of algorithm compute nonconvex paths here.

Exact coordinate steps. solve_path(f, MCP(1, a), λs) and its relatives (SCAD, GroupMCP, GroupSCAD) take each coordinate to the exact minimizer of the loss along it plus the penalty, as ncvreg and grpreg do (Breheny and Huang, 2011). λmax, the strong rule and the optimality checks all test a coordinate at zero by the slope of the penalty there, $|\nabla_k f| \le \lambda w_k$. This test agrees with the coordinate step only when the coordinate problem is convex:

\[a L_k > 1 \ \text{(MCP)}, \qquad (a - 1) L_k > 1 \ \text{(SCAD)},\]

where $L_k$ is the curvature of the step along coordinate $k$: the diagonal entry of the Hessian for a quadratic loss, the bound on the curvature of a GLM, the largest eigenvalue of the Gram matrix of a group, and on a SymmetricSparseIterate the curvature over the two entries of an off-diagonal coordinate, divided by two. When it fails, the step can leave zero where the test says that zero is optimal, so λmax is wrong and screening changes the local solution. A path, and continuation, then throw an ArgumentError that names the coordinate. With least squares, $\|y - X\beta\|^2/(2n)$ on columns of unit variance has $L_k = 1$, so that ncvreg's conditions $a > 1$ and $a > 2$ suffice; logistic regression by coordinate descent has $L_k = 1/4$ on such columns, and MCP needs $a > 4$. Otherwise standardize the columns, take a larger $a$, or use LLA. A single solve does not use the test and is left alone. Paths of ProxNewton, whose curvatures change with the weights of the Newton steps, are not checked.

Local linear approximation (LLA), also called reweighted ℓ1. The capped ℓ1, log, MCP and SCAD penalties are $\sum_k p_k(|x_k|)$ with each $p_k$ concave and nondecreasing on $[0, \infty)$, so they lie below their tangent at the current solution. Each stage of LLA minimizes the loss plus the tangent: the weighted lasso with the weights $p_k'(|x_k|)$ of the previous stage, from ProximalBase.lla_weights!, or the weighted group lasso with $p_k'(\|x_{G_k}\|_2)$ for group MCP and SCAD. The slopes are taken from the right, so that at zero they are $p_k'(0+)$ and the first stage from zero is the lasso. Each stage, solved exactly, decreases the loss plus the penalty (majorization-minimization), and each is convex, so λmax, the strong rule and the gap-safe rule are exact inside it, and the condition above does not arise.

solve_path(f, g, λs, LLA(inner = CDSolver(); stages = 2, tol, start = :lasso)) runs the stages at each level:

  • The first stage is, with start = :lasso, the lasso with the weights $p_k'(0)$, warm started from the lasso at the previous level, so that every level starts again from the lasso, as the papers do. With start = :previous it takes its weights from the solution at the previous level and starts from it, which follows the path more cheaply.
  • The later stages start from the stage before. stages bounds their number, the first included, and they stop early once the weights change by at most tol, relative to the largest; the default tol is rounding.
  • Screening. The first stage uses the strong rule for the step from the previous level. A later stage starts from the coordinates that are nonzero or violate their condition at the stage before: the weights change only on the nonzero coordinates, so the zero ones keep their condition. The optimality conditions are checked after every stage.
  • λmax is that of the first stage, lambda_max(f, g, x), which for CappedL1(λ, a, w) and LogPenalty(λ, ε, w) uses the slopes at zero, $\lambda w_k$ and $\lambda w_k/\varepsilon$.
  • The results are PathResults, whose values are the loss plus the nonconvex penalty, with the passes and checks summed over the stages and the number of stages at each level.

solve!(x, f, g, LLA()) runs the stages at the level of g, the first with the weights at x, and returns the ReweightResults of reweight!, on which it is built.
