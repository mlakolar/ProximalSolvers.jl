Losses for coordinate descent

Coordinate descent needs, for coordinate $k$, the derivative of the loss along it and its curvature, and after a step the update of whatever it caches. cdmodel(f, x) builds the CDModel of a loss of ProximalBase, which holds the cache; solve! builds it on its own, or takes one built beforehand so that many solves share it.

The first four may be scaled, as in (1/n) * LeastSquaresLoss(y, X), which the formulas below write as a factor $s$.

Least squares

For $f(\beta) = \frac{s}{2}\sum_i w_i (y_i - o_i - x_i^\top\beta)^2$, with observation weights $w_i$ and an offset $o$ (both optional), the model caches the residuals $r = y - o - X\beta$. Along coordinate $k$,

\[\partial_k f(\beta) = -s \sum_i w_i X_{ik} r_i, \qquad a_k = s \sum_i w_i X_{ik}^2,\]

so the step is $\beta_k^+ = \operatorname{prox}_{g_k/a_k}\big(\beta_k + \sum_i w_i X_{ik} r_i / \sum_i w_i X_{ik}^2\big)$, followed by $r \leftarrow r - (\beta_k^+ - \beta_k) X_k$. Both cost $O(n)$, and a coordinate that does not move costs only the inner product. The curvatures are computed on the first visit to each coordinate and cached. The gradient that screening and paths need is $-s X^\top W r$, one product with $X^\top$. With intercept = true the columns and the response are centered on the fly; see Coordinate descent.

The multi-response lasso

For an $n \times q$ response matrix $Y$, $f(B) = \frac{s}{2}\lVert Y - 1\beta_0^\top - XB\rVert_F^2$ with a $p \times q$ matrix $B$, iterated as an AtomIterate over vec(B) whose groups are the rows of $B$, [k:p:p*q for k in 1:p]. With GroupNormL2 on these groups it is the multi-response lasso, which selects the same predictors for all the responses. The model caches the residuals $R = Y - 1\beta_0^\top - XB$. The curvature of the loss over row $k$ is exactly $s\lVert X_k - \bar x_k\rVert^2$ times the identity, so each block step minimizes the loss plus the penalty over the row exactly, at a cost of $O(nq)$. With an intercept, $\beta_0 = \bar y - B^\top\bar x$ for each response, and intercept returns the vector of them.

Quadratics

For $f(x) = s\,(\tfrac12 x^\top A x + b^\top x + c)$ with $A$ symmetric positive semidefinite, the model caches $u = Ax$:

\[\partial_k f(x) = s\,(u_k + b_k), \qquad a_k = s A_{kk}, \qquad u \leftarrow u + (x_k^+ - x_k)\, A_{:k},\]

an $O(p)$ step for a dense $A$. A SparseMatrixCSC is kept sparse and its update visits only the stored entries of column $k$; a Diagonal updates only $u_k$. These floating-point matrices are shared with the loss, so a new solve or initialize! sees changes made to them in place. A sparse Symmetric wrapper is copied into a full sparse matrix once. Refits form a dense Hessian only on the selected support.

With $A = X^\top X/n$ and $b = -X^\top y/n$ this is the lasso of $\|y - X\beta\|^2/(2n)$ up to a constant, at a cost per step independent of $n$: the form to use when $n \gg p$, or when many problems share one Gram matrix, as the columns of a covariance matrix do.

Kronecker quadratics

KronQuadratic(A, B, C) is the quadratic of a $p \times q$ matrix $\Theta$,

\[f(\Theta) = s\Big(\tfrac12\langle\Theta, A\Theta B\rangle + \langle C, \Theta\rangle\Big) = s\Big(\tfrac12\operatorname{vec}(\Theta)^\top (B\otimes A)\operatorname{vec}(\Theta) + \operatorname{vec}(C)^\top \operatorname{vec}(\Theta)\Big),\]

for symmetric positive semidefinite $A$ and $B$. The model caches $M = A\Theta B$ and never forms the $pq \times pq$ matrix $B \otimes A$.

Entries of $\Theta$ (a SparseIterate holding $\Theta$ or $\operatorname{vec}\Theta$). Along the entry $(r, c)$,

\[\partial_{rc} f = s\,(M_{rc} + C_{rc}), \qquad a_{rc} = s A_{rr} B_{cc}, \qquad M \leftarrow M + h\, A_{:r} B_{c:},\]

a rank-one update in $O(pq)$ for a change $h$.

A symmetric $\Theta$ (a SymmetricSparseIterate). An off-diagonal coordinate $(r, c)$ moves along $D = E_{rc} + E_{cr}$. The derivative along $D$ is $s\langle D, A\Theta B + C\rangle$, and the curvature is $s\langle D, ADB\rangle$. With $\langle e_i e_j^\top, A\, e_k e_l^\top B\rangle = A_{ik} B_{jl}$ for symmetric $A$ and $B$, the four terms of $\langle D, ADB\rangle$ give

\[\partial_{rc} f = s\,(M_{rc} + M_{cr} + C_{rc} + C_{cr}), \qquad a_{rc} = s\,(A_{rr} B_{cc} + A_{cc} B_{rr} + 2 A_{rc} B_{rc}),\]

and the update is the rank-two $M \leftarrow M + h\,(A_{:r} B_{c:} + A_{:c} B_{r:})$. A diagonal coordinate is an entry as above. The step then follows Coordinates of the iterates, with the penalty counted twice off the diagonal. The gradient over symmetric matrices, which screening uses, is $s\big(\tfrac12(M + M^\top) + \tfrac12(C + C^\top)\big)$.

The D-trace loss. For two covariance matrices, KronQuadratic(Σx, Σy, Σx - Σy) is $\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma_x\Delta\Sigma_y\Delta) - \operatorname{tr}(\Delta(\Sigma_y - \Sigma_x))$, whose gradient vanishes at $\Delta = \Sigma_x^{-1} - \Sigma_y^{-1}$ (Zhao, Cai and Li, 2014): with an ℓ1 penalty it estimates the difference of two precision matrices directly. The inverse Hessians of DiffPrecTest.jl are Kronecker quadratics on $\operatorname{vec}\Theta$ too.

Generalized linear losses

For $f(\beta) = s\sum_i w_i\,\ell(\eta_i, y_i)$ with $\eta = X\beta + o$, the model caches $\eta$. A scalar loss whose curvature is bounded, $\ell'' \le \bar\ell$, gives the majorization step of Coordinate descent with

\[\partial_k f(\beta) = s\sum_i w_i\,\ell'(\eta_i, y_i)\, X_{ik}, \qquad a_k = s\,\bar\ell \sum_i w_i X_{ik}^2, \qquad \eta \leftarrow \eta + (\beta_k^+ - \beta_k)\, X_k .\]

The bound is $\bar\ell = 1/4$ for the logistic loss $\log(1 + e^\eta) - y\eta$, whose curvature is $\sigma(\eta)(1 - \sigma(\eta))$, and $\bar\ell = 1$ for the Huber loss. A step evaluates $\ell'$ at every observation, and the bound is far above the curvature where the fitted probabilities are near 0 or 1, so the steps are short. ProxNewton uses the curvature at the current point instead, and is usually much faster; it also handles the Poisson loss $e^\eta - y\eta$, whose curvature is unbounded and which coordinate descent therefore refuses.

The square-root lasso

SqrtLeastSquaresLoss(y, X) is $f(\beta) = \|y - X\beta\|$, the loss of the square-root lasso (Belloni, Chernozhukov and Wang, 2011). Its penalty level does not depend on the noise level, since the gradient at the true coefficients, $-X^\top\varepsilon/\|\varepsilon\|$, does not depend on the scale of the noise $\varepsilon$. The loss is not differentiable where the residuals vanish, so coordinate descent does not take a majorization step but minimizes $f + \lambda\sum_k w_k|\beta_k|$ exactly over each coordinate.

Fix every coordinate but $k$. With the partial residual $r = y - X\beta + X_k\beta_k$ and $x = X_k$, let $a = \|x\|^2$, $\sigma = x^\top r$ and $\rho = \|r\|^2$, and write $\lambda_k = \lambda w_k$. The coordinate problem is

\[\min_t\ \varphi(t) = \sqrt{\rho - 2\sigma t + a t^2} + \lambda_k |t| .\]

$\varphi$ is convex, and its subdifferential at zero is $-\sigma/\sqrt\rho + [-\lambda_k, \lambda_k]$, so $t = 0$ is the minimizer exactly when $|\sigma| \le \lambda_k\sqrt\rho$. Otherwise the minimizer has the sign of $\sigma$. Write the quadratic as $a(t - \sigma/a)^2 + d$ with $d = \rho - \sigma^2/a \ge 0$ (by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality), and $u = t - \sigma/a$. Setting $\varphi'(t) = 0$ gives

\[a u = -\lambda_k \operatorname{sign}(\sigma)\sqrt{a u^2 + d} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad u^2\,(a^2 - \lambda_k^2 a) = \lambda_k^2 d ,\]

which has a solution only if $\lambda_k^2 < a$; this holds, because $\lambda_k^2\rho < \sigma^2 \le a\rho$. Taking the root with the sign of $-\sigma$,

\[t^\star = \frac{1}{a}\left(\sigma - \operatorname{sign}(\sigma)\,\lambda_k\sqrt{\frac{\rho - \sigma^2/a}{1 - \lambda_k^2/a}}\right),\]

which has the sign of $\sigma$ exactly when $\sigma^2 > \lambda_k^2\rho$. A step costs $O(n)$: two inner products and the update of the residuals. Rounding can break $\lambda_k^2 < a$ when $r$ is nearly parallel to $x$, and the code then sets the coordinate to zero.

The square root of a quadratic

SqrtQuadraticFunction(A, b, c) is $f(x) = \sqrt{q(x)}$, $q(x) = x^\top A x + 2b^\top x + c$, for symmetric positive semidefinite $A$ and $q \ge 0$. With $A = X^\top X/n$, $b = -X^\top y/n$ and $c = y^\top y/n$ it is $\|y - Xx\|/\sqrt n$: the square-root lasso from sufficient statistics. With $x_k = t$ and the other coordinates fixed,

\[q = q_0 - 2\sigma t + a t^2, \qquad a = A_{kk}, \qquad \sigma = a x_k - (Ax + b)_k, \qquad q_0 = q(x) - x_k\big(2(Ax + b)_k - a x_k\big),\]

the form of the square-root lasso with $\rho = q_0$, so the same closed form gives the step. The model caches $u = Ax$, updated in $O(p)$, and the value $q$. An infinite weight keeps a coordinate at zero. With $A = S$, $b = -S_{:j}$ and $c = S_{jj}$ for a covariance matrix $S$, and an infinite weight on coordinate $j$, the loss is $\|Z_j - Z x\|/\sqrt n$ for the data $Z$ behind $S$: the scaled lasso of variable $j$ on the others, from which Sun and Zhang (2013) build a sparse precision matrix estimator, column by column.
