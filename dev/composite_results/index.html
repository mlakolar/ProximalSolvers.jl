Composite splitting: measurement record

Read Choosing a splitting for a composite problem for the objectives, derivations, certificate, and interpretation of the algorithms. These tables are generated from the retained CSV by benchmark/composite_lessons/report.py.

The measurements use Julia 1.13.2 on Apple M3, one Julia thread and one BLAS thread, with JuMP 1.32.1 and HiGHS.jl 1.26.1. Each row is the run with median total time among three repetitions of one seeded instance. Setup and solve times below are milliseconds. Compilation and data generation are excluded; norm bounds, normal-matrix formation, factorizations and QP model construction are included where required. All iterative methods are accepted at the same relative certificate target, 10⁻⁶, checked every 25 steps. HiGHS solves to tighter internal tolerances and passes the same external acceptance check.

The iteration cap is 200,000 and the iterative solve-time limit is 15 seconds. A row marked limit did not attain the target. Its elapsed time is not a time to convergence. QP iterations are not recorded. These are CPU single-fit measurements, not a comparison of warm-started paths or a hardware survey.

At p = 256, ordinary ADMM with ρ = 1 is faster than Condat–Vũ. At p = 1,024 their total costs are close; these few repetitions do not establish a meaningful speed advantage. Condat–Vũ is competitive here while avoiding a factorization. The ordinary ADMM Cholesky factor at p = 1,024 contains 511,035 nonzeros, despite sparse design and incidence inputs. This exposes a storage tradeoff, but we did not measure peak memory and should not turn a factor count into a total-memory claim.

The QP reference is slower in this experiment and returns a substantially smaller certificate. Its time is useful context for choosing a method, not evidence of superiority at equal achieved numerical error. Reusing an ADMM factor or a QP model across a path could change the comparison.

The specialized TV-split ADMM is the fastest tested method at both lengths. Its advantage is a better decomposition: a tridiagonal solve and a direct TV proximal operation. Ordinary ADMM improves markedly when ρ increases from 1 to 100; comparing only ρ = 1 would give a misleading picture. At ρ = 1 it hits the iteration cap with a certificate still above the target.

Condat–Vũ remains a valid general solution, but these data favor the specialized splitting for this unweighted chain problem. The ranking of QP and first-order methods also changes with sequence length. These are teaching implementations and a small parameter comparison, not a claim about the best available specialized trend-filtering software.

Accuracy, variability and reproduction

The raw CSV records min/max total times across repetitions, objectives, coefficient discrepancies from the QP reference, and strong-convexity distance bounds. All reference comparisons check those discrepancies against the certificates, including results that hit a limit. A correct loose bound does not mean such a result reached the requested accuracy.

The normal test suite executes both lecture examples. The independent oracle suite checks the common lower bound at arbitrary primal/dual points against the QP optimum, and checks the exact dual expression for trend filtering. The test results are correctness evidence separate from elapsed-time claims.

See the recreation recipe and retained records. The record includes package versions, source revisions, source-file Git blob hashes and fit CSVs used for the figures. The solver implementation is the merged issue-23 code; the teaching helpers and benchmark runner are additional sources identified individually by their hashes.
