# Matrix PGD KKT scale: measurements

This page records the measurements behind the entrywise KKT scale of [PGD-08](@ref) (issue
[#492](https://github.com/mlakolar/ProximalSolvers.jl/issues/492)). They are observations at stated revisions, not
solver guarantees.

## [evidence-kkt-scale-492 Study and provenance](@id evidence-kkt-scale-492)

**Statement.** Measured at ProximalSolvers master `fa6e31c6` with ProximalBase `bc9e888d`. The probes ran on a cluster (Slurm) with Julia 1.13.4; the issue's table was also reported on Julia 1.10.13. The option sweeps below replaced the solver's KKT-score helper by each candidate rule in the same process and recomputed every score in BigFloat at the returned point. Before the change, matrix PGD divided every entry's violation by the single scale `max(1, maximum(abs, S), maximum(C))` with `C = T(λ0) Λ`, and counted a completed step as converged when the largest ratio was at most `max(gradtol, 64eps(T))`, subject to the exits of [PGD-08](@ref). The maintainer chose rule F below. The first rule-F implementation (`560a9075`) predates the merge of pull request [#483](https://github.com/mlakolar/ProximalSolvers.jl/pull/483) (`17e33ddd`), which permits nonnegative diagonal weights ([PGD-06](@ref)). The integration of #483 into this branch followed (`a5e672f3`), and the diagonal-weight routes below are reachable through `solve!` from there.

- **Routes at the single scale.** Start `2I` (or `θ = 2`), `maxiter = 100`, `ftol = 0`. Every row was reproduced at the head `76077c98` of pull request [#483](https://github.com/mlakolar/ProximalSolvers.jl/pull/483), which accepts a positive diagonal weight. The first row was also reproduced on master `fa6e31c6`, whose [PGD-06](@ref) required zero diagonal weights, and so was the Float32 row below it.

  | Case | Data | `gradtol` | Returned point | Optimum | `converged` | Reported score |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Zero diagonal weight, PSD `S`, Float64 | `S = [1 0.1; 0.1 1]`, off-diagonal `C = 1e9`, diagonal `C = 0` | `5e-10` | `Θ₁₁ = 1.5`, one step | `I` | true | `3.3e-10` |
  | Same, Float32 | off-diagonal `C = 1e4` | 0 (floor `64eps`) | `Θ₁₁ = 1.0238` | `I` | true | `2.3e-6` |
  | Positive diagonal weight, indefinite `S`, Float64 | `S = -2^32`, `C = 2^32 + 1` | `5e-10` | `θ = 1.5`, one step | 1 | true | `7.8e-11` |
  | Covariance-shift form | `Sshift = S + C = 1`, `Cshift = 0`, Float64 | `5e-10` | `1 + 9.4e-14` | 1 | true | `9.4e-14` |
  | Positive diagonal, Float32 | `S = -2^16`, `C = 2^16 + 1` | 0 | `θ = 1.5` | 1 | true | `5.1e-6` |
  | Positive diagonal, Float32 | `S = -4096`, `C = 4097` | 0 | `θ ≈ 1.024` | 1 | true | `5.7e-6` |
  | Positive diagonal, Float64 | `S = -1000`, `C = 1001` | `5e-10` | `θ ≈ 1 + 3.1e-7` | 1 | true | `3.1e-10` |

  At `θ = 1.5` in the third row the unnormalized residual is `|S + C - 1/θ| = 1/3` and the scale is about `2^32`. In the first row the off-diagonal penalty `1e9` suppresses an order-one diagonal violation.
- **Wider than the table.** With `S = [1 0.1; 0.1 1]`, Float64 `λ0 = 1e8` stopped at `θ₁₁ = 1.0238`, and Float32 `λ0 = 100` stopped `5.5e-4` from the optimum. Raising `maxiter` to 20000 changed nothing.
- **Seeded problems with large weights.** Positive semidefinite problems with a few structural-zero weights at `1e6`, or adaptive weights `0.05/|θ̃ᵢⱼ|` capped at `1e4`, `1e6` or `1e8`, 48 problems per precision. The single scale returned `converged = true` at a point whose BigFloat per-entry score, rule F below, exceeded `100 g_tol` in 23 of 48 problems (Float32) and 24 of 48 (Float64).
- **Cancellation in the numerator.** With the representable Float64 inputs `S = -2^51`, `C = 2^51 + 1`, `θ = 1.25`, the order `(S - inv(θ)) + C` gave `0.0` and `(S + C) - inv(θ)` gave about `0.2`. This is IEEE arithmetic on a scalar, not a completed solve. A change of the denominator alone cannot recover a numerator that has been rounded to zero.
- **A scale that contains the penalty.** [STOP-03](@ref) requires each entry score to be strictly below `τ max(cᵢ, λmax)`, a threshold with the penalty `cᵢ` in its scale. Rule F has no separate penalty-magnitude term such as `max(cᵢ, λmax)`; the diagonal weight enters through the sum `aᵢ = Sᵢᵢ + Cᵢᵢ` before the root. With `Sᵢᵢ = -2³²` and `Cᵢᵢ = 2³² + 1`, at `Θᵢᵢ = 3/2` the violation is `1/3`; over a scale near `2³²` it is about `7.8e-11`, below `gradtol = 5e-10` (the third row of the routes table).
- **Two routes found while the plan was critiqued, both measured on master.**
  1. Indefinite `S` with an off-diagonal entry that cancels its penalty. Float64 `S = [1 1e9; 1e9 1]`, `C₁₂ = 1e9 - 0.4`: the single scale converged after one step, 26% from the optimum `inv([1 e; e 1])` (the original measurement used start `2I`, `maxiter = 200`, `ftol = 0`). Here `e = S₁₂ - C₁₂` is the effective stored difference, `0.3999999761581421` in Float64; the displayed `0.4` approximates it. Float32 with `S₁₂ = 2¹⁶` also stopped after one step, at a relative max-entry error of 28.9% against 26.0% in Float64. These two errors were re-measured on master `fa6e31c6`, Julia 1.13.4, from `2I` with `maxiter = 100`.
  2. One large variance, no penalty. Float64 `S = diag(1e9, 1.4)` converged at `θ₂₂ = 1` against an optimum of 0.714, and Float32 `S = diag(1e5, 1.5)` at `θ₂₂ = 1` against 0.667. PGD itself makes almost no progress on `θ₂₂` here, because its step is limited by the curvature in `θ₁₁`.
- **Options compared (the historical sweeps, by helper replacement and BigFloat recomputation).** Every option kept the violations and `g_tol = max(gradtol, 64eps(T))`; only the denominator changed. Write `a = diag(S + C)`. The denominators in the table are mathematical or prototype formulas, not the merged code: the later rule-C sweep formed rule C's `maximum(abs, inv(Θ))` as `maximum(abs, S - G)`, a proxy for the inverse (the earlier sweep formed `inv(Symmetric(Θ))`), and rule F's diagonal product was formed before the root, `sqrt(|aᵢ| |aⱼ|)`; the merged rule takes each root first (`sᵢ sⱼ`). "Ordinary" means 4 seeds × `p ∈ {5, 10, 20}` × uniform `λ ∈ {0.02, 0.05, 0.1, 0.3}`, plus `λ` at 0.9 to 2 times `λmax`: 48 problems per regime and precision.

  | Rule | Denominator | Routes of the issue | Route 1 (indefinite) | Route 2 (large variance) | Large weights: converged solves with independent `R > 100 g_tol`, F32 / F64 | Ordinary PSD problems |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | single scale | `max(1, maximum(abs, S), maximum(C))` | certifies far points | certifies | certifies | 23 / 24 of 48 | baseline |
  | A | per entry: diagonal `max(1, \|aᵢ\|)`, off-diagonal `max(1, \|Sᵢⱼ\|, Cᵢⱼ)` | fixed | fixed (stagnated, `converged = false`) | fixed (`converged = false`) | 0 / 0 | F32: 4 of 192 lost, up to 76× more iterations; F64: up to 14.7× |
  | B | `max(1, maximum(abs, S + Diagonal(C)), max off-diagonal C)` | master route not fixed | certifies | certifies | not measured | identical to the single scale |
  | C | `max(1, maximum(abs, S + Diagonal(C)), maximum(abs, inv(Θ)))` | fixed | certifies | certifies | 0 / 0 | identical to the single scale |
  | G | `max(1, maximum(abs, a))` | fixed | fixed (stagnated) | certifies | 0 / 0 | identical to the single scale |
  | F, adopted | per entry `max(1, sqrt(\|aᵢ\| \|aⱼ\|))` | fixed | fixed (stagnated) | fixed (`converged = false`) | 0 / 0 | F32: 1 of 48 lost, up to 23×; F64: up to 1.57× |

  In the 48-problem large-weight sweep per precision, each of the rules A, C, G and F gave the same rounded median iteration ratio against the single scale (82× in Float32, 3.2× in Float64) and the same stop count within 20000 iterations (21 of 48 in Float32, 27 of 48 in Float64); the single scale stopped 39 and 40 of the 48 problems, 23 and 24 of them at a point with independent `R > 100 g_tol`. These are aggregates over the sweep. They do not establish an increase in iterations for each problem that the single scale had stopped. "Identical to the single scale" in the last column refers to the convergence and the iteration counts of the 48 ordinary problems per precision. A sweep with variances spread over `10^±1` to `10^±4` stopped 1 of 48 under each of the five rules measured, so the stop counts tie, while its largest iteration ratios differ (A and F up to 1.41 in Float32 and 1.14 in Float64, C and G 1.00). A per-entry rule can be stricter than the single scale where an entry's denominator is smaller than the global scale; the issue's analysis attributed the Float32 losses under rules A and F to that, for `max|S| > 1`. The critique rejected rule C because its `maximum(abs, S + Diagonal(C))` keeps the off-diagonal `|Sᵢⱼ|` in the scale, which lets route 1 through. Its term `maximum(abs, inv(Θ))` can change a decision relative to rule G: for the scalar `S = 1`, `C = 0`, `Θ = 225/256` and `g_tol = 1/8`, rule G scores `31/225 > 1/8` and rule C scores `31/256 < 1/8`. In exact arithmetic, when rule C's fixed part of the scale equals rule G's and the tolerance is `τ` with `0 ≤ τ < 1`, a point that rule C accepts is accepted by rule G at `τ/(1 - τ)`, because `maximum(abs, inv(Θ))` is at most that scale plus the violation (here `τ/(1 - τ) = 1/7 > 31/225`). The off-diagonal term of rule C's fixed part can change that part separately from rule G's.
- **Rounding in the historical arithmetic, where the violation came from `G`.** In the cancellation cases, where `|Sᵢⱼ|` or `Cᵢⱼ` is much larger than the scale and the terms cancel, the rounding error of the computed violation could be of the order of `eps(T) max(|Sᵢⱼ|, Cᵢⱼ)`. The threshold could then be out of reach, and a solve could end by stagnation or at the iteration limit with `converged = false`. The computed violation could also be exactly 0 while the true one is not: `fl(S - 1/θ) = -C` holds over a band of `θ`. [PGD-08](@ref) now marks a point uncertain when its test flags any entry, and then attempts to evaluate every entry from the inputs, with the handling of nonfinite terms stated there.
- **Two inputs found in the review of the first implementation** (3 × 3, zero weights except `C₁₂ = C₂₁ = B`). At the prescribed starts the exact score is `R = 1/4`, the violation of entry `(1, 2)`; the 2048-bit BigFloat score, reported after conversion to Float64, is `0.25` in every case. The first implementation is commit `560a9075` and the code observed is `7fe5d8a1`, on Julia 1.13.4 and 1.10.13 with identical values.
  1. A small variance: `D = diag(2ᵃ, 2⁻ᵇ, 1)`, `Θ = D [1 0 1; 0 1 -1; 1 -1 3] D`, `S = [2^(-2b-1) 1/4 0; 1/4 2^(2b+1) 2ᵇ; 0 2ᵇ 1]`, `B = 2^(b-a)`, `(a, b) = (30, 58)` in Float32 and `(55, 110)` in Float64. `W₁₂ = -B` in exact arithmetic, and the unit floor of the small diagonal lets `|W₁₂|` exceed the scale of entry `(1, 2)`. The first implementation converged after one step, reporting `9.31e-10` (Float32) and `2.78e-17` (Float64). At `7fe5d8a1` the start score is `0.25` (Float32) and `0.24999999999999994` (Float64, two representable steps below `0.25`), and the solve stagnates after one step with `converged = false`.
  2. An overflowing unused endpoint: `B = 0.75 floatmax(T)`, `Θ = [2 0 1; 0 2 -σ; 1 -σ 2]` with `σ = ±1`, and `S` equal to `Θ⁻¹` except `S₁₂ = S₂₁ = σB`. The first implementation converged after one step, reporting `5.96e-8` (Float32) and `4.44e-16` (Float64). At `7fe5d8a1` the start score is `0.2499999701976776` (Float32) and `0.2499999999999999` (Float64), against the exact `1/4`. The Float32 solves stagnate after one step with the iterate unchanged. The Float64 solves reach `maxiter = 50` with `converged = false`, and their iterate moved: the independent score at the returned point is about `0.2499999892761321`.
- **What `test/logdet_kkt_scale.jl` measured** (revision `7fe5d8a1` for the observations below, Julia 1.10.13 and 1.13.4). The BigFloat comparisons use the stored data at the prescribed start or the returned point: 256 bits, and 2048 bits for the two inputs above, a chosen reference precision. The first of those inputs has a large condition number and the second spans an exponent gap between `B` and `1/4`; a standalone 256-bit BigFloat Cholesky calculation matched the analytic inverse of the first in both configurations. The comparisons check the reported score and the stop rule against the definition of [PGD-08](@ref), with an estimate of the rounding of the computed score as the allowance. The oracle returns its BigFloat results converted to Float64. It chooses its allowance from a BigFloat estimate of the gradient, scale and trigger, so its branch can differ from the solver's where working-precision rounding differs. The analytic tests (the `1 × 1` and `2 × 2` fixtures and the two inputs above) compare against exact-arithmetic values with the tolerances stated in their assertions; a working-precision diagonal residual there may be zero or may contain rounding error. The cancellation input with a small variance covers one sign, and the endpoint input covers both.
  - Heterogeneous weights: 27 seeded problems per precision, with `p ∈ {5, 8, 10}`, three seeds, and structural weights `1e6` or block-structured adaptive weights capped at `1e4` and `1e6`. Within 20000 iterations, 11 of 27 reached the stop in Float32 on both versions, and 15 (1.10.13) and 13 (1.13.4) of 27 in Float64. No converged solve exceeded the bound (asserted by the test).
  - Diagonal weights, `1 × 1` with `S + C = 1` (Float64 `S = -2^32`, `C = 2^32 + 1` and `S = -1000`, `C = 1001`; Float32 `S = -2^16`, `C = 2^16 + 1` and `S = -4096`, `C = 4097`), start `θ = 2`, `maxiter = 100`, `ftol = 0`, on both Julia versions with identical values: each solve converged in 4 to 6 iterations at `θ` within `1.2e-13` of 1, where the single scale had converged at `θ = 1.5`, `1 + 3.1e-7`, `1.5` and `1.024`. The start score at `θ = 1.5` was `0.33333333333333315` (Float64) and `0.3333333730697632` (Float32), the definition being `1/3`.
  - The `2^51` numerator through `solve!` (`S = -2^51`, `C = 2^51 + 1`, `θ = 1.25`; Float32 with `2^22`): `g_residual` was `0.20000000000000007` in Float64 and `0.20000004768371582` in Float32, and `G + C` was `0.0` in both.
  - Covariance shift: for the four `1 × 1` cases and for a `3 × 3` positive-semidefinite `S` with positive diagonal weights, the direct and the shifted formulations gave the same start score (difference 0 in both precisions), and both solves converged. Their points differed by `3.3e-16` (Float64) and `1.3e-6` (Float32) in the `3 × 3` case.
  - Ordinary problems (`p ∈ {6, 10}`, uniform and moderately varied weights): 6 of 6 converged in each precision on both versions.
  - Allocation of a warmed call of the evaluation, with the factor already in the scratch matrix, on the test's Wishart fixtures (seed 5, with `S₁₂ = 1000` for the uncertain path) in Float64 and Float32 at `p = 12` and `p = 36`: 0 bytes on Julia 1.13.4 in both evaluation paths, and 16 bytes on Julia 1.10.13, where master's evaluation also reported 16 bytes. The test asserts equal bytes at the two dimensions, at most 16. These observations are for those two Julia versions, fixtures and sizes.
  - Against the source of master `fa6e31c6`, the first version of this file (at `560a9075`) reported 233 failures and 151 errors among 665 tests on each version; the errors are calls to helpers that master does not have. In the heterogeneous testset, 53 of the 54 solves returned `converged = true` at a point whose BigFloat score exceeded the bound. On the master route in Float64, master reported `g_residual = 3.3e-10` at `Θ = 1.5I`, where the definition gives `1/3`, and its solve from `2I` returned a point `0.5` from the optimum `I`.

The one Float32 problem that lost convergence under rule F in the ordinary sweeps named there is analysed in the [Float32 section](@ref evidence-kkt-scale-492-float32) below, and the cost of the rule on ordinary problems is in the [performance section](@ref evidence-kkt-scale-492-performance).

**Applies to.** Matrix PGD with `LogDetLoss` and matrix-weighted `NormL1` ([PGD-06](@ref)).

## [evidence-kkt-scale-492-float32 Float32 convergence loss and the line search](@id evidence-kkt-scale-492-float32)

**Statement.** Measured on a cluster (Slurm), Julia 1.13.4, at master `fa6e31c6` with the KKT helper replaced in-process by rule F (the arithmetic of the pass from `G`). The line-search acceptance of [PGD-07](@ref) is tracked in [issue #512](https://github.com/mlakolar/ProximalSolvers.jl/issues/512); rule F is unchanged by it.

- Over the ordinary Float32 sweeps (48 problems: 4 seeds × `p ∈ {5, 10, 20}` × uniform `λ ∈ {0.02, 0.05, 0.1, 0.3}`, Wishart `n = 2p`; and 192 problems: 4 seeds × `p ∈ {5, 10, 20, 30}` × the same `λ` × data scale `∈ {1e-3, 1, 1e3}`), one problem lost convergence under rule F, and it is the "up to 23×" iteration case: `p = 5`, `λ = 0.05`, seed `Xoshiro(1052)`. The 23.4× is the cap of 20000 iterations over the 854 iterations at which the earlier rule stopped, at BigFloat rule-F score `7.93e-6` (`g_tol = 7.63e-6`). `cond(Θ*) = 7.55` and the max-entry relative error `1.04e-6` of that point use the converged Float64 reference.
- Under rule F the computed score fell to `2.25e-5` by iteration 199, rose to about `1e-3` by iteration 999, and stayed between `1.03e-5` and `1.39e-3` over the last 201 iterations, in which the iterates took 50 distinct values; the trace does not establish a periodic cycle up to `maxiter = 20000`. Float64 PGD on the same data converged in 389 iterations. Rounding the Float64 reference to Float32 gives a point that scores `8.24e-8` in BigFloat (computed in Float32: `1.19e-7`), below `g_tol`, so the threshold is attainable in Float32 storage.
- Proposed diagnosis for that problem: [PGD-07](@ref) scales its majorization allowance by the largest of one and the model terms, which include the loss, and its separate descent allowance by the composite objective. Near the optimum `‖D‖²/t` can fall below these allowances, so steps longer than the local curvature allows can be accepted. A probe-only direct-curvature acceptance `⟨G(Z) - G(Θ), D⟩ ≤ ‖D‖²/t` (with an allowance on those two terms) replaced the majorization comparison and kept the composite descent comparison. It converged in 175 iterations (BigFloat score `7.31e-6`). On a Float32 neighbour (`λ = 0.02`) it took 169 iterations instead of no convergence in 20000; on Float64 (`p = 5`, `λ = 0.02`) 350 instead of no convergence in 20000; on Float64 `p = 20`, 742 → 501 (`λ = 0.02`) and 324 → 220 (`λ = 0.1`). These results support the diagnosis; the comparison does not isolate the allowance as the only cause. Changing the acceptance changes [PGD-07](@ref) and is tracked in [#512](https://github.com/mlakolar/ProximalSolvers.jl/issues/512); rule F is unchanged.

**Sources.** [Issue #492](https://github.com/mlakolar/ProximalSolvers.jl/issues/492), [issue #512](https://github.com/mlakolar/ProximalSolvers.jl/issues/512).

## [evidence-kkt-scale-492-performance Performance at 7fe5d8a1 against master 1a9f196b](@id evidence-kkt-scale-492-performance)

Workloads: Wishart `n = 2p`, start `I`, `maxiter = 20000`, `gradtol = 5e-10`, `ftol = 0`. Iteration counts were identical for base and head in every case. A/A medians were within about ±1.5% except one base A/A of 1.017 (Julia 1.13.4, F32 `p = 10`, process 2) and two head A/A medians on Julia 1.10.13 of 1.025 (F64 `p = 30` weighted, process 1) and 1.023 (F64 `p = 100`, process 3). The added scale vector is `p·sizeof(T)` bytes plus array overhead, while the whole-solve allocation totals vary between copies: comparing corresponding copies, the change ranged from −1200 to +1552 bytes on Julia 1.13.4 and from +96 to +896 bytes on Julia 1.10.13. The repository's 5% investigation threshold applies to ordinary Julia 1.13 solves (`CONTRIBUTING.md`); the Julia 1.10.13 values are reported. The paired results measure the whole change (the entrywise scale, the added bound and the scale vector) and do not isolate the cost of the bound. The timed F32 `p = 10`, `λ = 0.2` workload hits `maxiter` under both rules; it is a different problem from the `p = 5`, `λ = 0.05` case of the [Float32 section](@ref evidence-kkt-scale-492-float32), which stopped under the earlier rule and not under rule F, and that case is not timed here.

The large-weight sweep is not timed here. Its medians are in the options table above (82× in Float32 and 3.2× in Float64 for each of A, C, G and F); those aggregates do not establish an increase for each problem that the single scale had stopped.
