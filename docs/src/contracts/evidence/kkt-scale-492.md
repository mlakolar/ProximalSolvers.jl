# Matrix PGD KKT scale: measurements

This page records the measurements behind the entrywise KKT scale of [PGD-08](@ref) (issue
[#492](https://github.com/mlakolar/ProximalSolvers.jl/issues/492)). They are observations at stated revisions, not
solver guarantees.

## [evidence-kkt-scale-492 Study and provenance](@id evidence-kkt-scale-492)

**Statement.** Measured at ProximalSolvers master `fa6e31c6` with ProximalBase `bc9e888d`. The probes ran on a cluster (Slurm) with Julia 1.13.4; the issue's table was also reported on Julia 1.10.13. The option sweeps below replaced the solver's KKT-score helper by each candidate rule in the same process and recomputed every score in BigFloat at the returned point. Before the change, matrix PGD divided every entry's violation by the single scale `max(1, maximum(abs, S), maximum(C))` with `C = T(λ0) Λ`, and counted a completed step as converged when the largest ratio was at most `max(gradtol, 64eps(T))`, subject to the exits of [PGD-08](@ref). The maintainer chose rule F below. The rule was implemented after pull request [#483](https://github.com/mlakolar/ProximalSolvers.jl/pull/483) merged (`17e33ddd`), which permits nonnegative diagonal weights ([PGD-06](@ref)), so the diagonal-weight routes below are reachable through `solve!`.

- **Routes at the single scale.** Start `2I` (or `θ = 2`), `maxiter = 100`, `ftol = 0`. Every row was reproduced at the head `76077c98` of pull request [#483](https://github.com/mlakolar/ProximalSolvers.jl/pull/483), which accepts a positive diagonal weight. The first row was also reproduced on master `fa6e31c6`, whose [PGD-06](@ref) required zero diagonal weights, and so was the Float32 row below it.

  | Case | Data | `gradtol` | Returned point | Optimum | `converged` | Reported score |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Zero diagonal weight, PSD `S`, Float64 | `S = [1 0.1; 0.1 1]`, off-diagonal `C = 1e9`, diagonal `C = 0` | `5e-10` | `Θ₁₁ = 1.5`, one step | `I` | true | `3.3e-10` |
  | Same, Float32 | off-diagonal `C = 1e4` | 0 (floor `64eps`) | `Θ₁₁ = 1.0238` | `I` | true | `2.3e-6` |
  | Positive diagonal weight, indefinite `S`, Float64 | `S = -2^32`, `C = 2^32 + 1` | `5e-10` | `θ = 1.5`, one step | 1 | true | `7.8e-11` |
  | Covariance-shift form | `Sshift = S + C = 1`, `Cshift = 0`, Float64 | `5e-10` | `1 + 9.4e-14` | 1 | true | `9.4e-14` |
  | Positive diagonal, Float32 | `S = -2^16`, `C = 2^16 + 1` | 0 | `θ = 1.5` | 1 | true | `5.1e-6` |
  | Positive diagonal, Float32 | `S = -4096`, `C = 4097` | 0 | `θ ≈ 1.024` | 1 | true | `5.7e-6` |
  | Positive diagonal, Float64 | `S = -1000`, `C = 1001` | `5e-10` | `θ ≈ 1 + 3.1e-7` | 1 | true | `3.1e-10` |

  At `θ = 1.5` in the third row the unnormalized residual is `|S + C - 1/θ| = 1/3` and the scale is about `2^32`. In the first row the off-diagonal penalty `1e9` suppresses an order-one diagonal violation.
- **Wider than the table.** With `S = [1 0.1; 0.1 1]`, Float64 `λ0 = 1e8` stopped at `θ₁₁ = 1.0238`, and Float32 `λ0 = 100` stopped `5.5e-4` from the optimum. Raising `maxiter` to 20000 changed nothing.
- **Seeded problems with large weights.** Positive semidefinite problems with a few structural-zero weights at `1e6`, or adaptive weights `0.05/|θ̃ᵢⱼ|` capped at `1e4`, `1e6` or `1e8`, 48 problems per precision. The single scale returned `converged = true` at a point whose BigFloat per-entry score, rule F below, exceeded `100 g_tol` in 23 of 48 problems (Float32) and 24 of 48 (Float64).
- **Cancellation in the numerator.** With the representable Float64 inputs `S = -2^51`, `C = 2^51 + 1`, `θ = 1.25`, the order `(S - inv(θ)) + C` gave `0.0` and `(S + C) - inv(θ)` gave about `0.2`. This is IEEE arithmetic on a scalar, not a completed solve. A change of the denominator alone cannot recover a numerator that has been rounded to zero.
- **Two routes found while the plan was critiqued, both measured on master.**
  1. Indefinite `S` with an off-diagonal entry that cancels its penalty. Float64 `S = [1 1e9; 1e9 1]`, `C₁₂ = 1e9 - 0.4`: the single scale converged after one step, 26% from the optimum `inv([1 0.4; 0.4 1])` (the original measurement used start `2I`, `maxiter = 200`, `ftol = 0`). Float32 with `S₁₂ = 2¹⁶` also stopped after one step, at a relative max-entry error of 28.9% against 26.0% in Float64. These two errors were re-measured on master `fa6e31c6`, Julia 1.13.4, from `2I` with `maxiter = 100`.
  2. One large variance, no penalty. Float64 `S = diag(1e9, 1.4)` converged at `θ₂₂ = 1` against an optimum of 0.714, and Float32 `S = diag(1e5, 1.5)` at `θ₂₂ = 1` against 0.667. PGD itself makes almost no progress on `θ₂₂` here, because its step is limited by the curvature in `θ₁₁`.
- **Options compared (the historical sweeps, by helper replacement and BigFloat recomputation).** Every option kept the violations and `g_tol = max(gradtol, 64eps(T))`; only the denominator changed. Write `a = diag(S + C)`. "Ordinary" means 4 seeds × `p ∈ {5, 10, 20}` × uniform `λ ∈ {0.02, 0.05, 0.1, 0.3}`, plus `λ` at 0.9 to 2 times `λmax`: 48 problems per regime and precision.

  | Rule | Denominator | Routes of the issue | Route 1 (indefinite) | Route 2 (large variance) | Large weights: converged solves with independent `R > 100 g_tol`, F32 / F64 | Ordinary PSD problems |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | single scale | `max(1, maximum(abs, S), maximum(C))` | certifies far points | certifies | certifies | 23 / 24 of 48 | baseline |
  | A | per entry: diagonal `max(1, \|aᵢ\|)`, off-diagonal `max(1, \|Sᵢⱼ\|, Cᵢⱼ)` | fixed | fixed (stagnated, `converged = false`) | fixed (`converged = false`) | 0 / 0 | F32: 4 of 192 lost, up to 76× more iterations; F64: up to 14.7× |
  | B | `max(1, maximum(abs, S + Diagonal(C)), max off-diagonal C)` | master route not fixed | certifies | certifies | not measured | identical to the single scale |
  | C | `max(1, maximum(abs, S + Diagonal(C)), maximum(abs, inv(Θ)))` | fixed | certifies | certifies | 0 / 0 | identical to the single scale |
  | G | `max(1, maximum(abs, a))` | fixed | fixed (stagnated) | certifies | 0 / 0 | identical to the single scale |
  | F, adopted | per entry `max(1, sqrt(\|aᵢ\| \|aⱼ\|))` | fixed | fixed (stagnated) | fixed (`converged = false`) | 0 / 0 | F32: 1 of 48 lost, up to 23×; F64: up to 1.57× |

  On the 48 large-weight problems per precision, the measured rules A, C, G and F together needed more iterations: the median ratio over those four rules was 82× in Float32 and 3.2× in Float64, and 21 (Float32) and 27 (Float64) of 48 converged within 20000 iterations. These are aggregates over the four rules, not per-rule values. "Identical to the single scale" in the last column refers to the convergence and the iteration counts of the 48 ordinary problems per precision. A sweep with variances spread over `10^±1` to `10^±4` converged for 1 of 48 under every rule, so it cannot tell the rules apart. The Float32 losses under rules A and F appeared with a per-entry score that is stricter than the single scale where `max|S| > 1`. The critique rejected rule C because its `maximum(abs, S + Diagonal(C))` keeps the off-diagonal `|Sᵢⱼ|` in the scale, which lets route 1 through. Its term `maximum(abs, inv(Θ))` can change a decision relative to rule G: for the scalar `S = 1`, `C = 0`, `Θ = 225/256` and `g_tol = 1/8`, rule G scores `31/225 > 1/8` and rule C scores `31/256 < 1/8`. When rule C's fixed part of the scale equals rule G's and the tolerance is `τ` with `0 ≤ τ < 1`, a point that rule C accepts is accepted by rule G at `τ/(1 - τ)`, because `maximum(abs, inv(Θ))` is at most that scale plus the violation (here `τ/(1 - τ) = 1/7 > 31/225`). The off-diagonal term of rule C's fixed part can change that part separately from rule G's.
- **Rounding in the historical arithmetic, where the violation came from `G`.** In the cancellation cases, where `|Sᵢⱼ|` or `Cᵢⱼ` is much larger than the scale and the terms cancel, the computed violation had an absolute rounding error of about `eps(T) max(|Sᵢⱼ|, Cᵢⱼ)`. The threshold could then be out of reach, and a solve could end by stagnation or at the iteration limit with `converged = false`. The computed violation could also be exactly 0 while the true one is not: `fl(S - 1/θ) = -C` holds over a band of `θ`. [PGD-08](@ref) now marks a point uncertain when its test flags any entry, and then evaluates all entries from the inputs, subject to the fallback to `G` for entries whose terms are not finite.
- **Two inputs found in the review of the first implementation** (3 × 3, `Θ` a fixed point or a start, zero weights except `C₁₂ = C₂₁ = B`; the exact score is `R = 1/4`, equal to the 2048-bit BigFloat value in every case). The first implementation is commit `560a9075`; the current code is `7fe5d8a1`, and the observations were made on Julia 1.13.4 and 1.10.13 with identical values.
  1. A small variance: `D = diag(2ᵃ, 2⁻ᵇ, 1)`, `Θ = D [1 0 1; 0 1 -1; 1 -1 3] D`, `S = [2^(-2b-1) 1/4 0; 1/4 2^(2b+1) 2ᵇ; 0 2ᵇ 1]`, `B = 2^(b-a)`, `(a, b) = (30, 58)` in Float32 and `(55, 110)` in Float64. `W₁₂ = -B` exactly, and the unit floor of the small diagonal lets `|W₁₂|` exceed the scale of entry `(1, 2)`. The first implementation converged after one step, reporting `9.31e-10` (Float32) and `2.78e-17` (Float64). At `7fe5d8a1` the start score is `0.25` (Float32) and `0.24999999999999994` (Float64, one ulp below `1/4`), and the solve stagnates after one step with `converged = false`.
  2. An overflowing unused endpoint: `B = 0.75 floatmax(T)`, `Θ = [2 0 1; 0 2 -σ; 1 -σ 2]` with `σ = ±1`, and `S` equal to `Θ⁻¹` except `S₁₂ = σB`. The first implementation converged after one step, reporting `5.96e-8` (Float32) and `4.44e-16` (Float64). At `7fe5d8a1` the start score is `0.2499999701976776` (Float32) and `0.2499999999999999` (Float64), against the exact `1/4`; the Float32 solves stagnate after one step and the Float64 solves reach `maxiter = 50`, all with `converged = false`.
- **What `test/logdet_kkt_scale.jl` measured** (revision `7fe5d8a1` for the observations below, Julia 1.10.13 and 1.13.4). The BigFloat comparisons use the stored data at the fixed point or the returned point: 256 bits, and 2048 bits for the two inputs above, the first for its condition number and the second for the exponent gap between `B` and `1/4`, although its iterate is well conditioned. Those comparisons check the reported score and the stop rule against the definition of [PGD-08](@ref), with an estimate of the rounding of the computed score as the allowance; the oracle rounds its values to Float64 and selects its allowance from a BigFloat estimate of the gradient, which can differ from the working-precision trigger near its boundary. The analytic tests (the `1 × 1` and `2 × 2` fixtures and the two inputs above) also assert exact values, and the cancellation input with a small variance covers one sign while the endpoint input covers both.
  - Heterogeneous weights: 27 seeded problems per precision, with `p ∈ {5, 8, 10}`, three seeds, and structural weights `1e6` or block-structured adaptive weights capped at `1e4` and `1e6`. Within 20000 iterations, 11 of 27 reached the stop in Float32 on both versions, and 15 (1.10.13) and 13 (1.13.4) of 27 in Float64. None stagnated and no converged solve exceeded the bound. The largest difference between `g_residual` and the BigFloat score was 0.08 of its allowance.
  - Diagonal weights, `1 × 1` with `S + C = 1` (Float64 `S = -2^32`, `C = 2^32 + 1` and `S = -1000`, `C = 1001`; Float32 `S = -2^16`, `C = 2^16 + 1` and `S = -4096`, `C = 4097`), start `θ = 2`, `maxiter = 100`, `ftol = 0`, on both Julia versions with identical values: each solve converged in 4 to 6 iterations at `θ` within `1.2e-13` of 1, where the single scale had converged at `θ = 1.5`, `1 + 3.1e-7`, `1.5` and `1.024`. The start score at `θ = 1.5` was `0.33333333333333315` (Float64) and `0.3333333730697632` (Float32), the definition being `1/3`.
  - The `2^51` numerator through `solve!` (`S = -2^51`, `C = 2^51 + 1`, `θ = 1.25`; Float32 with `2^22`): `g_residual` was `0.20000000000000007` in Float64 and `0.20000004768371582` in Float32, and `G + C` was `0.0` in both.
  - Covariance shift: for the four `1 × 1` cases and for a `3 × 3` positive-semidefinite `S` with positive diagonal weights, the direct and the shifted formulations gave the same start score (difference 0 in both precisions), and both solves converged. Their points differed by `3.3e-16` (Float64) and `1.3e-6` (Float32) in the `3 × 3` case.
  - Ordinary problems (`p ∈ {6, 10}`, uniform and moderately varied weights): 6 of 6 converged in each precision on both versions.
  - Allocation of a warmed call of the evaluation, with the factor already in the scratch matrix, for `p = 12` and `p = 36`: 0 bytes on Julia 1.13.4 in both evaluation paths, and 16 bytes on Julia 1.10.13, where master's evaluation also reported 16 bytes.
  - Against the source of master `fa6e31c6`, the first version of this file (at `560a9075`) reported 233 failures and 151 errors among 665 tests on each version; the errors are calls to helpers that master does not have. In the heterogeneous testset, 53 of the 54 solves returned `converged = true` at a point whose BigFloat score exceeded the bound. On the master route in Float64, master reported `g_residual = 3.3e-10` at `Θ = 1.5I`, where the definition gives `1/3`, and its solve from `2I` returned a point `0.5` from the optimum `I`.

The one Float32 problem that lost convergence under rule F is analysed in the [Float32 section](@ref evidence-kkt-scale-492-float32) below, and the cost of the rule on ordinary problems is in the [performance section](@ref evidence-kkt-scale-492-performance).

**Applies to.** Matrix PGD with `LogDetLoss` and matrix-weighted `NormL1` ([PGD-06](@ref)).

**Code.** `src/solvers/logdet_proximal_gradient.jl`: `_logdet_pgd_preflight`, `_logdet_pgd_violation`, `_logdet_pgd_gform`, `_logdet_pgd_wform`, `_logdet_pgd_kkt!`.

**Tests.** `test/logdet_kkt_scale.jl` and the independent score in `test/pgd_graphical_lasso.jl`.

## [evidence-kkt-scale-492-float32 Float32 convergence loss and the line search](@id evidence-kkt-scale-492-float32)

**Statement.** Measured on a cluster (Slurm), Julia 1.13.4, at master `fa6e31c6` with the KKT helper replaced in-process by rule F (the arithmetic of the pass from `G`). The line-search acceptance of [PGD-07](@ref) is tracked in [issue #512](https://github.com/mlakolar/ProximalSolvers.jl/issues/512); rule F is unchanged by it.

- Over the ordinary Float32 sweeps (48 problems: 4 seeds × `p ∈ {5, 10, 20}` × uniform `λ ∈ {0.02, 0.05, 0.1, 0.3}`, Wishart `n = 2p`; and 192 problems: 4 seeds × `p ∈ {5, 10, 20, 30}` × the same `λ` × data scale `∈ {1e-3, 1, 1e3}`), one problem lost convergence under rule F, and it is the "up to 23×" iteration case: `p = 5`, `λ = 0.05`, seed `Xoshiro(1052)`, `cond(Θ*) = 7.55`. The earlier rule stopped at iteration 854 at BigFloat rule-F score `7.93e-6` (`g_tol = 7.63e-6`), with a max-entry relative error of `1.04e-6`.
- Under rule F the computed score fell to `2.25e-5` by iteration 199, rose to about `1e-3` by iteration 999, and stayed between `1.03e-5` and `1.39e-3` over the last 201 iterations, cycling among 50 distinct iterates until `maxiter = 20000`. Float64 PGD on the same data converged in 389 iterations. The Float32-rounded optimum scores `8.24e-8` in BigFloat (computed in Float32: `1.19e-7`), so the threshold is attainable in Float32 storage.
- Diagnosis for that problem: the [PGD-07](@ref) acceptance allowances scale with `|f|`, so near the optimum `‖D‖²/t` falls below them and steps longer than the local curvature allows are accepted. A probe-only direct-curvature acceptance `⟨G(Z) - G(Θ), D⟩ ≤ ‖D‖²/t` (with an allowance on those two terms) converged in 175 iterations (BigFloat score `7.31e-6`). On a Float32 neighbour (`λ = 0.02`) it took 169 iterations instead of no convergence in 20000; on Float64 (`p = 5`, `λ = 0.02`) 350 instead of no convergence in 20000; on Float64 `p = 20`, 742 → 501 (`λ = 0.02`) and 324 → 220 (`λ = 0.1`). Changing the acceptance changes [PGD-07](@ref) and is tracked in [#512](https://github.com/mlakolar/ProximalSolvers.jl/issues/512); rule F is unchanged.

**Sources.** [Issue #492](https://github.com/mlakolar/ProximalSolvers.jl/issues/492), [issue #512](https://github.com/mlakolar/ProximalSolvers.jl/issues/512).

## [evidence-kkt-scale-492-performance Performance at 7fe5d8a1 against master 1a9f196b](@id evidence-kkt-scale-492-performance)

**Statement.** In-process paired timing: base and head compiled from source in one process, two copies of each (A/A controls), random order per round, 5 batches per round of about 50 ms, minimum per side; 3 processes × 7 rounds; one BLAS thread; shared cluster (Slurm) nodes, with load averages about 68 (Julia 1.13.4) and 20 (Julia 1.10.13). Workloads: Wishart `n = 2p`, start `I`, `maxiter = 20000`, `gradtol = 5e-10`, `ftol = 0`. Iteration counts were identical for base and head in every case.
