Architecture

ProximalSolvers composes solver settings, model caches and iterate storage through Julia fields and specializes public operations with multiple dispatch. Concrete structs do not inherit from one another. The diagrams below describe source relationships; grouped boxes abbreviate several concrete types rather than introducing additional Julia types.

The DOT sources are authoritative. Their SVG renderings are included here. An empty triangular arrow means subtyping, a diamond means composition through a field, a dashed blue arrow means dispatch or method extension, and a solid blue arrow means a runtime call. Dashed red arrows indicate shared storage or a private dependency. Each diagram labels its edges where their meaning could be ambiguous. Select a diagram to open the full-size SVG.

Solver and model types

ProximalSolver is the abstract supertype for the solver settings. LLA is a separate driver holding a CDSolver; it is not a subtype of ProximalSolver. CDModel is the public abstract type for coordinate models. Its built-in concrete implementations are internal.

The declarations and include order are in src/ProximalSolvers.jl, src/types.jl and src/cd/models.jl. A solver's immutable settings can contain mutable state owned elsewhere: for example, CDSolver keeps a reference to its random-number generator.

Dispatch and accepted iterates

The entry point dispatches on the method, loss, penalty and iterate types. The gradient engine in src/solve.jl constructs or resets state, takes an accepted step, assesses the selected stopping rule, and returns diagnostics. Rejected gradient trials reuse the iterate buffer. A failed line search raises an error and solve! restores the last accepted point. A failed numerical step is not convergence.

ADMM and Condat–Vũ have their own splitting and residual equations. Proximal Newton specializes on a GLM, a log-determinant loss, or a generic quadratic oracle. The generic route uses the original gradient for stationarity; GLM objective stagnation also requires a check of that gradient. QUIC stops on the graphical-lasso certificate. SLOPE drivers check the full proximal equation after restricted fits. See Calling the solvers for supported storage, options and result types, and the method pages for equations and assumptions.

An issmooth or Hessian trait selects a method; it does not prove Lipschitz continuity, positive semidefiniteness or oracle accuracy. Those mathematical conditions remain part of a custom function's contract. Finite precision, restarts and approximate inner solves do not by themselves inherit a cited algorithm's convergence rate.

Coordinate-model operations

The public extension surface is CDModel with initialize! and either coordinate_step! or the pair coordinate_derivatives and update_cache!. Optional scales control the change rule; loss_value provides reporting and Anderson acceptance; gradient! supports penalty levels and screening. A coordinate descent model of your own gives the signatures.

The scheduling kernels in src/cd/solver.jl and block kernels in src/cd/groups.jl are internal. Scalar updates use an exact quadratic curvature or a majorizing bound. Group updates use a bound on the full block Hessian, not its diagonal. For symmetric storage an off-diagonal coordinate moves both entries and counts both penalty terms. A single helper prepares the penalty for coordinate descent, QUIC, generic symmetric Newton and the gradient solvers on symmetric storage: it averages parameters that enter linearly and rejects other asymmetric per-entry parameters, as described in Coordinates of the iterates.

A coordinate with zero curvature targets min G(x - xₖ) + gₖ(x). Supported convex norm and box cases use the minimizer of the penalty nearest zero (for a norm, while |G| ≤ λ) or the minimizing bound of a box. Proximal fixed points certify convex subproblems only. A nonzero slope with a nonconvex penalty is rejected, as are unsupported flat subproblems. An unbounded direction raises an ArgumentError, and kkt_residual reports Inf there. The square-root losses check a relative duality gap when dual scaling is available; unpenalized coordinates can require a fallback to the change rule. See Losses for coordinate descent.

Ownership and cache validity

SolverWorkspace owns reusable buffers and shares the caller's x. Results share the minimizer and supplied splitting dual arrays. Saved path solutions and extended-trace arrays are copies. Copy a result array before reusing its workspace if the old fit must remain available.

Loss data, scales, group partitions, dimensions and solver configuration must stay fixed during workspace reuse. A new penalty must have the original type and partition. reinit! changes the starting coefficients and clears ADMM's dual warm start. solve!(workspace; warm_start = true) is the explicit ADMM route for retaining its dual and penalty parameter. These rules appear in src/workspaces.jl.

Models can reference input matrices, responses and weights. initialize! refreshes the iterate cache; it is not a general invalidation API for changed data. In particular, centering constants and factorization caches can depend on same-shape values. Construct a new model or workspace after changing them. A RefitCache belongs to one unchanged loss. Separate workspaces, models, mutable function buffers and random generators are needed for concurrent solves. An immutable outer struct does not make its buffers safe to share between tasks.

Paths and estimator drivers

solve_path and continuation change penalty levels while reusing the iterate and model. Strong screening is a prediction; full checks reactivate excluded violations. Gap-safe screening instead requires a valid dual point and nonnegative gap. Restricted gaps do not replace full-problem checks. The paths save independent snapshots and retain unsuccessful levels.

LLA and reweight! use repeated weighted fits. Their outer stopping flags describe weight changes, while inner convergence is reported separately. Nonconvex stages do not certify a global optimum. refit! zeros coordinates outside the requested support and solves on that support, using pivoted QR or an eligible cached factor. The relevant sources are src/cd/path.jl, src/cd/lla.jl and src/cd/drivers.jl.

Package boundaries

ProximalBase defines function oracles and iterate storage. ProximalSolvers owns solver scheduling, coordinate caches and results. HighDimensionalModels adds statistical preprocessing, estimators, paths and cross-validation; it also extends the public coordinate-model operations. CovarianceSelection calls ADMM, gradient and refit operations. DiffPrecTest uses coordinate solves and paths for structured inverse-Hessian problems.

Built-in model types and helpers such as SqrtQuadModel, _saturated and _hasconjugate are internal and may change. The public model protocol supplies the supported route for extensions. Callbacks and traces follow each solver's documented behavior.
