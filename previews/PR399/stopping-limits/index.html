Limits of the stopping tests

This page collects what converged = true says for scalar CD, group CD, PGD and APG, and the measured limits of their stopping tests. The numbers come from the Stage 0 audit of issue #352, run at revision b8a2c04 on Julia 1.13.4; Julia 1.10.13 was run as well and gave close but not identical counts. The audit used 11 small designs (20 to 60 rows, 8 to 60 columns) and evaluated each solve in 600-bit arithmetic on the rounded inputs the solver received. At that revision solve! refused a weighted NormL1, so the NormL1 problems of the audit were unweighted, and the counts on this page do not cover a weighted NormL1. These are high-precision evaluations, not proofs, and the counts are for those designs: they are not rates to expect on other problems. The subsections from The cached residual on report a separate, later study (issue #378, revision 8cc0222) with its own designs and starts; its counts are a different population and are not to be combined with those of the audit.

The criterion and a certificate

Meeting the implemented stopping criterion is not the same as holding an independently certified optimum. The solvers stop on three kinds of evidence:

In the CD rows, h_k is the change of coordinate (or group) k in the pass and s the loss scale. In the first two rows the changes are computed from the solver's cached residual, and the flag does not say that this cache is close to y − Xx at the returned point (see The cached residual).

Of the problems audited, only the square-root lasso carries a certificate, and that certificate has the assumptions stated in The certificate and Tightening and the uncertified stop. For the other solvers converged = true reports that the criterion of its row was met. Check the returned point with the independent KKT residual when the result matters.

What the change test of coordinate descent implies

Assume exact arithmetic, a full pass without extrapolation, exact minimization at each scalar visit, and relative scaling (relative = true, y ≠ 0), so that the threshold is τ = tol √s ‖y‖ with s the loss scale. Let X_k be column k of X, p the number of columns, h_j the change of coordinate j in the stopping pass, and G_k the minimal-norm KKT residual of coordinate k at the end of the pass. Coordinate k is at its minimizer when it is visited, and only the updates of the coordinates visited after it move its gradient, so

|G_k| ≤ s ‖X_k‖ Σ_{j visited after k} ‖X_j‖ |h_j| ≤ (p − 1) · tol · s ‖X_k‖ ‖y‖     (lasso and elastic net)
‖G_k‖ ≤ K · tol · s √ℓ_k ‖y‖                                                          (group lasso, K groups)

with ℓ_k the curvature bound of group k. The elastic net adds no cross term. The first inequality holds for any visiting order. Momentum does not enter, because the stopping pass has no extrapolation. For the group lasso the visit is a majorization step with 0 ⪯ X_kᵀX_k ⪯ ℓ_k I, not a block minimization: the residual of group k after its own step is at most √(s ℓ_k) τ, and each group visited after it adds at most the same, which gives the factor K.

With the absolute threshold (relative = false, or y = 0) the scale ‖y‖ drops out:

|G_k| ≤ s · tol · ‖X_k‖ Σ_{j visited after k} ‖X_j‖
‖G_k‖ ≤ s · tol · (ℓ_k + √ℓ_k Σ_{j visited after k} √ℓ_j)                            (group lasso)

The scalar bounds are not strict: both sides are 0 for p = 1 and for a flat column. What remains unguaranteed, and why the flag is not a KKT certificate:

  • the factor p − 1 or K;
  • the unit s ‖X_k‖ ‖y‖ in place of λmax (a factor 5.5 on the equicorrelated design with correlation 0.95 of the audit, and unbounded in the cancellation example below);
  • rounding (see the warm start below);
  • range (see the underflow example below).

Measured at the default threshold, with a positive penalty. The benchmark target of the repository, a relative suboptimality of 1e-8 in Float64 and 1e-5 in Float32, is a target of the repository's benchmark. It is not part of the documented contract of converged = true.

  • Scalar CD (lasso and elastic net): all 16,218 converged solves that have a tight reference were within the benchmark target, and the worst was 1.3e-9. A further 478 converged solves on the rank-deficient design have no tight reference; their suboptimality is between 0 and 1.4e-6, which does not decide the comparison with 1e-8.
  • Group CD: 48 of 3,529 converged solves were above the benchmark target. With relative = true these are 12 solves, in Float64 only, with the largest excess between 2.8e-7 and 3.7e-7 (a lower and an upper bound; two underdetermined designs at the lowest penalty level). With relative = false they are 36 solves, with the largest excess between 4.6e-5 and 4.7e-5, on the design whose column norms span six decades; its KKT residual was 3.4e-4 λmax in Float64 and 5.3e-4 λmax in Float32.

These group results are inside the contract of converged = true: the flag reports the change test, and the bounds above are not a bound on the suboptimality. See also The stopping statistic of the group lasso.

Products formed in working precision

CD, PGD and APG form the product of X with a residual in the working type, before the loss scale is applied: CD forms rᵀX_k for the coordinate step, and PGD and APG form Xᵀd for the gradient. When that product underflows or cancels, the stop of each of the three can be met at a point that is not a minimizer. The solvers do not recover such a product (see the sentence on an Xᵀd that underflows or overflows before the scaling in Working precision: Float32 and Float64).

Underflow. One column with entries 2^-500, y entries 2^-700, two rows, loss scale 2^300, and λ = α Xᵀy / 8 for the exact Xᵀy:

using ProximalBase, ProximalSolvers

n = 2
X = fill(2.0^-500, n, 1)
y = fill(2.0^-700, n)
α = 2.0^300                      # loss scale
λ = 2.0^-902                     # α Xᵀy / 8, with the exact Xᵀy = 2 * 2^-1200
sum(y .* vec(X))                 # the computed Xᵀy is 0.0

loss = α * LeastSquaresLoss(y, X)
for solver in (CDSolver(order = :cyclic, momentum = false),
               ProximalGradientDescent(), AccProxGradDescent())
  x = zeros(1)
  r = solve!(x, loss, NormL1(λ), solver)
  println(nameof(typeof(solver)), ": x = ", x[1], ", converged = ", r.converged)
end

All the numbers are normal Float64 numbers, and the exact minimizer is 5.4e-61. CD, PGD and APG return x = 0 with converged = true; CDSolver() with its default options does as well. The exact KKT residual, evaluated in 600-bit arithmetic, is 0.875 of max(λ, λmax), and the objective is 4.27 times the minimum ((F − F*)/F* = 3.27). PGD and APG pass the stationarity check of a stop because it reads the same rounded gradient product and the same rounded λmax product: the computed gradient is 0, λmax is computed as 0 for the same reason, so S = λ, and the computed residual 0 is below the bound.

Cancellation. One column X = [1, d, −1], y = [1, 1, 1], d = eps(T)/2 and λ = d/8:

using ProximalBase, ProximalSolvers

for T in (Float64, Float32)
  d = eps(T) / 2
  X = reshape(T[1, d, -1], 3, 1)
  y = ones(T, 3)
  λ = d / 8
  for solver in (CDSolver(order = :cyclic, momentum = false),
                 ProximalGradientDescent(), AccProxGradDescent())
    x = zeros(T, 1)
    r = solve!(x, LeastSquaresLoss(y, X), NormL1(λ), solver)
    println(T, " ", nameof(typeof(solver)), ": x = ", x[1], ", converged = ", r.converged)
  end
end

The exact Xᵀy is d; it is computed as 0. All three solvers return x = 0 with converged = true, in Float64 and in Float32. The exact KKT residual is 0.875 λmax, and the relative objective error is negligible (1.6e-33 in Float64, 4.5e-16 in Float32). This is the underflow example without the range: the exact value of Xᵀy is at the rounding level of ‖X_k‖ ‖y‖, and the residual is large only relative to a λmax that is itself at that level. A check that reads the same working-precision products computes 0 there as well.

In CD the same product enters the coordinate step. In both examples the full pass changes nothing, and a pass with no change meets the change test.

What you can do: keep the loss scale and the data in a range where Xᵀr is representable, and evaluate the KKT residual independently, in higher precision, for results that matter.

A warm start far above the solution scale (coordinate descent)

The start of solve! can place coefficients far above the scale of the solution. With X = ones(1, 2), y = [1/64], λ = 1/1024 and the start [2.0^49, -2.0^49] (Float32: 2f0^20):

using ProximalBase, ProximalSolvers

for (T, M) in ((Float64, 2.0^49), (Float32, 2f0^20))
  X = ones(T, 1, 2)
  y = T[1 / 64]
  λ = T(1 / 1024)
  start = T[M, -M]
  for solver in (CDSolver(order = :cyclic, momentum = false),
                 ProximalGradientDescent(), AccProxGradDescent())
    x = copy(start)
    r = solve!(x, LeastSquaresLoss(y, X), NormL1(λ), solver)
    println(T, " ", nameof(typeof(solver)), ": x == start is ", x == start,
            ", converged = ", r.converged)
  end
end

CD returns the start with converged = true after one pass. The exact KKT residual is 1.06 of max(λ, λmax), and (F − F*)/F* is 7.4e16 (1.4e8 in Float32). PGD and APG also return the start, at maxiter with converged = false. The mechanism for this example: the residual is initialized as y − Σ X_k x_k in working precision, which loses y against the coefficients M; each coordinate step is then smaller than half a unit in the last place of the coefficient, so the new value equals the old one; and a full pass with no change satisfies 0 < tol.

Related degradation appears in the sweep. With λ = 0 on the rank-deficient design, the exact least-squares minimizer has large cancelling coefficients, and the warm starts taken from it give 80 converged solves per precision at the default threshold, with an exact KKT residual up to 0.063 λmax in Float64 and 0.16 λmax in Float32; the cold starts on the same problems end at 1.7e-8 λmax or below in Float64 and 5.7e-8 λmax or below in Float32. The stopping pass changed nothing in 24 of the Float32 rows and in none of the Float64 rows; the others stopped on a small nonzero change. The empty-pass mechanism is therefore established for the example above, not for all of these rows.

The cached residual

The example is one form of a wider limit. For a least-squares loss, CD keeps the residual r = y − Xx in a cache. solve! computes the cache from the start in working precision, and each coordinate or group step then subtracts its own change from it. The steps read the cache, and the change test reads the steps. In a solve without momentum, which includes every group-lasso solve, the cache is not computed again from the coefficients. With momentum (the default for a vector iterate with the random order) it is computed again before a full pass when an extrapolation has moved it since it was last computed; the first pass of a solve is a full pass, and no such computation precedes it.

The intermediate sums of y − Σ X_k x_k can be much larger than the residual itself, and their rounding can then lose y; this happens where column images X_k x_k of the start are much larger than y. In exact arithmetic a step changes the cache and y − Xx by the same amount, so an error e of the cache is not removed by the steps that follow: until the cache is computed again, the passes are those of the problem with the response y + e, and the change test can be met at a point that is not near a minimizer of the problem that was posed. For CDSolver with a least-squares loss, converged = true reports the change test as computed from the cached residual. It is not a certificate of stationarity, and it does not say that the cache is close to y − Xx at the returned point.

What the study of issue #378 measured

Issue #378 measured how far this limit reaches: revision 8cc0222, Julia 1.13.4 and 1.10.13, two CPU kinds, and 1,667,350 solves per run on 49 designs (ten of the designs of the audit and 39 generated ones, up to 5000×20 and 50×200) with lasso, elastic-net, ridge and group-lasso penalties, in Float32 and Float64. Each returned point was evaluated in 600-bit arithmetic. Most of the starts were built to provoke the failure: x_ref + 2^j z for a reference solution x_ref, up to five directions z per design, scaled so that the largest column image of z has the norm of y, and j up to 28 in Float32 and 60 in Float64. The counts are for those designs and starts; they are not rates to expect on other problems. They are from one run under Julia 1.13.4. The other three runs give close counts: 264,651 to 264,960 false stops where this run has 264,795, and 443 to 464 where it has 434.

A false stop below is a solve with converged = true whose returned point is above the benchmark target (a relative suboptimality of 1e-8 in Float64 and 1e-5 in Float32) and at which an exact first visit would change a coefficient or group by more than p times the threshold, in the scaled unit of the change test, with p the number of columns. In exact arithmetic a converged full pass does not leave that much: a visit changes its coordinate by at most the KKT residual divided by the curvature, so the bounds of What the change test of coordinate descent implies limit that change to p − 1 times the threshold for the lasso and the elastic net, and to the number of groups times the threshold for the group lasso.

  • The cache was stale at each false stop. With relative = true the run has 264,795 false stops, 163,901 with a vector penalty and 100,894 with the group lasso. At each of them the cache differed from y − Xx at the returned point by at least tol ‖y‖ in the Euclidean norm, which is the threshold divided by √s. Converged solves above the target occur with relative = false as well (3,606 Float32 solves with a vector penalty from the starts x_ref + 2^j z ended above the target after a pass that changed nothing); the study did not classify them further.

  • A pass that changed nothing is not the marker. 30,258 of the false stops (11%) ended, as the example does, with a full pass in which no coefficient or group changed. In the others the last pass moved coefficients or groups, each by a scaled change below the threshold. One hand-built case of the study: from the start 2^50 e_1 on the Gaussian design of the audit, a Float64 solve with cyclic order and no momentum converged in 16 passes at a point whose exact KKT residual is 5.9e-3 of max(λ, λmax); with the default options the same start ended at 2.3e-10.

No change to the solver

The study built four changes to the solver and compared them with requirements fixed before the measurements: stop without converged = true on the example above and on its λ = 0 variant; with constants fixed on the designs of the audit, lose none of the solves that must keep converging, there and on the generated designs (ordinary starts, a positive penalty, converged at a point that is not above the target); and stay within the repository's limit of 5% on the time of an ordinary solve. None of the four met them.

  • A statistic of the visits that changed nothing caught 6% of the false stops, and not the λ = 0 variant of the example.
  • Two rules triggered by a statistic of the rounding accumulated along the solve, one that compares the cache with a recomputed residual and reports, and one that recomputes the residual and continues, left 22 and 0 of the 114,053 false stops (vector penalties) of their comparison with the unchanged solver. They lost 64 and 17 Float32 solves that must keep converging, and 23 and 51 of 260 timing cases, small warm-started solves among them, were more than 5% slower.
  • A rule that recomputes the residual when a first-order estimate of the rounding error of the last computation of the cache is large, and stops without converged = true when that estimate is large at the stop, lost none of those solves and left 18 of the 114,053. It was designed after the results were known, and its constant has no validation on designs that were held out. It reports converged = false at some exact minimizers with heavy cancellation, its group form is looser than its vector form and not range-safe, and one of its timing cases was above 5% in both of two runs. Its estimate is not a bound on the error of the cache, so passing its tests would not certify the residual either.

The stopping rule, its defaults and the work of a solve are therefore unchanged, and the limit of this section remains; issue #392 tracks it. The measurements are in the report; the scripts, tables and prototype patches are on the branch archive/issue-378-evidence.

What you can do:

  • Start from zero, or from a point at the scale of the solution such as the previous point of a λ path.
  • Do not warm-start from a point whose column images are far larger than the response. An unpenalized fit whose column images are large and cancel, as on the rank-deficient design of the study, is such a point; the study gives no size below which a start is safe.
  • Evaluate the KKT residual independently at the returned point. It forms y − Xx from the returned coefficients, so it does not inherit the error of the cache; where the returned coefficients are still far above the scale of the solution, as in the example, evaluate it in higher precision.
  • Calling solve! again from the returned point also computes the cache from the returned coefficients. The study did not measure this, and it is not guaranteed to improve the result: it still fails on the example above, which returns its start.

Float32

  • Coordinate descent with relative = false. At the default tol = 1e-7 with relative = false, 130 of 5,700 Float32 scalar CD solves and 40 of 1,830 Float32 square-root solves ran to maxpasses = 100000 in the sweep; the scalar ones that have a tight reference ended within 5.1e-8 of the minimum. With the default relative = true, no scalar solve of the sweep reached maxpasses in Float32. The CD bullet under What does converged mean? says that Float32 rounding of the coefficients can keep the change above the threshold. Keep relative = true, or choose a tol well above eps(Float32) times the size of the coefficients.

  • PGD and APG with the default options. In the sweep, 70 of 570 Float32 PGD solves and 67 of 570 Float32 APG solves ended on the objective-increase exit, with converged = false, at points within the 1e-5 benchmark target; the KKT residual there was a median of 4 times the bound. The exit is described in PGD stops when the computed objective increases, which says that a Float32 solve can end on it. On the five benchmark designs with the benchmark options (10 draws each, Julia 1.10.13 and 1.13.3, before and after #319), Float32 PGD ended above the 1e-5 target in 36 to 39 of 50 solves on 1.10.13 and in 37 of 50 on 1.13.3, and 22 to 25 and 15 to 16 of those solves had converged = true; Float32 APG missed 40 to 41 of 50, with 26 to 29 converged = true. For the Float32 PGD solves with converged = true that missed the target, the gap was 2.3 to 7.0 times the independent KKT residual, which was below the bound 64 eps(Float32) ≈ 7.63e-6. The bound is on a KKT residual and the target is on a duality gap, so a solve can meet the former and miss the latter. The conditions and counts are in the benchmark harness notes and in issue #320.

  • Square-root lasso. Certified solves at the default tol with relative = true, on the seven designs of the audit that are not exact or near-exact fits (210 solves per cell). The penalty level is a fraction of the level at which the solution is 0. The last column is an upper bound of the suboptimality of the worst uncertified Float32 point:

ftol = 0 at a fixed point

Under ftol = 0 an iteration whose accepted iterate equals the previous one proposes no stop (the bullet "An exact stall" under Strict accuracy), so the stationarity check, which runs where a test proposes a stop, is not asked there. A solve that reaches its floating-point fixed point can then run to maxiter, or end with stagnated = true, with converged = false, at a point whose exact KKT residual is below the bound. This behaviour is kept.

In the sweep, with the documented strict configuration (ftol = 0, allow_f_increases = true, xtol = 1e-300, gradtol = 1e-11), Float32 PGD reported converged = true in 12 of 570 solves, and 538 did not converge although the exact residual was below the bound (338 at maxiter = 20000, 200 with stagnated = true). The table counts solves that did not converge although the exact normalized KKT residual is below max(gradtol, 64 eps). It uses the problems with a positive scale S (the 12 solves per cell with y = 0 and λ = 0 are excluded: the check refuses a zero scale by design), 570 solves per cell, and compares the exact residual with the bound; it is not a replay of the computed check. What you can do: with ftol = 0, a result with converged = false at maxiter, or with stagnated = true, can be at a point whose KKT residual is below the bound; evaluate it independently. With the default ftol > 0 an unchanged iterate proposes a stop, and the stationarity check decides.
