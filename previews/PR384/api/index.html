API reference

This is the complete callable reference, including some historical constructor keywords that intentionally reject unsupported features. For practical options, see Choosing a solver; for deferred keyword combinations, see Supported problems.

ProximalGradientDescent() provides its supported backtracking line search by default. The qualified type ProximalSolvers.LineSearches.BackTracking appears below for API completeness. Ordinary users do not need to import or instantiate it separately.

ProximalSolver

Abstract supertype of CDSolver, ProximalGradientDescent and AccProxGradDescent, passed to solve!.

ProximalSolvers.CDSolver — Type
CDSolver(; maxpasses=100_000, tol=1e-7, order=:random, relative=true,
           momentum=nothing, rng=Random.default_rng(), anderson=0,
           gaptol=0, gapsafe=false)

Coordinate descent for the least-squares lasso and elastic net, and, with ProximalBase's SqrtLeastSquaresLoss, the square-root lasso (described below). Call solve!(beta, f, g, CDSolver()) with matching Float32 or Float64 design/response data and coefficients. The coefficient storage can be a dense Vector or a one-dimensional ProximalBase SparseIterate. With the least-squares loss, the penalty is NormL1(lambda), NormL1(lambda, w) or an unweighted ElasticNet(lambda1, lambda2) (the square-root lasso, below, takes an unweighted NormL1 with a positive level). The weights w of a NormL1 are a Vector{T} (T the element type of the data) with finite nonnegative entries (see solve!): solve! checks the coefficient T(lambda) w[j] of entry j, the product rounded in T, to be zero or a normal number of T (with T(lambda) zero or normal as well), and a zero weight leaves it unpenalized. The coordinate step in the normal range is ProximalBase's prox_block, which forms its threshold from lambda and w[j] in its working type; see Supported problems for how that threshold and the checked coefficient can differ. For an ElasticNet the coordinate step of a visit in the normal range is ProximalBase's prox_block of the penalty. Let s be the loss scale and a the squared norm of the column, finite and positive. At a visit where s * a or its reciprocal is below floatmin of the type that combines s with the working type, the step with lambda2 = 0 is the lasso's range-safe form (the threshold formed without the product or its reciprocal), and with lambda2 > 0 the visit is refused with an ArgumentError that names the coordinate; for a NormL1 with weights the step there is the lasso's range-safe form at the coefficient T(lambda) w[k] of the coordinate. The refusal happens during the pass: the iterate passed to solve! is unchanged, and a borrowed RNG may have advanced. A visit to a flat column (all entries 0) sets its coordinate to 0, at the levels that solve! accepts, for each of these penalties (also where a weight is zero); a visit to a column that is not flat and whose a underflows to 0 is refused as for the lasso. With maxpasses = 0 no coordinate is visited. For a group lasso it can be a ProximalBase BlockIterate, as described below.

CDSolver() uses random coordinate order and the algorithm's default momentum behavior, a reasonable exploratory choice. For deterministic tutorial examples use CDSolver(order=:cyclic, momentum=false); for repeatable random order supply rng=Xoshiro(seed) from Random. The borrowed RNG advances when used. Cyclic order does not support momentum=true.

tol (default 1e-7) measures coefficient change, not independent optimality (the square-root lasso adds a certificate, below). With relative=true, the stopping threshold is tol times the response scale sqrt(s * sum(abs2, y)), s the loss scale; the sum of squares and its product with s may leave the range of the working type. For y = 0 the threshold is the absolute tol. A response scale whose computed value, rounded to the working type, is not a normal number is refused with an ArgumentError before the first pass: rescale the data or use relative=false (an objective that overflows is still refused). tol times a normal response scale can be subnormal or 0. The test change < threshold cannot hold for a threshold of 0, so the run cannot converge by it and ends at maxpasses with a warning, unless an independent numerical failure (for example a non-finite coordinate step) stops it first with an error. A column whose squared norm a is not finite in the working type is refused with an ArgumentError before the first change of its coordinate, and the starting point is unchanged. Where the product s * a or its reciprocal is outside the normal range of the type that combines s with the working type, the coordinate step forms its threshold lambda / (s * a) without them. relative=false makes the threshold an absolute change threshold; maxpasses limits the run. For least squares (lasso and elastic net) and the group lasso, an end-of-pass convergence flag does not guarantee a small KKT residual; for reported fits, check optimality independently. What the change test implies, and where it can be met away from a minimizer (a product that underflows or cancels, a warm start far above the solution scale), is described in the numerical guide. Anderson acceleration, a user-set gap target and screening are not implemented: anderson, gaptol and gapsafe are accepted only at their defaults, are not stored, and other values are rejected.

Once the active passes since the last full pass have together visited 20 times as many coordinates as a full pass, the next pass is a full pass even when the active changes have not fallen below the threshold. Float32 rounding of the coefficients can also keep the largest change of every full pass at or above the effective threshold (the reported tolerance; with relative=true and a positive response scale, each coordinate change is first multiplied by sqrt(s * a), a the squared norm of its column); the run then ends at maxpasses without converging, with the warning. A threshold well above that rounding level avoids this; Float64 data has a much lower rounding level.

A SparseIterate is solved on a private copy retaining its initial active zeros and coordinate order. After a completed finite run the caller receives the complete final state, including any zero-coordinate compaction. The copy needs O(p) extra storage.

Group lasso. With T equal to Float32 or Float64, x = BlockIterate(T, p, g) and g = GroupNormL2(lambda, groups) (or with weights), the call solve!(x, f, g, CDSolver(order=:cyclic, tol=1e-10)) minimizes the least-squares loss plus lambda * sum(w[k] * norm(x[groups[k]])). The loss is as for the lasso. g is a GroupNormL2{Float32} or GroupNormL2{Float64} whose level and weights are finite, nonnegative and representable in T, and its groups and those of x agree in number, order and ordered members, otherwise a DimensionMismatch is thrown. The coordinates are the groups, and a pass visits whole groups. momentum = true is refused with an ArgumentError; nothing and false give plain block coordinate descent. The change of a group is norm of its displacement, which relative=true multiplies by sqrt(s * l) for a positive response scale, where l bounds the largest eigenvalue of the Gram matrix of the columns of the group; the forced full pass counts group visits. As for the lasso, converged reports this change test and is not a KKT certificate. The curvature of a flat group, and of a group of one column when n eps(T) <= 1/8 (n the number of rows), is set before the first pass. That of any other group is computed at the first visit that needs it when cheap bounds show that no refusal can occur (under the eigenvalue model for groups of three or more columns and for groups of two columns outside the range of their closed-form bound), and before the first pass otherwise. A visit to a nonflat group whose block is zero, active or not, first applies ProximalBase's prox_block! to the correlations at the step 1/s (skipped when 1/s is not a normal number of the scale's arithmetic type, or when the correlations are not all zero and the largest magnitude is below 2^53 * floatmin(T)) and ends without a change, and without computing a still-deferred curvature, when the result is zero. Before the first pass, and before any draw from the RNG, an ArgumentError that names the group refuses a curvature that underflows or overflows in T and a step outside the normal range, for each group whose curvature is computed then. An exception of the eigenvalue routine (which a group of two columns inside the range of its closed-form bound does not call), a refusal of a lazily computed curvature, and a trial point or displacement that is not finite can occur during a pass; x is then unchanged, and the RNG may have advanced. The curvature rule and the refusals are described in the numerical guide.

Multi-response group lasso. With T equal to Float32 or Float64, X::Matrix{T} (n by p), Y::Matrix{T} (n by K), B::Matrix{T} (p by K), f = LeastSquaresLoss(Y, X) (or one positive scalar multiple of it, with loss scale s) and g = GroupNormL2(lambda, [collect(j:p:p*K) for j in 1:p]) (or with weights, by default sqrt(K)), the call solve!(B, f, g, CDSolver(order=:cyclic, momentum=false)) minimizes (s/2) * sum(abs2, Y - X*B) + lambda * sum(w[j] * norm(B[j, :]) for j in 1:p) from the original X and Y, without a Kronecker design, and returns B itself as minimizer. The groups of g must be the rows of B, g.groups[j] == j:p:p*K, in this order, otherwise a DimensionMismatch is thrown. A visit to row j costs O(n K), and the solver keeps an n by K residual and O(p K) further storage. The run is on a private BlockIterate over vec(B), and B is written only after a completed run with a finite objective. A BlockIterate over vec(B) with the same groups and loss is accepted too. The penalty, the loss scale, the refusal of momentum = true and the meaning of converged (the change test, not a KKT certificate) are those of the group lasso above. B must share no memory with X, Y, the penalty weights or the scratch array of the loss. With maxpasses > 0, an ArgumentError that names the row refuses, before the first pass, a column of X whose squared norm a is not finite or is below floatmin(T), unless the column is zero, and a column for which s * a or its reciprocal is below floatmin of the type that combines s with T. With relative=true and a positive response scale, the change of row j is norm of its displacement times sqrt(s * a) and the response scale is sqrt(s * sum(abs2, Y)), both up to rounding in the normal range. A zero column of X sets its row to 0 at a visit where the level and the weight are positive, and leaves the row as it is where either is 0; with maxpasses = 0 no row is visited, the column checks are not made and B keeps its values. ProximalGradientDescent, AccProxGradDescent, a NormL1 or ElasticNet penalty, SqrtLeastSquaresLoss and a vector response with a Matrix coefficient are refused with an ArgumentError.

Square-root lasso. With T equal to Float32 or Float64, X::Matrix{T}, y::Vector{T}, ProximalBase's f = SqrtLeastSquaresLoss(y, X) and g = NormL1(lambda), the call solve!(x, f, g, CDSolver(...)) minimizes P(beta) = norm(y - X*beta) + lambda * norm(beta, 1), without a 1/sqrt(n) factor, for a dense Vector{T} or a one-dimensional SparseIterate{T} x. The normalized objective norm(y - X*beta)/sqrt(n) + lambda_n * norm(beta, 1) has the same minimizers at lambda = sqrt(n) * lambda_n, and its values are those of P divided by sqrt(n); a scaled-lasso noise estimate is then norm(y - X*beta)/sqrt(n) at the solution. lambda must be positive (at lambda = 0, use LeastSquaresLoss, which has the same minimizers), finite and representable in T; it may be given in another Real type. A weighted NormL1, an ElasticNet, Scaled(SqrtLeastSquaresLoss, alpha) (for a positive finite alpha, use the unscaled loss at the level lambda/alpha), a BlockIterate, and ProximalGradientDescent or AccProxGradDescent (ProximalBase provides no gradient for this loss; for X != 0 it is not differentiable where y = X*beta) are refused with an ArgumentError before x changes. A visit computes the minimizer of P over its coordinate, up to rounding: a soft threshold at the least-squares centre with a threshold that depends on the residual. The order, momentum, the change threshold and maxpasses are as for least squares.

A full pass whose largest change is below the threshold runs a duality certificate: a dual point along the residual y - X*beta, recomputed in Float64, with a proved bound on the rounding of each computed term. converged = true only when it proves (P(beta) - P*)/P* <= 1e-4, P* the minimum, and gap then holds that bound, rounded up; its denominator is a lower bound of P* from the dual point. When the certificate does not pass, the change threshold (the reported tolerance) is divided by 100 and the run continues, at most twice per solve, where tolerance/100 is finite, positive and below tolerance (an effective threshold that overflowed to Inf is not lowered), and not below a floor in the units of the threshold: with a relative threshold (relative=true and y != 0), 16 eps(T) times norm(y); with an absolute one (relative=false, or y = 0), 16 eps(T) times the largest abs(beta[k]) at the check, and no tightening at beta = 0. Otherwise the run stops with converged = false, gap = NaN and a warning that differs from the maxpasses warning. This happens where the recomputed residual is 0 with a nonzero coefficient, so that the dual direction is undefined: the usual outcome at an exact fit (y = X*beta) with a positive penalty, where a coordinatewise minimum need not be a minimum (the residual can also round to 0 where y - X*beta is not 0, and an exact fit whose recomputed residual is not 0 can be certified, validly); after a full pass that changed nothing; with y = 0 and a nonzero start, where P* = 0, so that no relative bound can be certified unless beta reaches 0, and coordinate descent can approach 0 without reaching it; and when the threshold cannot be lowered further, for example where the rounding of the recomputed residual is not small against the residual itself (as where the fitted values dwarf it), or for Float32 data at the default tol with a relative threshold, where tol/100 is below that floor. With tol = 0, or a threshold below the changes that rounding produces, the change test does not pass, the certificate does not run, and the run ends at maxpasses with that warning. At a stop of the certificate, minimum is evaluated from the residual that it recomputed, when every entry of it is representable in T.

See solve! and the solver guide.

ProximalSolvers.ProximalGradientDescent — Type
ProximalGradientDescent(; ls=LineSearches.BackTracking(), normalized=false,
                         debias=0, preconditioner=:none)

Proximal gradient descent (PGD) for least squares with a NormL1 (lasso, with optional weights), ElasticNet, NormLinf or GroupNormL2 (group lasso) penalty and a dense Vector{Float32} or Vector{Float64} coefficient iterate. Each iteration takes a gradient step for the smooth least-squares loss and applies the proximal operator of the penalty (ProximalBase's value_and_prox!): soft thresholding for NormL1 (at the level λ wⱼ of entry j for NormL1(λ, w)), soft thresholding followed by the ridge weight for ElasticNet, for NormLinf, the point minus its projection onto the ℓ1 ball of radius λ/L (a clip of the entries at a common level), and for GroupNormL2 the block soft threshold (each group block v_G of the input scaled by max(0, 1 - γ λ wₖ/‖v_G‖₂) at the step γ; a zero block stays zero); backtracking adjusts the step size. The curvature estimate before the first iteration is min(1, α_f ‖X‖_F²) for the loss scale α_f, with ‖X‖_F an upper bound, rounded to T and raised to floatmin(T) when it is positive and rounds below that; it is 1 when that product is at least 1 or X = 0.

ProximalGradientDescent() uses the supported LineSearches.BackTracking() line search. A trial that it rejects is decided again, except in the cases that the LineSearches.BackTracking docstring lists, by the computed curvature c of the step from one product X d: it is accepted when L ≥ c, and otherwise the retry uses c in place of the estimate L̂ when c is finite (see LineSearches.BackTracking and numerical behavior). The options normalized=true, debias>0, preconditioner!=:none and custom line searches are not implemented and are rejected. These constructor keywords remain for compatibility with earlier versions; their default values are accepted and not stored.

Pass ProximalOptions(...) as the fifth argument to solve! to configure stopping tolerances and iteration limits. A stop that the xtol, ftol or gradtol test proposes counts only if the computed KKT residual of the iterate is below max(gradtol, 64 eps(T)) * max(λ, λmax), with λmax = α‖Xᵀy‖∞ and λ the ℓ1 level of the penalty (λ of NormL1(λ), the largest coefficient T(λ) * maximum(w) of NormL1(λ, w), λ1 of ElasticNet; see ProximalOptions); for GroupNormL2(λ, groups, w) the residual is the group KKT residual, λmax = maxₖ α‖[Xᵀy]_{Gₖ}‖₂ and λ = T(λ) * maximum(w); for NormLinf the generic proximal-gradient check of ProximalOptions applies instead. For NormL1, ElasticNet and GroupNormL2, converged=true guarantees only that the computed, normalized KKT residual ‖G‖∞ / max(λ, λmax) is below max(gradtol, 64 eps(T)) (the threshold is formed in T, which can round or overflow; see ProximalOptions); it is not an exact-arithmetic certificate, does not bound the distance to a minimizer and does not guarantee coefficient accuracy on ill-conditioned designs (see the stationarity check), and the default tolerances are not a specified number of correct digits. In Float32, a solve can report converged = true at a relative duality gap above 1e-5, as in measurements on small benchmark designs (see the limits of the stopping tests). Tighten tolerances and check KKT conditions independently before reporting numerical results. See the solver guide and numerical behavior.

ProximalSolvers.AccProxGradDescent — Type
AccProxGradDescent(; β = 0.5, α = 0.9, preconditioner = :none)

Accelerated proximal gradient descent (APG) for least squares with a NormL1 (lasso, with optional weights), ElasticNet, NormLinf or group lasso (GroupNormL2, see ProximalGradientDescent) penalty and a dense Vector{Float32} or Vector{Float64} coefficient iterate (Parikh and Boyd, Proximal Algorithms, §4.3). Iteration k (counted from the start or the last restart) extrapolates y = x + k/(k+3) (x - x') from the last two accepted iterates and takes a proximal gradient step from y: a gradient step for the least-squares loss followed by the proximal operator of the penalty (soft thresholding for NormL1, at the level λ wⱼ of entry j for NormL1(λ, w), with the ridge weight after it for ElasticNet, for GroupNormL2 ProximalBase's block soft threshold, and for NormLinf the point minus its projection onto the ℓ1 ball of radius λ/L). It is a FISTA-family method; its momentum sequence k/(k+3) is not the textbook FISTA recurrence.

The step size 1/L is found by backtracking on the least-squares bound f(x) ≤ f(y) + ⟨∇f(y), x - y⟩ + L/2 ‖x - y‖², which holds exactly when √α_f ‖X(x - y)‖ ≤ √L ‖x - y‖. A trial is accepted only when that inequality, with a relative slack of 4eps on √L, is certified by conservative rounding-error bounds (assuming IEEE arithmetic with gradual underflow): first from the difference of cached products, and when that is inconclusive from a freshly computed X(x - y); a trial that is still inconclusive is rejected. See numerical behavior. A trial with x == y entrywise is accepted when its value is finite. A rejected trial sets L ← L/β, β ∈ (0, 1). Before each iteration L ← α L, α ∈ (0, 1], so that the step can grow again where the curvature is smaller; α = 1 keeps L from decreasing. The estimate before the first iteration is min(1, α_f ‖X‖_F²) for the loss scale α_f, with ‖X‖_F an upper bound, rounded to T and raised to floatmin(T) when it is positive and rounds below that; it is 1 when that product is at least 1 or X = 0.

When an extrapolated step would increase the computed objective, the momentum is restarted: the step is repeated from y = x' and k returns to zero, so the next iteration extrapolates with 1/4. An accepted step that used momentum therefore never increases the computed objective. A step without momentum (the first iteration, and a step repeated after a restart) can increase it through rounding of the step and of the objective; as for ProximalGradientDescent, that stops the solve unless allow_f_increases = true in ProximalOptions.

Backtracking raises an error when the next curvature estimate L/β is not a finite increase of L; solve! then restores the last accepted iterate and rethrows. A β close to one makes each rejected trial raise L only slightly. preconditioner = :diagonal is deferred and rejected. Pass ProximalOptions(...) as the fifth argument to solve!. A stop that the xtol, ftol or gradtol test proposes counts only if the computed KKT residual at the accepted x is below max(gradtol, 64 eps(T)) * max(λ, λmax), with λ the ℓ1 level of the penalty (λ of NormL1(λ), the largest coefficient T(λ) * maximum(w) of NormL1(λ, w), λ1 of ElasticNet) and, for NormL1 and ElasticNet, λmax = α‖Xᵀy‖∞ (see ProximalOptions); for GroupNormL2(λ, groups, w) the residual is the group KKT residual and λmax = maxₖ α‖[Xᵀy]_{Gₖ}‖₂, λ = T(λ) * maximum(w); for NormLinf the generic proximal-gradient check of ProximalOptions applies instead. For NormL1, ElasticNet and GroupNormL2, converged = true guarantees only that the computed, normalized KKT residual ‖G‖∞ / max(λ, λmax) is below max(gradtol, 64 eps(T)) (the threshold is formed in T, which can round or overflow; see ProximalOptions). It is not an exact-arithmetic certificate and does not guarantee coefficient accuracy on ill-conditioned designs (see the stationarity check). In Float32, a solve can report converged = true at a relative duality gap above 1e-5, as in measurements on small benchmark designs (see the limits of the stopping tests). The check forms ∇f(x) from the cached image A x and counts that evaluation in grad_calls. The gradient-mapping residual of the result, L ‖x - y‖∞, is measured from the extrapolated point y, not from x; check KKT conditions independently before reporting numerical results. See the solver guide and numerical behavior.

ProximalSolvers.solve! — Function
solve!(x, f, g, CDSolver())
solve!(x, f, g, ProximalGradientDescent(), options=ProximalOptions())
solve!(x, f, g, AccProxGradDescent(), options=ProximalOptions())
solve!(x::BlockIterate, f, g::GroupNormL2, CDSolver())

Minimize a least-squares lasso (with optional weights), elastic net or ℓ∞-penalized problem (the last with ProximalGradientDescent and AccProxGradDescent), or with CDSolver a square-root lasso, overwriting x. x is a dense Vector{T}, T=Float32 or Float64; CDSolver also accepts a one-dimensional SparseIterate{T} (see below), while the gradient methods (ProximalGradientDescent, AccProxGradDescent) refuse it. The BlockIterate form of CDSolver minimizes the group lasso with a GroupNormL2 penalty on a BlockIterate{T} with the same groups; only CDSolver accepts a BlockIterate, and CDSolver describes it. ProximalGradientDescent and AccProxGradDescent also minimize the group lasso on a dense Vector{T} (see below). The loss uses same-type Matrix X and Vector y. The penalty of the gradient methods is a NormL1(λ) or NormL1(λ, w), an unweighted ElasticNet(λ1, λ2) (λ1 ‖x‖₁ + (λ2/2) ‖x‖²) or NormLinf(λ) (λ max|xᵢ|), whose levels are finite and nonnegative and representable in T without overflow or underflow; for a dense Vector or a SparseIterate, CDSolver accepts the same penalties except NormLinf (see CDSolver), which it refuses (its coordinate step needs a separable penalty) before it changes x or uses its RNG; its BlockIterate form takes a GroupNormL2. The weights w of NormL1(λ, w) are a Vector{T} with one finite nonnegative entry per entry of x (T the element type of the data and of x), and x must not alias them. solve! checks the coefficient T(λ) wⱼ of entry j, the product rounded in T: it must be finite and, where T(λ) and wⱼ are both positive, a normal number of T (a zero or subnormal product, and a level T(λ) that is subnormal, are refused). The stopping check of PGD and APG uses it; ProximalBase's proximal steps form their thresholds from λ and wⱼ in their working type (see Supported problems). A zero level or weight gives the coefficient 0, which is accepted; a zero weight leaves its entry unpenalized (it is not fixed at 0). Any other weight array type (a view, a range, a vector of another element type), a weighted ElasticNet and any Scaled penalty is refused. Use (one(T)/T(n))*LeastSquaresLoss for normalized lasso; raw LS keeps its unnormalized meaning. One positive finite scalar LS wrapper is supported. CDSolver also accepts ProximalBase's SqrtLeastSquaresLoss(y, X) with an unweighted NormL1(λ), λ > 0, on a dense Vector or a SparseIterate (see CDSolver); the gradient methods refuse it, and CDSolver refuses it with a weighted NormL1.

Group lasso with ProximalGradientDescent. On a dense Vector{T} with the least-squares loss above, ProximalGradientDescent accepts g = GroupNormL2(λ, groups, w) (or with the default weights) when g is a GroupNormL2{T} (its level and weights have the element type of the data; convert with GroupNormL2(T(λ), groups, T.(w))), its groups partition 1:p (none empty, no index in two groups, none in no group, none above p) and match the layout stored in the penalty at construction (a change of g.groups after construction is refused where it disagrees with that layout; construct the penalty again), its weights are finite and nonnegative, T(λ) is zero or a normal number of T, each coefficient λ wₖ, rounded in T, is finite and, where λ and wₖ are both positive, a normal number of T, and x does not alias w. A zero weight leaves its group unpenalized. The proximal step is ProximalBase's block soft threshold, and the stopping check uses the group KKT residual of ProximalBase's kkt_violation (see ProximalOptions). AccProxGradDescent accepts the same GroupNormL2 with the same stopping check; CDSolver on a dense Vector or a SparseIterate refuses it.

Borrowed X/y must not alias mutable x or change during a solve. Results share x; copy it to retain earlier coefficients. Unsupported inputs/options fail before mutation. For PGD and APG, a stop of the stopping tests counts only at a point whose computed KKT residual (for a NormLinf penalty, the proximal-gradient residual) meets the bound of ProximalOptions, which is not an exact-arithmetic certificate; CD stops by its change test, and for the square-root loss reports converged = true only when a duality certificate passes (see CDSolver). Finite intermediate arithmetic is required. PGD step errors restore its last accepted x and rethrow, without a transaction guarantee for internal counters.

CD on a dense vector. A numerical error, including a nonfinite final objective, leaves x unchanged.

CD on a SparseIterate. All checks of inputs, options, shapes, finiteness of the active values, representable parameters and detectable aliases of x with X or y (through its backing storage, whether or not it has active entries) happen before the iterate is copied and before the RNG is used, so a refused call neither changes x nor advances the RNG. The solve then runs on work = copy(x), which keeps the values, active zeros and the order of the active coordinates, with the same passes, activation, per-pass removal of zero coordinates, momentum and stopping as for a dense vector. It needs O(p) extra storage, and nothing is reconstructed through a dense vector.

When the run completes, with a finite objective, x receives the whole final state of work by a same-shape copyto!(x, work): this includes a finite maxpasses outcome with converged = false and its warning, and maxpasses = 0, which leaves exactly the entry state, including active zeros and slot order. Zeros that a pass removed from work are not restored, and the order is not sorted. The result's minimizer is x itself. A numerical error (such as a nonfinite step or objective) throws with the values, the active set, signed zeros and the order of x unchanged; the borrowed RNG may already have advanced. After a successful call, take activevalues(x) and activecoordinates(x) again: handles taken earlier may no longer describe the active set.

CD on a BlockIterate. The checks of the receiver, momentum, penalty, groups, parameters, aliasing and finiteness happen before the iterate is copied and before the RNG is used, and a refused call neither changes x nor advances the RNG. The curvature and step refusals that can be decided from the data follow, before the first pass, before any RNG draw and only when maxpasses > 0, with x unchanged; the curvature of a group that is neither flat nor a one-column group with n eps(T) <= 1/8 is computed in that preparation, or at the first visit that needs it when cheap bounds exclude a refusal (under the eigenvalue model for groups of three or more columns and for groups of two columns outside the range of their closed-form bound), and an exception of its eigenvalue routine or a refusal at that point happens during a pass, with x unchanged and the RNG possibly advanced. The solve then runs on work = copy(x) and x receives the final state of work by one copyto!. Zero groups are deactivated after a pass, so the active groups of x after a solve with at least one pass are the groups with a nonzero block; with maxpasses = 0 the entry state is kept, including active groups whose block is zero. A numerical error leaves the values and the active groups of x unchanged, though the borrowed RNG may already have advanced. The result's minimizer is x itself.

source
solve!(x, f, g, AccProxGradDescent(), options=ProximalOptions())

Minimize the same least-squares lasso, elastic net or NormLinf problem as ProximalGradientDescent with accelerated proximal gradient descent, overwriting x, a dense Vector{T} with T = Float32 or Float64. The inputs, the options and their validation are those of ProximalGradientDescent, including a GroupNormL2 penalty with its group stopping check (see ProximalGradientDescent and ProximalOptions); in addition β and α must satisfy their bounds after conversion to T, and the dimensions must satisfy max(n, p) + 4 < 1/(4 eps(T)) and n p + 3 < 1/(4 eps(Float64)) (for Float32, max(n, p) < 2,097,148), which the rounding-error bounds of the line search need. Every refusal of an input, option or method setting happens before x is modified. A step error restores the last accepted x and rethrows, without a transaction guarantee for internal counters. The result shares x.

ProximalSolvers.ProximalOptions — Type
ProximalOptions(; maxiter=200, ftol=1e-6, xtol=1e-8, gradtol=1e-5,
  allow_f_increases=false, store_trace=false, show_trace=false,
  extended_trace=false, print_every=1, callback=nothing, time_limit=Inf,
  f_calls_limit=0, grad_calls_limit=0, prox_calls_limit=0, gaptol=0, gapevery=10)

Options for proximal gradient descent (PGD); AccProxGradDescent uses the same options and tests. The defaults prioritize an exploratory solve rather than a guaranteed number of accurate digits. PGD stops when any of the iterate-change (xtol), objective-change (ftol), or gradient/proximal-residual (gradtol) criteria proposes a stop and the stationarity check below passes.

The stationarity check. At an iteration at which one of the three tests holds, the solver computes the minimal-norm KKT residual G at the accepted iterate x: the element of ∇f(x) + λ ∂‖x‖₁ of least infinity norm for NormL1(λ), and the element of ∇f(x) + λ2 x + λ1 ∂‖x‖₁ of least infinity norm for ElasticNet(λ1, λ2) (ProximalGradientDescent and AccProxGradDescent). For NormL1(λ, w) with weights w it is the element of ∇f(x) + ∂(λ Σⱼ wⱼ |xⱼ|) of least infinity norm, with the coefficient T(λ) wⱼ of entry j (T the element type; w is a Vector{T}); a zero weight gives the coefficient 0, so its entry is unpenalized and its score is |∇fⱼ(x)|. Below, λ is the ℓ1 level of the penalty (λ of NormL1(λ), the largest coefficient T(λ) * maximum(w) of NormL1(λ, w), λ1 of ElasticNet); λ2 does not enter the bound. For GroupNormL2(λ, groups, w) (ProximalGradientDescent and AccProxGradDescent), ‖G‖∞ below stands for ProximalBase's group residual max(0, maxₖ scoreₖ), with scoreₖ = ‖∇_G f(x) + cₖ x_G/‖x_G‖₂‖₂ at a group with a nonzero entry and ‖∇_G f(x)‖₂ - cₖ at a zero group (cₖ = T(λ) wₖ rounded in T), λ for the largest coefficient T(λ) * maximum(w) and λmax for maxₖ α‖[Xᵀy]_{Gₖ}‖₂, the largest group norm of ∇f(0). The stop counts only if ‖G‖∞ < max(gradtol, 64 eps(T)) * max(λ, λmax), with T the element type and, for NormL1 and ElasticNet, λmax = α‖Xᵀy‖∞ for the loss scale α (1 for a plain LeastSquaresLoss). The check also requires that S = max(λ, λmax) is finite, that the product max(gradtol, 64 eps(T)) * S, formed in T, is at least floatmin(T), and that ‖G‖∞ is finite; it does not require that product to be finite (for a huge gradtol it can be Inf). Otherwise x_converged, f_converged and grad_converged are all false, and the solve continues. With y = 0 and λ = 0 both λ and λmax are 0, and nothing converges. This holds when the computed S = max(λ, λmax) is 0, with λmax as the solver computes it: for example ElasticNet(0, λ2) with y = 0, where the ridge solution is x = 0 but the solve runs to maxiter with converged=false, unless another exit applies. An exact Xᵀy = 0 need not give a computed λmax = 0 (rounding), and the check then uses the rounded λmax. ElasticNet(λ1, λ2) on the loss ½α‖Xb - y‖² is the lasso with level λ1 on the data X̃ = [√α X; √λ2 I], ỹ = [√α y; 0], whose gradient is ∇f(x) + λ2 x and whose λmax is the λmax above: the check is that of the lasso on those data. For a dense Vector or a one-dimensional SparseIterate, the coordinate descent solver accepts an unweighted NormL1 or ElasticNet; it stops by its change test, not by this check (see CDSolver). A start x0 with a finite f(x0) is accepted even if g(x0) overflows in T (for example a ridge term (λ2/2)‖x0‖² above floatmax(T)). An error raised in the step of an iteration (its line search, or the check that the accepted objective is finite) restores the last accepted iterate and is rethrown, as for NormL1. converged=true means that a test proposed a stop and that the computed (working-precision) KKT residual ‖G‖∞ at the returned x is below that bound. The code forms τ = T(max(gradtol, 64 eps(T))) and the threshold τ * max(λ, λmax) in T, and compares ‖G‖∞ with it. The normalized form ‖G‖∞ / max(λ, λmax) < max(gradtol, 64 eps(T)) is the exact-arithmetic reading: in working precision the conversion to T and the product can round, or overflow to Inf for a huge gradtol, in which case a finite residual passes against the infinite threshold. converged = true guarantees only that the computed, normalized KKT residual ‖G‖∞ / max(λ, λmax) is below max(gradtol, 64 eps(T)), in that sense. It is not an exact-arithmetic certificate, it does not bound the distance to a minimizer, and it does not guarantee coefficient accuracy on ill-conditioned designs (see the stationarity check). The check uses the solver's working-precision products, so a gradient product that underflows or cancels can pass it at a point that is not stationary (see the limits of the stopping tests). A gradtol below 64 eps(T), including 0, gives the bound 64 eps(T) max(λ, λmax). For AccProxGradDescent the check forms ∇f(x) from the cached image A x at each iteration at which a stop is proposed, and counts that evaluation in grad_calls.

The generic check (NormLinf(λ)). A penalty without a dedicated check above uses the proximal-gradient mapping at the accepted x: with t = 1/L (the accepted curvature), r = ‖x - prox_{t g}(x - t ∇f(x))‖∞ / t, computed with the same ProximalBase value_and_prox! as the iterations. The stop counts only if r < max(gradtol, 64 eps(T)) * S with S = max(‖∇f(0)‖∞, ‖∇f(x)‖∞); it is refused when t is not finite and positive, when S or r is not finite, when that threshold is below floatmin(T) (so with S = 0, as for X = 0 or at x = 0 when y = 0, no stop counts), when it does not exceed ρ = L eps(T) ‖x‖∞, or when t times it is below floatmin(T)/eps(T). Each proposed stop with a finite positive t costs one proximal evaluation, counted in prox_calls (and for AccProxGradDescent one gradient, counted in grad_calls). A small accepted L makes t large and r a weaker measure of stationarity. converged = true is then a working-precision indication, not a certificate.

The gradient test holds when r < gradtol * m or r < gradtol * N, with r the gradient/proximal residual of the iteration, m the largest r so far in the solve and N the larger of the gradient's and the proximal step's infinity norms, each only against a scale that exceeds ρ = L eps(T) ‖x‖∞, the rounding resolution of L x. No absolute term is added to m or N. The comparisons use the floating-point products gradtol * m and gradtol * N: a residual of 0 holds only against a positive product, so it does not hold against m = N = 0 or against a product that underflows to 0.

ftol must be finite and nonnegative. With ftol > 0, including the default, the objective test holds when |F - F'| / |F| < ftol or |F - F'| < eps(|F| + |F'|), where F = f(x) + g(x) is the computed objective and F' the previous one. The quotient is 0 when F == F' and Inf when F = 0 ≠ F'. The second form holds when F == F' and |F| + |F'| is finite, so a flat computed objective proposes a stop through the objective test, which is not evidence of an accurate point (the stationarity check decides); in the ordinary range, where F and F' are normal floating-point numbers and |F| + |F'| is finite, it is about two units in the last place of F. (If |F| + |F'| overflows, eps is NaN and the second form does not hold.) ftol = 0 switches the objective test off: f_converged is false at every iteration. A positive ftol that rounds to 0 in Float64 is refused, so that a tiny value does not mean "off", and -0.0 is stored as 0.0.

With ftol = 0 only, an iteration whose accepted iterate equals the previous one elementwise (compared by value, so -0.0 == 0.0) is no progress: it sets neither x_converged nor grad_converged, and the solve continues, subject to the other tests, the limits and, with allow_f_increases=false, the objective-increase stop. A solve whose iterate stays unchanged until maxiter, and does not stop as described below, ends with converged=false and iteration_converged=true; the flags then report no progress, not that the point is wrong. An iterate that differs from the previous one in any entry is evaluated as usual, including when the norm of the difference rounds to 0. With ftol > 0, an unchanged iterate proposes a stop through the xtol test, and the stationarity check decides. For AccProxGradDescent, an extrapolated point y equal to x while x differs from the previous iterate is not a stall: its residual L * maximum(abs, x - y) is 0, so the gradient test holds when 0 < gradtol * N and ρ < N, or 0 < gradtol * m and ρ < m.

With ftol = 0, ProximalGradientDescent on Vector{Float32} data also stops at an iteration in which both of these hold: the accepted iterate equals the previous one elementwise, and the last rejection of that iteration's line search was decided by the computed curvature of the rejected step, which was finite and replaced the line search's curvature estimate L̂ (the retry's L is then min(Lexact, max(c, L / β)) for the rejected trial's L, so it need not equal the curvature c). A rejection that is not checked (a trial value that is not finite, a NaN estimate, a gradient estimate from a gradient with a nonfinite entry) or whose computed curvature is not finite does not count, and neither does an iteration whose first trial is already a zero step (it has no rejection). A later rejection of the same iteration replaces an earlier one, and nothing carries over from an earlier iteration. The stagnation check comes after tracing and after any callback call that is due at that iteration under print_every; it does not change the callback cadence. The result then has stagnated=true and converged=false (see OptimizationResults); this is not a convergence or accuracy certificate. For Float64 data, for AccProxGradDescent and with ftol > 0, stagnated is false and the stopping behavior is as described above.

With allow_f_increases=false (the default), PGD also stops at the first iteration whose computed objective F = f(x) + g(x), in the working precision, exceeds the previous iteration's F'. This stop is not a convergence test. At the iteration where any stop occurs, the result reports f_increased = F > F' and converged = x_converged || f_converged || grad_converged, so an objective-increase stop reports converged=false unless one of those tests also held and the stationarity check passed. With ftol > 0, in the ordinary range (F and F' normal floating-point numbers, |F| + |F'| finite), an increase smaller than about two units in the last place of F passes the objective test, and the result reports both converged=true and f_increased=true if the stationarity check also passes, and converged=false with f_increased=true if it does not. A Float32 solve with the default options can therefore end on the objective-increase stop, with converged=false, where it used to stop on the objective test. With ftol = 0, f_converged is false, so an increase of any size reports converged=false unless the x or gradient test held and the check passed. Near a minimizer the computed change in F is of the size of the rounding error of evaluating F, so whether a strict-tolerance solve ends with converged=true or with an objective-increase stop can depend on the last bits of its trajectory (CPU, BLAS kernel, compilation) rather than on the accuracy of the point. allow_f_increases=true removes this stop; f_increased then only reports the comparison at the last iteration. iteration_converged is iterations == maxiter, which also holds when a test succeeded at that last iteration. See the numerical behavior page, which also describes a strict-accuracy configuration with ftol = 0.

maxiter caps iterations; store_trace, show_trace and extended_trace control reporting. print_every sets display/callback cadence; a callback receives the trace when it is stored, otherwise the current state. The result display labels an internal gradient/proximal residual as |g(x)|: it is not the value of the supplied penalty.

Call/time limits are checked after complete iterations, so counts can exceed limits by one iteration. A zero call limit means unlimited. Float32/Float64 iterate buffers retain native arithmetic; some scalar tolerances and diagnostics are held in Float64, without increasing coefficient accuracy. Gap certificates are not implemented: gaptol and gapevery are accepted for compatibility but not stored, a positive gaptol is refused when the options are constructed, and gapevery (which must be positive) has no effect.

ProximalSolvers.OptimizationResults — Type
OptimizationResults

Result from proximal gradient descent, plain or accelerated. minimizer aliases the caller's coefficient vector; minimum is the reported objective at that iterate. iterations counts steps and converged means that at least one stopping test proposed a stop and that the stationarity check passed (see ProximalOptions): the computed KKT residual at minimizer is below max(gradtol, 64 eps(T)) * max(λ, λmax) (for GroupNormL2, the group residual and scale that ProximalOptions defines). It is a working-precision value, not an exact-arithmetic certificate; it guarantees only that the computed, normalized residual ‖G‖∞ / max(λ, λmax) is below max(gradtol, 64 eps(T)) (the threshold is formed in T, see ProximalOptions), not coefficient accuracy on ill-conditioned designs. For a NormLinf penalty the generic proximal-gradient check of ProximalOptions is used instead. iteration_converged reports reaching the iteration limit, not successful convergence.

The result also stores stopping flags, residuals and tolerances, optional trace, call counts and backtracking counts. x_converged, f_converged and grad_converged are the flags after the stationarity check, so all three are false at an iteration where the check refused a proposed stop; the residual fields are those of the tests at the last iteration. grad_calls of AccProxGradDescent includes one gradient at x for each check. For PGD, backtrack_steps includes accepted nonzero line-search trials; for APG it counts every trial, including the accepted one. gap=NaN means a dual gap was not calculated, not that the gap is zero.

f_increased is F > F' at the last iteration, where F is the computed objective and F' the previous one. It reports the comparison: an increase stops the solve only when allow_f_increases=false, a setting the result does not store. With ftol = 0 (see ProximalOptions), f_tol is 0.0 and f_converged is false, because the objective test is off. f_residual is then reported only and does not decide a stop; it is |F - F'| / |F|, with the value 0.0 for F = F' = 0 and Inf for F = 0 and F' != 0. With ftol > 0 it is the same quantity, the one that the objective test compares with ftol. On an iteration whose iterate equals the previous one elementwise, ftol = 0 sets neither x_converged nor grad_converged (see ProximalOptions for the accelerated method's case x == y).

stagnated is true when ProximalGradientDescent on Vector{Float32} data with ftol = 0 stopped after an iteration in which both of these held: the accepted iterate equals the previous one elementwise, and the last rejection of that iteration's line search replaced the line search's curvature estimate L̂ by the finite computed curvature of the rejected step (see ProximalOptions for the condition). At such a stop converged, x_converged, f_converged and grad_converged are false and x_residual is 0. stagnated=true is not a certificate of convergence or accuracy: it reports that an iteration left the iterate unchanged after the last rejection of its line search was decided by that step's computed curvature, and the returned point need not meet any accuracy target. stagnated is false for AccProxGradDescent, for Float64 data and with ftol > 0. Each stopping flag reports its own condition, so stagnated can be true together with call_limit_reached, with iteration_converged, or at an iteration at which the callback returned true.

ProximalSolvers.LineSearches.BackTracking — Type
BackTracking(; α = 0.9, β = 0.5, Lexact = Inf)

The backtracking line search of TFOCS (private/tfocs_backtrack.m). Each iteration starts from L ← α L, where 1/L is the step size, and while the local Lipschitz estimate L̂ of the step exceeds L it retries with L ← min(Lexact, max(L̂, L / β)). L̂ comes from function values until they are too close to compare in floating point, and from gradients after that. An LS trial whose image or value overflows is never accepted: it gives no estimate, and the search retries with L ← L / β.

For ProximalGradientDescent on its supported least-squares model, a rejected trial with step d̂ = x - x' is decided again by the computed curvature of the step, c = m α ‖fl(X d̂)‖²/‖d̂‖², from one product X d̂, with m = 1 for function-value estimates and m = 2 for gradient estimates (TFOCS's gradient test 2α ‖X d‖² ≤ L ‖d‖²). When L ≥ c the trial is accepted and the estimate mode is unchanged; otherwise the retry uses L ← min(Lexact, max(c, L / β)) when c is finite, and TFOCS's L ← min(Lexact, max(L̂, L / β)) when it is not. c carries no rounding certificate: when L is within rounding of the curvature along d̂, the decision can go either way. For Float32 data the squared norms are accumulated in Float64, and two allowances are added to ‖fl(X d̂)‖ before squaring: 2√n p floatmin(Float32) for underflow of the results, and √n floatmin(Float32) ‖d̂‖₁ for subnormal entries of X that a thread flushing subnormals to zero (the calling thread or a BLAS thread) drops from the product. They assume that flushing affects only subnormal operands and results, and give no certificate. The curvature value compared with L, in Float64, is then positive when finite, so a product lost to underflow or flushing does not make it zero. The estimate used for a retry is that value rounded to Float32, which may round to Inf (when the conversion overflows). TFOCS's update is kept when the product overflowed, when the calling thread flushes subnormals to zero, and when d̂ has a subnormal entry. For Float64 data, overflow or underflow of ‖fl(X d̂)‖², underflow of ‖d̂‖², and overflow of ‖d̂‖² for a step with a nonfinite entry or in a narrow band around the overflow threshold of ‖d‖² (where norm(d)^2 can be finite and sum(abs2, d) not) can make c nonfinite (TFOCS's update is then kept) or spuriously zero (the trial is then accepted). A rejection is not checked, and keeps TFOCS's update, when its trial value is not finite, when the computed ‖d̂‖² is zero (the zero-step exit, which includes a tiny nonzero step whose square underflows), when L̂ is NaN, or when a gradient estimate comes from a gradient ∇f(x) with a nonfinite entry. Accepted trials are not checked.

When the step d = x - x' of a trial has finite entries, norm(d)^2 overflows in the type of x (the squared norm is Inf) and the trial value f(x) is finite, the trial is decided on the scaled step d̃ = s d, s = 2^(-e) with e = min(exponent(max |dᵢ|), exponent(floatmax(T)) - 1) for the type T of x, so that s is a normal number and the largest entry of d̃ is in [1, 2), or in [2, 4) when exponent(max |dᵢ|) is exponent(floatmax(T)). A trial whose value f(x) is not finite is not scaled: it takes the exit for a nonfinite trial value described above. L̂ is formed from d̃, from f(x) and f(x') multiplied by s² (by shifting their exponents, since s² can underflow), and from the products of d̃ and ∇f multiplied by s; in exact arithmetic it equals the estimate at d. The trial is then decided as at a representable scale, up to the rounding of the computed f and ∇f, overflow of the products ⟨d̃, ∇f(x')⟩ and ⟨∇f(x) - ∇f(x'), d̃⟩ before they are multiplied by s (an infinite product, or a NaN from overflowed terms of opposite sign), and underflow of an entry of d̃, of a scaled function value or of L̂. Such a product is not recovered by the scaling: L̂ is formed from it by the same expressions as for an unscaled step, with the scaled quantities, so it can be finite, infinite or NaN (for example, with finite f(x) and f(x'), a +Inf function-value product gives L̂ = L, and the trial is accepted without the check). The check of a rejected trial reads d̃, with ‖d̃‖² between 1 and about 16n; for Float32 data it is also subject to the allowances above, and an entry of d̃ that is subnormal keeps TFOCS's update, while an entry of d with |s dᵢ| ≤ 2^-150 rounds to zero in d̃, is not detected, and drops out of both sums of squares and of the product. After an accepted trial the solver state holds x - x' again. A step with a nonfinite entry is not scaled, and neither is a step whose squared norm underflows.

In gradient mode a zero gradient-difference product gives L̂ = 0, and the trial is accepted. This includes Float64 data with ‖d̂‖² below 2/floatmax(Float64) (about 1.1e-308), where 2/‖d̂‖² overflows and scaling the product would give 0 · Inf = NaN. A subnormal ‖d̂‖² can make the Float64 estimates unreliable, and this does not make a solve converge at such scales: a solve can still end with converged = false or with a line-search error. One case is not handled: a subnormal ‖d̂‖² with a nonzero product, or with function-value rounding noise (f(x) > q, with q the quadratic model at the trial x), can give L̂ = Inf, and when sum(abs2, d̂) underflows to 0 while ‖d̂‖² > 0 (every entry of d̂ below about 1.57e-162), the computed curvature of the step is not finite and does not replace that estimate. See numerical behavior.

The object holds settings only and can be shared between solves; the bookkeeping lives in the solver state.
