Working precision and numerical boundaries

The supported array types and parameter conversions are defined by IN-01 and IN-02. These clauses describe the limits of computations on accepted inputs. They distinguish a library kernel's recovery from a solver's complete execution.

FP-01 Native precision and ownership of arithmetic

Statement.

Iterates retain their native Float32 or Float64 working accuracy. Float32 may stagnate earlier. Some tolerances, residual reports and scalar diagnostics are stored in Float64; that storage does not promote the iterates or certify their accuracy. A tolerance tighter than the working precision does not guarantee corresponding coefficient accuracy. BigFloat is useful for independent test references, not a supported solver working type. The loss-scale type refusals are in IN-02.

With subnormal numbers flushed to zero on the solving thread (set_zero_subnormals(true)), the stopping decisions of PGD and APG are unspecified, the exact zero cases of STOP-21 included: the solver's arithmetic and its compiled comparisons can treat a stored subnormal value as zero.

Finite input entries do not guarantee finite intermediate arithmetic. ProximalBase's least-squares kernels selectively recover some overflow/underflow cases with representable results. ProximalSolvers adds its own matrix products, curvature computations, state updates, stopping and finiteness checks, and does not promise end-to-end recovery of every failed product, curvature, step or objective.

The least-squares solvers read Y, X and the stored loss scale through ProximalBase.leastsquares_data. ProximalBase owns the least-squares value in all three solvers and the coefficient gradient in PGD/APG, with the scale as its computation uses it. PGD/APG use value_from_predictor and value_and_gradient_from_predictor!; CD uses value_from_residual, which returns only a value. ProximalSolvers does not repeat those oracle computations. It owns the image X*x and CD residual/curvature caches, CD coordinate correlations and updates, the lambda_max product of the λmax-based stationarity checks, the column norms of the coordinatewise check (STOP-21), steps and line searches, APG's certified bound, stopping rules and rollback, and on the ℓ1-ball route of IN-12 the widened Float64 decision, the budgets and the full check of FP-05. The coordinatewise check reads ProximalBase's least-squares value at the accepted point for its scale and does not recompute ‖Xx - y‖. Exact oracle accuracy and range statements belong to the section “Least squares at a predictor or residual that the caller holds” in the pinned ProximalBase smooth-function guide. ADMM's use of proximal operators and working-precision diagnostics is specified in ADM-02, and its Cholesky checks in T for the graphical lasso in ADM-04. For the least-squares lasso, ADM-05 specifies the Float64 arithmetic, the proximal operator that ProximalBase computes from a cached SVD and the stationarity score. It states that no cutoff on ρ, the penalty or the coefficients applies.

ProxNewton (PN-01) works in Float64 only. It forms its predictors, the weighted design, the model gradients and the objective in Float64, takes the logistic value and gradient from ProximalBase's predictor oracles, and converts the penalty level to Float64 once; the weighted model's saturated rows and the range errors of its inner solve are specified in PN-02 and PN-05. LogisticADMM (LA-01) also works in Float64 only, with ρ in [1e-10, 1e10]. It forms the image X b, the response and residual of its coefficient step, the scalar roots and the residual tests in Float64, takes the loss value and gradient from ProximalBase's predictor oracle and adds the penalty itself, and holds the dual as w = ρ u. Its scalar root forms σ(t) - y from exp(-|t|); for the label 0 and t between about -745.13 and -708.40 this value is subnormal rather than 0, and below that range it is 0 (LA-03). The accuracy of the root is stated there as a tested backward error, and the failure boundary in LA-05.

converged has the algorithm-specific meaning in RES-01. Independently evaluate the objective and KKT residual when accuracy matters, especially for Float32. For nonnegative lasso, use the constrained residual in STOP-16, which accounts for feasible directions at a zero coefficient.

Applies to. All supported solvers and precisions.

FP-02 Recovered values and different oracle boundaries

Statement.

CD asks value_from_residual for the loss at its cached residual. Its finite-final- objective refusal and the loss's subnormal error bound are specified in CD-09. A recovered finite loss can still give a nonfinite loss-plus-penalty sum. The exceptional recovery is conditional on ProximalBase's range and margin statements; near the overflow threshold a result can remain Inf.

PGD and APG obtain start/trial values from ProximalBase. PGD's start and gradient refreshes use value_and_gradient_from_predictor!, as does APG's stationarity check; the other value-only evaluations use value_from_predictor. When the guide's conditions hold, these oracles can recover a finite value even if the sum of squares would overflow in T. This does not promise a finite gradient.

In particular, if subtraction z-y overflows although the entries of z and y are finite, value_from_predictor can recover the value while value_and_gradient_from_predictor! returns a nonfinite value and nonfinite gradient entries. At such a start PGD refuses a nonfinite combined-oracle value with ArgumentError("start from a point where f is finite"), even if the value-only oracle would be finite. APG can accept the recovered value-only start, then fail its first step with ErrorException("the gradient step did not produce a finite objective"). The recorded example, unchanged-start observations and issue tracking are in ORACLE-02; the general restoration boundaries are in RES-03.

Code-derived possibility, not a reproduced guarantee. PGD evaluates a trial with value_from_predictor and refreshes the gradient at an accepted trial with value_and_gradient_from_predictor!. In a branch that accepts from function values, a finite-valued accepted iterate could therefore hold a nonfinite gradient. The baseline notes explicitly state that this accepted-iterate case was not reproduced and has no test. If another step is attempted, x-gradient/L is nonfinite for every finite L; for a penalty whose prox preserves nonfinite entries (such as NormL1), trial values are nonfinite. The expected code path raises L until next_curvature cannot increase it finitely and raises ErrorException("line search failed: the curvature estimate cannot increase finitely"), after which solve! restores the last accepted iterate and rethrows. maxiter or another stop can end the solve first. The separate probe in ORACLE-02 rejected its nonfinite-gradient trial and raised a different line-search error; it does not establish this possibility for every setting.

Applies to. Least-squares values, PGD/APG gradient refreshes and extreme-range oracle inputs.

Code. src/solvers/image_models.jl: oracle calls; src/line_searches/backtracking.jl: finite-curvature retry; src/solve.jl: last-accepted-point restoration.

Tests. Existing range coverage is in test/core_regressions.jl and test/initial_curvature.jl; the historical nonfinite-gradient accepted-iterate possibility has no reproducing test, as stated above.

FP-03 Products before loss scaling

Statement.

CD forms the coordinate correlation r' * X_k in the working type before applying the loss scale. PGD/APG form the rounded product X' * d, with d = z-y and z = X*x, then multiply by the scale. If such a product underflows, overflows or cancels before scaling, the solvers do not recover it, even if the exact scaled gradient would lie in range.

A stopping test that reads the same rounded product can be met at a nonminimizer. The λmax-based PGD/APG stationarity checks can read a computed zero gradient and a computed zero lambda_max even where the exact products are nonzero. The coordinatewise check of STOP-21 can read a computed zero gradient entry where the exact product is not zero; its score is then -cᵢ at a zero coefficient and |λ2 xᵢ + cᵢ sign xᵢ|, which can be positive, elsewhere. Its threshold is formed from Φ, the stored loss value plus the ridge contribution (λ2/2)‖x‖²: a computed Φ = 0 refuses each coordinate outside its exact zero scales (a column of stored ±0 entries with no ridge term, and x = y = 0), while a positive ridge contribution can give a normal threshold at a zero stored loss, and a design whose columns are all of the first kind can pass at a nontrivial point of zero loss when every computed score is at most 0. In CD, a zero computed correlation can make a full pass change nothing and satisfy the change test. ORACLE-03 preserves executable underflow and cancellation examples, their high-precision residuals and their objective errors. Keep data and scales in a range where X' * r is representable, and assess important results using independently computed higher-precision products.

The line-search failure mode involving a subnormal or zero computed squared displacement, including its zero-product guard and unhandled nonzero-product case, is specified once in LS-08. It is another limit of working-precision products, not a general promise of convergence at small scales.

Applies to. CD coordinate correlations and PGD/APG gradients and stationarity products.

Code. src/cd/leastsquares.jl: column correlations; src/solvers/image_models.jl: gradient model; src/utils.jl: _lambda_max!, _filled_scales!; src/line_searches/backtracking.jl: displacement products.

FP-04 Equivalent formulations and curvature scaling

Statement.

The PGD/APG initial curvature, its normal-number floor and the relation of its Frobenius bound to the spectral curvature are defined in PGD-02.

An equivalent formulation can change the arithmetic range. For a NormL1 penalty, substituting x = s*u with s > 0 replaces X by s*X and lambda by s*lambda, multiplies curvature by s^2, and maps a start x0 to x0/s. By contrast, replacing X by c*X, y by c*y, and the loss scale alpha by alpha/c^2, while retaining the penalty, preserves the objective and curvature; it preserves the initial curvature up to rounding. These mathematical transformations do not guarantee recovery of an arbitrary extreme-scale solve. ORACLE-01 records the observed examples, solver counts and Julia versions. Overflow of a trial's squared step norm and its scaled handling are specified in LS-06.

Applies to. Least-squares PGD/APG initialization and equivalent lasso rescalings.

Code. src/solvers/image_models.jl: _initial_curvature, _frobenius_upper; src/solvers/proximal_gradient_descent.jl; src/solvers/accelerated_proximal_gradient_descent.jl; src/line_searches/backtracking.jl.

Tests. test/initial_curvature.jl and test/core_regressions.jl; historical illustrative outcomes are in ORACLE-01.

FP-05 The widened ℓ1 decision, the budgets and the full check of the ℓ1-ball route

Statement.

On the route of IN-12 the coefficients, the trials and ProximalBase's projection stay in T. The decision, the budgets, and the full check's witness, residual, gradient, support gap, noise terms and comparisons below are formed in Float64; the full check's mapping point, its division by the curvature and its projection are formed in T, and the differences from the projection, their maximum and its scaling by the curvature are formed in Float64. Write ρ = Float64(r) for the radius, a = Float64(s) for the loss scale as supplied (1.0 for a raw loss), u = eps(Float64)/2 and γ(k) = k u / (1 - k u). The norm ‖v‖₂ of an array is computed as m * sqrt(sum(abs2(Float64(vᵢ) / m))) with m = maxᵢ |Float64(vᵢ)|, and is m when m is zero or not finite.

Decision. For x with p entries and the cap c, the widened mass is σ = sum(a -> abs(Float64(a)), x), the margin m = σ - ρ and the noise ν = γ(p+2) max(σ, ρ). The code is :pass when m - c < -ν, :over when m - c > ν, and :unresolved otherwise. A margin that is not finite gives :unresolved, with noise NaN. The decision classifies no membership in the ball.

Budgets, once per solve, from C = maxⱼ ‖Xⱼ‖₂ over the columns, F = ‖X‖₂ over all entries, ‖y‖₂, M = √a C, ŷ = √a ‖y‖₂ and the scale S = max(ŷ, √a C ρ)² / 2:

  • the gap budget G = objective_rtol * S / 2;
  • the cap c = min(ρ, coefficient_atol + coefficient_rtol * ρ, G / (M ŷ + 1.5 M² ρ)), the last term being Inf when its denominator is 0;
  • the curvature κ = a C², or 1.0 when C = 0;
  • the mapping budget H = mapping_rtol * √a C * max(ŷ, √a C ρ).

Full check at x, with the decision of x cached by its trial (IN-12). A cached code other than :pass fails the check at once, with neither a gradient call nor a prox call; the check still records that it ran. Otherwise:

  1. The witness w = φ * Float64.(x), with φ = 1 when σ + ν < ρ and φ = min(1, (ρ - ν) / σ) otherwise, from the cached σ and ν.
  2. The residual e = X w - y and the gradient g = a * (Xᵀ e) in Float64: for Float64 data by BLAS products, for Float32 data by column loops over Float64(Xᵢⱼ). Let δ = maxⱼ |gⱼ| and ζ = a * (γ(p+2) (F ‖w‖₂ + ‖y‖₂) + γ(n+2) ‖e‖₂).
  3. The support gap max(0, ρ - sum(abs, w)) δ + Σⱼ |wⱼ| (δ ± gⱼ), with -gⱼ where the sign bit of wⱼ is set and +gⱼ otherwise, and its noise 2ρ C ζ + γ(2p+12) (2ρ δ) + γ(p+2) ρ δ.
  4. With L = T(κ): the trial T.(w) .- T.(g) ./ L in T, its projection q in T by ProximalBase's prox! for the solve's private IndBallL1(r) at step one(T)/L, the mapping Float64(L) * maxⱼ |Float64(T(wⱼ)) - Float64(qⱼ)| and its noise C ζ + Float64(eps(T)) (Float64(L) maxⱼ |wⱼ| + δ).
  5. Each noise then gains the underflow allowance below. The check passes when the support gap, the mapping and both noises are finite, support gap + its noise ≤ G and mapping + its noise ≤ H.

The check tests its intermediates in groups. After item 2 has evaluated, and counted, the gradient, it tests the entries of w, e and g together; then the entries of the trial, before its projection, which therefore never sees a nonfinite input; then those of q. A nonfinite entry in any group fails the check, and so does a nonfinite support gap, mapping or noise in item 5. An infinite product in a selected term of the underflow allowance (below) makes that allowance Inf and fails the check; a min with an independent finite cap can replace an infinite inner product, which is safe because the cap is independently an upper bound for that error contribution. The guarded vectors are w, e, g, the mapping's trial point and q; the final values are the support gap, the mapping and their two noises. The check counts the gradient call of item 2 and the prox call of item 4 only when it evaluates them: one each when it reaches the projection, one gradient call and no prox call when it fails between them, and neither when the cached code is not :pass.

The noises of items 3 and 4 are model estimates of the rounding of these sums and products, without directed rounding; they are not interval enclosures. The decision compares the computed margin with computed allowances, and a passing check compares computed quantities with computed budgets: neither is an exact-arithmetic certificate, and a margin within the cap does not bound the distance to the exact constrained minimizer. Apart from the underflow allowance, the arithmetic formulas and the reduction order, for comparable finite inputs, are those of the research reference B1 (norm2_64, budgets, norm_decision without its membership class, numerical_witness!, stop_scores!) and of the B0 helpers it imports (gamma64, residual_score64!, estimate_gap).

Underflow allowance. Under gradual underflow a nonzero product can round to a subnormal number or to zero, and the computed gap and mapping can then be zero at a point that is not stationary. The gate of IN-12 refuses a design with a nonzero column whose curvature (in Float64 and in T), gap budget or mapping budget is not a normal positive number; the allowance adds, to the noises of items 3 and 4, model bounds for the absolute underflow errors of the operations listed below. Besides these, PGD's step raises at an accepted nonzero step whose squared norm is computed as zero (IN-12); these are the route-specific provisions for underflow, beside the shared machinery of the engine (for APG, the scaled norm and the line-search bound of APG-06). With subnormals flushed to zero on the solving thread, the decisions are unspecified, as in FP-01.

The model, per precision P of the operation, for finite operands and a finite result: each product, quotient and fused multiply-add of the check obeys fl(a ∘ b) = (a ∘ b)(1 + δ) + ε with |ε| ≤ min(η_P, |a ∘ b|) and ε = 0 when a factor is an exact zero, where η_P is the least positive subnormal number of P; in Float64 (the residual, the gradient, the support gap, the mapping's differences and its final product), |δ| ≤ u and η_P = η = nextfloat(0.0); in T (the conversions of w and g, the division by L and the projection), |δ| ≤ eps(T)/2 and η_P = η_T = nextfloat(zero(T)). An overflow is outside the model. The fail-closed rules below cover an overflow in the guarded quantities (the mapping's trial point, the returned projection q, the other guarded vectors and the final values) and in the selected allowance terms, with the independent-cap exception at their end; they make no statement about the intermediates of ProximalBase's projection, which can recover internally, for example from an overflowing unscaled norm sum, and return a finite projection that the check then tests. Additions and subtractions add no absolute error. The relative noises of items 3 and 4 are meant to account for the δ; each term below bounds, under this model, the ε of the operations it names, carried to the gap or the mapping. A term's magnitudes are computed from the operands that the check actually uses (the computed w, r, g and trial point); an error already present in an operand is carried by the earlier term that bounds it, such as E_g for the gradient, and not by the term's magnitude. With n × p the design, α the loss scale, the witness w, the computed residual r, δ = ‖g‖∞ of the computed gradient, L = T(κ), t the mapping's trial point in T, m₀ the computed maxⱼ |Float64(T(wⱼ)) - Float64(qⱼ)| and the computed mapping m = fl(Float64(L) m₀):

  1. The residual: A_r = min(n p η, √n C ‖w‖₁), from the n p products Xᵢⱼ wⱼ.
  2. The transposed product, per coordinate: A_s = min(n η, C ‖r‖₂), plus the carried residual error C A_r.
  3. The gradient error per coordinate: E_g = α (C A_r + A_s) + min(η, 2α C ‖r‖₂).
  4. The support gap: 2 max(ρ, ‖w‖₁) E_g + min((p + 1) η, δ (ρ + 2 ‖w‖₁)) + min(p η, ‖w‖₁) δ.
  5. The mapping L (v - P(v - g/L)) at v = T(w). The exact ℓ1-ball projection P(z)ᵢ = sign(zᵢ) max(|zᵢ| - τ(z), 0) moves its level τ by at most ‖Δz‖∞, so it is 2-Lipschitz in the ∞-norm (for r = 3, z = [3, 3, 3] and z' = [3.125, 2.875, 2.875] the ratio is 4/3), and an error of the projection input enters with the factor 2: the gradient error adds 2 E_g; for Float32 data the conversion of w adds 3L min(η_T, ‖w‖∞) and that of g adds 2 min(η_T, δ); the division of the converted operand T(g) by L loses at most min(η_T, |T(g)| / L) in its absolute component, besides its relative rounding. The code's term before the inflation is 2 min(L η_T, δ), with δ the Float64 gradient magnitude rather than |T(g)|; FP-05's factor of two for computed magnitudes covers the difference for a finite conversion under gradual underflow. The check forms it without the quotient δ / L, which can round to zero: when the L η_T arm wins it uses the stored double of L η_T and its product by 2, and when δ wins it uses _mulup(2.0, δ). The computed projection in T enters through its own assumption below, not through this factor 2: it adds L min(p η_T, ‖t‖∞). The final product by L adds 0 when m₀ = 0, where the product is exact, and η when m₀ > 0, also when m rounds to zero (the check evaluates this as min(η, m) when m > 0).

The gap allowance is 4 × (term 4) and the mapping allowance 4 × (term 5): a factor 2 for the growth (1 + u)^k ≤ 2 through at most k = n + p + 2 later Float64 roundings, which needs (n + p + 2) eps(Float64) ≤ 1, times 2 for the rounding in forming the magnitudes (the norms, sums and products of the computed operands above) in place of their exact values. The factor 2 cannot restore a magnitude that is zero although the corresponding exact value is positive; the division and final-product terms are formed so that their magnitudes are not such rounded results. That condition gives (1 + u)^k ≤ exp(1/2), which leaves room for the at most four roundings in T of each trial entry (two conversions, the division and the subtraction), (1 + eps(T)/2)^4 < 2 / exp(1/2); the projection in T enters only through the assumption below. The products of the allowance's terms are formed by _mulup, which for finite nonnegative factors returns an upper bound of their exact product: 0 when a factor is 0, otherwise fl(a b)(1 + 4 eps) + η, which is Inf when the product overflows. _mulup returns Inf when a factor is not finite, whatever the other factor: an overflowed factor, such as 2α for α above floatmax(Float64) / 2, carries no bound, and the other factor can be a computed zero whose exact value is positive, so an exact-zero rule could drop a positive term. The remaining operations of the allowance (sums, min, max and the products by 2, 3 and 4) are evaluated in round-to-nearest without that inflation. When (n + p + 2) eps(Float64) > 1 both allowances are Inf; an infinite product in a term that its min selects makes that term, and so its allowance, Inf, and a NaN mapping gives a NaN allowance; in each case the check fails. These bound and fail-closed statements concern the values the check tests: the entries of the guarded vectors w, e, g, the trial and q, and the four final values (the support gap, the mapping and their noises). An overflowed _mulup result inside a min whose other arm is an independent finite cap, one that is a bound by itself, leaves that cap and the check can pass: the cap is independently an upper bound for that error contribution. For example, with a zero start and response, X = 1e-100, α = 1e308 and r = 1e-55, 2α = Inf, the scaling-error arm is clipped to η and the exact gradient is zero.

The projection's own term assumes that ProximalBase's ℓ1 projection at the pinned revision bc9e888d (src/functions/indballl1.jl), which soft-thresholds at a level formed from sums, power-of-two scalings and divisions, has an absolute underflow error of at most p η_T per output entry beyond the relative rounding of the eps(T) term; this rests on reading the dependency's source. B1's relative mapping noise of item 4 carries the gradient's relative error with the factor 1 and is kept for parity with B1; this argument covers only the absolute terms. Not covered: threads that flush subnormals; the arithmetic of the trials and the line search (LS-02); and any projection error beyond that assumption.

Applies to. The ℓ1-ball route of IN-12, Float32 and Float64.

Code. src/solvers/l1ball.jl: _norm2_64, _l1_mass64, _gamma64, _l1_noise, _l1_decision, _l1_budgets, _l1_witness!, _residual_score64!, _l1_support_gap, _mulup, _l1_underflow_allowance, _l1_underflow_terms, _stationary!.

For the scaled case 1e300 * LeastSquaresLoss([0.0], reshape([1e-155], 1, 1)), x = [1e-15], r = 1e-14, which the gate admits and whose computed gradient is exactly zero, the full check fails, neither method reports converged, and the returned point misses the budgets in MPFR. For a Float32 case with a tolerance override whose mapping trial overflows in its first entry (projecting it, as the check did before this guard, gives a finite vertex; the guarded check skips that projection), neither method reports converged, the MPFR mapping at the returned point exceeds its budget, and a direct call of the check fails with one gradient call and no prox call. Two Float64 cases with mapping_rtol = 1e-180 and start 0 cover the division term of term 5, where the computed δ / L is zero: one whose curvature exceeds floatmax(Float64) / 2, so that 2L overflows (X = 1e154, y = 1e-171, r = 1e-150, APG with β = 1 / 10^(308.1 / 60)), and one with L ≈ 1e306, so that 2L is finite (X = 1e153, y = 1e-172, r = 1e-150, APG with β = 1 / 10^(306.1 / 60)), where the term 2L min(η_T, δ / L) evaluated with the quotient was zero and the check passed. In each, neither method reports converged, the MPFR mapping at the returned point exceeds its budget, and a direct call of the check fails after one gradient call and one prox call. A zero design with a zero response, whose stopping budgets (gap and mapping) are zero, passes the check at the exactly stationary start 0 and converges at the first iteration, so the final-product term adds nothing when m₀ = 0. It also checks one expansion example of the projection in term 5, with ProximalBase's projection in both precisions.
