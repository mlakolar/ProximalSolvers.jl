Proximal Newton for sparse logistic regression

ProxNewton solves the sparse logistic regression of IN-09 by proximal Newton steps. Each step minimizes a weighted least-squares model of the loss with the scalar coordinate step of CD-01 and then searches along the result on the true objective. The input gate is that of IN-09; the clauses below specify the solver, its models, budgets, search, result and failure boundary.

PN-01 Inputs, result and stop reasons

Statement.

ProxNewton(; inner, maxiter = 100, tol = 1e-7) is passed to solve! with ProximalBase's raw LogisticLoss(y, X), a NormL1(lambda) or NormL1(lambda, w) penalty and a dense Vector{Float64} start. It minimizes the summed logistic objective and penalty in IN-09, starting at the supplied coefficients. A column of ones with penalty weight zero supplies an unpenalized intercept. The level lambda is converted to Float64 once, and the penalty weights are used as supplied; ProximalBase's kkt_violation receives that converted penalty.

The inputs and the order of refusals are those of IN-09, with ProxNewton in the place of ProximalGradientDescent, except that the first check is the loss: any loss other than a raw LogisticLoss or one Scaled wrapper of it is refused with an ArgumentError that names ProxNewton and the least-squares solvers. A Scaled(LogisticLoss) is refused as in IN-09. The refusal of a nonbinary response names ProxNewton. CDSolver, in a call that reaches its logistic refusal, refuses the logistic loss with an ArgumentError that names ProximalGradientDescent, AccProxGradDescent, ProxNewton and LogisticADMM; the precedence of the owner gate is listed in IN-09. Keywords of solve! and positional options after the solver are refused as for the other solvers. All refusals precede any write to the coefficients.

The constructor is validated. It refuses an inner that is not a CDSolver in cyclic order (order = :cyclic), a negative maxiter, and a tol that is not finite and nonnegative or that is positive but rounds to zero in Float64. inner = CDSolver(order = :cyclic, momentum = false, relative = false, tol = 1e-11, maxpasses = 2000) is the default. The fields of inner that PN-03 names are used; its relative, momentum and rng settings are not.

The start must have a finite predictor X * b, a finite objective, a finite gradient and a finite KKT score; otherwise solve! raises an ArgumentError ("start from a point where f is finite", or the gradient or score variant) before any write. The solve is private: the accepted point, the trial points and the models are buffers of the solve, and the caller's coefficients are written once, at a normal return (PN-05).

solve! returns a ProxNewtonResults whose minimizer is the caller's vector, with

FieldMeaning
minimumthe objective F at the returned point, the loss from ProximalBase plus the penalty
kktthe largest ProximalBase KKT score of the original problem at the returned point, from a gradient formed at that point
tolerancethe tol of the solver
iterationsthe accepted outer iterations
passesall coordinate passes of the inner solves, including those of a model whose step the search then rejected
convergedisfinite(kkt) && kkt <= tolerance
stop_reason:converged, :maxiter or :stalled

The accessors minimizer, minimum, iterations and converged read the fields of the same name. At each accepted point, including the start, the solver tests convergence first: kkt <= tol gives :converged, also for maxiter = 0 and with no pass budget, so a stationary start returns with iterations = 0 and passes = 0. Otherwise iterations == maxiter gives :maxiter. Otherwise an iteration is attempted; if no model gives an accepted step the solve ends with :stalled. A stalled or exhausted solve returns the last accepted point with converged = false. No warning is logged for any stop.

Applies to. ProxNewton with a raw LogisticLoss{Float64} and NormL1 on a dense Vector{Float64}.

PN-02 The weighted and the fixed least-squares model

Statement.

At an accepted point with predictor η = X b and observation derivative d = σ(η) - y (from ProximalBase, which evaluates it without cancellation in the saturated tails), a model is a least-squares problem in the step whose gradient at b is the gradient Xᵀd of the loss. There are two models, a weighted one and a fixed one that is conditional on the first (below), and each is solved with the scalar LSModel of CD-01.

Weighted model. Row i has the root s = q / (1 + q²) with q = exp(-|ηᵢ| / 2), which is √(σ(ηᵢ)σ(-ηᵢ)) in exact arithmetic. The design is A[i, :] = s X[i, :] and the response is r[i] = s ηᵢ - dᵢ / s, so that, in exact arithmetic, Aᵀ(A b - r) = Xᵀd and AᵀA = Xᵀ diag(s²) X, with the loss scale 1. A row is replaced when |dᵢ| > s 2²⁶ (2²⁶ = 1/√eps(Float64); the comparison is on the product s 2²⁶, which cannot overflow, not on the ratio dᵢ/s): it takes the fixed row root 1/2, which keeps its tangent (r[i] = ηᵢ/2 - 2dᵢ) and gives it the curvature 1/4 of the global bound. Only the unsafe row is replaced. A row whose root and derivative are both zero is a zero row (s = 0 and r[i] = 0). Examples for yᵢ = 0: η = -150 has s = e⁻⁷⁵ and a derivative of order e⁻¹⁵⁰, a small ratio, so the row keeps its small curvature; η = 800 has d = 1, the ratio exceeds the bound, and the row takes s = 1/2; η = -2000 gives s = 0 and d = 0, a zero row. A saturated row on the side of its label keeps its small root even where the square of that root underflows.

Fixed model (conditional). It is formed and solved in an iteration only when the weighted model is unavailable or gives no accepted step (PN-03 for its budget). It is the LSModel with loss scale 1/4, the borrowed X and the response η - 4d. In exact arithmetic its gradient at b is Xᵀd and its Hessian is XᵀX/4, which bounds that of the loss, so that f(b + Δ) ≤ f(b) + ∇f(b)ᵀΔ + ‖XΔ‖²/8 for every Δ.

A model whose design or response has a nonfinite entry is unavailable for that iteration. The adaptive model is also unavailable when a nonzero column has every squared entry round to zero in Float64. The construction checks the square of the largest magnitude in the column. Since rounded nonnegative squares are monotone, a zero result for a positive maximum means every square rounded to zero, which CD-04 would refuse. For example, an adaptive column with one entry exp(-400) and all other entries zero triggers this fallback. An all-zero column remains eligible under the flat-column step in CD-01.

The weighted model keeps one n × p design with vectors of length n and p. The fixed model borrows X. The inner solver refreshes the residual and curvature caches before its first run for a model, from the observation derivative d and not from the stored coefficients, and carries that state through refinements (the cache refresh of PN-03). The fixed model follows an unavailable weighted model or one that gives no accepted step, within the remaining pass budget. Both forms use coordinate operations on the model's design columns.

Applies to. ProxNewton.

PN-03 The inner solve: cyclic coordinate descent, tolerances and the pass budget

Statement.

Each inner solve runs the scalar coordinate step of CD-01 in cyclic order, without momentum, on a private SparseIterate loaded from the accepted point. The loader zeroes and drops the stored entries and then writes the nonzero entries in index order, so the solve and its order of passes depend on the accepted point alone. The solve uses the model's LSModel and the penalty with its level converted to Float64; it calls the private pass loop of CD, not the public solve! of CDSolver, so it repeats none of its validation and logs none of its warnings.

Tolerances. The change test is absolute, in the units of the coefficients: a run stops after a full pass in which every coordinate changed by less than the threshold δ. The first run of each model in an iteration has δ = inner.tol; 0 turns the test off, so that each run spends all of its passes. The solver's tol is a different quantity: an absolute bound on the largest KKT score of the original problem, in the units of the gradient of the summed loss (PN-05). inner.relative is not used.

Budget. inner.maxpasses = B is the budget of coordinate passes of one outer iteration, shared by the weighted model, its refinements and the fixed model. For B > 1 the weighted model, whose runs and refinements together may use B - ⌈B/4⌉ passes, leaves the rest for the fixed model, which may use whatever the weighted model did not spend; for B = 1 the weighted model has the single pass. A model with no passes left makes no run and gives no direction. With B = 0 no direction exists, and the iteration is stalled unless the point is already stationary.

Cache refresh. Before the first run of a model, the private residual of the LSModel is the model response minus its weighted predictor at the accepted point, which in exact arithmetic is -dᵢ/sᵢ for row i of the weighted model (0 for a zero root sᵢ = 0, whose represented derivative is zero, so no division is made) and -4dᵢ for the fixed model, and the curvature cache is cleared. The solver sets these values from the observation derivative d of PN-02, and does not subtract the images of the stored coefficients from the response (the initialize! of CD-01): where those images are large and cancel, a small response can be rounded away and the cache loses a representable gradient; CD-05 describes this mechanism for the CD initializer. For example, at η = 0 with y = 0, d = 1/2 and s = 1/2, the residual is -1 for the weighted model and -2 for the fixed model, and with y = 1 the signs reverse. Refinements continue with the maintained residual. The solve from b = (2⁶⁰, -2⁶⁰, 0) with X = [1 1 -1; 1 1 1], y = (0, 1) and the penalty 0.5 on the third coefficient only has the predictor zero and a score of 1/2; seeded this way both models move the third coefficient to 1, and a residual formed from the stored images would be zero. This initialization belongs to ProxNewton's inner solve only: CDSolver keeps its own, and ProximalGradientDescent has no such cache.

Forcing target and refinement. At an accepted point with score ρ = kkt, a run meets the target when the surrogate KKT score at its candidate is at most max(0.1 tol, min(1/4, √ρ) ρ). The surrogate gradient is the original gradient plus the curvature action of the model on the displacement, ∇f(b) + scale · Mᵀ(M (cand - b)) with M = A and scale = 1 for the weighted model and M = X and scale = 1/4 for the fixed model. The calculation is independent of the residual maintained by coordinate descent. The score is ProximalBase's point-aware kkt_violation of that gradient at the candidate; it is not the score of the original problem, which PN-04 takes from the logistic gradient at a trial. While the target is missed, the threshold δ is divided by ten and the run continues from the candidate, at most four times and within the allowance. The candidate is the point of the last run whether or not the target was met.

A finite candidate with a negative composite slope (PN-04) goes to the search even if the target was missed; the target is a measure of the inner accuracy, not a permission. A candidate equal to the accepted point has slope zero and gives no direction. Every pass of every run is counted in passes, also where the search rejects the result.

Applies to. ProxNewton.

PN-04 The composite slope and the objective search

Statement.

For the accepted point b, its gradient G, the candidate u and the coefficients cⱼ, the composite slope is the linearized loss change plus the full penalty change from b to u, G·(u - b) + Σⱼ cⱼ(|uⱼ| - |bⱼ|), formed by coordinate before the terms are added. In exact arithmetic it is an upper bound on the directional derivative of F along u - b, with equality unless a coordinate changes sign; for bⱼ = 1 and uⱼ = -2 its penalty term is +cⱼ, while the penalty's directional derivative is -3cⱼ. The search uses it as the Armijo quantity below. Where bⱼ and uⱼ have the same sign (zero counts for either), the term is (Gⱼ + cⱼ sign) (uⱼ - bⱼ) with the common sign; where they differ it is split at zero, (Gⱼ + sign(uⱼ) cⱼ) uⱼ - (Gⱼ + sign(bⱼ) cⱼ) bⱼ. For bⱼ = 1, uⱼ = 2 and cⱼ = λ the term is (Gⱼ + λ)(2 - 1); for bⱼ = 1 and uⱼ = -2 it is (Gⱼ - λ)(-2) - (Gⱼ + λ)(1). The grouping keeps a coordinate at its subgradient (Gⱼ + cⱼ = 0) exactly at zero slope, which a difference of the two large penalty totals does not. A direction whose slope is not finite and negative is not searched.

The search tries the steps t = 1, 1/2, …, 2⁻³⁹ (at most 40 trials) at b + t (u - b). A trial whose predictor, objective, gradient or score is not finite is ineligible. With actual the difference of the two computed finite objectives and required = 10⁻⁴ t · slope, a trial is accepted when actual < -8 eps(F) and actual ≤ required, F being the objective at the accepted point; or when |actual| ≤ 8 eps(F) and |required| ≤ 8 eps(F), the near-tie band, and the trial's ProximalBase KKT score of the original problem is strictly smaller than that of the accepted point. The second rule uses the KKT score to decide near ties in the computed objective: with F = 100, changes of order -10⁻¹⁵ and scores 4·10⁻⁸ at the accepted point and 2·10⁻⁸ at the trial, the trial is accepted; with equal scores it is rejected. A step that rounds to no displacement is not a success and ends the search, and so does the last trial. The first accepted trial becomes the accepted point with its own objective, gradient and score, computed at that point. The near-tie rule can accept an increase of at most 8 eps(F) in the computed objective.

If the weighted model gives no accepted step, the fixed model is tried within the remaining budget (PN-03); if it gives none either, the solve is :stalled.

Applies to. ProxNewton.

PN-05 Convergence, mutation and failure boundary

Statement.

converged = true reports a computed result: the largest ProximalBase KKT score of the original problem at the returned point, max(0, |Gⱼ| - cⱼ) at a zero coefficient and |Gⱼ + cⱼ sign(bⱼ)| elsewhere, with G a gradient formed at that point by ProximalBase's fused logistic oracle, is finite and at most tol. It is not an exact-arithmetic certificate, it does not bound the error of the coefficients, and the gradient it uses is rounded. The scale of the residual is that of the gradient of the summed loss; a mean-scale level is passed as n * λ (IN-09). The problem need not have a minimizer (IN-09, Unattained minima): for a one-class sample with an unpenalized intercept the score can fall below tol at a finite point and converged = true can be reported. The solver does not detect that case.

The solve computes on private buffers and writes the caller's coefficients once, with the accepted point, at a normal return (converged, :maxiter or :stalled). An exception from the inner solve or the logistic oracle propagates and leaves the caller's coefficients unchanged; so does a refusal. The borrowed X, y, the penalty weights and the scratch of the loss are never written. The adaptive column check and fallback are specified in PN-02. An error from CD-04 that reaches the caller, such as a curvature that underflows in the fixed model or overflows in a coordinate step, propagates as an ArgumentError. A trial with a nonfinite quantity is ineligible under PN-04.

Applies to. ProxNewton; the result and mutation rules of RES-01 and RES-03 refer here for it.
