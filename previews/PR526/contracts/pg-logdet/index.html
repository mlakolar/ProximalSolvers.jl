Matrix proximal gradient for the graphical lasso

PGD-06 Matrix inputs and ownership

Statement. solve!(Θ, f, g, method::ProximalGradientDescent, options) solves the full-matrix objective in ADM-04 with ProximalBase f = LogDetLoss(S) and g = NormL1(λ₀, Λ). The start is a nonempty square Matrix{T} for T ∈ {Float32, Float64}. The stored S and Λ are Matrix{T} of that same size. The three matrices have finite entries and exact symmetry by entrywise equality. Θ must pass Cholesky in T. The loss weight must be one, Λ must be nonnegative, and λ₀ must be finite, nonnegative and exactly representable in T. Diagonal weights are permitted. On a positive-definite Θ, each Θᵢᵢ is positive, so the penalty λ₀Λᵢᵢ|Θᵢᵢ| is linear in the diagonal. Each effective weight Cᵢⱼ = T(λ₀) Λᵢⱼ, diagonal included, must be finite, and so must each sum Sᵢᵢ + Cᵢᵢ evaluated in T. Pairwise alias checks include separately wrapped arrays whose memory overlaps. The solver borrows the inputs for reads and assumes the caller leaves them unchanged while the solve runs.

The method requires BackTracking with Lexact = Inf, and the converted factors satisfy 0 < α ≤ 1 with finite 1/α and 0 < β < 1 in T. Accepted options are maxiter, ftol, xtol, gradtol, time_limit and the three oracle-call limits. Objective increases, tracing, callbacks and print cadence require their defaults. Additional solve keywords, unsupported functions or buffer types, and invalid data or parameters raise ArgumentError; mismatched dimensions and empty or nonsquare starts raise DimensionMismatch. Preflight and start diagnostics run before writes to the caller's matrix.

Applies to. Dense matrix PGD with the graphical-lasso function pair.

Code. src/solvers/logdet_proximal_gradient.jl and the shared array validation primitives in src/solvers/admm.jl.

Tests. test/pgd_graphical_lasso.jl exercises both precisions, invalid methods, options, dimensions, weights, storage, borrowed buffers and starts. It accepts positive diagonal weights. It refuses negative and nonfinite diagonal weights, a diagonal effective weight that overflows in T and a diagonal sum Sᵢᵢ + Cᵢᵢ that overflows in T.

Sources. Approved graphical-lasso PGD scope.

PGD-07 SPD backtracking and rollback

Statement. At the current SPD point Θ, ProximalBase evaluates the logdet gradient G = S - inv(Θ). The solver requires finite entries. It replaces each pair of off-diagonal entries by their mean if their difference is at most 8eps(T) times the largest of one and the magnitudes of the two entries and the corresponding entry of S. Larger skew raises an error. The first search starts at t = 1. Subsequent searches start at min(1, t_last / T(α)).

The solver forms exactly symmetric V = Θ - t G in private storage and calls the ProximalBase prox!(Z, g, V, t) if V is finite. The proposal must be finite, exactly symmetric and pass Cholesky in T before the solver evaluates its smooth value. With D = Z - Θ, it requires both

\[f(Z) \le f(\Theta) + \langle G,D\rangle + \frac{\|D\|_F^2}{2t}, \qquad f(Z)+g(Z) \le f(\Theta)+g(\Theta)\]

with a working-precision allowance on each comparison. The allowance is 16eps(T) times the largest of one and the magnitudes of the two loss values, the linear term and the quadratic term in the first comparison. For the composite comparison it scales with the largest of one and the two composite magnitudes. The term-based scale allows for rounding in the oracle values and arithmetic near equality, including cancellation in the model. The two inner products run over the full matrix. A nonfinite objective or quadratic model fails the comparison.

Each rejection shrinks the step by T(β) if the solve has budget to continue. A search attempts at most 64 trials, with finite positive step and reciprocal required before each trial. Failure to reduce the step, an invalid step or 64 rejections raises an error. ProximalBase evaluates the candidate gradient after its SPD and acceptance checks. The Cholesky factor of the accepted candidate is in the solver's scratch matrix at entry to the KKT diagnostic of PGD-08. That diagnostic overwrites it with the inverse when it takes its uncertain path, and the next trial that reaches its factorization writes it again. A trial can fail before it factors, on a nonfinite argument or a nonfinite or nonsymmetric proposal. The solver commits the candidate only after the gradient and KKT diagnostics succeed. A thrown numerical error or an interrupted search leaves the last committed SPD point in the caller's matrix. The step depends on local comparisons because the logdet gradient has no global Lipschitz constant on the SPD cone.

Applies to. Matrix PGD after preflight in PGD-06.

Code. src/solvers/logdet_proximal_gradient.jl implements the matrix step within _solve_gradient! in src/solve.jl.

Tests. test/pgd_graphical_lasso.jl verifies an indefinite first trial, a nonfinite objective, finite backtracking and bounded failure, invalid reciprocal and rollback after a completed step. It also tests a diagonal threshold that zeroes a proposal's diagonal, which the Cholesky check rejects before the search backtracks to a positive-definite step.

Sources. Approved graphical-lasso PGD iteration.

PGD-08 KKT stopping, limits and results

Statement. Let C = T(λ₀)Λ, aᵢ = Sᵢᵢ + Cᵢᵢ and sᵢ = sqrt(|aᵢ|), with the sum and each root computed in T, each root before any product. PGD-06 requires every aᵢ to be finite. Entry (i, j) has the scale dᵢⱼ = max(1, sᵢ sⱼ), computed in T. At the start and at each committed point the score is R = maxᵢⱼ rᵢⱼ / dᵢⱼ over the upper triangle (Θ, S, Λ and the symmetrized G are exactly symmetric), where rᵢⱼ is the violation of entry (i, j) defined below. The score is relative to each entry's own scale. g_residual is Float64(R), and a nonfinite score raises an error. After a completed step, convergence requires g_residual ≤ g_tol, where g_tol = max(gradtol, 64eps(T)). The check is independent of the trial step and of the iterate and objective change tests. An accepted elementwise unchanged point outside the KKT threshold sets stagnated=true and converged=false.

First pass. With G = S - inv(Θ) symmetrized as in PGD-07, the first-pass violation of entry (i, j), diagonal included, is evaluated in T as rᵢⱼ = |Gᵢⱼ + Cᵢⱼ sign(Θᵢⱼ)| where Θᵢⱼ ≠ 0 and rᵢⱼ = max(|Gᵢⱼ| - Cᵢⱼ, 0) where Θᵢⱼ = 0.

Uncertainty test. This is a heuristic test on computed values, evaluated in T for each entry of the upper triangle. Let c̃ᵢⱼ = Cᵢⱼ if Θᵢⱼ ≠ 0 or 2|Gᵢⱼ| ≥ Cᵢⱼ, and c̃ᵢⱼ = 0 otherwise, and let mᵢⱼ = (|Sᵢⱼ| + |Gᵢⱼ|) + c̃ᵢⱼ. The entry is uncertain if mᵢⱼ T(0.25) > dᵢⱼ. An mᵢⱼ that overflows is uncertain.

Evaluation from the inputs. If some entry is uncertain, W = inv(Θ) is rebuilt in place from the upper Cholesky factor that the SPD check of that point left in the solver's scratch matrix (PGD-07); this adds one inverse computed from an existing factor. The pass then attempts to evaluate the violation of every entry from Sᵢⱼ, Wᵢⱼ and Cᵢⱼ without forming Sᵢⱼ ± Cᵢⱼ. All operations are in T. TwoSum is Knuth's algorithm, an exact decomposition under round-to-nearest with gradual underflow when both of its outputs are finite. Let (h, l) = TwoSum(Sᵢⱼ, -Wᵢⱼ).

  • At a nonzero Θᵢⱼ, let (h2, l2) = TwoSum(h, Cᵢⱼ sign(Θᵢⱼ)). Then rᵢⱼ = |h2 + (l + l2)|.
  • At a zero, let (u, lu) = TwoSum(h, -Cᵢⱼ) and (v, lv) = TwoSum(-h, -Cᵢⱼ). Then rᵢⱼ = max(u + (lu + l), v + (lv - l), 0).

The additions of the recombinations round. Three cases handle nonfinite terms:

  1. If h or l is not finite, the entry takes its first-pass violation.
  2. At a zero, each side is handled separately. If its high term (u or v) is not finite, the side is -Inf and its first-pass value, Gᵢⱼ - Cᵢⱼ or -Gᵢⱼ - Cᵢⱼ, is discarded. If the high term is finite but the recombination is not, the side takes that first-pass value.
  3. At a nonzero Θᵢⱼ, if h2 + (l + l2) is not finite, the entry takes its first-pass violation.

Limits. These are possibilities, not guarantees.

  • Where |Sᵢⱼ| is large relative to dᵢⱼ, the step Θ - tG uses G as ProximalBase rounds it, so a solve can end by stagnation or at a limit with converged=false, as the cancellation runs recorded on the evidence page did.
  • Rounding in the computed inv(Θ) can change the violation and the normalized score, in either pass, and this rule does not bound it.
  • Where a diagonal weight cancels a negative Sᵢᵢ, state the problem as S + Diagonal(C) with zero diagonal weight.

The evidence page also records the comparison with the per-entry scale of STOP-03, the Float32 convergence loss and the timing of the rule.

OptimizationResults.minimizer aliases the caller's matrix. minimum is f(Θ) + g(Θ) there. x_residual is ‖Z - Θ_previous‖_F, and f_residual follows the relative objective change in RES-02. Both are NaN before the first completed step. x_converged and f_converged require convergence and their respective change to be below the tolerance. ftol=0 disables the objective proposal. grad_converged equals converged. f_increased records an actual increase on the last completed step, which can occur within the comparison allowance. The trace is empty and gap=NaN.

iterations counts completed steps, including an accepted unchanged point. backtrack_steps counts attempted proposals, including accepted ones and proposals rejected before the prox. f_calls counts value-only loss calls and the initial combined value/gradient oracle, grad_calls counts gradient-only calls and the same initial combined oracle, and prox_calls counts actual penalty proximal calls. Penalty value evaluations are outside the smooth loss count. The initial diagnostics consume one loss and one gradient call. A zero call limit is unlimited. The solver checks positive call limits and time before the search and after rejections and completed steps. A completed step may set a limit flag even when the retained point meets the KKT threshold. An exit by the iteration, time or call limit returns converged=false. iteration_converged records exhaustion of maxiter, and call_limit_reached records consumption of any positive call budget.

Applies to. Normal matrix PGD returns.

Code. src/solvers/logdet_proximal_gradient.jl supplies the entry scales, the uncertainty test, the two evaluations of the KKT score and the rounding-stall checks. _solve_gradient! in src/solve.jl handles limit exits and constructs OptimizationResults.

Tests. test/pgd_graphical_lasso.jl checks, in Float32 and Float64, the returned points of analytic and seeded problems with zero and positive diagonal weights against BigFloat recomputations of positive definiteness, the primal objective, the entrywise score and a numerical estimate of a dual lower bound. These are those of the problem with the rounded effective coefficients T(λ₀)Λ, which is the original problem for the fixtures whose coefficient products are exact (test/TESTING.md lists the fixtures). It compares the reported score with the recomputed one, for direct PGD and for PGD on the covariance-shifted problem, and checks reported counters, the diagonal score at a start with a positive diagonal weight, the convergence flags at a known fixed point, a rounded Float32 stall outside the threshold and limit precedence. A call limit during a rejected trial checks the retained iterate, the reported objective and the x and f residuals. On its fixtures, test/logdet_kkt_scale.jl checks the reported start score at prescribed points against the definition, the stop rule at the converged solves of its seeded heterogeneous and ordinary problems, two inputs on which the first pass rounds a decisive violation to zero (a cancellation of W against a weight where S is small, with one sign, and a zero entry with an overflowing unused endpoint, with both signs, each in both precisions), the diagonal-weight cancellation cases and the 2^51 numerator through solve! (the Float32 case is an analogue), the direct and covariance-shifted formulations, and a power-of-two rescaling where the unit floor is inactive. It also asserts directly on the scales, the uncertainty test and the evaluation of single entries, and compares the allocation of warmed evaluations at dimensions 12 and 36. Its BigFloat references use 256 bits, and 2048 bits for the two inputs above as a chosen reference precision. Its analytic values are exact-arithmetic references, and its BigFloat scores are reported after conversion to Float64 and compared with an allowance for the rounding of the computed score.

Sources. Approved graphical-lasso PGD stopping rule. Entrywise KKT normalization.
