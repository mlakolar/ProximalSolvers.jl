Two-function consensus ADMM

ADM-01 Inputs, settings and ownership

Statement. solve!(z, f, g, method::ADMMSolver, options=ProximalOptions(); u=nothing) accepts ProximalBase f = SqrNormL2(λ, c) and unweighted g = NormL1(τ); ADM-05 adds the raw least-squares loss. The center c, initial consensus variable z and any explicit scaled dual u must be finite nonempty Vector{T} buffers of equal length, with one common T of Float32 or Float64. The loss level λ must be finite and strictly positive and the penalty level τ finite and nonnegative. Both levels must convert exactly to T. The solver borrows c for reads. It checks each pair among z, c and explicit u. A pair is refused when Base.mightalias reports an alias or the vectors' memory ranges overlap.

ADMMSolver(; ρ=1, α=1, abstol=1e-6, reltol=1e-4, abstol_dual=abstol, adaptive=false) takes a finite positive penalty, relaxation strictly between 0 and 2, and finite nonnegative tolerances. The solver also checks these bounds after converting to T. A strictly positive tolerance must stay positive. The working proximal step inv(ρ) must be finite and strictly positive in T. A positive subnormal penalty or reciprocal is allowed when both pass these checks.

Function, type, scalar, alias, setting and entry checks precede writes to the caller. A LogisticLoss, raw or in a Scaled wrapper, is refused for a z of any type, after the keyword and option checks, with an ArgumentError that names LogisticADMM, the predictor-splitting method of LA-01. Incompatible lengths and empty vectors raise DimensionMismatch. Other preflight refusals raise ArgumentError. The solve runs in private storage. With u=nothing, the initial dual vector is zero. With explicit u, the solve starts from its entries. On normal return, the caller's z and explicit u receive the final iterates, and result.minimizer and result.u refer to those buffers. With u=nothing, result.u holds the solver's dual vector. Reuse the returned z, u and ρ together for a warm start. Copy the buffers to retain the previous result's vectors. During a solve the caller must keep the buffers and problem data stable.

Applies to. ADMMSolver on a dense vector with centered SqrNormL2 and unweighted NormL1. ADM-04 states the inputs for the graphical lasso on a dense matrix, and ADM-05 those for the least-squares lasso with a tall or square design. The settings, ownership and refusal timing above are common to all three.

Code. src/solvers/admm.jl.

Tests. test/admm.jl covers input refusals, initial states, ownership and restart. test/logistic_pgd.jl and test/logistic_admm.jl check the logistic refusal on a vector and a matrix z, with the raw and the scaled loss and a supplied u that is left unchanged.

Sources. Issue #433 and archived ADMM.

ADM-02 Iteration, diagnostics and termination

Statement. For each iteration with previous consensus variable z₀ and scaled dual u₀, compute

\[x = \operatorname{prox}_{f/\rho}(z_0-u_0),\qquad \hat x = \alpha x+(1-\alpha)z_0,\qquad z = \operatorname{prox}_{g/\rho}(\hat x+u_0),\qquad u = (\hat x+u_0)-z.\]

Use ProximalBase prox! with step inv(ρ) for each proximal update. For α=1, use x for x̂. For α<1, form z₀ + α(x-z₀) in working precision. For α>1, form x + (α-1)(x-z₀). With N = length(z), the working-precision diagnostics are

\[r_{\rm norm}=\|x-z\|_2,\quad s_{\rm norm}=\rho\|z-z_0\|_2,\quad \varepsilon_{\rm pri}=\sqrt{N}\,{\tt abstol}+{\tt reltol}\max(\|x\|_2,\|z\|_2),\quad \varepsilon_{\rm dual}=\sqrt{N}\,{\tt abstol\_dual}+{\tt reltol}\,\rho\|u\|_2.\]

When reltol is zero, omit the relative terms. A residual stop requires both finite residuals to be at most their respective finite tolerances. When both computed residuals are zero, also check each component's native stationarity violation at z. For qᵢ = λ(zᵢ-cᵢ), the score is abs(qᵢ + τ*sign(zᵢ)) at a nonzero zᵢ and max(abs(qᵢ)-τ, 0) at zero. If a finite score exceeds eps_dual, stop with status=:stagnated and converged=false. A nonfinite score is a numerical failure. This guard addresses a rounded zero-residual point at which the two proximal updates have lost resolution. A successful status=:converged reports the implemented tests in working precision. The tutorial verifies its fit independently from the observations.

The supported ProximalOptions settings are maxiter, prox_calls_limit and time_limit. Before beginning an iteration, check the iteration cap, space for two proximal calls in the remaining call budget, and elapsed time, in that order. A zero call budget denotes unlimited calls. Time is measured from the end of preflight and checked before each step, so a step may run past its limit. A nonpositive time limit permits zero iterations. A completed step's convergence or stagnation takes precedence over reaching a limit. Nondefault values for the other stored options are refused. The shared compatibility-gap settings retain their constructor rules in PGD-05.

On normal return, iterations counts complete iterations and prox_calls is twice that count. minimum is f(z)+g(z) evaluated once after termination. The ProximalSolvers.f_calls(result) and ProximalSolvers.grad_calls(result) accessors report one evaluation of f(z) and zero gradient oracle calls. The four residual and tolerance fields describe the last completed iteration at the returned ρ. With zero iterations, they are NaN and converged=false. Limits return :iteration_limit, :prox_calls_limit or :time_limit in status. A numerical failure, including nonfinite proximal arguments or outputs, updated dual entries, residuals, tolerances or the final objective, raises an error before committing either caller buffer.

Applies to. Every ADMMSolver solve and the ADMMResults it returns. For the graphical lasso on a dense matrix, ADM-04 replaces the stationarity score, requires a stored α equal to 1 and adds the symmetry and Cholesky rules. For the least-squares lasso, ADM-05 replaces only the stationarity score.

Code. src/solvers/admm.jl.

Tests. test/admm.jl compares with raw objective and KKT references and checks recurrence, diagnostics, termination, counts and numerical failures.

Sources. Issue #433 and the phase-0 audit.

ADM-03 Adaptive penalty

Statement. With adaptive=true, use the previous iteration's residuals and tolerances to propose a penalty change before the next iteration. Compare the scores r_norm/eps_pri and s_norm/eps_dual. The score is zero for zero residual, including 0/0, and infinite for positive residual and zero tolerance. Two infinite scores tie. A primal score greater than ten times the dual score proposes doubling ρ. A dual score greater than ten times the primal score proposes halving it. The finite scores are compared with scaling to avoid overflow or underflow in the individual divisions. Take at most 50 changes.

For a candidate multiplier m, rescale the dual as u/m, preserving the unscaled dual ρ*u up to working-precision rounding. Skip a candidate if its penalty or reciprocal is nonfinite or zero, the penalty cannot be scaled by m exactly, or any rescaled dual entry would become nonfinite or round from nonzero to zero. Updates occur between iterations. The reported ρ was used for the final completed iteration and matches both the diagnostics and the returned scaled dual.

Applies to. ADMMSolver(adaptive=true) on a dense vector, including the least-squares lasso of ADM-05. ADM-04 refuses it for a matrix.

Code. src/solvers/admm.jl.

Tests. test/admm.jl checks change directions, scaled dual values, zero tolerances, range limits and agreement at independently measured accuracy.

Sources. Issue #433 and archived ADMM.

ADM-04 Graphical lasso on dense matrices

Statement. With a dense Matrix{T} start Θ, where T is Float32 or Float64, solve!(Θ, f, g, method::ADMMSolver, options=ProximalOptions(); u=nothing) applies the iteration below to the problem

\[\min_{\Theta=\Theta^\top\succ0}\; P(\Theta)=-\log\det\Theta+\operatorname{tr}(S\Theta)+\lambda_0\sum_{i,j}\Lambda_{ij}|\Theta_{ij}|,\]

with ProximalBase f = LogDetLoss(S) and g = NormL1(λ₀, Λ), and stops by the residual tests, limits and working-precision exits of ADM-02. The penalty counts each off-diagonal pair twice and each diagonal entry once. Positive definiteness gives Θ[i,i] > 0, so the diagonal penalty is linear in those entries. LogDetLoss must have weight one. When S is a sample covariance, a normalization such as 1/n is part of S.

After the entry checks of solve! (keywords, options, and the storage and element type of the start), the matrix path checks, in this order: f is a LogDetLoss{T} with weight w == 1; g is a NormL1 whose stored weights g.w are a Matrix{T}; Θ is nonempty and square; size(S) == size(Θ) == size(Λ); the α stored in method, after the ADMMSolver constructor's conversion, equals 1, and method.adaptive == false; the level λ₀ is finite, nonnegative and exactly representable in T; the entries of Θ, S and Λ are finite; Λ is nonnegative, including its diagonal, and exactly symmetric (Λ[i,j] == Λ[j,i] for all i, j); S and Θ are exactly symmetric; an explicit u is a Matrix{T} of the same size with finite, exactly symmetric entries; no pair among Θ, u, S and Λ aliases, by Base.mightalias or overlapping memory ranges; the ADM-01 settings hold in T, with a finite positive inv(ρ); and a Cholesky factorization of a private copy of Θ succeeds in T. These checks precede any write to Θ or u. The first failing check determines the exception: DimensionMismatch for an empty or non-square Θ, a size mismatch or a wrong-size u, and ArgumentError for the others. The solver does not average or symmetrize its inputs. With a matrix start, other function pairs, mixed element types, a stored α other than 1 and adaptive=true are refused. The entry check refuses start types other than the dense vectors of ADM-01 and these dense matrices.

Ownership follows the protocol of ADM-01 with matrices in place of vectors. The solve runs in private storage. With u=nothing, the initial scaled dual is the zero matrix; with explicit u, the solve starts from its entries. On normal return, the caller's Θ and explicit u receive the final iterates, and result.minimizer and result.u refer to those buffers. With u=nothing, result.u holds the solver's dual matrix. Reuse the returned Θ, u and ρ together for a warm start. The solver borrows S, stored in f, and Λ, retained by reference in g, for reads. During a solve the caller must keep the buffers and problem data unchanged.

Each iteration applies the ADM-02 recurrence with α = 1: x = prox_{f/ρ}(z₀-u₀) by the ProximalBase LogDetLoss prox!, z = prox_{g/ρ}(x+u₀) by the prox! of NormL1(T(λ₀), Λ), and u = (x+u₀)-z. The weighted proximal step acts on every entry. After each logdet proximal step, x must be finite, exactly symmetric and pass a Cholesky factorization in T; otherwise the solve raises an error before any write to Θ or u. The consensus iterate z after the ℓ₁ step can be indefinite. The loop does not test its definiteness and does not clip or project it; a nonfinite z fails the dual-update check of ADM-02. The residuals and tolerances are those of ADM-02 with Frobenius norms and N = p² for a p × p matrix, so √N = p. The limit order, counters and nonfinite failures are those of ADM-02.

The returned point is the consensus z. Before a normal return at any status, z must be exactly symmetric and pass a Cholesky factorization in T; otherwise the solve raises an error, with no write to Θ or u. This is a working-precision test; it does not prove that a matrix close to singular is positive definite. On a normal return after zero completed iterations, the returned point is the validated start. ProximalOptions requires maxiter ≥ 2, so zero iterations follow from prox_calls_limit = 1 or from a time limit that has expired before the first iteration, such as a nonpositive one.

When both computed residuals are zero and pass their tolerances, and z has passed the return-point check above, the solver checks stationarity at z in place of the ADM-02 score. With G = S - inv(z), the inverse formed from a Cholesky factorization, the score of each entry is |Gᵢⱼ + Cᵢⱼ sign(zᵢⱼ)| at a nonzero entry and max(|Gᵢⱼ| - Cᵢⱼ, 0) at an exact zero, with Cᵢⱼ = λ₀Λᵢⱼ formed in T. The diagonal uses the nonzero rule at a positive-definite point. The product can overflow even for finite inputs. A nonfinite score raises an error before writing either caller buffer. Otherwise, if a score exceeds eps_dual, the status is :stagnated and converged=false.

On a normal return, status is :converged when both residual tests passed and the stationarity check passed where it applies, and :stagnated as above. :iteration_limit, :prox_calls_limit and :time_limit report termination by the limit checks of ADM-02, with converged=false; a residual or stagnation decision on the last permitted iteration takes precedence. minimum follows the objective rule of ADM-02 at the returned z, so a nonfinite objective raises an error after the status decision and before any write. The result is an ADMMResults{T, Matrix{T}}. A converged status reports these working-precision tests and is not an optimality certificate.

Applies to. ADMMSolver with a dense Matrix{Float32} or Matrix{Float64} start, LogDetLoss and NormL1 with Matrix weights, fixed ρ, a stored α = 1 and adaptive=false.

Code. src/solvers/admm.jl: the Matrix method of _solve_admm!, _admm_issymmetric, _admm_passes_cholesky! and _admm_logdet_stationary.

Tests. test/admm_graphical_lasso.jl checks analytic and seeded fits with zero and positive diagonal weights. It compares direct and shifted formulations against the original BigFloat objective, KKT violations and dual candidates checked for exact affine feasibility and BigFloat Cholesky. The tests cover an exact positive-diagonal fixed point, rounded stagnation, one-step replay, limits, warm starts, an indefinite intermediate consensus point, and bitwise rollback on refusals and numerical failures. They also cover penalties with declared abstract weight types.

Sources. Issue #436, parent issue #425 and the Stage 0 report.

ADM-05 Least-squares lasso with a tall or square design

Statement. solve!(β, f, g, method::ADMMSolver, options=ProximalOptions(); u=nothing) with the raw ProximalBase loss f = LeastSquaresLoss(y, X) and unweighted g = NormL1(τ) minimizes

\[\tfrac12\|y-X\beta\|_2^2+\tau\|\beta\|_1 .\]

The lasso (1/2n)‖y-Xβ‖² + λ‖β‖₁ is this problem with τ = nλ, and minimum is then n times the normalized objective. A scaled loss, such as (1/n)*LeastSquaresLoss(y, X), is not accepted: the scale belongs to τ.

The coefficients β and an explicit u must be Vector{Float64}. Only the data are stored in f: the response y, a Vector{Float64}, and the design X, a Matrix{Float64}. Float32 data and mixed element types are refused with an ArgumentError. The lengths must satisfy length(y) == size(X,1) and length(β) == size(X,2), and an explicit u must have the length of β; empty or incompatible lengths raise DimensionMismatch. The shape must be tall or square, with n = size(X,1) ≥ p = size(X,2) ≥ 1. A wide design, n < p, is refused with an ArgumentError. Entries of X, y, β and an explicit u must be finite, and τ finite, nonnegative and exactly representable in Float64. No two of β, an explicit u, y, X and the scratch of f, including a cached decomposition, may alias or overlap in memory, by the check of ADM-01, and f must have the internal storage that its public constructor creates. These checks precede every write to the caller's β and u. All other settings of ADM-01 and ADM-03 apply, including α, tolerances and adaptive, as do the ownership, initial dual, commit-on-return and warm-start rules of ADM-01.

Each iteration is the recurrence of ADM-02 in Float64. The update of x is the ProximalBase prox! of f with step inv(ρ), which solves (XᵀX + ρI)x = Xᵀy + ρ(z₀-u₀) through a thin SVD of X. The update of z is the prox! of NormL1(τ). The SVD is computed on the first prox! of f and is kept in f. Every later iteration, later solve and other ρ or τ with the same f reuses it, and the solver neither copies nor resets f. Therefore f is borrowed exclusively for each solve: no other solve or thread may use it concurrently, and the caller must call ProximalBase reset_cache! on f, or construct a new loss, after changing entries of X or y in place. The solver imposes no cutoff on ρ, τ or the coefficients beyond the finiteness and range checks of ADM-01 and ADM-03, and it uses no scaling or extended precision.

The residuals, tolerances, limits, counters and numerical failures are those of ADM-02, with N = p. minimum is f(z) + g(z), evaluated once after termination. The stationarity check of ADM-02 at two zero residuals uses the score below in place of the centered one. With q = Xᵀ(Xz - y) formed in Float64, the score of coordinate j is |qⱼ + τ sign(zⱼ)| at a nonzero zⱼ and max(|qⱼ| - τ, 0) at an exact zero. A finite score above eps_dual gives status=:stagnated and converged=false; a nonfinite score or q is a numerical failure. The solver does not retry, rescale ρ or otherwise recover from that status. A status of :converged reports these working-precision tests, not an optimality certificate. Verify a fit from the data with the objective and KKT conditions of the lasso.

Applies to. ADMMSolver with a dense Vector{Float64} start, a raw LeastSquaresLoss{Float64} with a vector response and a Matrix{Float64} design with n ≥ p, and an unweighted NormL1. A wide design is outside this clause. Support for it depends on ProximalBase and is a follow-up of issue #458. The matrix path of ADM-04 and the centered path of ADM-01 are unchanged.

Code. src/solvers/admm.jl.

Tests. test/admm_leastsquares.jl compares with orthogonal closed forms, a correlated tall design, an independent objective and KKT score, one iteration of the recurrence, fixed, relaxed and adaptive settings, statuses, warm starts and refusals without writes, including the wide design. The examples examples/problems/lasso.jl and examples/comparisons/lasso_solvers.jl check the returned point against the closed form or an independently computed objective and KKT violation, and the reported objective divided by n against the objective computed from the data.

Sources. Issue #467 and issue #458.
