Coordinatewise least-squares stopping: measurements

This page records the measurements behind STOP-21 (issue #487). They are observations at stated revisions, not solver guarantees.

evidence-coordinatewise-scale-487 Study and provenance

Statement. The rule was chosen from the round-2 study of #487, which replaced only the stationarity check of master 1a9f196b (ProximalBase bc9e888d) by candidate rules in the same process, kept the iterates, step sizes, momentum, proximal operators and starting point, and measured each return against a 256-bit BigFloat reference on the stored data (Julia 1.13.4 and 1.10.13, a cluster with AMD EPYC 7513 nodes). The study, its fixtures, its raw rows and its scripts are in the round-2 report and on branch research/issue-487 at e7f35093. Its probe recomputed the residual from Xx at each check; the implementation reads the stored loss value instead, so the decisions of the two can differ by rounding.

  • Rules compared. The λmax-based scale (with the #464 entry thresholds), a profiled reference scale (A), the coordinatewise rule (B), a tighten-only hybrid of the two, an absolute-plus-relative threshold (C), a backward-error classification (D) and a duality gap (E). On the 72 free-coordinate, tiny-weight, rescaling and ill-conditioning rows per type, converged = true at a relative objective excess above 1e-6 occurred in 30 Float64 rows (29 in the replicate draw) under the λmax-based scale and in 1 (1) under B, the remaining one an ill-conditioned free block (RD1); in Float32 in 30 (27) rows against 0 (0). On the 80 ordinary least-squares rows per type B changed one Float64 decision (a false stop removed), and lost certification on 8 (5) accurate Float32 rows while gaining it on 1 (2). Iterations of Float64 rows certified under both rules: median ratio 1.00, largest 1.32.
  • Consistent systems. The study's exact-fit fixture (F* ≈ 0) was refused under B and E. For the implementation the limitation is that an exact or near-exact fit can be left uncertified (STOP-21); the maintainer accepted it for this change.

The validation of the implementation ran production code at the base a9fb96c8 and at 00a67e3d of pull request #521.

  • Validation. The round-2 fixtures and the zero-scale, exact-fit and parity fixtures of the plan, Float32 and Float64, PGD and APG, with the default options and with gradtol = 1e-8, on Julia 1.10.13 and 1.13.4 as separate draws (each version on one node), with 256-bit BigFloat references on the stored inputs: 1640 in-scope rows per side and 96 rows of the problems that keep the λmax-based checks. A row is accurate when its objective excess is at most 1e-6 (also reported at 1e-4 for Float32): relative to F* when F* is positive and the row is not one of the consistent fixtures; otherwise relative to the progress F0 - F* from the start when that is positive, and otherwise the excess must be exactly zero. Measured outcomes:
    • converged and inaccurate: at the base 108 Float64 rows (PGD 48, APG 60) and 87 Float32 rows (37, 50; 55 at 1e-4); at the head 2 Float64 rows, APG on the nearly collinear free columns of RD1 with the default options in both draws (relative excess 6.3e-2; also converged and inaccurate at the base), and 0 Float32 rows (0 at 1e-4). No row is converged and inaccurate at the head that was not at the base.
    • The 96 rows of the other problems have identical outcomes at the base and the head.
    • Certified and accurate at the base, accurate and not certified at the head: in Float64, 58 rows of the consistent (exact-fit) fixtures and 25 others. Of the 25, 23 are at gradtol = 1e-8 (the offset fixtures O1 and O2, the rescalings RS, the duplicated free column RD2, and N2 with APG); they ended by an objective increase or at maxiter with relative excess at most 7.2e-12. The other 2 are N2 (100×200, 0.01 λmax, unweighted) with PGD and the default options, certified at the base after 154 and 146 iterations, and ended at maxiter = 200 at the head with relative excess 3.7e-9 and 4.1e-9. In Float32, 67 rows (99 at 1e-4).
    • Not certified at the base and certified and accurate at the head: 96 rows, among them the zero-response and X = 0 fixtures.
    • Iterations, head over base, where both certified accurately: median 1.00 for each precision and method; largest 1.27 (Float64 PGD), 1.37 (Float64 APG), 1.18 (Float32 PGD), 1.21 (Float32 APG).
    • The diagnostic of the validation script applied the earlier case D to a column scaled by 1e-170, so in eight rows (two draws, two methods, two gradtol) it disagreed with the solver, which rescales that column and certified; the maintainer chose the scaled norm.

At the head each proposal is refused, by the ordinary comparison where the computed smooth scale is positive (for the one-column fixtures the computed residual is parallel to the column) and by case C where the stored loss, and so the smooth scale, is zero, and the solve ends at maxiter with converged = false. The one-column fixtures returned the minimizer for the stored data (relative error 0); the diagonal fixture ended at a point fitted to working precision (relative residual about 5.0e-17 in BigFloat).     • X = ones(Float32, 5, 1) with y = fill(1f20, 5) and Float64 loss scales 1e-10 and 1e-30, y = fill(1f25, 5) with loss scale 1f-20, and y = fill(1f30, 5) with loss scale 1f-30;
    • X = diag(1, 1e-6), y = (0, 1e-156), raw loss, maxiter = 20_000 (PGD).
  • In the Learn chapter on floating point, the underflow example (case 2) now returns converged = false from PGD and APG at the coefficient 0 (converged = true at the base); the other Learn examples keep their flags.
  • Benchmark campaign baselines of STOP-20 (gradtol = 1e-11 in the ordinary and #320 settings, 1e-10 in the Float64 acc- settings) were measured under the earlier threshold of a least-squares lasso stop; a comparison across this change compares two stopping rules and is reported with accuracy and work, as in the paired benchmarks of pull request #521.

Applies to. PGD and APG on least squares with NormL1 or ElasticNet on a dense vector.

Sources. Issue #487 and pull request #521.
