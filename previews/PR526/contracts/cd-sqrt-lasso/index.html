Square-root lasso coordinate descent

This loss has its own coordinate step and duality certificate. Its ordinary scheduling comes from scalar CD, while input and ownership rules also apply through IN-01, CDS-01 and RES-01.

CDQ-01 Objective, normalization and supported calls

Statement.

CDSolver with SqrtLeastSquaresLoss(y, X) and an unweighted NormL1(lambda) solves P(beta) = norm(y - X*beta) + lambda * norm(beta, 1), with no 1/sqrt(n) factor. X::Matrix{T}, y::Vector{T} and the dense Vector{T} or one-dimensional SparseIterate{T} coefficient use one T, either Float32 or Float64. lambda must be positive, finite and representable in T; another Real scalar type is accepted when representable. Use f = SqrtLeastSquaresLoss(y, X) and g = NormL1(lambda) in solve!(x, f, g, CDSolver(...)) with the supported scalar coefficient storage.

The normalized objective norm(y - X*beta)/sqrt(n) + lambda_n * norm(beta, 1) has the same minimizers at lambda = sqrt(n) * lambda_n, and its values are P/sqrt(n). ProximalBase can express the normalized loss as Scaled(f, inv(sqrt(n))); the solver accepts the equivalent unscaled problem with the transformed level, as specified below. The scaled-lasso coefficient estimate at level lambda_0 uses the same transformation. CDQ-06 specifies its noise estimate norm(y - X*beta)/sqrt(n) when the residual is nonzero, what a square-root certificate proves for the joint objective, and why an exact-fit certificate does not supply a positive noise-scale minimizer. There is no separate scaled-lasso solver; the scaled-lasso tutorial shows the computation.

Before copying the iterate or using the RNG, ArgumentError refuses a weighted NormL1 even with all weights one, an ElasticNet, a SqrNormL2, a BlockIterate, or Scaled(SqrtLeastSquaresLoss, alpha). For positive finite alpha, use the unscaled loss with level lambda/alpha instead. lambda = 0 is refused; LeastSquaresLoss has the same unpenalized minimizers. ProximalGradientDescent and AccProxGradDescent are refused because ProximalBase provides no gradient for this loss and, for X != 0, it is nondifferentiable at y = X*beta.

The order, momentum and pass limits are those of scalar CD, with loss scale s = 1. For relative = true and y != 0, the threshold is tol * norm(y) and a coordinate change is scaled by norm(X[:, k]); otherwise the threshold is absolute tol and changes are unscaled. A change-based stop must also pass the certificate to report convergence; otherwise the bounded fallback can run.

Applies to. Unscaled square-root loss, positive unweighted L1 penalty, scalar CD iterates.

CDQ-02 Coordinate minimization and range rules

Statement.

For the unsmoothed step (delta = 0), with a = ‖X_k‖², b = X_kᵀr, the centre c = old + b/a and d the distance of r from the span of X_k, the objective along coordinate k is √(a (z − c)² + d²) + λ|z|. Its minimizer is 0 for λ ≥ √a and shrink(c, λ d/√(a (a − λ²))) otherwise; the step uses the level T(lambda). It forms a, b and ‖r‖² in one pass over the column and the residual, and d² = ‖r‖² − b²/a; where that is below ‖r‖²/16 it forms d again as the norm of r − (b/a) X_k, because the subtraction loses accuracy there. Where a is below n floatmin(T)/eps(T), or ‖r‖² or that recomputed d² is below it or not finite (a column whose squares can underflow, a residual whose squares underflow or overflow, and r = 0), the step is formed in Float64 from the column and the residual scaled by powers of two. In that scaling a small entry can underflow or lose bits, which changes √a, b/√a and d by absolute amounts of at most 3n 2^-1074 times ‖X_k‖ or ‖r‖ (to first order); the error bound of the step, derived in the source, covers this (a tiny d can become 0). Where d/√(a (a − λ²)) or its product with λ is outside the normal range of T, the threshold is formed in Float64 without that product. At every visit, before any of these branches, a residual with an entry that is not finite is refused with an ArgumentError, and so are a column whose squared norm is 0 but which is not zero and a column whose squared norm is not finite; the coordinate and the residual are then unchanged.

During the fallback, a positive smoothing level delta replaces the coordinate objective by √(a (z − c)² + d² + delta²) + λ|z|. This is the same one-coordinate problem with an appended response entry delta and a zero design entry. The quantities a and b stay the same, while ‖r‖² and the recomputed d² gain delta². The effective delta is a value of T, stored in Float64; its square is rounded in T. The appended-row interpretation extends the step's rounding bound to n + 1 rows and residual (r, delta).

Both sums include the rounded delta² before the clean-sum range tests. If its square overflows in T, the step takes the power-of-two-scaled Float64 route. If the exact square is below floatmin(T), it is at most eps(T)/n relative to a sum that passes the clean-sum test; the fast path may lose the smoothing by that amount. Rounding that square contributes at most nextfloat(zero(T))/2, the usual allowance for an underflowing square. The clean-sum threshold still counts n terms rather than n + 1; the total underflow allowance changes from eps(T)^2/2 to at most (n + 1)/n * eps(T)^2/2 <= eps(T)^2, a second-order term covered by the step bound's existing 3.6u margin.

In the scaled route, the residual scale uses max(maximum(abs, r), delta), the scaled delta contributes its square to the residual norm, and the recomputed distance is the hypot of the difference norm and that scaled delta. Thus r = 0 with positive delta gives b = 0 and d = delta instead of the unsmoothed return of old. The extra Float64 hypot rounding is covered by the distance allowance: its derivation uses (3γ_n + 4u)‖r‖, below the bound's (16γ_n + 13u)‖r‖. The certificate and objective-decrease test always use the original unsmoothed objective, so their validity does not depend on how accurately a smoothed step minimizes its coordinate objective. At delta = 0, every step branch is bit for bit the pre-fallback implementation.

Applies to. Each square-root CD coordinate visit, with the supplied level converted to T; smoothing is private to the fallback stages.

CDQ-03 Outward-bounded duality certificate

Statement.

For P* > 0, converged = true is reported only when a certificate proves (P(beta) - P*)/P* <= 1e-4; gap then holds the proved upper bound, rounded up to T. At the exact-data special case y = 0 and beta = 0, the result instead certifies P = P* = 0 with gap = 0. The residual-direction check below can certify a point directly. CDQ-05 can also certify the returned point with a valid dual bound obtained at another point during the fallback.

Acceptance compares the internal Float64 ratio with prevfloat(1e-4); the reported gap is then rounded upward to T. For Float32, that final conversion can put the stored value just above the decimal target even though the accepted internal bound proves the stated 1e-4 objective target. The target should not be inferred by comparing an outward-rounded Float32 gap with a Float64 literal.

At an unsmoothed full pass whose largest change is below the threshold, the residual r̂ = y − Xβ is recomputed in Float64 over the nonzero coefficients, with the row magnitudes |y_i| + Σ_k |X_ik β_k|. The dual point is θ = t r̂/‖r̂‖ with t ≤ min(1, λ/max_k |X_kᵀr̂|/‖r̂‖), where the maximum is replaced by an upper bound and t is rounded down, so that ‖θ‖ ≤ 1 and ‖Xᵀθ‖∞ ≤ λ hold exactly; weak duality then gives P* ≥ θᵀy. The gap P − θᵀy is bounded by a sum of terms that are nonnegative in exact arithmetic, (1 − t)‖r̂‖ + Σ_k (λ|β_k| − t β_k X_kᵀr̂/‖r̂‖) plus (1 + t) times a bound on the error of r̂, without a difference of θᵀy and P. Each computed term carries a relative and an absolute rounding allowance, and the operations after the passes over the data round outward. The run is certified when the resulting upper bound of (P − P*)/P* (with a lower bound of θᵀy as denominator), rounded up, is at most prevfloat(1e-4), and that bound is reported in gap. The check is undefined if the recomputed residual r̂ is 0 in every entry with a nonzero coefficient (the direction is undefined; this is the usual outcome at an exact fit y = Xβ, and r̂ can also round to 0 where y − Xβ is not 0), if the sum of squares of r̂ is below n 2^-969 or not finite, if a required intermediate value is not finite, or if the penalty level cannot be bracketed. An exact fit whose r̂ is not 0 goes through the bound like any other point, and a certificate there is valid. The check also replaces the cached residual by r̂ rounded to T when every entry is representable in T, and minimum is evaluated from it at a stop of the check. A check costs one pass over X and one over its columns with nonzero coefficients. The proof of the allowance is a comment in src/cd/sqrtlasso.jl.

The rounding of r̂ in Float64 is of the order of eps(Float64) times the row magnitudes |y_i| + Σ_k |X_ik β_k| (larger than eps(Float64) ‖Xβ‖ where terms of a row cancel). Where these magnitudes dwarf the residual, for example where the fitted values do, that rounding enters the bound, and a point that is optimal can fail to be certified.

The same evaluation stores outward primal bounds P_lo(beta) <= P(beta) <= P_up(beta) before rejecting a zero recomputed residual or an out-of-range residual sum of squares. These bounds are used by CDQ-05, including at exact fits where this point cannot supply a residual direction. To specify their construction, let sr be the computed sum of squared r̂ entries, nb the number of nonzero coefficients, sb their computed sum of absolute values, epsilon = eps(Float64), eta = nextfloat(0.0) and psi = (n + 1)*epsilon. Let E_up be the residual-error norm bound already used above, and let lo and hi bracket the supplied penalty level used by the objective. With up and down denoting nextfloat and prevfloat of the Float64 round-to-nearest result, the stored bounds are formed as follows:

N_up = up(sqrt(up(up(sr*(1 + psi)) + n*eta)))
N_lo = sr >= n*2^-969 ? down(sqrt(down(sr*(1 - psi)))) : 0
S_up = up(sb*(1 + nb*epsilon))
S_lo = down(sb*(1 - nb*epsilon))
P_up = up(up(N_up + E_up) + up(hi*S_up))
P_lo = down(max(0, down(N_lo - E_up)) + down(lo*S_lo))

The proof uses ‖r̂‖² <= sr*(1 + psi) + n*eta, ‖y − Xβ‖ <= ‖r̂‖ + E_up, the corresponding lower norm bound where sr >= n*2^-969, and lo*S_lo <= lambda*‖beta‖₁ <= hi*S_up with the displayed outward rounding. Thus the primal upper bound does not require the residual-squares range restriction of the residual-direction certificate; below that range the lower residual norm contribution is zero. The proof requires finite residual and row-magnitude square sums and coefficient sum, together with level brackets. When an aggregate is nonfinite or brackets are unavailable, P_up is Inf or NaN, and the fallback does not use that point's bounds. It also explicitly requires both initial primal bounds to be finite. The stored dual bound is the check's finite positive D_lo <= P*, or NaN when it was not formed or is not positive. At y = 0, beta = 0, the exact-data special case stores P_up = P_lo = 0 and no dual lower bound.

Applies to. The residual-direction check at an unsmoothed full-pass change stop, and the original-objective bounds recomputed before and after fallback stages.

CDQ-04 Tightening, uncertified stops and result flags

Statement.

At an unsmoothed full-pass change stop, a check that does not certify can divide the threshold by 100 and continue. It does so at most twice in that run, only when the certificate is defined, the full pass changed something, and tolerance/100 is finite, positive, strictly below tolerance and at least the roundoff floor in the threshold's units. A threshold that overflowed to Inf is not lowered and spends no tightening. With relative = true and y != 0, the floor is 16 eps(T) norm(y) and changes are scaled by norm(X[:, k]). With an absolute threshold (relative = false, or y = 0), the floor is 16 eps(T) times the largest abs(beta[k]) at the check, and no tightening is allowed at beta = 0.

Otherwise the unsmoothed run makes an uncertified stop: the certificate is undefined, the full pass changed nothing, the two tightenings were used, or the threshold cannot be lowered. This is the entry point for CDQ-05, rather than an immediate final result when that fallback can run. Each fallback stage starts with the original solver tol and a reset tightening count; its final unsmoothed stage can therefore make up to two new tightenings. A positive smoothing stage ends at its first full-pass change stop without running this check inside the stage.

With relative = true, y != 0 and the default tol = 1e-7, Float32 data get no tightening because 1e-9 is below 16 eps(Float32). With y = 0, both the threshold and floor are absolute and tightening can occur. At y = 0 from a nonzero start, P* = 0, so no relative bound can be certified unless beta reaches zero. CD can approach zero without reaching it and stop uncertified; the fallback can then reach zero and certify gap = 0, but need not do so.

At an exact fit with a positive penalty, a point can minimize every coordinate without minimizing the whole objective. Its residual-direction certificate is usually undefined because the recomputed residual is zero; that also happens at an exact-fit optimum. The fallback can certify the stopped point with a dual bound from another point or return a point with a proved lower objective, under CDQ-05. It does not guarantee recovery: a final uncertified point can still be a nonoptimal exact fit. The tutorial includes two exact fits that the fallback certifies and a noiseless, underdetermined small-penalty case that it does not.

A run ending at an uncertified stop or after an uncertified fallback reports converged = false, gap = NaN and the warning coordinate descent stopped without a duality certificate. An uncertified stop at the last allowed pass has no fallback budget and uses this warning. A fallback that reaches maxpasses also uses this warning, so passes == maxpasses alone does not distinguish it from an ordinary pass-limit exit. By contrast, with tol = 0, or with a threshold below every change that rounding permits, the strict change test never passes: no certificate or fallback runs, and the solve reaches maxpasses with the pass-limit warning. A failed certificate followed by a retry and then a pass-limit exit likewise supplies no terminal uncertified stop and does not trigger the fallback.

At a certificate stop or completed fallback, minimum is evaluated from the residual recomputed at the returned point, when every entry is representable in T. CDQ-05 specifies which point and change statistics are returned, pass accounting and failure recovery. The pre-fallback final-stop assertions are retained as historical evidence in EVIDENCE-SQRT-406.

Applies to. Unsmoothed square-root CD stopping, including the initial solve, the fallback's final unsmoothed stage, exact fits, zero responses, final warnings and pass limits.

CDQ-05 Bounded fallback after an uncertified stop

Statement.

The fallback runs only after the initial unsmoothed CD run ends in the terminal uncertified stop defined by CDQ-04. It continues on the private working iterate from that stopped point x0. A solve that simply exhausts maxpasses does not trigger it. Let base be the passes used at the stop. All fallback stages share one additional budget

B = min(maxpasses - base, max(2000, 20*base)).

No stage runs when B <= 0. Otherwise the certificate is evaluated again at x0, and fallback entry also requires finite P0 = P_up(x0) and L0 = P_lo(x0), P0 > 0, and finite T(P0). There is no separate requirement that T(P0) be positive: conversion can round a positive bound to zero. The bounds and their range limits are in CDQ-03. For example, an overflowing Float64 primal upper bound skips the fallback even if the original objective can still be evaluated finitely; a finite Float64 bound above the Float32 range also skips it for Float32 data.

Stages and continuation

Stages 1 through 8 use the smoothed coordinate step in CDQ-02, followed, if the budget and earlier return rules allow, by stage 9 with zero smoothing. Each stage uses the caller's order, momentum choice, RNG, tol and relative, starts a fresh momentum history, resets its tightening count and starts with the original solver threshold rather than a tightened threshold from the preceding run. The stages continue from the preceding stage's point; they do not restart from x0.

A Float64 continuation value delta starts at P0. Each of the first eight stages uses Float64(T(delta)) as its effective smoothing level and its square rounded in T. After an evaluated stage that does not end the fallback, delta = min(delta/5, Fbest), where Fbest is the least finite primal upper bound found at x0 or any evaluated stage. Fbest controls the next smoothing level; it does not save an earlier iterate for return. While the effective smoothing level is positive, the stage ends at its first full pass satisfying the change test, without an in-stage certificate, or at the remaining budget. If conversion rounds the effective level to zero, the ordinary unsmoothed stop check runs instead. Stage 9 always uses that unsmoothed check and its possible tightenings.

After each stage, smoothing is cleared and the certificate's original-objective bounds are recomputed at the stage point beta. No smoothed objective value is used for certification or the decrease test. Let D be the largest finite positive dual lower bound found at x0 or any evaluated stage, or zero before one is found. Every such bound satisfies D <= P*, even when obtained at a different point from the one eventually returned.

Ordered return rules

For a positive objective bound, define ratio(P, D) = up(up(P - D)/D) when D > 0, and Inf otherwise, with the outward Float64 rounding from CDQ-03. The exact-data case P = 0 has ratio zero: the certificate produces that bound only for y = 0, beta = 0, where P = P* = 0. After each stage, the first applicable rule ends the fallback:

  1. Return x0 certified if ratio(P0, D) <= prevfloat(1e-4).
  2. Otherwise return the current stage point beta certified if its P_up is finite, P_up(beta) < L0, and ratio(P_up(beta), D) <= prevfloat(1e-4).
  3. Otherwise, after stage 9 or at the shared budget, return beta uncertified if P_up(beta) is finite and strictly below L0; return x0 uncertified if that decrease test fails.

If none applies and budget remains before stage 9, continue to the next stage. In particular, a changed returned point always has the proved strict decrease P(beta) <= P_up(beta) < P_lo(x0) <= P(x0). Returning x0 first can preserve an exact-fit optimum whose own residual direction is undefined while certifying it with a stage's dual bound. A zero point reached from a zero response is returned certified with gap = 0 when it satisfies the strict decrease test against L0 > 0.

For a positive optimum, a certified fallback ratio bounds (P - P*)/P* at the returned point because 0 < D <= P*. The ratio is rounded up to T in the result. The fallback recomputes its ratio even when stage 9's own stop certificate passed. When D is that stage's own dual bound, the fallback's (P_up - D)/D is at least the stage's own certificate ratio. A point whose own check passes only within a margin of order (P_up - P_lo)/D can consequently be returned uncertified. A passing internal stage flag alone never bypasses the ordered return rules.

Refusals, results and ownership

An ArgumentError thrown during a stage, including a refused nonfinite step or stopping scale, ends the fallback with x0, uncertified. It counts all completed passes of that stage and the refused pass itself. Any other exception is rethrown after the smoothing fields have been cleared. This recovery rule applies to errors inside the stage run; it does not swallow a later failure of the final objective check.

When x0 is returned, its copied coefficient state is restored, its residual is reinitialized and recomputed by the certificate, and maxchange and tolerance are restored to the values at the initial stop. When the current stage point is returned, those two statistics describe the last stage. In both cases, passes includes the initial passes and every fallback pass, active and full, including a refused pass under the recovery rule, and is at most maxpasses. kkt_checks = 0 remains a compatibility sentinel; it is not the number of certificates or stages. The borrowed RNG continues advancing through the fallback and is not restored when x0 is returned.

A certified result has converged = true and the proved gap; otherwise it has converged = false, gap = NaN and the uncertified warning described in CDQ-04. minimum evaluates the original objective from the certificate's recomputed residual at the returned point when that residual is representable in T. A finite completed result then commits under CDS-03, including an uncertified x0 restored after a caught stage refusal. If that final objective is nonfinite, ArgumentError still propagates before the caller is changed; the warning can already have been emitted. CDS-04 and RES-03 specify that outer transaction boundary.

The budget and proved-decrease rule do not guarantee recovery at exact fits. The result can remain uncertified at x0 or at a lower-objective point that is not optimal. A nonoptimal exact fit can therefore still have converged = false; check such a point by other means. The measured recovery rates and hard cases reported with this change are preserved as observations in EVIDENCE-SQRT-406, not as guaranteed rates.

Applies to. Square-root lasso CD on dense vectors or one-dimensional SparseIterate owners after a terminal uncertified stop. The shared model hook is a no-op for models without a certificate.

CDQ-06 Scaled-lasso profile and exact-fit interpretation

Statement.

For lambda = sqrt(n)*lambda_0, the scaled-lasso joint objective is

\[L(\beta, \sigma) = \frac{\|y-X\beta\|_2^2}{2n\sigma} + \frac{\sigma}{2} + \lambda_0\|\beta\|_1, \qquad \sigma > 0.\]

For a fixed coefficient point with nonzero residual r = y - X*beta, its conditional minimizer is sigma_hat = norm(r)/sqrt(n) > 0: L(beta, sigma) - L(beta, sigma_hat) = (sigma - sigma_hat)^2/(2sigma) >= 0. At that scale, L(beta, sigma_hat) = P(beta)/sqrt(n). Therefore an actual square-root-lasso minimizer with nonzero residual gives a scaled-lasso minimizer at this scale. The package implements this through square-root CD and the profile calculation, without a separate scaled-lasso solver; the square-root input refusals, including weighted penalties, still apply.

The joint infimum over beta and sigma > 0 is P*/sqrt(n), including when it is approached at an exact fit as sigma decreases to zero. For y != 0 and positive lambda, P* > 0: a zero objective would require both beta = 0 and y - X*beta = 0. A certified square-root result with nonzero residual therefore also proves (L(beta, sigma_hat) - inf L)/inf L <= 1e-4 in exact arithmetic at the profiled scale, and the reported gap is an outward upper bound on that relative error. Its final conversion to Float32 can round it just above the decimal target, as specified in CDQ-03. Evaluation of the residual, scale and joint objective in Float32 or Float64 introduces its own rounding, covered by the scaled-lasso tests. The objective certificate does not bound the distance of sigma_hat from an optimizing scale, coefficient recovery, or noise-variance estimation.

At an exact fit, L(beta, sigma) = sigma/2 + lambda_0*norm(beta, 1) decreases strictly as sigma decreases and approaches P(beta)/sqrt(n) without attaining it for any positive sigma. Thus even a certified square-root point at an exact fit has no conditional positive noise-scale minimizer. It is not presented with a sigma_hat or as a scaled-lasso solution. This statement concerns the returned coefficient point; it does not settle whether another square-root minimizer with a nonzero residual attains the joint minimum. A very small nonzero residual is different: its conditional positive sigma_hat exists.

For y = 0, the zero point has P = P* = 0, so the solver's gap = 0 is an exact-optimality special case rather than a relative gap and supplies no positive noise-scale minimizer. A run started at zero certifies it directly. From a nonzero start the fallback can reach and certify zero, or end uncertified. An uncertified returned point with nonzero residual still has a conditional scale minimizer at that point, but no certificate for the joint optimization.

The updated exact-fit fixture X = [1 2; 0 1], y = (1, 0), lambda = 0.1 starts at zero, reaches (1, 0) and is certified by the fallback using another point's dual bound. Its stored residual is zero, so the scaled-lasso tutorial continues to demonstrate unattained conditional scale minimization even though the square-root objective is now certified. The old statement that this fixture was uncertified is retained in EVIDENCE-EXAMPLES-01.
