PGD and APG stopping

Scope

This is the stopping contract for ProximalGradientDescent and AccProxGradDescent on their supported least-squares problems. See the support matrix. Coordinate descent has its own stopping contract.

Problem

The shared engine minimizes F(x) = f(x) + g(x), with f(x) = s‖Xx - y‖²/2. The penalty determines the stationarity residual below.

Notation

T is the coefficient type, s is the loss scale, x is the accepted iterate, x_previous is the preceding accepted iterate, and L is the accepted inverse step. In the retained proof of STOP-13, s has this same meaning. APG uses y for its extrapolated point in algorithm formulas; the response is identified explicitly where needed. τ and S are defined in STOP-04; the coordinatewise thresholds of least squares with NormL1 or ElasticNet are defined in STOP-21.

Preconditions and refusals

Input acceptance is specified by IN-01. Option validation and reporting are specified by PGD-04 and PGD-05. A refused stationarity check clears stopping flags; it is not an exception.

Algorithm

STOP-01 A proposed stop is checked at the accepted point

Statement. The iterate, objective and gradient/proximal-residual tests combine by OR: any one can propose a stop. A proposal counts only if the applicable stationarity check passes at the accepted iterate. Otherwise x_converged, f_converged and grad_converged are all cleared, converged=false, and the solve continues unless another exit applies. The residual fields continue to report their diagnostics. The exact-stall rule in STOP-10 applies before this proposal is checked.

converged=true therefore records a computed working-precision check. It is not an exact-arithmetic certificate, a bound on distance to a minimizer, or a promise of a specified number of correct coefficient digits. PGD and APG use the same acceptance policy; their iteration residuals differ. Independent KKT or duality-gap evaluation is needed to assess a returned point.

Applies to. PGD and APG; dense Vector{T}; T = Float32 or Float64.

Stopping

STOP-02 The three proposal tests

Statement. The iterate test is norm(x - x_previous) < xtol * max(norm(x), 1). For norm(x) < 1, its threshold is absolute. Its strict inequality and the exact-stall exclusion are intentional.

For ftol > 0, the objective test holds if either abs(F - F_previous) / abs(F) < ftol or abs(F - F_previous) < eps(abs(F) + abs(F_previous)). The quotient is evaluated as a diagnostic in Float64, is 0 when the objectives are equal (including both zero), and is Inf when F = 0 ≠ F_previous. There is no absolute term in the denominator. The second test holds for equal finite-sum objectives and, when both values are normal and their absolute-value sum is finite, permits a difference of about two units in the last place. If that sum overflows, eps is NaN and this second test does not hold. Neither form establishes accuracy by itself.

For the gradient test, let r be the iteration's gradient/proximal residual, m the largest such residual so far, N = max(‖∇f‖∞, ‖q‖∞) with q the residual of the proximal step, and ρ = (L * ‖x‖∞) * eps(T), the rounding resolution of L x. Form L * ‖x‖∞ first to avoid premature underflow of L * eps(T). If the first product overflows to Inf, the resolution comparison rejects both normalizers. APG uses ∇f and the proximal step at its extrapolated point. The test is

(r < gradtol * m  and  ρ < m)   or   (r < gradtol * N  and  ρ < N).

The comparisons use the floating-point products gradtol * m and gradtol * N; neither normalizer has an absolute term. A zero residual passes only against a positive product, so it does not pass when m = N = 0 or the relevant product underflows to zero. A scale no larger than ρ decides nothing. The running maximum is updated even on an exact-stall iteration.

Applies to. PGD and APG; dense Vector{T}; T = Float32 or Float64.

STOP-21 Coordinatewise stationarity for least squares with NormL1 or ElasticNet

Statement. What a kept stop records. For least squares with NormL1 or ElasticNet on a dense vector, a kept stop records that every coordinate passed the computed comparison of the rule below: its computed KKT score was below its computed threshold or, where its scale is an exact zero (cases A and B), the computed score was finite and at most 0, with no threshold formed. The comparison is between computed quantities. Its exact-arithmetic analogue is approximate coordinatewise KKT stationarity relative to the smooth part Φ of the scoring model F below, with the decrease bound and its conditions stated under Meaning below; neither form bounds the error of the objective or of the coefficients in general. An exact or near-exact fit can return converged = false at an accurate point, and the rounding of the computed gradient can prevent the check from passing at an accurate point (in Float32 at the default gradtol, and in Float64 at a small one). With subnormal numbers flushed to zero on the solving thread (set_zero_subnormals(true)), the stopping decisions of PGD and APG, cases A and B included, are unspecified: the solver's arithmetic and its compiled comparisons can treat a stored subnormal value as zero (FP-01).

Problem and scores. The check scores the model F(x) = (s/2)‖Xx - y‖² + Σᵢ cᵢ|xᵢ| + (λ2/2)‖x‖², with the loss scale s (s = 1 for a raw loss) and the coefficients that the score and the scale use, those of the penalty gT with its levels converted to T (IN-02, IN-03): cᵢ = T(λ) for NormL1(λ) and T(λ) wᵢ rounded in T for NormL1(λ, w), cᵢ = T(λ1) or T(λ1) wᵢ for ElasticNet, λ2 = T(λ2) for ElasticNet and 0 for NormL1. The exact-arithmetic statements of this clause are for F; they apply to the objective of the solve when its coefficients agree with those of F. The score of coordinate i is ProximalBase's signed Gᵢ = kkt_violation(gT, ∇f(x), x, i), computed in T from the computed gradient: |∇fᵢ| - cᵢ at xᵢ = 0, negative when |∇fᵢ| < cᵢ, and |∇fᵢ + λ2 xᵢ + cᵢ sign(xᵢ)| otherwise. A zero weight gives cᵢ = 0, so a zero-weight coordinate is scored by its full |∇fᵢ| at zero and |∇fᵢ + λ2 xᵢ| elsewhere. ∇f(x) is the gradient that PGD holds and that APG forms at the accepted point (STOP-08).

Thresholds. With τ = T(max(gradtol, 64 eps(T))), a = Float64(s), nᵢ = ‖Xᵢ‖ (the column norm) and Φ = f(x) + (λ2/2)‖x‖², where f(x) is the loss value that ProximalBase computed at the accepted point (FP-01), the threshold tᵢ of coordinate i is the value of τ hᵢ ρ formed as described under Arithmetic, with hᵢ = √(a nᵢ² + λ2) and ρ = √(2Φ). In exact arithmetic τ hᵢ ρ = τ sᵢ with sᵢ = √(2μᵢΦ) and μᵢ = s nᵢ² + λ2.

Rule. A proposed stop is kept when every coordinate passes, in one pass that ends at the first failure:

  1. Exact zero scales. (A) When λ2 is a stored ±0 and every stored entry of column i is ±0, coordinate i passes when its computed score Gᵢ is finite and at most 0. (B) When every stored entry of x and of y is ±0, every coordinate passes when its computed score is finite and at most 0; this includes a column of case D. The predicates read the stored bits of the column, x, y and λ2 (under gradual underflow a subnormal is not zero); case A is the class that the column pass stores, and x is scanned for case B only when y is zero. Neither case is inferred from a computed threshold of 0. In exact arithmetic a column of zeros gives ∇fᵢ = 0, so its score is -cᵢ at xᵢ = 0 and cᵢ elsewhere, and case A passes exactly when cᵢ|xᵢ| is already minimal (xᵢ = 0 or cᵢ = 0); at x = 0 and y = 0, F(0) = 0 is the minimum. The implemented decision reads the computed score: a zero residual with every column in case A still refuses when a computed score is positive (X = [0.0;;], y = [0.0], x = [1.0], NormL1(1.0)).
  2. Every other coordinate passes when Gᵢ is finite, tᵢ is finite and at least floatmin(T), and Gᵢ < tᵢ.

Outside cases A and B, these refuse the stop. (C) A computed smooth scale ρ = 0 (for example a stored loss f(x) = 0 with λ2 = 0) refuses; so an exact fit away from x = y = 0 refuses when its computed smooth scale is 0 (X = [1.0;;], NormL1(0.0): x = y = [0.0] passes by case B, while x = y = [1.0] refuses). (D) A nonzero column is classified D when its norm computed with scaling is 0 or not finite; its coordinate refuses. A nonzero column with a positive finite scaled norm goes to the ordinary comparison, where it can still refuse. Also refused: a nonfinite τ, a nonfinite stored loss and, with a ridge term, a nonfinite entry of x; and a converted threshold that is not finite or is below floatmin(T). So a gradtol whose conversion T(gradtol) is Inf refuses at every coordinate outside cases A and B; the acceptance of an infinite threshold in STOP-04 does not apply. No absolute floor or replacement scale is used.

Arithmetic. At the first check of a solve one pass over the columns forms a Float64 sum of squares per column. When that sum is finite and at least floatmin(Float64), nᵢ is its square root; otherwise the column is read again, and a column whose stored entries are all ±0 is a zero column while any other column gets its norm computed with scaling by its largest magnitude. The factors of the threshold are carried as a Float64 significand times a power of two, where finite nonnegative factors do not overflow or underflow. Each threshold is converted to T once, and a converted threshold that is not finite or is below floatmin(T) refuses. The conversion and the earlier operations round, so a computed threshold can differ from τ sᵢ by rounding, including at the boundary of the normal range.

Meaning. The following holds in exact arithmetic for the exact scores and scales of F; it is not a property of the computed comparison. Let g⁺ᵢ = max(Gᵢ, 0), the magnitude of the least-norm subgradient of t ↦ F(x + t eᵢ) at t = 0. When μᵢ > 0 that function is μᵢ-strongly convex, and the decrease of F available from coordinate i alone is at most (g⁺ᵢ)²/(2μᵢ); when also Φ > 0, Gᵢ < τ sᵢ makes that decrease less than τ²Φ. When μᵢ = 0 (a zero column with λ2 = 0), F changes along eᵢ only through cᵢ|xᵢ|. For λ2 = 0, cᵢ = 0, a nonzero column and a nonzero residual r = Xx - y, the test is |Xᵢᵀr| < τ ‖Xᵢ‖ ‖r‖, a bound on the cosine of column i and the residual. ElasticNet is the lasso with the same coefficients on [√s X; √λ2 I] and [√s y; 0], and hᵢ ρ is the same product for the augmented column and residual. If H = sXᵀX + λ2 I is positive definite (full column rank, or λ2 > 0, which includes p > n; a nearly collinear full-rank design is positive definite but ill-conditioned), Φ > 0 and every coordinate satisfies Gᵢ < τ sᵢ, then ‖g⁺‖₂² < 2τ²Φ tr(H) and F(x) - F* < τ²Φ tr(H)/λ_min(H) ≤ τ² F(x) tr(H)/λ_min(H); when F(x) > 0, the relative error satisfies (F(x) - F*)/F(x) < τ² tr(H)/λ_min(H), a bound that is large when H is ill-conditioned. Case B with positive-definite H has F(x) = Φ = 0 and x is then the minimizer. Without positive definiteness (rank deficiency with λ2 = 0) this bound is not available, although a particular problem can still have one (for example two identical nonzero columns with λ = 0, where the objective depends on their sum only). Invariances of the positive-scale test (Gᵢ < τ sᵢ with sᵢ > 0): a common positive factor k on y and on every cᵢ, at the corresponding points kx, leaves each test and the relative bound unchanged and multiplies the absolute decrease bound by k²; a common positive factor on s, every cᵢ and λ2 leaves the tests and the relative bound unchanged; with λ2 = 0, adding a constant m to y while a free intercept column of ones (cᵢ = 0) shifts its coefficient by m leaves the residuals, the gradients and the positive-scale tests unchanged; case A, which depends only on X and λ2, is unchanged, and case B can become case C (the example above); with λ2 > 0 the intercept is ridge-penalized even with a zero L1 weight, so the shift changes Φ unless it is zero or maps the intercept to its negative; and with λ2 = 0 and k > 0, replacing column i by kXᵢ and cᵢ by kcᵢ, with the coefficient xᵢ/k, leaves the residual, Φ and the test of coordinate i unchanged.

Exact fits. When F* = 0 (for example a consistent system with λ2 = 0 and no L1 penalty at the minimizer, such as NormL1(0) with y in the range of X), hᵢ ρ tends to 0 at the minimizer. Near enough to it the computed residual can be dominated by rounding error, which need not be orthogonal to the columns, so the check need not pass, and a computed smooth scale of 0 refuses each coordinate outside cases A and B (C). If no proposal passes, another exit ends the solve (maxiter, an objective increase, a call or time limit, a callback, the Float32 stagnation exit or a numerical failure), and its point is assessed independently. For example X = ones(5, 1), y = fill(1e160, 5), loss scale 1e-100, NormL1(0) from x = 0 with the default options (without the loss scale the loss at the zero start overflows and solve! refuses the start): each proposal fails the ordinary comparison (the computed residual is parallel to the column) or, where the computed smooth scale is 0, case C, and PGD and APG return x = 1e160, the minimizer for the stored data, at maxiter (measured in the evidence). An exact fit at x = 0 with y = 0 is case B and passes.

gradtol. Above the floor 64 eps(T) (1.42e-14 in Float64, 7.63e-6 in Float32), the exact product τ sᵢ is proportional to gradtol, before the rounding and range guards above; a gradtol below the floor, including 0, uses the floor. In Float32 the default gradtol = 1e-5 is 1.3 times the floor, and near a good fit τ hᵢ ρ can be close to the rounding error of the computed gradient, so an accurate solve can end by the objective-increase exit or maxiter without passing. In Float64, accurate solves on problems with a large offset ended uncertified at gradtol = 1e-8, and the coordinatewise scale can need more iterations than the default maxiter where the λmax-based scale stopped earlier; the evidence records both.

Work. The column data occupy one Float64 and one Int per column in the state, allocated when the state is created. At the first check of a solve the column pass reads X once (O(np)), reads again (up to three times) each column whose plain sum is not finite or is below floatmin(Float64), and scans y once (O(n)). The check computes no λmax (the state's λmax stays -1) and no Xᵀy. Each check is O(p) beyond the gradient: the pass over the coordinates, ‖x‖ when λ2 is not a stored ±0, the stored-zero scan of x when y is zero and, for NormL1(λ, w), the scan of the weights by the constructor of the converted penalty. The scale reads the stored loss value and does not form the residual; APG's gradient at x, the only product with X of a check, forms it from the cached image (STOP-08). A check changes no counter other than that gradient's grad_calls. The column data are filled at the first check. In the tests, a check after the first allocates nothing with levels of type T, a BigFloat level of NormL1 and Rational{Int} levels of ElasticNet; a penalty whose levels are not of type T is converted at each check, which can allocate (for example with Rational{BigInt} levels).

Applies to. PGD and APG; least squares, raw or with one positive scale (IN-02), with NormL1(λ), NormL1(λ, w), ElasticNet(λ1, λ2) or ElasticNet(λ1, λ2, w), including all-zero weights and zero levels; dense Vector{T}; T = Float32 or Float64. The logistic loss and the matrix response use STOP-03, GroupNormL2 STOP-05, NormL2 STOP-17, NormL1Nonneg and IndNonnegative() STOP-16, and the other penalties STOP-07.

STOP-03 Scalar and weighted lasso residuals

Statement. For NormL1(λ0), the residual is the least-infinity-norm element of ∇f(x) + λ ∂‖x‖₁, with the working level λ from IN-02. The entry score is max(|∇fᵢ| - λ, 0) at xᵢ = 0 and |∇fᵢ + λ sign(xᵢ)| elsewhere. Without weights, the full-point call is ProximalBase's kkt_violation with the working penalty used for the objective. With NormL1(λ0, w) (in this check, only with the logistic loss), the call is kkt_violation(NormL1(T(λ0), w), ∇f(x), x), whose coefficients can differ from those of the objective. Entry i uses cᵢ = T(λ0) wᵢ, multiplied and rounded in T. Its score is max(|∇fᵢ| - cᵢ, 0) at zero and |∇fᵢ + cᵢ sign(xᵢ)| elsewhere. The Vector{T} weights are used as supplied.

For least squares on a dense vector, the coordinatewise check of STOP-21 holds the rule: it uses ProximalBase's signed entry scores (|∇fᵢ| - cᵢ at xᵢ = 0, not clipped at 0) with its own thresholds. The clipped full-point residual above, the scale and the entry thresholds below form the λmax-based check of the matrix response of IN-10 and of the logistic loss of IN-09.

The scale is S = max(λ, λmax), where λ = T(λ0) without weights and λ = maxᵢ cᵢ with weights, and λmax is the computed ‖∇f(0)‖∞: s‖XᵀY‖∞ for the least-squares matrix response and ‖Xᵀ(½ - y)‖∞ for the logistic loss (below). The equality maxᵢ cᵢ = T(λ0) * maximum(w) uses monotonicity of rounding. Unit weights reproduce the unweighted coefficients and scale. All-zero weights remove the penalty and give the scale S = λmax; a computed λmax = 0 then falls under the zero-scale rule in STOP-04.

For IN-10, use the unweighted scores above at every entry of B with ∇f(B) = s Xᵀ(XB − Y). Take the largest score over all entries, with λmax the largest absolute entry of the computed s XᵀY. Both quantities use the working arithmetic of the vector test. This is the entrywise maximum over the full matrix, compared with τ S in the unweighted full-point comparison of STOP-04; IN-10 accepts no weights, so no entry pass applies.

For a raw LogisticLoss with PGD or APG, ∇f is the logistic gradient and λmax = ‖Xᵀ(½ - y)‖∞, the norm of its value at zero (IN-09).

Entry thresholds with weights. With NormL1(λ0, w) and the logistic loss, a proposed stop must also hold entry by entry. Let sᵢ be ProximalBase's signed entry score kkt_violation(NormL1(T(λ0), w), ∇f(x), x, i): |∇fᵢ| - cᵢ at xᵢ = 0, which can be negative, and |∇fᵢ + cᵢ sign(xᵢ)| elsewhere. The maximum of these scores and zero is the full-point residual above. Each sᵢ must be strictly below τ max(cᵢ, λmax), computed in T, and that threshold must be at least floatmin(T). A negative score passes any threshold that passes the guard, so clipping at zero does not change the decision.

Each entry threshold is at most τ S, so the full-point comparison of STOP-04 is necessary, and it is made first. The entries are then compared one by one, stopping at the first failure, only when the full-point comparison passes and the largest coefficient λ = T(λ0) * maximum(w) exceeds the computed λmax. Otherwise every entry threshold equals τ S and the full-point comparison decides alone. A weight vector of ones with T(λ0) > λmax takes the entry pass when the full-point comparison passes, but its thresholds all equal τ S and its decision is that of NormL1(λ0).

Without the entry pass, one coefficient far above λmax (a large weight, or one that excludes a column) would set S for every entry, and a free or lightly penalized entry could pass with a derivative of order τ maxᵢ cᵢ (#464). The entry thresholds still contain λmax, which a free intercept can inflate (STOP-12). The entry scale max(cᵢ, λmax) contains cᵢ because near a stationary point a nonzero entry has |∇fᵢ| close to cᵢ, so its score combines two terms of size about cᵢ. It contains λmax as the gradient scale of the unweighted rule. In exact arithmetic, a common positive factor on loss and penalty scales every entry threshold and every score alike. In working precision, rounding and the normal-threshold guard can change the outcome, as in STOP-04.

A zero-weight coordinate has cᵢ = 0, is unpenalized and is scored by its full |∇fᵢ|, even with a large λ0. It is held to τ λmax, the threshold of the unpenalized problem. When the computed λmax is zero, its threshold is zero (NaN if τ is infinite), which fails the guard. No stop then passes, as with all-zero weights (STOP-04), although x = 0 may be the minimizer. Each coefficient must already satisfy IN-01: it is finite and normal when both factors are positive; a zero level or weight gives zero. An infinite coefficient iterate gives a nonfinite residual (Inf, or NaN if a NaN is read), which cannot pass.

STOP-04 Threshold arithmetic and scale guards

Statement. This clause is the threshold rule of the λmax-based checks (STOP-03, STOP-05, STOP-06, STOP-16, STOP-17). STOP-12 Accuracy interpretation and strict-option examples

Statement. Defaults maxiter=200, ftol=1e-6, xtol=1e-8 and gradtol=1e-5 are exploratory settings. Tightening them does not establish a specified error, and reducing gradtol below the floor in STOP-04 does not tighten that computed check. The residual uses rounded gradient products: underflow or cancellation can make a nonstationary point pass. The checks whose scale is formed from ∇f(0) (its ∞-norm λmax for the logistic loss and the matrix response with NormL1, STOP-03, and for NormL1Nonneg and IndNonnegative(), STOP-16; its largest group norm Λ for GroupNormL2, STOP-05; its Euclidean norm for NormL2, STOP-17) have this limit: a coordinate on a much smaller gradient scale than that reference can remain inaccurate even when the global relative residual is small. The reference is measured at zero. When a free coordinate is far from zero at the solution, as an intercept is with an uncentered (offset) response, its entry of ∇f(0) can make the reference norm, and so every threshold, much larger than the gradients near the solution. converged = true can then be reported with a materially wrong objective. The entry thresholds of STOP-03 do not change this; measured cases are in the entry-threshold evidence. For least squares, centering the response leaves the problem unchanged when an unpenalized intercept with no ridge term absorbs the shift, and can then help when the inflated entry belongs to that intercept (a logistic response must stay binary, IN-09). A smaller gradtol can help while it stays above the floor 64 eps(T). Neither is a general remedy. For least squares with NormL1 or ElasticNet on a dense vector, the coordinatewise check of STOP-21 replaces that scale (#487); its own limits (ill-conditioning, exact fits, Float32) are stated there. See working precision and stopping evidence.

For PGD, xtol bounds a step scaled by 1/L; the implied proximal-gradient residual is roughly L * xtol * max(norm(x), 1), depending on curvature, coefficient scale and penalty level. Even xtol = 1e-300 can accept a nonzero step, such as from [0.0] to [1e-310]. It is not an accuracy target.

STOP-13 Independent lasso gap bound

Statement. A check of a lasso solution. Let f(x) = s‖y - Xx‖²/2 with s > 0 and g(x) = λ‖x‖₁ with λ > 0 (an unweighted NormL1), let F = f + g, and let G be the element of ∇f(x) + λ ∂‖x‖₁ of smallest infinity norm, which is the independent KKT residual. Put k = norm(G, Inf) / λ. With the dual point θ = κ s (y - Xx), where κ = min(1, λ / norm(∇f(x), Inf)) and κ = 1 when ∇f(x) = 0, and D(θ) = ⟨θ, y⟩ - ‖θ‖²/(2s), the relative duality gap satisfies

\[\frac{F(x) - D(\theta)}{F(x)} \le 2k + k^2\]

in exact arithmetic, for s > 0, λ > 0 and F(x) > 0. This follows from F(x) - D(θ) = (1-κ)² f(x) + (1-κ) λ‖x‖₁ + κ⟨G, x⟩, 1 - κ ≤ k and F ≥ max(f, λ‖x‖₁). Evaluating k in Float64 from the returned x is an independent numerical check, not a rigorous floating-point certificate: the rounding errors of that evaluation are not bounded here.

Applies to. Unweighted NormL1; positive loss scale s, positive λ, and positive objective F(x).

Code. Analytic statement; no new solver certificate is implemented.

Tests. No implementation change; the stated exact-arithmetic derivation is retained for independent numerical checks.

STOP-19 The gap ceiling for a lasso check when the exact residual meets the threshold

Retired by #487. Least squares with unweighted NormL1 on a dense vector is now checked by the coordinatewise rule of STOP-21, whose thresholds are not τ max(λ, λmax), so the hypothesis below is not the check of any dense-vector least-squares lasso route. What a kept least-squares stop means is stated in STOP-21; STOP-13 remains the independent gap check. The text below is preserved as historical provenance.

Statement. Take the problem of STOP-13: unweighted NormL1(λ), s > 0, λ > 0 and F(x) > 0. Let τ and the scale S = max(λ, λmax) be those of STOP-03 and STOP-04, with λmax the computed value. A passed check compares only the computed residual with the rounded threshold, and STOP-12 allows a pass at a nonstationary point. The ceiling below needs the separate hypothesis that the exact residual satisfies ‖G‖∞ ≤ τ S. Under that hypothesis, put

a = τ S / λ = τ max(1, λmax / λ),   B = 2a + a².

Then k = ‖G‖∞ / λ ≤ a in STOP-13, and in exact arithmetic the relative duality gap at its dual point is at most 2k + k² ≤ B. The pointwise bound 2k + k² can be zero, for example at the zero minimizer with λ ≥ λmax; the ceiling B is the bound obtained from the stated exact-residual hypothesis.

B is at least 2τ + τ². At the Float32 floor τ = 64 eps(Float32) ≈ 7.63e-6, B is 1.53e-5 for λ ≥ λmax, 1.02e-4 for λ = 0.15 λmax and 7.6e-4 for λ = 0.02 λmax. Since STOP-04 does not let gradtol go below the floor, the ceiling of a Float32 check exceeds 1e-5 at every penalty level and gradtol. At the Float64 floor (τ ≈ 1.42e-14), B at λ = 0.02 λmax is 1.4e-12. The ceiling certifies a relative gap ε exactly when τ ≤ (λ/S) ε / (1 + √(1 + ε)), approximately ε λ / (2S) for small ε.

B is an upper bound, and gaps observed at Float32 stops are usually smaller (Float32 stopping evidence). The clause does not bound the difference between the computed and exact residuals, and it does not apply to weighted, group, elastic-net or constrained penalties.

Applies to. PGD and APG with unweighted NormL1; dense Vector{T}; T = Float32 or Float64; positive s, λ and F(x).

Change log

#487: least squares with NormL1 or ElasticNet (with or without weights) on a dense vector is checked by the coordinatewise rule of STOP-21, which replaces the scale max(maxᵢ cᵢ, λmax) and the #464 entry thresholds for these problems and computes no λmax. Exact zero scales (a column of stored ±0 entries with λ2 a stored ±0, and x and y of stored ±0 entries) pass when the computed score is finite and at most 0. So a zero-response solve started at x = 0 with a zero level or all-zero weights, whose proposals the λmax-based check refused (S = 0), now keeps its first proposal (under ftol = 0 the unchanged iterate makes none, STOP-10); with positive coefficients that start passed the earlier check too when a proposal was made and its threshold τ S cleared the normal-range guard. Other zero and nonfinite thresholds refuse, including an infinite threshold that STOP-04 accepts, and an exact or near-exact fit can return converged = false. The thresholds are carried as significand and exponent and converted to T once. A nonzero column whose plain sum of squares is below floatmin(Float64) or not finite gets its norm computed with scaling, and a nonzero column is classified as case D when that norm is 0 or not finite: the plan refused columns whose squares all underflow, and the maintainer chose the scaled norm (decision), together with declaring the stopping decisions with flushed subnormals unspecified. STOP-04 is scoped to the λmax-based checks; STOP-03, STOP-06 and STOP-08 summarize the coordinatewise route and keep the λmax-based rules for the other problems; the STOP-12 offset limitation is retired for these problems and kept for the others; and STOP-19 is retired. The STOP-06 example of a computed λmax that cancellation leaves at 1 described the λmax-based elastic-net check, which solve! no longer reaches. Measurements: coordinatewise-scale evidence.

#320, Stage 1: STOP-18 classifies returns by their flags, STOP-19 states the lasso gap ceiling that holds when the exact residual meets the check's threshold, and STOP-20 separates benchmark settings from library contracts. No solver behavior changes. Measurements: Float32 stopping evidence.

#464: weighted NormL1 holds each entry to τ max(cᵢ, λmax) when its largest coefficient exceeds λmax (STOP-03), and GroupNormL2 holds each group to τ max(cₖ, Λ) when its largest coefficient exceeds Λ (STOP-05); see also STOP-04 and STOP-08. Otherwise the decisions are unchanged. The offset-response limitation is stated in STOP-12. Measurements: entry-threshold evidence.
