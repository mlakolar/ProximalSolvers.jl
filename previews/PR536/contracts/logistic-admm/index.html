ADMM for sparse logistic regression

LogisticADMM solves the sparse logistic regression of IN-09 by the alternating direction method of multipliers on the predictor split t = X b. Each iteration approximately solves a lasso subproblem in the coefficients with the scalar coordinate step of CD-01, solves one scalar logistic proximal problem per observation, and updates the dual. The input gate is that of IN-09; the clauses below specify the solver, its subproblems, its stopping test, its result and its failure boundary. It is a solver of its own, not a method of the consensus ADMMSolver of ADM-01: its predictor and dual have one entry per observation.

The ideal iteration, with exact minimizations in exact arithmetic, is scaled two-block ADMM for min ℓ(t) + g(b) subject to t = X b, with ℓ(t) = Σᵢ {log(1 + e^{tᵢ}) - yᵢtᵢ}, the penalty g and the scaled dual u:

\[\begin{aligned} b^{k+1} &= \arg\min_b\ g(b)+\tfrac{\rho}{2}\|Xb-t^k+u^k\|_2^2,\\ t^{k+1} &= \arg\min_t\ \ell(t)+\tfrac{\rho}{2}\|t-Xb^{k+1}-u^k\|_2^2,\\ u^{k+1} &= u^k+Xb^{k+1}-t^{k+1}. \end{aligned}\]

The computed iteration replaces the first minimization by a coordinate-descent run within a pass budget, which can stop before the minimizer (LA-02), and the second by computed scalar roots (LA-03).

LA-01 Inputs, result and stop reasons

Statement.

LogisticADMM(; ρ = 0.25, maxiter = 10_000, tol = 1e-7, abstol = 1e-8, reltol = 1e-6, abstol_dual = abstol, inner) is passed to solve!(b, LogisticLoss(y, X), g, method; t = nothing, u = nothing) with ProximalBase's raw LogisticLoss(y, X), a penalty g that is NormL1(lambda) or NormL1(lambda, w), and a dense Vector{Float64} start b. It minimizes the summed logistic objective and penalty of IN-09 and PN-01. The level lambda is converted to Float64 once, the penalty weights are used as supplied, and ProximalBase's kkt_violation receives that converted penalty.

The constructor refuses, with an ArgumentError: a ρ whose conversion to Float64 is not finite or lies outside [1e-10, 1e10] (so ρ = 1e-11, 1e11, 0, a negative value, NaN and Inf are refused); a negative maxiter; a tol, abstol, reltol or abstol_dual that is not finite and nonnegative, or that is positive but rounds to zero in Float64; a keyword inner that is not a CDSolver; an inner that is not in cyclic order (order = :cyclic); and an inner with maxpasses = 0, with which the coefficient step could not move. A keyword ρ, tol, abstol, reltol or abstol_dual that is not a Real, or a maxiter that is not an Integer, raises a TypeError from the keyword signature. The positional constructor LogisticADMM(ρ, maxiter, tol, abstol, reltol, abstol_dual, inner) takes Real values, an Integer maxiter and a CDSolver; other argument types raise a MethodError. maxiter is stored as an Int: a nonnegative Integer above typemax(Int) raises an InexactError. The default inner is CDSolver(order = :cyclic, momentum = false, relative = false, tol = 1e-8, maxpasses = 1000); LA-02 uses its maxpasses and tol, not its relative, momentum or rng. The range of ρ bounds the exact width of the bracket of the scalar root by 1/ρ ≤ 1e10 (LA-03) and the dual u = w/ρ by 1e10 |w|. The default ρ = 1/4 is the bound on the curvature σ(1 - σ) of the loss of one observation. ρ is a tuning parameter: the iteration count depends on it, and the method is not presumed to be faster than ProximalGradientDescent or ProxNewton.

The inputs and the order of refusals are those of IN-09, with LogisticADMM in the place of ProximalGradientDescent, except that the first check is the loss: any loss other than a raw LogisticLoss or one Scaled wrapper of it is refused with an ArgumentError that names LogisticADMM and the least-squares solvers. A Scaled(LogisticLoss) is refused as in IN-09. The refusal of a nonbinary response names LogisticADMM. Keywords of solve! other than t and u and positional arguments after the solver are refused as for the other solvers. After that gate, LogisticADMM adds a check on memory ranges to the alias rule of IN-09: coefficients b whose memory range overlaps that of X, y, the scratch f.tmp of the loss or the penalty weights are refused with an ArgumentError, also where Base.mightalias reports no alias (an offset unsafe_wrap of the storage of X, for example). Then the start: a nonfinite predictor X * b, objective, gradient or KKT score at b raises an ArgumentError ("start from a point where f is finite", or the gradient or score variant). Then a supplied t and u, each in turn: it must be a Vector{Float64} (otherwise an ArgumentError) with one entry per row of X (otherwise a DimensionMismatch), its memory must not overlap that of b, X, y, f.tmp, the penalty weights or the other supplied vector (by Base.mightalias or by overlapping memory ranges, an ArgumentError), and its entries must be finite (an ArgumentError). Last, each with an ArgumentError: a supplied u whose product ρ * u is not finite; a supplied u for which the difference t - u of the starting predictor and u, formed directly in Float64, is not finite; and a working response t - w/ρ that is not finite. All refusals precede any write to b, t and u.

ADMMSolver refuses a LogisticLoss or a Scaled one, for every z, with an ArgumentError that names LogisticADMM. The refusal follows its keyword and option checks and precedes the check of z and any write to z or to a supplied u. CDSolver, in a call that reaches its logistic refusal (the precedence of its owner gate is listed in IN-09), refuses the loss with an ArgumentError that names ProximalGradientDescent, AccProxGradDescent, ProxNewton and LogisticADMM. AccProxGradDescent accepts the raw LogisticLoss within APG-08 and refuses the Scaled one as in IN-09.

State. The start is t⁰ = t when t is given and X * b otherwise, and u⁰ = u when u is given and (σ(t⁰) - y)/ρ otherwise, with σ(t) - y formed as in LA-03. The solve holds the dual as w = ρ u. It reports u = w/ρ, except that a supplied u is returned with its entries when no iteration completes. The default initial w lies in [-1, 1], and a later computed w can leave that interval by rounding (LA-03). With the default dual, an exact minimizer b of the same objective, with t⁰ = X b, is a fixed point of the ideal iteration. A warm start from another solver's solution or from a fit at another level can move; a computed solution can also coincide with an exact fixed point (an intercept that is exactly optimal, for example), and the number of iterations a warm start takes depends on the fit. In the tests a start at a ProxNewton solution converges in one iteration. u is the scaled dual of the ρ it was computed with: a restart with another ρ passes u * ρ_old / ρ_new. The start b warm-starts the first coefficient step and sets the default t⁰.

No warning is logged for any stop.

LA-02 The coefficient step and its inner accuracy

Statement.

The coefficient step approximately minimizes g(b) + (ρ/2) ‖X b - resp‖² with the response resp = t - w/ρ formed from the predictor and dual of the last completed iteration; within its pass budget it can stop before the minimizer. It runs the scalar coordinate step of CD-01 in cyclic order, without momentum, on the LSModel with response resp, the borrowed X and the loss scale ρ, and with the penalty with its level converted to Float64. It calls the private pass loop of CD, not the public solve! of CDSolver, so it repeats none of its validation and logs none of its warnings. The iterate is a private SparseIterate, loaded once from the start (the nonzero entries in index order) and carried from one iteration to the next, so that each step starts from the coefficients of the last completed iteration.

Seeding. Before each step the residual of the model is set to resp - X b, with the image X b formed by a matrix-vector product after the previous step (or at the start); the response alone changes between iterations, so the curvature cache of the model, the squared norms of the columns of X, is kept from one step to the next.

Tolerances and budget. The change test is absolute, in the units of the coefficients: a run stops after a full pass in which every coordinate changed by less than the threshold δ. The first run of each step has δ = inner.tol; 0 turns the test off, so that the run spends its passes. inner.maxpasses is the budget of coordinate passes of the step of one iteration, shared by its run and its refinements. inner.relative is not used.

Inner score and forcing target. After each run the score of the step is ProximalBase's kkt_violation of q = ρ Xᵀ(X b - resp) at the run's coefficients b, with X b from a fresh product; it does not use the residual maintained by coordinate descent, and it is NaN when q has a nonfinite entry. The forcing target is max(0.1 tol, 0.1 kkt), with kkt the score of the original problem at the coefficients of the last completed iteration. While the target is missed, δ is divided by ten and the run continues from its coefficients, at most four times and within the budget. The coefficients of the last run are the result of the step whether or not the target was met; inner_kkt and inner_met report its score and the comparison. The target does not decide the stop of the solve (LA-04): the score is formed from resp, whose rounding grows with max |t| and max |w|/ρ, so its computed value can stay above a target that the score of the original problem at the same point meets.

Every pass of every run is counted in passes, also in an iteration that a failed root discards.

Applies to. LogisticADMM.

LA-03 The scalar logistic proximal root and the dual update

Statement.

After the coefficient step, for each observation i, v = (X b)ᵢ + wᵢ/ρ and the predictor is the root of

\[h(s) = \sigma(s) - y_i + \rho\,(s - v),\qquad h'(s) = \sigma(s)\{1-\sigma(s)\} + \rho > 0 .\]

Residual. σ(s) - y is formed from e = exp(-|s|): for y = 0, 1/(1 + e) at s ≥ 0 and e/(1 + e) at s < 0; for y = 1, -e/(1 + e) at s ≥ 0 and -1/(1 + e) at s < 0. No exponential overflows. For y = 0 and s < 0 the value e/(1 + e) is positive while exp(-|s|) is positive (s above about -745.13), and subnormal for s below about -708.40 until exp(-|s|) underflows to 0; at s = -720 it is about 2.0e-313. The formula 1/(1 + exp(-s)) gives 0 once exp(-s) overflows, for s below -log(floatmax(Float64)) ≈ -709.78. σ(1 - σ) is e/(1 + e)².

Bracket. h is increasing and |σ - y| < 1, so in exact arithmetic the root lies in [v - 1/ρ, v] for y = 0 and in [v, v + 1/ρ] for y = 1. For y = 0, v = 0 and ρ = 1 the root solves s + σ(s) = 0, about -0.40106, inside (-1, 0); for y = 1 it is the mirror +0.40106. The working bracket is computed as follows: γ = inv(ρ) is rounded to Float64, then the far end v - γ (y = 0) or v + γ (y = 1) is rounded and clamped to ±floatmax(Float64), and v is the other end. It can be wider than 1/ρ (for v = 2⁸⁶, y = 1 and ρ = 1e-10 its width is 2³⁴ ≈ 1.7e10), and its far end can differ from the rounded exact end (for ρ = 3, v = inv(3.0) and y = 0 the lower end is 0). Near ±floatmax the exact root can lie outside it (for y = 0 and v = -floatmax(Float64), h(v) > 0 puts the root below v). A returned root lies in the working bracket.

Outcomes. With the computed terms r = σ(s) - y and q = ρ(s - v), the computed h = r + q and the threshold B = 4 eps(Float64) (|r| + |q|), the following hold; the order of operations of the loop is described at _la_root in the source.

  • A root with a nonfinite v fails, with no step. With a finite v, a root fails only at the step-budget test, which in each pass comes after the test |h| ≤ B and the test for a Float64 strictly inside the bracket. With a budget of one step, the root at y = 0, v = 500 and ρ = 0.001 succeeds; with budgets of one and two steps, the root at y = 0, v = 0 and ρ = 1 fails after one and two steps.
  • B is formed from the computed terms and is not a bound on the error of the computed h: for y = 0 and v = -746, exp(-746) underflows to 0, so r, h and B are 0 at s = v, and v is returned although the exact h(v) is positive.
  • When no Float64 lies strictly inside the bracket, the end with the smaller computed |h| is returned, lo on a tie: for y = 0, v = 2⁵⁵ + 8 and ρ = 1/4, |h| is 1 at both ends and lo is returned with no step.
  • At an s returned with success, the dual update w = ρ (v - s) is formed from the same v, and the computed h equals the computed σ(s) - y - w up to the sign of zero, since ρ(s - v) = -ρ(v - s) in floating point. After a failed root the caller leaves the loop over the observations before it forms w. A successful root can have |h| = 1 and need not keep w in [-1, 1]: in the example above h = -1 and w = 2.
  • root_iterations adds up, over the roots, the points that become s after v (Newton points or midpoints).

Step budget. cap defaults to _LA_ROOT_CAP = 3 (34 + 1074 + 2) = 3330. No bound on the number of steps a root needs is claimed. A failed root ends the solve with :prox_failed (LA-05).

Accuracy. The accuracy is stated as a backward error. For a returned s, let v' be the argument for which s is an exact root, v' = s + (σ(s) - y)/ρ in exact arithmetic. The tests check |v' - v| ≤ 32 eps(Float64) max(|v|, |s|) on the arguments they cover; this is a tested bound, not a proved one. These formulas and stopping rules do not establish a small error in units of the last place of s. For y = 0, v = 500 and ρ = 0.001 the root is about 4.15e-17, the computed h is exactly 0 at s = 0, 1e-17 and 1e-16, and the returned s = 0 has a backward error of about 0.09 units of eps(Float64) · 500. For y = 1, v = 40 and ρ = 1 the returned root is 40, while the root is 40 + 4.2e-18.

Reported dual. predictor_residual is formed at the return from the reported u, through ŵ = ρ .* u (LA-01), which can differ from the working w by rounding.

LA-04 Residuals, stopping and certification

Statement.

With b, t and w from the last completed iteration and b⁺, t⁺ and w⁺ from the current one, the residuals and thresholds are

\[r=\|Xb^+-t^+\|_2,\quad s=\rho\|X^\top(t^+-t)\|_2,\quad \epsilon_{\rm pri}=\sqrt n\,\text{abstol}+\text{reltol}\max(\|Xb^+\|_2,\|t^+\|_2),\quad \epsilon_{\rm dual}=\sqrt p\,\text{abstol}_{\rm dual}+\text{reltol}\,\|X^\top w^+\|_2,\]

with p = length(b), which counts an intercept column of X, and w⁺ = ρ u⁺ the working dual. Each residual and threshold is formed from a product of its own: X b⁺ from the coefficient step, Xᵀ(t⁺ - t) and Xᵀw⁺. In exact arithmetic Xᵀw⁺ also equals q - ρ Xᵀ(t⁺ - t), with q the vector of the last run of the coefficient step (LA-02); the rounding of those two terms can leave a nonzero value where the direct product is 0, so the threshold does not use that form.

After each completed iteration the solver forms the objective F at b⁺, from ProximalBase's value_and_gradient_from_predictor! at X b⁺ plus the penalty, and the KKT score kkt = kkt_violation(g, G, b⁺) of the original problem from the gradient G that the same call forms. The iteration converges when r ≤ ε_pri, s ≤ ε_dual and kkt ≤ tol; a residual or threshold that is not finite fails its test. The score of the coefficient step does not enter the test. Small residuals with a score above tol cannot give :converged, and neither can a score below tol with a residual above its threshold; such an iteration continues the solve or ends it with :stalled or :maxiter (LA-01). With mismatched settings, such as a large abstol and a small tol, the solve can run to maxiter.

Products. A completed iteration whose coefficient step makes m runs (1 ≤ m ≤ 5) forms 2m + 3 products with X or Xᵀ of cost O(np): X b and Xᵀ(X b - resp) per run (the last X b is the X b⁺ that the primal residual and its threshold share), Xᵀ(t⁺ - t), Xᵀw⁺, and the gradient Xᵀd in ProximalBase's oracle. An iteration discarded by a failed root forms the 2m products of its coefficient step only. The start forms X b and Xᵀd, and the return forms Xᵀŵ for dual_kkt (LA-01). The coordinate passes are counted separately in passes.

converged = true reports computed checks: the residual tests above, and the largest ProximalBase KKT score of the original problem at the returned point, max(0, |Gⱼ| - cⱼ) at a zero coefficient and |Gⱼ + cⱼ sign(bⱼ)| elsewhere, finite and at most tol. It is not an exact-arithmetic certificate, it does not bound the error of the coefficients, and the gradient it uses is rounded. The scale of the score is that of the gradient of the summed loss; a mean-scale level is passed as n * λ (IN-09). The problem need not have a minimizer (IN-09, Unattained minima); the solver does not detect that case. In exact arithmetic the score is 1-Lipschitz in the gradient and σ is 1/4-Lipschitz, so |dual_kkt - kkt| ≤ maxⱼ ‖Xⱼ‖₂ (√n predictor_residual + r/4) at the returned b, t and u, with r = ‖X b - t‖₂.

Applies to. LogisticADMM.

LA-05 Mutation and failure boundary

Statement.

The solve computes on private buffers: the coefficients, their image, the predictor, the dual, their values in the current iteration, the response and the gradients. At a normal return (:converged, :maxiter, :stalled or :prox_failed) it writes the caller's b once, with the returned coefficients. A supplied t and u are written then too, with the predictor and u = w/ρ of the last completed iteration, when an iteration completed; when none completed they keep their entries and are the returned t and u. dual_kkt and predictor_residual are formed from the returned b, t and u before any of these writes; the residuals, thresholds, inner_kkt and inner_met are those of the last completed iteration (the dual residual uses the predictor before that iteration, eps_dual the working dual, and the inner score the response of its coefficient step). The borrowed X, y, the penalty weights and the scratch f.tmp of the loss are not written.

A failed scalar root (LA-03), from a nonfinite v or the step cap, discards its iteration: the solve returns the last completed iteration, or the start, with :prox_failed and converged = false. Its residuals, thresholds, inner_kkt and inner_met are those of the last completed iteration; its passes and root_iterations include the work of the discarded iteration.

An exception propagates and leaves the caller's b, t and u unchanged; so does a refusal. The exceptions include an ArgumentError from CD-04 in the coefficient step, such as a column curvature that underflows or overflows or a coordinate step that is not finite, and an ErrorException when the objective or the KKT score of the original problem at the coefficients of an iteration is not finite. dual_kkt, the Float64 evaluation at the return from the reported u with the range limits stated in LA-01, is reported when it is not finite, without an exception.

Applies to. LogisticADMM; the result and mutation rules of RES-01 and RES-03 refer here for it.
