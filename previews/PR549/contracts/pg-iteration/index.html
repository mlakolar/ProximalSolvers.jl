Proximal gradient iteration

Scope

ProximalGradientDescent uses dense Float32 or Float64 vectors for the vector-response problems in the support matrix. PGD and APG share _solve_gradient!, stopping and results, including for the matrix lasso in IN-10. Graphical-lasso PGD also uses _solve_gradient!, with the step and stopping rules in PGD-07 and PGD-08.

Problem

Minimize F(x) = s‖Xx - y‖²/2 + g(x). The supported penalties are NormL1 and ElasticNet (each with optional weights, IN-03), NormLinf, NormL2 (IN-11), GroupNormL2, unweighted NormL1Nonneg and IndNonnegative(). NormL1Nonneg and IndNonnegative() enforce x ≥ 0 under IN-07. PGD also accepts IndBox (including IndBallLinf) and four-coefficient IndBallL2 under IN-08.

Notation

s denotes the loss scale. L is inverse step size, and α and β are line-search settings. The retained source uses α_f or model.α for s. See working precision.

Preconditions and refusals

PGD-05 Construction and supported settings

Statement. ProximalGradientDescent() uses LineSearches.BackTracking(). normalized=true, debias>0, preconditioner!=:none and custom line searches are not implemented and are rejected. The compatibility keywords at their defaults are accepted and not stored; their complete unsupported-feature policy is DEF-01.

Solvers, options and BackTracking each have a validating inner constructor. Settings are checked as given and after conversion, so solve! does not revalidate constructed settings; APG separately checks its two factors in the iterate precision before constructing state. Construct ProximalOptions(...) to configure the shared stopping rules in STOP-01. Its compatibility gap keywords do not request a PGD/APG certificate: a positive gaptol is refused, gapevery must be positive but has no effect, and neither is stored. Float64 storage of some tolerances and diagnostics does not increase native Float32 coefficient accuracy.

Applies to. PGD and APG; dense Vector{T}; T = Float32 or Float64.

Algorithm

PGD-01 Image, loss, gradient and proximal iteration order

Statement. PGD keeps the native least-squares image buffers and uses ProximalBase's value and gradient oracles. It forms the image before value and gradient, takes scalar steps, and calls value_and_prox! for each penalty step. Unweighted lasso levels follow IN-02.

At an iteration it saves the last accepted point, objective components and gradient; multiplies L by the line search's α; then repeats the following trial until LS-01 accepts it:

  1. Form the gradient-step point xhat = x_previous - ∇f(x_previous)/L.
  2. Apply value_and_prox! with step 1/L into the caller's iterate.
  3. Form the trial's least-squares image and value.
  4. Apply the line-search decision, refreshing the gradient when its mode requires it.

The accepted point's gradient is refreshed if the line search did not already update it, including a zero-step exit in gradient mode. The shared gradient driver checks the accepted objective for finiteness, counts completed steps, and records the trace. The default stopping sequence is in PGD-04, and the explicit-policy checkpoint and cap rules are in STOP-22.

The prox is soft thresholding for NormL1, with entry level λ wⱼ for weights; soft thresholding followed by the ridge weight for ElasticNet; the point minus its projection onto the ℓ1 ball of radius λ/L for NormLinf (a common clipping level); and block soft thresholding for GroupNormL2, scaling a block v_G by max(0, 1 - γ λ wₖ/‖v_G‖₂) at step γ, with a zero block left zero. For NormL2(λ), the exact-arithmetic formula at step γ is zero when ‖v‖₂ ≤ γ λ and (1 - γ λ/‖v‖₂) v otherwise. The solver delegates the evaluation to ProximalBase, which can round the cutoff comparison. The working-precision conventions for these oracles are FP-01. The level of NormL2 is the working level of IN-02, and its stop check is STOP-17.

For unweighted NormL1Nonneg(λ), the prox is componentwise max(v - λ/L, 0). For IndNonnegative(), ProximalBase projects componentwise with max(v, 0). Both operators enforce nonnegativity at each step. The feasible-start and input requirements are IN-07, and the dedicated constrained stationarity check is STOP-16.

For the box and ball indicators in IN-08, the proximal step projects onto the corresponding set using ProximalBase. The generic stationarity check is STOP-07.

For matrix-response entrywise lasso, the same sequence carries coefficient, gradient and displacement buffers of size p × K and predictor and residual buffers of size n × K. The predictor is XB, and ProximalBase uses it to form sXᵀ(XB − Y). The scalar proximal step applies the working level to every entry. The image, step and stopping norms use the corresponding entrywise array reductions.

For a raw LogisticLoss with NormL1, the same sequence forms the logistic image and value; IN-09 specifies the inputs and the oracles.

Applies to. ProximalGradientDescent; dense vectors and the matrices in IN-10.

PGD-02 Initial curvature scale

Statement. Before the first iteration, the inverse step is the T-rounded min(1, s ‖X‖_F²), using an upper bound for ‖X‖_F. A positive result that rounds below floatmin(T) is raised to that value. If the product is at least one, or X = 0, the start is 1.

For B ↦ XB in the Frobenius norm, each response column has the same operator. Summing their squared norms gives the operator bound ‖X‖₂, attained by taking one column to be a right singular vector. The initial curvature uses the same design Frobenius norm for any number of responses.

When the positive Frobenius bound is below one, it exceeds the spectral curvature s ‖X‖₂² by the factor ‖X‖_F² / ‖X‖₂², at most the rank of X, up to rounding and the floor. A positive bound far below one therefore starts below one; a bound at least one starts at one. For example, X = I(256)/16 with s = 1 has curvature 1/256 and starts at 1. The line search multiplies this estimate by its decrease factor (default 0.9) before each iteration and raises it on rejected trials. Historical extreme-scale measurements are retained in ORACLE-01.

PGD obtains the same initialization through a column-wise scan, with the Frobenius-bound calculation when required; APG uses the shared bound machinery directly. For a raw LogisticLoss, the initial estimate of PGD uses the factor ¼ in place of s (IN-09), and that of APG the same factor (APG-08). This is an initial estimate only; the acceptance tests that follow are those of the line-search clauses of each solver. A finite starting loss is required; penalty overflow at a finite starting point follows RES-01.

Applies to. PGD and APG; dense vectors and the matrices in IN-10.

Stopping

The shared default policy is STOP-01, including the three OR-combined proposals, the independent check of the accepted point and the separate objective-increase and stagnation exits. The dense vector least-squares pilot accepts an explicit policy at the accepted-step boundary specified in STOP-22.

Postconditions

PGD-04 Trace, callback and limit sequencing

Statement. store_trace stores an entry for every completed iteration; display and callback invocation are sampled by print_every. When storing, a callback receives the trace; otherwise it receives the current trace state. A callback returning true stops the solve. extended_trace requests the additional diagnostic data. These options use the existing engine and do not introduce a separate logging framework.

Call and time limits are checked after a complete iteration, including any proposed-stop check and callback. A zero call limit is unlimited; counts can overshoot a positive limit by the work of one iteration. maxiter caps completed iterations, and iteration_converged means iterations == maxiter, not successful convergence. The Float32 stagnation check follows tracing and any callback due at that iteration; it does not change cadence.

The displayed |g(x)| is an internal gradient/proximal stopping residual, not the user's penalty value. Displayed |x - x'| and trace dxx are iterate-change diagnostics, and backtrack_steps counts line-search trials subject to each method's zero-step and stationarity-check rules. They do not describe changes in the statistical model.

Applies to. PGD and APG; dense Vector{T}; T = Float32 or Float64.

Floating-point rules

PGD-03 Native products and diagnostics

Statement. The coefficient, residual and image buffers keep T arithmetic. ProximalBase evaluates the loss value and can recover a value whose intermediate squares leave the range of T; PGD does not recover an overflowing or underflowing image X x or gradient s fl(Xᵀd). Finite inputs therefore do not imply finite intermediates. The explicit range-safe and residual qualifications are in FP-01, LS-04 and STOP-04.

Applies to. PGD and APG; dense Vector{T}; T = Float32 or Float64.

Known limits

Native Float32 can stagnate before a requested accuracy is attained. The relevant exits and accuracy limitations are STOP-11 and STOP-12; line-search limitations are recorded in LS-07 and LS-08.

Tests

Existing tests listed at each clause cover image order, fused prox, counts, constructors, callbacks and rollback. No numerical implementation or new test tier is introduced here.

Retired historical wording

Retired: older penalty inventory

Retired by issue #416. The quotation below is preserved verbatim as historical provenance, not a current support restriction. The authoritative supported combinations are in IN-01, consistent with current src/solve.jl dispatch and the test/linf.jl and test/group_pgd.jl suites: NormLinf and GroupNormL2 are supported by both PGD and APG. The older inventory omitted these already-supported cases; no solver feature is added by retiring it.

The penalty is validated once by _check_penalty in _check_data. For an x that is not a BlockIterate it accepts a NormL1 for all solvers (with weights, _check_l1_weights: a Vector{T} of the length of x, finite and nonnegative entries, no alias with x, a level T(λ0) that is zero or normal, and the coefficients T(λ0) wⱼ finite and, for positive factors, normal) or an unweighted ElasticNet for all solvers and refuses any other penalty; a BlockIterate has its own method, for a GroupNormL2.
