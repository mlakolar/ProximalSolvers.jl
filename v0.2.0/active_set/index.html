Active-set proximal gradient

When the solution is sparse and there are many coordinates, most of the work of a proximal gradient method goes into coordinates that stay at zero. ActiveAccProxGradDescent runs the accelerated method of Proximal gradient methods on an active set of coordinates only, and grows the set by the coordinates that violate the optimality conditions. It applies to a penalty that separates over coordinates, such as the weighted ℓ1 norm, or over groups, as the group lasso does.

Optimality conditions

Let $g(x) = \sum_k g_k(x_k)$, with $x_k$ a coordinate or a group of coordinates, and $f$ smooth and convex. Then $x$ minimizes $f + g$ if and only if, for every $k$,

\[0 \in \nabla_k f(x) + \partial g_k(x_k).\]

For a coordinate at zero this reads $-\nabla_k f(x) \in \partial g_k(0)$, and kkt_violation(g, ∇f(x), x, k) of ProximalBase measures how far it fails:

Penalty$\partial g_k(0)$violation at $x_k = 0$
$\lambda w_k \lvert x_k\rvert$ (NormL1)$[-\lambda w_k, \lambda w_k]$$\lvert\nabla_k f(x)\rvert - \lambda w_k$
$\lambda w_k x_k + \iota_{x_k \ge 0}$ (NormL1Nonneg)$(-\infty, \lambda w_k]$$-\nabla_k f(x) - \lambda w_k$
$\lambda w_k \lVert x_{G_k}\rVert_2$ (GroupNormL2)$\{v : \lVert v\rVert_2 \le \lambda w_k\}$$\lVert\nabla_{G_k} f(x)\rVert_2 - \lambda w_k$

A positive violation means that moving the coordinate away from zero decreases the objective.

The algorithm

The active set $S$ is the set of stored entries of a SparseIterate, or of active groups of an AtomIterate. Each outer iteration

  1. removes the coordinates that are zero from $S$;
  2. computes the full gradient $\nabla f(x)$, and the violations of the coordinates outside $S$;
  3. stops if no coordinate outside $S$ has a positive violation and $x$ minimizes $f + g$ over $S$ (the last inner solve converged, or $x = 0$). It also stops after a converged inner solve that changed no entry of $x$, since the next solve would repeat it; this happens within rounding of the smallest penalty level with a zero solution, where the check flags a coordinate that the proximal step leaves at zero;
  4. adds violators to $S$, the largest violations first;
  5. minimizes $f + g$ over the vectors supported on $S$, by AccProxGradDescent warm started from the current values.

Stopping. Before success, the full proximal fixed-point residual norm(x - prox(g, x - gradient), Inf) must also be at most gradtol * max(1, norm(initial_gradient, Inf)). An inner objective-change flag alone does not establish full optimality. A repeated inner solution that fails this check returns converged = false. For a convex problem the exact zero fixed-point residual characterizes optimality; a small residual needs additional curvature assumptions to bound coefficient or objective error.

The restricted problem. Step 5 runs on restrict(f, S) and restrict(g, S), the functions of $x_S$ that $f$ and $g$ become on the vectors supported on $S$. For least squares, $\tfrac12\|y - Xx\|^2$ restricted to $S$ is $\tfrac12\|y - X_S x_S\|^2$, with the columns $S$ of $X$. An inner iteration then costs $O(n|S|)$ instead of $O(np)$, and the only step that involves all coordinates is the gradient of step 2.

How many to add. At a poor starting point, such as zero for a small penalty, most coordinates violate the optimality conditions, and adding all of them would make the first inner problem as large as the original one. With $s$ entries left in $S$ after step 1, step 4 adds the largest violations until they hold

\[\max\{\texttt{max\_add},\ (\texttt{growth} - 1)\, s\}\]

entries, together with any violators tied with the last one added (the coordinates of a group at zero share the violation of the group, so a tie keeps a group whole). The active set thus grows by a factor of at most growth over the nonzero entries, or by max_add entries if that is more: it reaches a large support in a few outer iterations, and stays close to the support. growth = 1 adds max_add entries at a time, and max_add = typemax(Int) removes the limit.

Groups

For GroupNormL2, an AtomIterate that shares the groups of the penalty, AtomIterate(x, g.groups), makes the groups the coordinates, so that a group joins and leaves the active set whole. A dense starting point takes this route when the groups of $g$ cover all coordinates. With a SparseIterate, the coordinates of a group at zero share the violation of the group, and a violating group joins whole as well.

Settings and results

ActiveAccProxGradDescent(; β, α, max_add, growth, max_outer) takes the backtracking factors $\beta$ and $\alpha$ of the inner method, the limits above, and the largest number of outer iterations. The ProximalOptions apply to each inner solve. The result, an ActiveSetResults, reports the outer and the summed inner iterations, whether the method stopped at step 3 (converged), whether every inner solve converged, the largest violation outside the active set at the solution, and the size of the active set.
